Nurses in Northern Ireland have voted overwhelmingly to take industrial action, including strike action, over pay.
They will take continuous action short of strike from 3 December - this sort of action includes working to rule and a ban on overtime.
The Royal College of Nursing (RCN) said it was working with other unions for a collective day of strike action, which one its leaders said would probably be early January.
The RCN said the industrial action was the result of the failure of the Northern Ireland Executive and Department of Health to implement the 2026-27 pay award.
A pay review body recommended a 3.3% rise for Agenda for Change staff, which includes nurses, health visitors, midwives, ambulance staff, porters and cleaners, from April this year.
While nursing staff in England and Wales have received their pay awards, nurses in Northern Ireland are still waiting for theirs.
RCN members working in health and social care can now be expected on the picket lines again, seven years after voting yes to taking strike action for the first time in Northern Ireland.
The union said 93% of its members who responded , voted to take action short of strike and strike action.
Prof Rita Devlin, executive director of the RCN in Northern Ireland, said the result sent "an unequivocal message" to the Northern Ireland Executive and the Department of Health that nursing staff would "no longer tolerate this annual cycle of uncertainty, delay and disregard when it comes to their pay".
She said nursing staff in Northern Ireland were being left behind "because of a failure to provide the necessary funding".
Devlin said members were "being forced into industrial action" to secure the pay award they are due, rather than being focused solely on delivering patient care.
"Nursing staff do not take the decision to strike lightly," she said.
"They understand better than anyone the challenges facing our health service and the impact industrial action can have.
"But they have reached a point where they feel they have no other option."
As winter pressure are building across the health and social care system, Devlin said the last place nurses want to be is on a picket line.
"Responsibility for this situation sits squarely with our political leaders, who have once again failed to ensure nursing staff are treated fairly and paid on time," she added.
