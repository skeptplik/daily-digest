For families living on either side of the Estonian-Russian border, the impending closure threatens months of separation. People carrying suitcases queued at the Friendship Bridge linking Narva and Ivangorod, rushing to visit relatives before restrictions take effect. Some had given up their jobs in Estonia to be closer to elderly parents in Russia.
The closure follows sabotage attacks targeting Estonia’s defence industry, attributed by its security services to Russian intelligence. Moscow dismisses the allegations as unfounded and accuses Tallinn of seeking to erect a new “Iron Curtain”.
Beyond the human cost, the decision threatens businesses dependent on cross-border travel. Local authorities estimate annual revenue losses of between €500,000 and €600,000 for Narva and Setomaa, while the government is preparing a €2 million support package over six months.
All five road crossings will shut at 7am on 1 December for an initial six months, with rail freight through Narva continuing subject to sanctions. Around 3,000 people have signed a petition calling for exemptions on family grounds, particularly for those with sick or elderly relatives. The government will review the situation after six months.
