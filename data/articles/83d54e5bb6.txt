Okayama – The government presented a draft outline Tuesday proposing to convert part of a loss-making railway line in western Japan to bus services.
The draft outline was shared at a meeting of a government-led council discussing the future of the 68.5-kilometer Geibi Line section between Bitchu-Kojiro Station in Niimi, Okayama Prefecture, and Bingo-Shobara Station in Shobara, Hiroshima Prefecture, operated by West Japan Railway, or JR West.
The council, comprising local governments, JR West and the central government, is expected to compile its conclusions in March 2027.
The latest move marks the first indication of the council’s direction and could affect discussions over other unprofitable local railway lines across Japan.
The number of passengers on the most loss-making part of the section stood at an average of only 25 per day per kilometer in fiscal 2023-2025.
The draft said that, from the viewpoint of building a sustainable transportation system, a bus-based transport service would be more appropriate.
Officials from Okayama and Hiroshima prefectures, the cities of Niimi and Shobara, and JR West attended Tuesday’s meeting.
The draft outline, presented by the transport ministry’s Chugoku Transport and Tourism Bureau, estimated that bus services would cut operating costs by around ¥530 million annually. It also pointed out that railways face a serious shortage of personnel needed to maintain infrastructure and that recovery from disasters takes time, leading the bureau to conclude that bus conversion would have advantages.
The council serves as a forum for the central government, local governments along the line, and railway operators to discuss the future of unprofitable railway lines and their potential conversion to bus services.
Initially, JR West and local governments along the Geibi Line discussed some sections of the line on their own, but talks stalled between JR West, which sought reorganization, and local governments, which wanted to maintain railway services. In March 2024, the council was formed with the involvement of the central government.
