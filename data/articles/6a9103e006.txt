Germany's flagship airline Lufthansa plans to cut 1,000 jobs at its Frankfurt and Munich hubs, according to a company letter seen by German news agency DPA and news outlets.
The letter was reportedly signed by Lufthansa CEO Jens Ritter and four other board members.
Why is Lufthansa cutting 1,000 jobs?
This is reportedly down to ongoing financial losses. In the first nine months of the year, the airline recorded an operating loss of €180 million ($197 million).
The executive board said on average the loss amounts to €20 million every month, according to media reports. The board stated in the letter that "this is the third year in a row of not being financially successful."
"Lufthansa is currently not competitive in terms of its cost structure, efficiency and productivity," the letter said.
At the end of 2025, the Lufthansa group employed some 67,100 people, up 1% from the previous year.
How else is Lufthansa planning to save money?
The company also plans to cut employee travel benefits, restructure its hub operations to reduce crew overnight stays abroad, and reduce short- and medium-haul capacity by replacing some A320 aircraft with A220 jets.
Furthermore, it plans to equip ten aircraft per month with Starlink, a satellite internet service developed by tech billionaire Elon Musk. The airline hopes to generate "additional revenue” by monetizing onboard internet access, according to the letter.
The time frame of the cuts is unclear. It's also unknown whether the cuts were a part of a previously announced downsizing.
In September 2025, Lufthansa said it would slash 4,000 administrative positions due to the implementation of AI and digitalization.
In regards to this year, Lufthansa has faced rising fuel costs spurred by the US-Israeli war with Iran, further complicating its financial outlook.
