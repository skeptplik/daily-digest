The US' national cyber director Sean Cairncross has been fired from his role, a source close to the White House told the AFP news agency on Thursday, following a wave of tech outages across the country.
Kirsten Davies, the defence department's chief information officer, is set to take over from Cairncross on a temporary basis, the source said.
"Congratulations to the new National Cyber Director Kirsten Davies — look forward to working together to strengthen America’s cybersecurity," US Secretary of State Marco Rubio wrote in a post on social media.
At the Pentagon, Davies has acted as the "principal advisor" to Secretary of Defence Pete Hegseth "on all matters relating to information management, information technology, cybersecurity, and information assurance," her bio says.
The move comes after outages have hit travel, communications and banking, as well as an internal system used by the National Security Agency in recent weeks.
The national cyber director is tasked with advising the president on cybersecurity policy and strategy, including information security and data protection as well as deterrence of "malicious" activity.
The director also coordinates cyber policy across the federal government. The office was first established in 2021.
In March, the Trump administration released a seven-page document detailing its cyber strategy.
It pledged to "act swiftly, deliberately, and proactively to disable cyber threats" by streamlining cyber regulations, modernising federal information systems, hardening critical infrastructure and maintaining an edge in AI.
