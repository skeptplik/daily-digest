WASHINGTON — A little-known but influential panel that recommends which preventive services must be covered by insurance will meet this week for the first time in more than a year, with a membership overhauled by health secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.
The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force will meet to review recommendations related to cervical cancer, perinatal depression, and unhealthy alcohol use in teens and adults, all of which have not been updated in at least five years, and have been awaiting finalization since last year, when Kennedy canceled the group’s meetings.
The task force will also hear an “evidence presentation on healthy lifestyles to prevent cardiovascular disease in adults,” and hear presentations from subject matter experts on screening for chronic kidney disease, colorectal cancer, and lung cancer, as well as a discussion on “task force methods,” according to a notice sent Wednesday by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality.
