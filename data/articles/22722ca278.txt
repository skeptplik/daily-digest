After stepping away from Formula 1 in 2002, Nike is reentering the sport after much speculation.
This time, Nike will be the official apparel and uniform supplier of the motorsport organization beginning in 2027, the brand announced on Thursday and ahead of the Las Vegas Grand Prix.
“United by a relentless drive to push boundaries,” Nike and Formula 1 said the partnership is uniting two brands sitting at the intersection of sport, speed and culture. No confirmation was given about how long the “long-term partnership” was inked.
Nike will create capsule collections, featuring sportswear styles, spanning footwear, apparel and accessories alongside co-branded uniforms worn by Formula 1 staff at its global events. Moreover, the brand will create race-weekend experiences, activations and products for fans to engage with the sport trackside and beyond.

A first look at the partnership will be released Friday with a limited-edition Las Vegas Grand Prix and Nike capsule collection at the F1 Hub and at the Nike Las Vegas store. More details are set to follow in 2027 as the partnership evolves its product, athlete innovation and race weekend experiences.
Also encompassed in the deal is its partnership with the women’s racing championship, F1 Academy, with Nike, which will focus on “advancing female racing” by working with the sport’s athletes to develop performance products using the brand’s innovation, design and technology and tapping into the athletes’ insights.
“Formula 1 is driven by competition, speed and a constant push to innovate,” said Elliott Hill, president and chief executive officer at Nike, in a statement. “That’s an athlete mindset and it’s one we share at Nike. Together, we have an incredible opportunity to bring new energy to the sport, serve its athletes and inspire fans around the world.”
On Thursday afternoon, Nike posted an Instagram teaser with a three-photo carousel of a Formula 1 car with the caption, “We usually sample size 9…” The photo features a blank car livery except for the Swoosh logo on the tail of the wing and the words “Sample. Property of Nike. Not for Resale” written on the side.
The 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix is an apt place for the brand to announce the return of its entry into the motorsport category — as the season comes to a close and at the last U.S. race weekend.
“Formula 1 and Nike are two of the world’s most iconic brands, united by a relentless pursuit of what’s next,” said Stefano Domenicali, president and chief executive officer of Formula 1. “Formula 1 is experiencing unprecedented global growth, reaching hundreds of millions of fans and engaging one of the most passionate, diverse and dynamic communities in sport. Nike’s unique ability to sit at the intersection of sport, culture and inspiration makes them the ideal partner as we enter our next chapter.”
This executive noted that the partnership is a powerful reflection of Formula 1’s scale, ambition and global momentum. “Together, we have an extraordinary opportunity to redefine how fans connect with our sport, create lasting impact across both Formula 1 and F1 Academy and inspire the next generation of racers, creators and innovators around the world. This is far more than a commercial partnership,” Domenicali said.
The history between Nike and the sport also runs deep.
Famously, the seven-time and record-holding World Drivers’ champion, Michael Schumacher, had a custom pair of sneakers made for him by Nike with red suede, black accents and a white logo. Consumer-facing footwear, the Nike Air Zoom Schu was also released as part of the deal. The nine-year personal endorsement between the brand and Schumacher was signed in 1996 when he started at Ferrari; it lasted until the brand exited motorsport sponsorships in 2002.
Speculation about a potential Nike deal was fueled earlier this year when Nike executives were seen in attendance at the 2026 Miami Grand Prix. The brand also made the Cadillac Formula 1 team — which inked a multiyear deal with Tommy Hilfiger as the official apparel partner in 2025 — a special-edition Dunk sneaker to celebrate its entry into the sport as the 11th team and commemorate its first home race in Miami.
Notably, this past September, LVMH executive and son of Bernard Arnault, Alexandre Arnault, joined the board of directors at Nike. LVMH entered into a 10-year global partnership deal with Formula 1, starting in 2025. And since then, the likes of other LVMH brands such as Tag Heuer, Moët & Chandon, Louis Vuitton and Sephora have all had a major presence within the paddock.
Nike’s entry into the category will see the brand going head-to-head with its rivals. Puma, which Nike will replace as an official Formula 1 partner, has been part of the sport since the 1980s, before signing partnership deals with teams in the early 2000s and now boasts Ferrari, McLaren and Aston Martin team deals. Adidas made its foray into the sport in 2025 as a partner with the Mercedes and Audi teams. Lululemon tapped into the category by partnering with Lewis Hamilton in February 2025.
Thursday also marked Nike’s investor day — president Amy Montagne said she expects the brand’s sportswear segment to contribute approximately $1 billion of revenue growth from the beginning of fiscal 2028 through fiscal 2031.
