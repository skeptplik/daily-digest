Sixty years ago, after two failed attempts, Ford won the 24 Hours of Le Mans with its GT40 race car. In the years since, the Blue Oval has been back to France more than a few times, with varying success. It could never replicate the GT40’s success winning overall, but it celebrated the 40th anniversary with a class win. Now it wants another shot at the really big trophy, and it debuted the car during the Detroit Lions-Chicago Bears football game today: meet the GT41.
“Revealing the Ford GT41 is a significant moment for everyone involved in this program,” said Dan Sayers, manager of Ford Racing’s Hypercar program. “It’s the result of an enormous amount of work across our engineering, design, and development teams and an important milestone on our journey towards 2027. We know the scale of the challenge ahead—the level of competition in Hypercar is incredibly high, and there are no shortcuts to success. Our focus has been on building the right foundations, bringing together the right people and making sure we learn as much as possible from every stage of development.”
Like many other entrants in the World Endurance Championship’s Hypercar class, Ford has chosen the rear-wheel-drive LMDh technical approach rather than building the GT41 entirely from scratch, as Ferrari and Aston Martin do with their cars. LMDh cars start with one of four approved carbon fiber chassis—in this case, Ford is working with Oreca, which has also partnered with Genesis and Acura.
