While Paris and London remain ‘firmly established favourites’ for train travellers, Rail Europe data shows that Ravenna, Bolzano, Linz and theme-park gateways are attracting ‘growing attention’.
The Italian city of Ravenna is among the European destinations seeing the fastest growth in searches by travel agents and tour operators, according to new data by Rail Europe.
The train booking platform’s “Track Record” report, which covers booking and search activity between 1 October 2025 and 31 October 2026, also points to growing interest in destinations from the Italian Alps to the station serving Disneyland Paris.
Paris, London and Italy’s major cities remain “firmly established favourites” for train travel, Rail Europe said. But the data suggests that the “next interesting client itinerary may begin somewhere less obvious” – from a city break in eastern Germany to a family trip to a theme park.
Ravenna tops the list for advance bookings
Known for its stunning mosaics, Ravenna is “attracting growing attention” and stood out in two parts of the report.
It was among the fastest-rising searches by travel professionals, while passengers who booked directly through Rail Europe reserved their journeys an average of 57 days before departure. That was the longest average booking lead time recorded in the report’s consumer analysis.
The two findings reflect separate groups, with one tracking where agents and tour operators searched and the other measuring how far ahead travellers booked.
Bolzano and the French Alps draw attention
Bolzano in South Tyrol also gained ground in searches by travel agents and tour operators following the 2026 Winter Olympic Games. Elsewhere in northern Italy, Bergamo drew growing interest from consumers.
Across the border in France, Chambéry and Grenoble were among the fastest-rising destinations for consumers on the platform.
Both cities are gateways to the French Alps, and travellers planning a rail trip that takes in both France and northern Italy can again use the Paris-Milan line, which reopened in 2025.
Theme parks and city breaks feature in searches
There is also a “different kind of destination emerging in the data”, Rail Europe said, with two train stations serving major European theme parks also seeing a rise in search interest among both consumers and travel professionals. Ringsheim is the railway gateway to Germany’s Europa-Park, while Marne-la-Vallée serves Disneyland Paris.
“Here, the railway station is closely tied to the experience the traveller wants to book,” Rail Europe said. “For travel professionals, knowing which stations are attracting interest can be as useful as knowing which cities are popular.”
Austria’s Linz and the German cities of Leipzig and Dresden were also among the fastest-rising searches overall. Rail Europe highlighted concert halls and Christmas markets among the attractions in these cities, offering ideas for trips built around a performance or a festive weekend.
