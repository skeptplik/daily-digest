Fashion by Informa just launched “The U.S. Fashion Playbook: How Global Brands Enter, Scale and Win,” which the organization described as a practical resource for global fashion brands and retailers looking to enter and scale in the United States.
The trade show organizer said it’s a guide to the realities of doing business in the U.S. and covers the key areas of legal compliance, supply chain and marketing strategy.
The overall U.S. apparel market is expected to grow from around $691 billion by 2030 from $363 billion in 2018, according to Fashion by Informa’s 2026 U.S. Fashion Consumer Outlook Report. In response, global fashion brands are increasingly looking to the region as a growth market. But the path to growth is complex, as companies face issues that include not fully understanding the U.S. consumer through to the legalities and the need to build a resilient supply chain.
The playbook was created to deal with these issues while providing a clear, practical framework for brands at every stage of their “U.S. expansion journey.”
Designed for both current Fashion by Informa event participants—across Coterie, Magic, Project, Sourcing and Offprice—and international fashion brands and retailers still evaluating their U.S. entry strategy, the playbook covers four essential areas: understanding American consumer behavior and market expectations, navigating key legal and regulatory compliance requirements, building an efficient supply chain from factory to customer and executing marketing strategies to drive brand awareness, trust and sales.
With consumer insights, the report’s authors said shoppers in the U.S. are not only buying products, “they are buying the story, purpose and identity associated with them.”
“This creates opportunities for international brands because heritage and craftsmanship can become powerful differentiators,” the report said. “French contemporary brands such as Sandro and Maje have expanded internationally by combining Parisian identity with accessible premium positioning, demonstrating how cultural origin can become a commercial advantage when translated effectively.”
But the playbook was quick to note that heritage alone is not enough to sway shoppers to a brand. Consumers in the U.S. expect convenience and speedy service that is also seamless. Shoppers are also more value-driven.
But where does a business start? The playbook said before entering the U.S. market, retailers and brands need to translate research into strategic decisions. The playbook’s authors said the priority is to define clear business objectives, which include expected revenue, investment levels, distribution strategy and a timeline.
“The second step is understanding the ideal customer,” the playbook stated. “Companies must identify who they want to reach, where those consumers are located, how they shop and what motivates purchasing decisions.” And the last step is to prioritize opportunities instead of attempting immediate nationwide expansion. “Selecting the right markets, channels and partnerships creates stronger foundations for sustainable growth.”
The playbook reiterates the need to understand the regulatory environment, compliance and also legal issues—such as protecting intellectual property.
Then there’s the supply chain, which the playbook authors described as critical to succeeding in the U.S. market. For international fashion companies entering the U.S., supply chain strategy has become one of the most important foundations of market success. The challenge is not simply producing and shipping products. Brands must manage long distances, import requirements, inventory complexity, customer expectations around speed and the growing demand for seamless experiences across physical and digital channels.
“In fashion, creativity attracts attention, but operational excellence determines whether growth can be sustained,” the playbook stated. “Behind every successful collection, campaign and retail experience lies an increasingly complex supply chain capable of moving products efficiently from factories to consumers.”
The playbook noted that a key strategic decision is whether to ship directly from origin countries—which minimizes upfront investment at the cost of slower delivery, customs delays and complex returns—or invest in local inventory to meet customer expectations faster. When establishing local infrastructure, warehouse location directly impacts delivery speed and operational costs, leading many international brands to choose major logistics hubs like New Jersey, Pennsylvania, California and Texas for their proximity to consumer markets and transportation networks.
The playbook includes a step-by-step 90-day action plan, designed to equip global fashion professionals with the insights and operational framework needed to move seamlessly from strategy to execution in the competitive U.S. market.
Emilie Lewis, vice president of events at Fashion by Informa, said the playbook was created “specifically for international brands who are serious about winning in the U.S. market, whether they’re just starting to explore the opportunity or actively planning their entry.”
“This resource isn’t just for our existing community—it’s an opportunity to reach new brands and retailers across the industry who are exploring U.S. expansion and to support them with the same level of expertise and insight we offer our current customers,” Lewis added.
