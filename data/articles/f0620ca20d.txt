Kimi Antonelli has won the F1 drivers' championship, his Mercedes team is constructors' champion, and there are just three rounds left in the season.
But the final trio of grand prix, starting this Sunday (AEDT) in Las Vegas, are not just for show.
These three races, for every team other than Mercedes, are the most important three weekends of the 2026 season.
Why? It's all about the money.
While fans mostly focus on the drivers' championship, it is the constructors' championship that carries the most significance for teams.
The prize money handed out at the end of the year is tied to where you finish in the team's championship. The higher you finish, the more money you receive.
While the exact figure each season is not made public, UK outlet The Independent published a rough guide to potential earnings this season, based of figures gathered from 2025.
As the champion, Mercedes is expected to be awarded about $US140 million ($208 million).
But the other 10 teams in this year's championship have not solidified their spot in the championship, meaning there is plenty to race for in Las Vegas, Qatar and Abu Dhabi.
There is a maximum of 129 points a team can win to finish the season; they would need their drivers to finish first and second in all three grand prix.
Ferrari currently sits in second place with 510 points, 95 ahead of Red Bull. The Scuderia just need 34 more points from the final three races to be certain of second place.
But the battle for third between Red Bull and McLaren should be a belter.
Red Bull sits on 415 points, led by superstar Max Verstappen, who sits second in the drivers' championship with 279 points to his name.
Red Bull sits just 17 points ahead of McLaren, which has been an up-and-down season for drivers Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri.
The difference between third and fourth this season appears to be worth roughly $US9 million ($13.3 million), and every point will be crucial.
"We'll do our best to make sure it goes down to the wire and in our favour," McLaren race director Randy Singh told the F1 Nation podcast this week about his team's battle with Red Bull.
"We're treating it like a mini championship on our side, so we're just aiming to do our best at each race."
Racing Bulls appears all but set to place fifth, and Alpine looks to be safe in sixth, with any team below them needing to increase their season tally by more than double just to match them before this weekend's action.
The final five teams in the championship: Haas (31), Audi (28), Williams (12), Aston Martin (7) and Cadillac (0) are separated by just 31 points.
Haas and Audi are separated by just three points with three rounds to go, while Williams and Aston Martin are separated by just five.
If either of these teams can secure a remarkable and unlikely podium placing in the final three races, they will almost certainly leapfrog at least one rival and bag themselves a few extra million dollars.
F1 to have full-time stewards from 2027
Formula 1 will have full-time stewards from next season in a bid to create greater consistency in officiating.
Stewards play a crucial, and at times defining, role in races, as they are in charge of regulating rule breaches and handing out penalties.
Until now, stewards for F1 races have been selected from a pool on a race-by-race basis.
But with the demand for greater consistency in how rules are enforced and penalties handed down, a full-time group will be introduced.
It is expected that a team of 12-15 stewards will make up the team, with a smaller pool starting next season, supported by members of the current stewards pool used.
"The recruitment of full-time stewards is a significant step in our longstanding mission of building a dedicated officiating ecosystem in the FIA Formula 1 World Championship; it is also an investment in the future of our sport," FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem said.
