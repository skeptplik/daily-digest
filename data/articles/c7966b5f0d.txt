From hotels with dedicated gaming rooms to real-life destinations featured in blockbuster titles, video games are increasingly influencing holiday plans. New data reveals a surge in searches for gaming-themed stays, with the release of Grand Theft Auto VI helping fuel the trend.
After years of waiting, video game fans have been celebrating the launch of Grand Theft Auto VI, with stories of people locking themselves away for days at a time to play the game going viral.
Now, it seems as if the long-awaited launch is inspiring travel.
New research – called “Get a Room” – from Hotels.com shows that travellers are on the hunt for gaming-inspired stays, which range from escapes which feel familiar to long-time players to immersive hotel stays designed for marathon gaming sessions.
It comes after experts said so-called “gami-vacations” would be one of the top trends for 2026, with gamers travelling to real-life locations featured in titles such as Ghost of Tsushima, Assassin’s Creed, and God of War. Tourism boards have also been partnering with game developers to tap into the trend.
It certainly seems to be paying off. The Hotels.com data reveals that global searches for properties with gaming rooms have surged by 220% from September to November 2026, while searches for hotels offering room service grew by 65% over the same period, suggesting people are taking trips in order to switch off and settle into their own virtual world.
Nostalgic destinations and gaming-ready hotels
In terms of trending destinations, it’s nostalgia which features the most. The data revealed that searches for Miami have jumped 70% year-over-year, while searches for Panama City Beach are up 130%. Both cities inspired the fictional “Vice City”, where GTA VI is set. It seems as if people are keen to experience neon-lit streets and palm-lined boulevards for themselves – even if they’re staying inside playing the game.
Some hotels are already getting involved with the trend, offering dedicated gaming facilities for guests who want to fit in a few sessions between sightseeing.
In Germany’s capital, the nhow Berlin hotel has purpose-built gaming rooms with high-spec equipment and console setups, while Sweden's Steam Hotel has a dedicated gaming suite designed for playing with friends.
Gaming getaways aren't just limited to city breaks, either. The Kandima Maldives resort has its own PlaySpace, complete with gaming facilities and social areas where guests can switch between virtual adventures and a spot of island relaxation.
In 2027, the trend will go one step further. A new Minecraft World Hotel is set to open near London next year, giving fans the chance to sleep inside the world of the best-selling video game of all time.
Gami-vacations go up a gear
It’s not just GTA and hotels which are part of the trend – gaming holidays, or “gami-vacations”, are becoming an increasingly popular way to travel, too.
Fans are increasingly heading to real-life locations featured in their favourite video games, seeking out experiences inspired by virtual adventures or travelling to major gaming conventions.
The trend is being driven by the popularity of titles such as Ghost of Tsushima and Assassin's Creed, alongside a growing appetite for hobby-based travel. Travellers are choosing to immerse themselves in places connected to their interests and bring their favourite fictional worlds into real life.
There are plenty of destinations to choose from. Norway's Jotunheimen mountains feature in God of War, while Scotland provides the backdrop for parts of Assassin's Creed.
Fans of Ghost of Tsushima can visit Tsushima Island in Japan, while Montana in the US is associated with Far Cry 5 and offers the chance to explore the spectacular landscapes of Glacier National Park.
Some trips take the gaming experience a step further, with apps offering missions, quests, challenges, rewards and badges, turning sightseeing into something resembling a real-life game.
Others plan their holidays around major gaming events and conventions, including Germany's Gamescom and Paris Games Week.
Tourism boards are also waking up to the potential of gaming tourism, with some partnering directly with developers to put their destinations on the map. Czechia, for example, has worked with the creators of Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2 to promote locations featured in the game.
