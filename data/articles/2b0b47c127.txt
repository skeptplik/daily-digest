With the launch of World of Warcraft: Forever there's a whole new world filled with niche gear and strange baubles to collect. But one item in particular has drawn the ire of the creators of a popular completionist add-on.
That add-on is All the Things, a must-install for collectors like me, as it acts as a database of all the gear, mounts, titles, crafting recipes, and super rare drops in the game. Once you collect something, it is hidden in the add-on, thus tracking your progress and making for a fantastic tool for picking the game apart.
On occasion, specific items have written notes attached by the All the Things development team, as is true of a special World of Warcraft: Forever toy, the Pouch of Violet Dust. This is a promotional item that was obtainable by watching two hours of WoW Forever streams between the 4th and 12th November.
It's a powerful toy! When used on a campfire, it changes the fire's colour to purple and extends the buffs provided by it from the usual one hour to two hours. But the sticking point with the item isn't its existence, rather the fact that it's going to be removed in early December, not long from now.
This is what the creators of the All the Things add-on don't like. "A week-long, Twitch-only window for an item that gets taken away a month later is exactly the kind of FOMO that disheartens collectors," the creators said in a note attached to the item in the mod. "It can't be earned by playing the game at all. Even those who get it don't get to keep it."
They aren't alone in feeling this way. Comments below the item's listing on the Wowhead database are equally scorching. "I love FOMO in their explicitly non-fomo game," wrote user TheWhiteRose.
"Just... Why?" added user Darrenic427. "They could very easily just patch out the camp buff duration and keep the visual effect, but Blizzard just doesn't like pretty fire I guess. Figures that they finally give us access to purple fire but then take it away after a month."
The battle between collectors and time-limited FOMO items is one that has been waged for about as long as these sorts of items have been a part of games. Though for the pouch, it's an especially weird case: an item that's cool to have but so temporary in nature that it makes it both important to completionists and strangely pointless to care about. Whether it makes a return in a later update for World of Warcraft: Forever and doesn't require out-of-game actions to obtain, and doesn't disappear, we'll have to wait and see.
WoW Forever itself is proving popular among players - me included. It's added a staggering amount of new quests and many new features to the original version of World of Warcraft, which, in turn, has injected the game with an impressive amount of new items to collect. Completionists will likely be glued to the game for a long time.
