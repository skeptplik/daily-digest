Just like the rest of us, developers the world over are playing GTA 6. Just like the rest of us, they’re also finding themselves blown away by just how dramatically advanced and impressive GTA 6 is proving to be. However, as professionals with a far, far firmer idea of how the sausage gets made than regular punters, precisely how impressed some developers are is speaking volumes about just what a technical achievement GTA 6 really does represent.
Naughty Dog world design director Michael Barclay, presently working on Intergalactic: The Heretic Prophet, minces no words.
“I’ve made video games for 19 years and GTA VI appears to me as a Bugatti Veyron would to a nervous Victorian child,” writes Barclay via social media.
Barclay is far from alone, with a host of developers in the replies to Barclay’s comment expressing like sentiments in the days following GTA 6’s release.
Payday 2 game director David Goldfarb, who previously worked on Mirror’s Edge and a variety of Battlefield games, is similarly stunned.
“Been making games over 30 years… Can’t even find words for what I’m playing now,” he writes, later noting he’s “never been this floored by anything ever.”
Character artist Del Walker (Suicide Squad: Kill the Justice League, The Last of Us: Part II, Star Wars: Jedi - Survivor) quips he simply can’t explain what he’s seeing despite his background in the business.
“Every GTA clip gets sent to me in the group chat with. ‘You’re a game dev. How did they do this?” he writes. “Brother, I wasn’t there when they opened the portal? This is not magic I recognize.”
GTA 6 arrived late last week, smashing records as the biggest entertainment launch of all time, with retail sales reaching $3.79 billion in its first 24 hours. In my review for IGN I called it “the single most impressive video game I have ever played,” something that is making increasingly more sense to me the more other developers I note making comments like the above.
