The FBI is investigating a suspected breach of a nationwide database used by federal, state and local law enforcement agencies to share criminal justice information, CNN reported on Friday (November 20, 2026), citing two people briefed on the investigation.
The apparent compromise of the National Crime Information Center database would mark the second intrusion involving an FBI system in recent months and the latest in a string of cyber incidents that have alarmed U.S. officials over the past week.
The agency did not respond to a request for comment from Reuters.
The FBI database is used by law enforcement agencies nationwide to store and share information on warrants, criminal histories and background checks.
The U.S. Agriculture Department was also breached by hackers in cyberattacks, U.S. media reported on Friday.
It remains unclear whether these two incidents are related and who is responsible for them.
U.S. President Donald Trump has sought to calm the public and urged people to "relax", while officials said the various outages were not linked to criminal activity.
The U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) told employees that a system used to facilitate payments to farmers was targeted in a cyberattack, NBC reported, citing two former USDA officials.
The hackers may use the payments system as a backdoor to access more sensitive material, the former officials told NBC.
The USDA deals with "a bunch of scary biologic issues" and "if an adversary or a competitor were to have access to that same information... they could use our own research against us," a former official was quoted as saying.
Previous high-profile cyber disruptions over the past week have included a global outage affecting hundreds of millions of Microsoft users and a nationwide halt to air travel that disrupted more than 18,000 flights.
The newly uncovered breaches at the FBI and USDA were described as hacks, which are different from outages like the ones that afflicted Microsoft Outlook and the Federal Aviation Administration.
In September, a hacking group known as ShinyHunters targeted sensitive employee personal data from an FBI jobs website.
The White House fired cybersecurity chief Sean Cairncross this week as criticism mounted over the string of major outages.
