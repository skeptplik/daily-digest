UNESCO World Heritage sites in Japan are becoming increasingly popular as wedding venues, attracting couples seeking to make lifelong memories at locations they believe will endure, even as many conventional wedding venues close due to financial difficulties.
Some couples even travel from abroad to hold weddings at such sites. Local governments are welcoming the trend, as they hope to balance the preservation and utilization of cultural properties.
Memolead Corp., a wedding and funeral services company based in the city of Maebashi, Gunma Prefecture, began accepting reservations in October for weddings at Tomioka Silk Mill in the city of Tomioka.
Couples can use a multipurpose hall in the West Cocoon Warehouse, a designated national treasure built in 1872 to store silkworm cocoons, and its front yard for wedding ceremonies and receptions. As open flames are strictly prohibited at the venue, meals are served in the form of boxed lunches.
Memolead plans to transport all necessary equipment into the venue by hand to avoid damaging cultural properties. “We have to be very careful with the preparations,” company official Mizuki Tamura said.
Nikko Toshogu Shrine in the city of Nikko, Tochigi Prefecture, is scheduled to host 45 weddings this year.
According to wedding planner Harue Honda, the shrine, known for its lavishly decorated buildings, previously attracted mainly couples who specifically sought to wed at a World Heritage site.
But this changed after many wedding venues closed amid the COVID-19 pandemic. In recent years, more couples choose Nikko Toshogu Shrine because they want to create memories at a place that will remain.
Tourists sometimes watch wedding ceremonies. One couple said they were happy to receive applause and words of congratulations from onlookers, according to Honda.
Another popular spot for weddings is Itsukushima Shrine in the city of Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima Prefecture. The shrine, known for its vermilion corridors that stand out against the blue sky and sea, once accepted around 400 weddings a year but now limits ceremonies to two couples per day. Slots are filled a year in advance.
Wedding planner Maki Shinbara said that many newlyweds look back on their weddings at Itsukushima Shrine as a special opportunity to exchange vows at a historic World Heritage site, and that attending relatives are often deeply moved.
Weddings at the Tomioka Silk Mill are expected to begin around next March. The Tomioka city government, which manages the facility, has offered the multipurpose hall in the West Cocoon Warehouse for public rental since 2022. It has even served as a concert venue.
A city official said Tomioka welcomes the use of the hall as a wedding venue, adding, “We hope many people will use the facility so that it can be preserved as a ’living heritage.’”
