Japanese companies operating in Russia are facing growing uncertainty as the Russian government is blocking foreign firms from withdrawing from the country.
Some Japanese companies have left the Russian market since the start of Moscow’s invasion of Ukraine in 2022, while others are maintaining a presence there.
Earlier this month, Russian President Vladimir Putin signed decrees placing Japan Tobacco’s assets in Russia under the temporary management of a Russian company.
The move deals a blow to Japan Tobacco as Russia is a key market for the company. The Japanese government has called on Russia to reverse the move at an early date, but the outlook remains uncertain.
Japan Tobacco launched operations in Russia in 1999 and now holds nearly 40% of the country’s cigarette market. Russia accounts for about 10% of the company’s revenue and roughly 25% of its adjusted operating profit.
Following its invasion of Ukraine, Moscow has introduced a rule that makes it difficult for foreign companies to withdraw from the Russian market.
Foreign companies seeking to leave Russia are generally forced to sell their assets at steep discounts, often receiving only a fraction of their market value, according to Takafumi Nakai, head of the Institute for Russian & NIS Economic Studies, a think tank in Japan.
A recent survey conducted by Yale University showed that 11 out of 76 Japanese companies surveyed have withdrawn from Russia. Many others suspended operations or froze investment plans in the country.
Fast Retailing, operator of the Uniqlo casual clothing chain, suspended its Russian business in 2022, while Toyota ended vehicle production in the country.
Ajinomoto has halted sales of seasonings and food products in Russia, while continuing some activities, including research operations.
“We have not yet decided whether to completely abandon our long-standing business in Russia,” Ajinomoto President and CEO Shigeo Nakamura said at a news conference on Nov. 9.
Moscow’s move against Japan Tobacco “may be intended to put pressure on Japan at a time when the United States and Europe are not fully aligned on sanctions against Russia,” Nakai said.
