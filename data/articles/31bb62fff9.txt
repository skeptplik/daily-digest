Sat, 7 Nov, 1:05 pm, Final score, Cats 52, Demons 56
Football is emotional, and big games even more so.
It's something North Melbourne players are becoming veterans at understanding as their club heads into its fourth straight grand final this week. They are aiming for a third consecutive premiership.
But for Kangaroo star forward Jenna Bruton, there's an extra layer to it.
This season, she marked her 100th game in the qualifying final against Carlton.
She deeply wished her mum and aunt, who both died from brain cancer a few years before, could have been there.
"It made me pretty sad, obviously. But it is sad," she said of achieving her milestone without them.
"I want people to know that mum was a massive part of my journey."
Bruton's story is well known in AFLW circles. In late 2015 her mum, Suzy, was diagnosed with cancer. A top draft prospect, Bruton omitted herself from the historic inaugural 2016 draft to become her full-time carer.
She didn't want to commit to anything if she couldn't manage her mum's treatments and help her where needed.
And with her mother's diagnosis, she "fell out of love with the game for a little bit".
"They did say mum, her life expectancy was probably lucky to get one year [after diagnosis], and she lasted five or six," she said.
During that time, Bruton made her way into the AFLW system after her partner and star player, Jasmine Garner, submitted her belated draft nomination for her.
"She did it for me," Bruton said. "I wasn't actually going to do it. She did the application and I didn't have a choice. I'm glad she did."
Bruton was taken with pick 11 by the Western Bulldogs in 2017, before signing with North Melbourne as an expansion player for the 2019 season.
Her mother died a week before the first game of the 2022 season.
Just three months earlier, her aunt Andrea, her mother's sister, died from the same cancer.
"You're more likely to win TattsLotto than two people in your family get the same brain cancer. So it was pretty hard on my nan and everyone," Bruton said.
"They said it's not genetic at all, which is crazy that it was both of them. They say it's just bad luck.
"We think about them a lot."
Bruton said her aunt's Facebook posts about her football journey now came up as memories.
If Garner had never put Bruton's name down, she would probably still be back in Trentham, working the 400-hectare potato farm she grew up on.
Her goal song, Hot Potato by The Wiggles, is a call back to this, along with being a favourite of her niece and nephew.
She is now a three-time premiership player; once with the Western Bulldogs and twice with North Melbourne.
A big part of that journey has been her family, Garner and their dogs Bronson and Honey.
"My mum's other sister is just up the road from dad, so I go up and see her a lot and she comes down to the footy. I feel like I'm closer to mum through them," she said.
"Then I've got a niece and nephew. Sort of feel like they're a part of mum. So, they've helped us a lot through losing mum.
"Polly, my niece, she was born a couple of months before mum passed away. So mum did get to meet her, which was so good. She definitely keeps the family together."
She said her sadness during big games was also mixed with a lot of good emotions.
"I actually said to Jammin [Garner] after my 100th, it was probably like the best day," she said.
"Just with family and it was just so good. I don't really like the attention, but it was one of my favourite days."
The pair will be hoping those emotions come flooding back this Friday with another premiership, the club's third straight, if they are able to defeat Melbourne in the grand final.
