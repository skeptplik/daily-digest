Somewhere between Halloween and the holiday party bonanza, Friendsgiving has become fashion’s favorite excuse for a long and unhurried soirée. This year, hosts went all out with candlelit suppers, art gallery takeovers, and apartment hangouts a’plenty. Here’s a glimpse inside the rooms.
Net-a-Porter x Jessica McCormack
The holidays are off to a twinkling start, courtesy of jewelry designer Jessica McCormack and Net-a-Porter. An elegant Friendsgiving dinner saw both parties host a takeover of Salon 94 on the Upper East Side, with a finely decorated table and a mega-watt guest list.
Sarah Paulson, Christy Turlington, Naomi Watts, Shalom Brune-Franklin, and Rebecca Henderson were among the crowd, along with clients, all ready to celebrate exclusive access to the celebrity-loved jewel’s pieces available on the e-tailer. (Christmas wish list, anyone?)
A long table in The Stone Room was lined with low, lush runners of crimson dahlias, burnt-orange chrysanthemums, and inky calla lilies, punctuated by sprays of yellow orchids and tall taper candles. The three-course dinner included sea bass dressed in rainbow carrots, and a dessert of maple pumpkin pie with salted pecan brittle: a seasonal finale worth saving room for.
For McCormack, the company mattered as much as the menu. “The best evenings are the ones that bring together an interesting, inspiring mix of people,” she said, calling the night “the perfect way to spend an evening in one of my favorite cities.” Net-a-Porter CEO Heather Kaminetsky agreed: “The holiday season is such a special time to bring people together.” —Freya Drohan
Thom Browne x Mr Porter
If there was ever a match made in sartorial heaven, it would be Thom Browne and Mr Porter. To mark the debut of the designer’s exclusive capsule—a seventeen-piece collection of signature knit and quilted styles in festive hues and classic patterns—created in collaboration with the luxury retailer, a debonair group convened at George McNally’s new restaurant Faux in Tribeca.
Co-hosted by Browne and Mr Porter’s buying director Daniel Todd, the evening commenced in the restaurant’s checkered-floor subterranean cocktail lounge, where a coterie of tastemakers including Tramell Tillman, Isaac Cole Powell, Laufey, and Michael Hainey sipped dirty martinis and discussed their upcoming Thanksgiving plans.
In a low-lit corner, Anna Toonk offered tarot readings as a nod to the hand-painted tarot-inspired tiles that lined the dining room above. To announce dinnertime, a brass trio from the Brooklyn Chamber Orchestra led the crowd upstairs with a musical procession, before guests settled in for generous platters of steak frites.
The evening’s details also paid a charming tribute to Browne’s beloved dachshund Hector, whose likeness appeared everywhere from matchbooks to menus, gingerbread cookies, and custom aprons to take home, just in time for holiday meal preparation. The designer, however, has given the real-life mascot the night off. “He’s not great in crowds,” Browne told Vogue, eyes twinkling. —Clara Hillis
Gabriella Khalil x Olivia von Halle
If any brand has earned the right to announce the start of pajama season, it’s Olivia von Halle. The luxury silk-loungewear label invited guests to dress in their nighttime finery to mark the end-of-year revelry over cocktails and a seated dinner in the private dining room of Le Veau d’Or, the beloved Upper East Side French bistro that has become near impossible to snag a reservation at.
Creative director Gabriella Khalil co-hosted the evening, which spotlighted the label’s playful yet polished sensibility. Among those who ‘slipped into something a little more comfortable’ for the occasion were Tina Leung, Marjon Carlos, Jessica Wang, Melanie Grant, Vogue’s Lisa Aiken, Christina Grasso, and Chloe Crane-Leroux.
After getting warmed up over festive tipples, the group sat down to a bespoke menu served family-style. Think: platters of steak au poivre and market fish, followed by decadent chocolate profiteroles. —Freya Drohan
Jordan Firstman
Is Friendsgiving the definitive holiday pregame? Actor and director Jordan Firstman certainly made the case in true Club Kid fashion with the help of Substack and A24.
Hosted in the actual Lower East Side apartment featured in the buzzy film, the evening gathered editors, writers, creatives, and Firstman’s inner circle for an intimate, home-cooked feast.
Warm air wafted from the oven upon entering the apartment, mingling with the autumnal breeze drifting in from the slightly open windows in the pre-war home. Martinis were stirred, not shaken; wine—a Tuscan red and a light-bodied white—and mead from Honey’s flowed; and Firstman’s drink of choice, Diet Coke, took residence in the ice-filled bathtub playing the role of cooler for the evening.
On the candlelit table lay intentionally broken loaves of rustic olive focaccia and crumbling wheels of Parmesan, framing a smorgasbord of radicchio salad, porchetta, roasted honeynut squash spread atop a bed of pistachio pesto, and towering platters of saucy meatballs, alongside a heartfelt nod to the film in the form of al dente bucatini tossed in red sauce.
Guests promptly twirled noodles onto ceramic plates before raising their glasses in a toast led by Substack’s head of entertainment Alison Werner and Firstman himself, setting the tone for a remarkable night among a chosen family full of vibrant cultural voices. For dessert? A rich chocolate mousse, served with dollops of hand-whipped cream or caramelized hazelnut topping.
Though the homemade spread was undeniably decadent, top billing was rightfully reserved for the conversation and unadulterated community as attendees lounged on plush sofas, lingered near the dining table, and roamed about the apartment to meet one another, catch up—or, as they say in the scene, kiki. —Edgar Gatsinzi
Pamela Anderson x Pandora
Pamela Anderson and Pandora amplified the lush, garden-like interiors of Palma restaurant in Manhattan’s West Village, transforming the beloved neighborhood Italian spot into a candlelit floral wonderland for a heartfelt Friendsgiving celebration.
The entire restaurant was reserved for the occasion, with rows of long tables dressed in pink peonies, garden roses, flickering candles, and boxes of Pandora necklaces. Festivities began in the front bar, where guests mingled over floral-inspired cocktails and mocktails before gathering for an intimate plant-based feast. Throughout the space, romantic floral arrangements and Pandora jewelry displays added to the warm, garden-party atmosphere, and guests were also able to personalize necklaces with custom engravings.
The entirely vegan menu featured recipes straight from Anderson’s cookbook, I Love You: Recipes from the Heart, with dishes drawn from her family favorites. From creamy pumpkin risotto to crispy portobello Milanese, each course surprised guests, but the crowd favorite seemed to be the decadent sourdough gingerbread Guinness cake.
“Pandora is like family,” Anderson told Vogue. “We’ve been together for three years now, and they’ve been such supporters of this stage in my career.” Expressing her gratitude for the brand and taking in the evening’s dreamy setting, she added, “They’ll go to the ends of the earth. I’m living inside my cookbook right now.”
As the evening came to a close, guests left with a little piece of Anderson’s world: a sourdough starter kit to bring her love of home cooking into their own kitchens, along with lip balm from her beauty brand, Sonsie.
For Anderson, this Friendsgiving celebration was ultimately about “creating memories and creating new traditions,” surrounded by lush flowers, friends, and good food. —Donna Kim
Urban Jürgensen
Friendsgiving is usually something of a misnomer when it comes to brands hosting dinner parties in the weeks leading up to Thanksgiving. Such was not the case at Urban Jürgensen’s night out, which was, quite literally, a Thanksgiving feast, complete with turkey, stuffing, cornbread, and cranberry sauce, all served around one long, convivial dinner table that could have given Norman Rockwell a run for his money.
Leading the resurrection of Urban Jürgensen, the revered watchmaker founded in 1773 that lay dormant until its revival in 2025, is CEO Alex Rosenfield, who played the role of consummate host. Welcoming guests to the upper floors of Pace Gallery in Chelsea, Rosenfield ensured the evening was imbued with all the warmth and charm of the places he holds dear, particularly those in and around his Sag Harbor home. This included a dinner menu devised by Shelter Island eatery Léon 1909, as well as the décor overhead: a hulking arrangement of autumn foliage plucked from trees in Sagaponack and transported into the city that very morning for assembly.
With this in mind, Rory Satran, the former Wall Street Journal editor turned founder of The Hamptons Chronicle, a “mischievous local print newspaper” distributed along Long Island’s South Fork, was a natural choice for co-host. “I laugh when people say, ‘What’s your distribution plan?’ and it’s just me in my Volvo,” Satran told guests. “This past summer was great, and it couldn’t be done without incredible partners and friends, and Alex and his team are very much a part of that, as well as all of you here. I could go around and do shout-outs to all of you, but that would be super awkward, but you are all special to me.”
Joining Satran around the table were Misha Kahn, Beverly Nguyen, Matthew Foley, Sophie Elgort, newly minted Vanity Fair columnist Chris Black, and vintner Joey Wölffer, who, like Satran, is a devout Hamptonite helping usher in a renewed appreciation for the charms of the East End that endure beyond its summertime glitz.
“There’s something so special about the Hamptons that you can’t really explain,” Wölffer told Vogue. “You just feel it. Everyone around this table feels that too, and that’s what makes this so magical. I love how Rory celebrates the Hamptons through the Chronicle, and clearly Urban Jürgensen values the importance of the community. I think the food is a star tonight, too, though, I mean, come on.” —Zachary Weiss
Jenny Bird
Jewelry designer Jenny Bird and Lipstick on the Rim co-host Emese Gormley recently toasted to female founders during a picture-perfect night at San Vicente in the West Village.
There was much cause for celebration as the pair hosted a serene evening at the members’ club: Gormley’s new perfume brand, Anyu, and her collaboration with Jenny Bird on the Anyu Baguette Signet Ring. (The striking piece, available in yellow or white gold, has a sentimental story behind it. Its name comes from the Hungarian word for “mother,” and it’s the content creator’s tribute to her own mother.)
Fittingly for an evening honoring a new business, the guest list was full of those who’ve successfully built their own in the worlds of fashion, lifestyle, and wellness. Around the table, the likes of Dria Murphy, Larissa Mills, Daphne Oz, and Julia McGuire all joined the hosts to raise a glass.
