South Korea scrambled fighter jets and briefly ordered residents of an island off its east coast to seek shelter underground after multiple Russian warplanes entered the country’s air identification zone, the latest in a string of similar incidents regionally.
An air raid warning Tuesday, which applied to the estimated 9,000 people who live on Ulleung Island, some 130 kilometers east of the Korean Peninsula, was lifted minutes after it was initially issued, according to alert messages posted on a local disaster portal.
The Russian jets entered the Korea Air Defense Identification Zone — or KADIZ — and flew near Ulleung Island, prompting the South Korean military to deploy its fighter jets, South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff said in a statement.
“The aircraft left following our military measures. There was no violation of South Korean airspace,” they added. The KADIZ is not South Korea’s territorial airspace but is an area where aircraft are supposed to identify themselves as they approach.
“The Russian military aircraft had approached the Ulleung Island to the point where it was just short of entering our airspace. Given the high speed at which military aircraft travel, as you are all aware, we anticipated a potential airspace violation and issued the alert preemptively to protect our people and ensure their safety,” JCS spokesperson, Jang Do-young, told a briefing.
The military declined to elaborate on how close to the airspace the jets flew.
There have been multiple instances of Russian warplanes entering the KADIZ, but it is rare for South Korean authorities to issue a warning in response to such flights, Jang said.
South Korea plans to summon Russia’s military attache to protest the flights, the Defense Ministry said.
The flights come after Russian President Vladimir Putin held a trilateral meeting with U.S. President Donald Trump and China’s Xi Jinping in Shenzhen last week, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit.
Last week, ahead of the meeting, South Korea, Japan and Taiwan all complained of Russian jets violating their airspace or air defense zones.
While the initial alert warned of Russian aircraft, urging residents to “evacuate to underground facilities or buildings,” one of the later alerts mentioned North Korean aircraft, stoking confusion. Authorities later clarified that the message had been issued by mistake.
