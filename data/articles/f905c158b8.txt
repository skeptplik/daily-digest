Japan’s economy contracted for the first time in a year as a spate of inclement weather weighed on consumer spending, highlighting the fragility of domestic demand.
The nation’s real gross domestic product shrank 0.1% on an annualized basis in the third quarter compared to the previous three months, according to a Cabinet Office report Monday. That was worse than economists’ median estimate of 0.2% growth.
Consumer spending, the biggest component of GDP, dropped 0.3%. In its latest Outlook report, the Bank of Japan noted that household spending had been weak due to “weather conditions such as typhoons, torrential rains, and prolonged rainfall.” Tokyo saw rain for 37 consecutive days from late August through early October, a record streak, according to the Japan Meteorological Agency.
Rising living costs have also continued to make consumers cautious, with pensioners in particular becoming ever more thrifty, the BOJ said.
Oil import costs rose as Japan diversified its crude procurement in response to the Middle East conflict, while the weak yen continued to make inbound shipments more expensive, overshadowing a rise in exports. That resulted in a negative contribution from net exports of 0.6 percentage point.
Corporate investment provided a bright spot, rising 1.6%, as demand related to artificial intelligence continued to boost spending. A BOJ business sentiment survey on Oct. 1 showed that Japan’s large firms plan to increase fixed investment by 11.3% in the fiscal year through March 2027, slightly below the 11.5% increase projected in the previous survey.
The first contraction in a year highlights the narrow path policymakers have to navigate.
Weak consumer spending suggests domestic demand remains insufficient to sustain inflation in a positive way, while external supply factors — namely higher import costs for energy and food — continue to drive inflation. The drop in consumer spending also highlights a missing link in the virtuous economic cycle policymakers are seeking, as a continued rise in wages fails to spur demand.
