Parker Selman and Karan Rai’s love story was born from a sunset. “Our paths crossed a few times before, but the first time we remember speaking was on a roof as we watched the sun go down our senior year at Tufts University,” Parker recalls. A few weeks later, they found themselves alone on a dance floor. “I looked down at my phone to see it was midnight and, therefore, officially my birthday,” Parker says. “I told him he should give me a kiss because it was my birthday, and he did. We were both smitten, and constantly in one another's orbit.” Despite the two lining up jobs on opposite coasts, they quickly realized being practical was not an option, and Karan managed to convince his company to transfer him to New York.
The couple got engaged on a crisp autumn walk around Governors Island—Parker’s favorite spot in the city. She owns and operates a wedding photography business, and as they strolled along, she asked to take a picture of Karan on her medium-format camera with their apartment building and skyline in the background. “While I was snapping the photo, he got down on one knee,” she says, “and I had the privilege of capturing my own proposal.” They rode the ferry back to Manhattan and went to Della’s, a local wine bar, where their families and friends were waiting to celebrate.
Two months later, she proposed back. “While we were walking in Sun Valley, I requested he take a picture of me sitting in the snow with our dog Grumpy,” she says. "I proposed the same way he proposed to me, the moment captured on my Rolleiflex.” Parker used diamonds and emeralds from her late great-grandmother’s engagement ring and gold from Karan’s late grandmother’s earrings to create his signet ring.
Their multi-day wedding unfolded in early September 2026 near Sun Valley, Idaho (where Parker’s family lives). They chose Central Idaho 4-H Camp, located 30 minutes outside of town, for its series of cabins and a large field up in the mountains. “The space was a blank canvas with no sound ordinance,” she says. “Studio Sully transformed the campsite into something magical.”
Going into the planning process, the couple knew they wanted the entire event to feel like a fusion of their cultures. In the days leading up to the Sun Valley celebration, Karan’s parents threw a series of traditional Indian wedding ceremonies and celebrations in his hometown of Pleasanton, California. Once guests arrived in Idaho, they all checked in at The Sun Valley Lodge, where each day the couple had organized group activities.
Festivities kicked off on Friday with a Western-themed welcome party at their favorite après-ski bar, Grumpy’s. Parker wore a tweed corset and skirt by Wiederhoeft accessorized with brown leather Manolo Blahniks and a wooden horse purse by Vin. When the weather cooled down, she tossed on a vintage suede fringe RRL jacket. Karan followed a similar script and wore a Bode suede fringe jacket and One of These Days pants with a painted horse and cowboy detail on the knees. His bandana was made by his mother. “She created a piece inspired by Marie Lichtenberg’s jeweled bandana, utilizing earrings and jewelry of Karan’s grandmothers, mother, and sister,” Parker says.
Guests were encouraged to wear textiles, paisley, and eclectic patterns to evoke a Wild West meets India atmosphere. In one corner, Boise Thread Studio set up shop to embroider initials onto trucker hats, shirts, and bandanas. Meanwhile, guests drank schooners (the name for Grumpy’s beer chalices) on the patio and ate burgers and hot dogs. “Karan and I named our beloved miniature dachshund Grumpy after the bar’s corn dogs, so it is only fitting that we ordered them until they ran out,” Parker says. Her mother welcomed the group with a sunset toast before moving inside. “We took over the electronic jukebox and closed down the restaurant until Karan had to stand up on the booth to usher everyone out the door.”
The next afternoon they hosted a sangeet by the Big Wood River. “The estate has a series of winding paths that lead to the riverside, and we wanted the afternoon portion to feel like a mountain garden party with activities and new discoveries tucked into each corner,” Parker says. The flowing river and live music by Boise-based Ashley Rose Band served as the soundtrack as guests played croquet, horseshoes, and badminton. Near the band, Henna by Sanjana created patterns on guests' hands.
Parker wore Danielle Frankel’s cream Mariela dress featuring two layers of hand-painted wildflowers. “I wanted a long braid with flowers woven in, and Jasmine Burnside helped bring the vision to life,” she says. Karan wore a light blue Jatin Malik sherwani with juttis from his personal collection.
As the sun set, guests were escorted to a clearing where a buffet was set up for dinner, and the dance floor was built around a large willow tree. The vignette was inspired by a 1905 Impressionist painting called Nighttime Festivities that depicts a turn-of-the-century party beneath the trees of a French palace. “I told Melissa [Sully] I wanted the sangeet to be based on this and we became obsessed with bringing the painting to life,” Parker explains. Lights and flowers were strung into the branches that draped overhead. Karan’s parents and Parker’s best friend, Ana, gave speeches before a series of traditional dances were performed by various family members and friends, followed by a lively dance floor courtesy of DJ Anupya.
The morning of the wedding, Parker and Karan got ready with their families and friends. “We are terrible at surprises, and he had seen my dress many times before, so I saw no purpose in a first look,” she says. The couple set off in a convertible (Parker’s hair protected in a vintage scarf from the year she was born), stopping at a vista for photos before arriving at the venue.
For her wedding look, Parker worked with Patricia Voto of One/Of to create a custom gown inspired by Christian Dior’s 1955 Soirée de Lahore. Early on, she knew she wanted something that felt like a combination of Karan and herself. “The original gown was made by the designer two years before his death and was based on a sari with the one-shoulder design and gold embroidery,” she explains. All of the embroidery was done in India, and Parker paired the gown with a custom One/Of veil featuring gold embroidery along the edge, as well as light green shoes by Paula Nadal.
Karan wore a custom Bode tuxedo with a golden sunflower detail, and as a boutonniere, he wore his own emerald kalgi (a traditional Indian headpiece for turbans). “His cufflinks and studs were my late grandfather’s, his cummerbund was unbranded vintage ’70s made in India with embellished sunflowers, and his shoes were suede Hermès.”
Guests arrived to the ceremony dressed in jewel tones and metallics, walking down a pathway with giant sunflowers on either side. Parker and her mother, an amateur florist, had collaborated with Madison of Hart Floral to create the dramatic entrance. “When I spoke of our love for sunflowers and our desire to have them serve as a recurring visual throughout the day, Madison and Melissa joined forces,” Parker explains.
For the ceremony, they stood within a chuppah structure with draped gold chainmail and burgundy sunflower motifs. Karan’s best friend Drew officiated the ceremony, and the fathers of her late friends Alex and Josh read an excerpt by Ovid of the Greek myth of Baucis and Philemon. “Karan’s vows and my own were mirrors of each other. Unknowingly, we made many of the same references, and we each ended by quoting the story of Baucis and Philemon, who, when they died, received the honor of becoming intertwined as trees so they could live on forever.”
Coming from mixed religious backgrounds (Karan’s parents are Sikh and Hindu and Parker’s are Episcopalian and Jewish), the couple chose to honor both. “Karan arrived on horseback as a nod to traditional Punjabi weddings. St. Christopher medallions were placed on each program, and my sister, Alison, shared a short speech about the patron saint of travelers, the namesake of the church we attended as children, and spoke about the new journey Karan and I were embarking upon,” Parker says. “My sister-in-law, Kareena, shared a speech about the jaimala ceremony as a nod to the Hindu wedding tradition, and spoke about the importance of seeing each other as equals.” Karan’s maternal uncle led the group in a Sikh Ardas prayer, and the ceremony ended with the Jewish tradition of the breaking of the glass. As the couple exited, guests threw dried bougainvillea from Karan’s family garden in Chandigarh.
Cocktail hour kicked off with oysters shipped from Island Creek Oyster Co. (the site of the couple’s first two dates). “We really wanted textiles to be an important decor motif throughout the celebration,” Parker says, noting that there was leveled seating overlaid with rugs, a rug-covered bar, and a rug-covered makeshift bandstand. Later on, their sisters shared speeches, and the couple danced to “Can’t Take My Eyes Off You,” then launching into “Dancing in the Moonlight” as tambourines were passed to guests. “Guests followed Karan and I through the field to reveal the tent, set among the cabins.”
The band continued to play as guests entered the tent and danced. At each seat, Parker and Karan placed a letter explaining what their friendship meant to the couple. After the buzz died down, Parker’s father opened the evening with a speech. “We then had a family-style dinner of our favorite bites: kale salad, focaccia, Idaho trout, pesto gnocchi, and vodka meatballs,” she says.
After dinner wrapped up, Parker and her father danced to “Wide Open Spaces” (a song they listened to as she was growing up) before everyone joined in. The couple snuck away to cut the cake—a 30-inch round flourless chocolate confection covered in raspberries—which was passed out on the outskirts of the dance floor. Indian sweets called ladoos were also passed around.
“As the night wound down, we walked through the trees lit by a path of lights to a large cabin filled with red light and a huge disco ball,” she says. They danced to DJ Daddybaby (a Boise-based duo) while Karan handed out Jell-O shots by Solid Wiggles and Parker passed out cookies. For late-night dancing, Parker changed into a custom dress made by the Indian Karigar based on Audrey Hepburn’s 1962 sari-inspired slip dress designed by Givenchy.
After a full week of wedding events between California for the traditional Indian celebration and four days in Sun Valley, the two were understandably exhausted. “It took weeks to recover, but the whole time all we could talk about was how deeply happy we were.”
