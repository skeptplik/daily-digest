Resident doctors are set to return to the picket lines on Friday in a strike over pay.
They will hold a full 24-hour strike starting at 07:00 GMT, marking their second such action this year.
British Medical Association (BMA) members voted in favour of industrial action after the union rejected a recommended 3.5% pay rise from an independent pay body.
Health Minister Robbie Butler said he remained committed to delivering the pay award and that he would "do everything in my power" to implement it this year - but said he was unable to do so in the absence of a Stormont budget.
Dr Steven Montgomery, the chairman of the BMA's Northern Ireland Resident Doctors Committee, said doctors should not have to strike for a second time but the issues that had originally taken them to the picket lines remained unresolved.
"Our pay is eroding and we need to address that in order to retain young doctors in Northern Ireland," he said.
Resident doctors are fully qualified medical practitioners who are completing postgraduate clinical training in a hospital or general practice under the supervision of a senior doctor.
Consultants and specialist, associate specialist and speciality grade (SAS) doctors will also take 24-hour action from 07:00 GMT on Monday.
They will provide what is known as Christmas Day cover, which means that most routine and elective services will be cancelled but full emergency cover will remain in place.
SAS doctors are specialist doctors employed by health trusts.
The three branches of hospital doctors also took strike action in June.
During this period of strike action more than 2,200 appointments were cancelled across the health and social care system, according to the health minister.
Butler said the fresh action planned for Friday and Monday would have "real consequences for patients, waiting lists and service delivery", especially during the busy winter period.
In England, consultant doctors have called off their two-day strike this month to hold talks with the government.
Montgomery said colleagues wanted the best for the health service and their patients but were facing burnout and emotional distress due to work pressure, with their pay failing to reflect the demands placed upon them.
He said doctors wanted full "pay restoration", adding that the annual pay rise they were due to receive would not make up for the value their pay had lost over many years.
Montgomery added that the BMA was "more than willing" to discuss with the minister and Department of Health how pay "erosion" could be addressed and what steps were needed to reassure members that there was a commitment to resolving it.
Butler said the recommended 3.5% rise was higher than that for other healthcare professionals working in Northern Ireland's health system.
He added that to go beyond the recommended offer would lead to "significant repercussions for nurses, teachers, police officers and indeed the entire public sector workforce".
"It comes on top of an average cumulative pay uplifts over the last three years of 30% for resident doctors, 25% for speciality, associate specialist, and specialist doctors, and 24% for consultants," he said.
The minister said he had met the BMA and outlined the financial constraints facing the Department of Health and his intention to issue the pay rises as soon as possible.
But he said that would require adequate funding to be given to his department.
