There is something about red that fashion simply cannot get enough of. Crimson, poppy, scarlet, cherry, oxblood—the shades are seemingly endless, and so is our fascination with them. What began as an easy way to inject color into an otherwise neutral wardrobe has evolved into something much bigger. At this point, I would say fashion has developed a full-blown case of scarlet fever.
Traditionally, I associated red with evening dressing. There was something inherently glamorous, perhaps even a little theatrical, about wearing it after dark. But that distinction has all but disappeared. Red has found its way into every corner of the wardrobe, from casual daytime looks to evening ensembles, and some of the most compelling ways I've seen it worn lately are decidedly understated.
Not so long ago, a red accessory was the easiest entry point: a handbag, a pair of shoes, or perhaps a flash of red socks. It was the little punctuation mark that made an outfit feel more considered. Today, however, we're embracing red as the starting point of an entire look. Take Polo Ralph Lauren's red Harrington jacket, a beautifully executed Chanel cap-toe pump, or even a luxurious red sweater casually tied around the waist. I particularly love the latter, bringing an unexpected flash of color to jeans or tailored trousers. It's a simple styling gesture that can transform an entire outfit.
And then there is the appeal of wearing red from head to toe. Who could forget the monochromatic look that came down Michael Rider's Celine runway this past season? There was an almost collective gasp. Seeing such a saturated look presented with that kind of conviction was a reminder of just how arresting the color can be. Red wasn't merely an accent; it was the entire statement.
There is a certain pleasure in committing to one color, allowing different textures, proportions, and shades to create interest. A scarlet knit paired with matching trousers, or a cherry-red anorak worn over an equally saturated ensemble, makes quite a statement. After seasons of pared-back palettes and quiet luxury, there is something refreshing about dressing with a little more conviction.
Depending on the shade, red can be romantic, provocative, sporty, polished, or even a little rebellious. Cherry has a playful quality, scarlet carries a certain theatricality, while oxblood offers a moodier, more understated interpretation. Perhaps that's why our infatuation with the color has lasted so long. It has been reinterpreted countless times, yet somehow manages to feel exciting all over again.
Whatever the reason, I'm not quite ready to give up on red. Whether worn as a subtle accent, an everyday staple, or embraced from head to toe, it has a way of making even the most familiar wardrobe feel exciting again. And judging by the way we're wearing it now, I don't see our scarlet fever subsiding anytime soon.

Is more more, or is less more? For years, we’ve been told that good taste is knowing when to stop. Coco Chanel’s famous advice about looking in the mirror before leaving the house and removing one thing has become something of a fashion commandment for many. But I’ve always wondered what happens when we do the opposite. What if, instead of taking something away, you add another necklace, another ring, another earring, another bracelet? What if the most interesting thing about getting dressed is not knowing when to stop, but having the confidence not to?
After seasons of quiet luxury and wardrobes edited down to the essentials, I find myself drawn to a more expressive approach to accessorizing. Somewhere along the way, restraint became synonymous with sophistication, and the absence of adornment was elevated into a marker of taste. But minimalism, for all its elegance, can become its own uniform. When everyone is wearing the same monogram pendant or oversized belt buckle, how much individuality is left?
Jewelry is most interesting when it disrupts that uniformity. I love the woman with rings on nearly every finger, a wrist stacked with cuffs and bracelets, necklaces layered at different lengths and crowded with pendants, and earrings that seem to be having a conversation of their own. There is something wonderfully assured about a look that refuses to be fully resolved. It doesn’t ask whether every piece matches, whether the proportions are balanced, or whether one more accessory might be too much. It simply embraces the pleasure of wearing what you love.
What strikes me most about this more-is-more approach is that it rarely feels calculated. The best examples are always a little imperfect: a delicate chain mixed with a heavy pendant; a diamond ring beside an inexpensive silver band; a charm-laden brooch that clinks with every movement. Nothing needs to match, nor should it. The most compelling combinations look as if they came together over time, collected from different places, people, and phases of one’s life. The result feels lived-in rather than meticulously styled.
Perhaps this is why the current appetite for accessorizing feels so refreshing. Not everything needs to be versatile, timeless, or sensible. Some things are worth wearing simply because they make you happy.
So the next time you reach for just one necklace, consider adding another. Then perhaps a third. Put on the rings you usually save for evenings out, stack your bracelets, and let the earrings be a little outrageous. You can always take something off. But there is a certain joy in discovering that you don’t want to.

In an industry as vast and layered as fashion, it’s worth asking: Who inspires the tastemakers? Who shapes the eye of the people who decide what is photographed and published, what earns a place on the runway, and what ultimately finds its way onto the racks of the world’s most coveted department stores?
Designers long occupied the highest tier of fashion’s hierarchy of influence. Together with editors, journalists, stylists, and buyers, they defined what we desired before we had the language to desire it ourselves. Now that we live under the influence of algorithms and celebrity and influencer culture, genuine style has become increasingly difficult to distinguish from mere visibility. True originality is quieter—and infinitely more compelling.
Several times a year, the industry converges on the world’s fashion capitals. Equal parts work and reunion, Fashion Week carries the unmistakable energy of summer camp, bringing together friends and colleagues who have spent months apart. Between the action of the shows themselves, another spectacle quietly unfolds on the sidewalks and in the queues. Fashion week remains one of the world’s greatest people-watching opportunities.
It is often here, unexpectedly, that the most memorable style reveals itself. Not on the runway, but on an unfamiliar face glimpsed again and again throughout the week. You begin to notice the subtle details: the effortless knot of a belt cinching a jacket, the unexpected layering of heirloom pendants, the confidence of someone dressing entirely for themselves rather than for the cameras. Before long, you’ve developed a style crush on someone whose name you may never learn.
This is an ode to those women: the ones who influence the influential. The insiders whose instinct, rather than their following, has earned the admiration of fashion’s most discerning insiders. They are, quite simply, your favorite fashion insider’s favorite fashion insider.

Tommy’s Take on the Joy of Accessorizing
Fashion experts have always emphasized investing in a wardrobe of foundational essentials: the perfect white shirt, a tailored suit, a little black dress, and so on. But once you’ve found those building blocks, the next question becomes: how do you avoid getting caught up in the constant seasonal cycle of trends just to make your closet feel relevant again?
The answer is quite simple: focus on accessories. Small accents, whether it’s a bag in an interesting color, a brooch that has a deeper meaning, or a belt that invites a closer look, can instantly give your wardrobe more clarity and a fresh perspective. Clothes come and go, but a beautifully curated collection of accessories stays in rotation for longer than a single season when you invest wisely in the right pieces. After all, no one ever said you can have too many accessories.

If there has been one brand that has sparked a renewed interest in accessorizing, it’s without a doubt Celine under Michael Rider. From the very first look of his debut collection, what immediately caught your eye was that wrist full of charm bracelets, the hand stacked with rings, and the oversized gold medallion belt buckle slightly peeking out from beneath a blazer. It was a sign that all those never-ending discussions about quiet luxury were finally going to be put to rest.
It was a clever exercise in merchandising as well, as you couldn’t help but be drawn to all those delicious accessories that perfectly accented each look. It was like a fashion enthusiast’s version of walking into a candy store. Whether it was a silk foulard effortlessly tied around a model’s jacket or half-tucked beneath a lapel, it made you want to replicate the styling and adopt it into your wardrobe immediately. You could sense that this collection was going to influence fashion at the highest level before trickling down to the masses; the true mark of an impactful collection is when its imprint can be seen on fashion insiders the following season.
Fast-forward nine months later to Paris Fashion Week, outside a show at the Petit Palais, and all those chic gestural styling cues from the Celine runway had made their way to the street in an unexpected masterclass in styling. Caught in conversation, it was quite a sight to see how three fashion professionals (Sarah Park, Ramya Giangola, and Lisa Aiken) unintentionally complemented one another and somehow captured the mood of the moment.
Everything that felt timeless yet modern was there: glimpses of bold primary colors, subtle accents of gold and silver hardware, small leather wallets and pouchettes, and classic black frames. It was very much in the French way of dressing, where the individual nuances of each look made you curious and encouraged you to reconsider what you already own. After closely scanning each look from head to toe, it became clear that each of these women had adopted elements of the Celine preppy bourgeois aesthetic. Yet it wasn’t a head-to-toe runway look that made their outfits stand out. Rather, it was the fact that each woman carefully borrowed styling cues from Celine and made them distinctly her own. You could easily sense the playfulness and joy in the way they got dressed.
What’s crucial when reevaluating your wardrobe each season is looking for pieces that offer day-to-night versatility and can be effortlessly thrown together without too much thought. Practicality matters, but it’s also about allowing yourself to step slightly outside your comfort zone. Whether it’s buying the right jewelry or embracing color, you don’t want to feel intimidated by your accessories or uncertain about when it’s appropriate to wear them. You want these timeless investment pieces to feel like lifelong treasured friends that come out to play when the occasion is right. They are ultimately what truly bring an outfit together.
