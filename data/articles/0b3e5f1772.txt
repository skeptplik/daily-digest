What was running through Macy Grimshaw’s head when she heard her name called as the winner of the 2026 H&M Design Award? “Shock,” the Central Saint Martins grad told Fashionista on Wednesday after the award ceremony in London. “I still haven’t processed it yet.”
It’s easy to see why this win is hard for her to wrap her head around: Grimshaw has not only won a €150,000 (about $168,000) endowment and a year-long mentorship with the retailer, but next fall, her winning collection will be sold worldwide at select H&M stores and online. Needless to say, this is a dream opportunity for any up-and-coming designer.
Since its launch in 2012, the H&M Design Award has provided early support to multiple now-beloved industry names, including Minju Kim, Richard Quinn, Stefan Cooke and Priya Ahluwalia. That is, until 2020, when H&M paused the competition indefinitely amid the pandemic. Six years later, it’s ended its hiatus.
“We missed it,” Ann-Sofie Johansson, creative advisor and head of design at H&M, said of the relaunch. “From the very beginning, it was about supporting and encouraging young fashion designers … with money but also with mentoring. We have so much knowledge and skills to aid them.”
It was essential for the brand to relaunch the award in a big way: This year, the competition opened its application pool to undergraduate and graduate design students from more than 60 participating schools from 25 countries, with Australia, Mexico, Poland, Spain and Colombia being included for the first time. Resultantly, over 1,000 students applied.
H&M narrowed the field to 28 semi-finalists and a jury of industry professionals — Paloma Elsesser, Beka Gvishiani, IB Kamara, Luke Leitch, Calum Knight, Emma Chopova, Laura Lowena, Hillary Taymour and Johansson — selected the eight finalists: Chico Yu (Bunka Fashion Graduate University, Japan), Ennis Finnerty Mackay (Central Saint Martins, UK), Evelina Kryvopust (Polimoda, Italy), Fantin Delattre Pirenne (La Cambre, Belgium), Jessica Lin (Institut Français de la Mode, France), Grimshaw (Central Saint Martins, UK), Yichun Liu (Royal Academy of Antwerp, Belgium) and Yvonne Schichtel (Royal Academy of Antwerp, Belgium). From there, the jury assessed the finalists’ submitted graduate collections to determine the winner.
All eight brought a new perspective and a distinct aesthetic, which made it difficult to choose one, Johansson confessed. In the end, the jury was sold on Grimshaw’s ability to keep them engaged — they wanted to see more of her work (which has already appeared on stars like Simone Ashley and Harry Styles).
“When we made the judgment, we didn’t think so much about commerciality. What we wanted to find was … someone you get really intrigued by and curious about,” Johansson shared. “In Macy’s case, there was that curiosity. You want to see more of her. You get intrigued. I want to see what she’s up to next. I also wanted to make a more wearable collection with her. What she’s doing now is not so wearable, but you can see the craft behind it.”
The other seven finalists still walked away with €10,000 (about $11,000) each. Johansson stressed that, ultimately, the award’s main priority is to provide a platform for all the participating young designers. In fact, she believes all big brands should do their part to champion young talent, arguing: “Whether it’s a high-end company or H&M, we all have a responsibility to support the future of fashion.”
