The German government has presented some grim statistics with regard to misogyny, rape, violence and murder, with reported cases of domestic violence at an all-time high.
At a press conference in Berlin on Tuesday, Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt, Family Affairs Minister Karin Prien and Justice Minister Stefanie Hubig released the latest figures on crimes that specifically target women: whether within families and intimate relationships, in public or online.
'Behind all these terrible numbers lie individual stories'
In contrast to the statistics pertaining to crime overall, which have remained the same or increased only slightly, reports of crimes against women have increased.
"Behind all these terrible numbers lie individual stories, people and particularly women and girls, whose lives are severely affected by these acts of violence," said Dobrindt.
In 2025, some 280,000 people were victims of physical abuse in their own homes, a 2.8% increase compared to the previous year. Over 70% of the victims were female. Each hour, 16 women are subjected to physical abuse behind closed doors.
The male victims included boys who had been assaulted by their fathers.
The number of misogynistic hate crimes has also risen dramatically, by almost 50%, to 819 cases.
"The figures show us what it can mean to be a woman in Germany," said Hubig. "It can mean being killed by one's partner or ex-partner. Being a woman can also mean being physically abused by one's partner or ex-partner in one's own home. Being a woman can mean that one's sexual autonomy is brutally disregarded."
Perpetrators, victims getting younger
According to the latest findings, both perpetrators and victims are getting younger. In 2025, nearly half of all female victims of sexual offenses were under the age of 18. And the number of incidents of online violence against women and girls rose by 15.7%. This includes the unauthorized sharing of videos and photos, verbal abuse, manipulation of images, threats and online blackmail, as well as cyberstalking.
Because many perpetrators and victims are still in their teens, Prien, who is also minister for education, said the government wanted to focus primarily on schools, which she explained should become places of prevention.
"Ultimately, we need to bring about a change in people's mindsets, especially those of men. That will be the deciding factor in the end," she said.
Femicides to be classified as murder, not manslaughter
Over the weekend, Justice Minister Hubig had already announced that in future all femicides, the intentional killing of girls and women because of their gender, would be classified as murders. Currently, such crimes are often classified as manslaughter, for which the penalty is less severe than for murder.
Hubig told German broadcaster ZDF that it was increasingly common that femicides be described as family tragedies or as domestic dramas revolving around jealousy and then classified as manslaughter.
Martina Link, the vice president of the Federal Criminal Police Office, was also at the press conference in Berlin. She called for everybody to pay closer attention to the issue, within their own families, neighborhoods or at work.
"Violence against women is not a private matter," she said. "Not in our immediate surroundings nor in the digital world."
She also pointed out that when it came to sexual offenses and domestic violence, only about 5-10% of cases were reported.
