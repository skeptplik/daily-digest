MILAN – David Koma is exiting Blumarine after a two-year tenure as its creative director, WWD has learned.
To be presented jointly in January, the pre-fall 2027 and fall 2027 collections will be the Georgian-born designer’s last at the brand. This will imply that Blumarine won’t stage a runway show during Milan Fashion Week in February.
A successor to Koma has yet to be named.
According to a statement shared with WWD by the company, the two parties “have mutually agreed to conclude their collaboration” and Koma will now dedicate “renewed energy and attention to the continued development of his eponymous label and the evolution of its creative identity.”
“I am grateful to Marco Marchi for his trust and for the opportunity to contribute to Blumarine’s creative evolution,” said Koma about the founder and chairman of Exelite group, the holding company formerly known as EIH Eccellenze Italiane and that controls Blumarine.
“I am proud of the work we have accomplished together and deeply appreciative of the talented teams and collaborators who brought this vision to life. I will carry with me the value of this experience and look forward to the next chapter of my creative journey,” added the designer.
“I would like to thank David for the personal commitment and creative energy he devoted to Blumarine, and for the value he brought throughout our collaboration,” echoed Marchi. “I appreciated his interpretation of the brand and the passion with which he approached this experience, leaving a meaningful mark on our shared journey. I wish him every success in his future endeavors.”
During his tenure Koma explored the duality between seductiveness and romance embedded in the brand, looking to balance the label’s codes with his own sleek handwriting and knack for sculpted tailoring, sensual cocktail dresses and occasional theatrics that punctuated his runway outings.
His last show was staged during Milan Fashion Week in September, when the designer paraded a spring 2027 collection in which he gave a Californian twist to the Gothic romanticism that he has pushed at the brand, conjuring a wardrobe rich in pastel shades and contrasting elements – a collaboration with Ugg included.
To be sure, Koma’s appointment in July 2024 — when he was called to succeed Walter Chiapponi, who exited Blumarine after only one collection — was intended to raise the global profile of the brand. Koma, who launched his eponymous brand in 2009 after completing his studies at Central Saint Martins College and has been a longtime fixture in the London Fashion Week calendar, made a name for himself for his sculptural silhouettes and bold eveningwear that have drawn to his brand the likes of Jennifer Lopez, Olivia Rodrigo and Gal Gadot over the years, among others.
The London-based designer, whose first collection at Blumarine bowed for pre-fall 2025 in January 2025, was Marchi’s ideal candidate precisely due to his background.
“He comes from a melting pot of culture and creativity. He took over a brand that has a very different aesthetic from his own and has refreshed it immediately, with a great respect for the Blumarine codes, this South European femininity and sensuality, use of colors and embroideries — all this sophisticated part, but without [making it corny],” Marchi told WWD in an interview earlier this year. “As a customer best put it, he maintained the brand’s ethos but gave it a scratch, a modern edge,” said the Italian entrepreneur, who is also the founder of contemporary label Liu Jo.
As reported, Exelite has recently taken over the management of Blumarine through a master licensing agreement covering the production, promotion and distribution of all product categories previously handled by the brand’s parent company Blufin, including the management of the associated existing licenses. These include deals for categories such as eyewear, kidswear under the Miss Blumarine banner, home textiles and wallpapers.
Earlier this year, Marchi said the goal was to scale up the Blumarine business, which is estimated to have generated around 20 million euros in revenues in 2025. To this end, the entrepreneur also recently invested to open a Blumarine store in Milan, which added to an existing unit in Rome and a wholesale footprint of roughly 200 doors worldwide.
Marchi acquired the Gruppo Blufin and its brands Blumarine, Blugirl, Anna Molinari and Be Blumarine from the founding Tarabini family in November 2019. The creation of the conglomerate that was previously called Eccellenze Italiane aimed to boost the visibility and business of small and medium-sized Italian companies on the global scene. In 2019, ahead of the COVID-19 pandemic, Marchi said the ultimate goal for the group was an initial public offering, but listing plans were postponed twice due to unfavorable market conditions, first with the pandemic and then with war in Ukraine.
