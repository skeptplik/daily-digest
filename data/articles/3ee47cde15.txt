Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor dies aged 65
Andy Taylor, whose guitar riffs helped shape Duran Duran's distinctive sound and propel the British band to global fame in the 1980s, has died aged 65 after a long battle with prostate cancer, the band and his family said Wednesday.
Andy Taylor, the former guitarist with new wave pop group Duran Duran, has died after a "long and courageous battle with prostate cancer", the band and his family said on Instagram late Wednesday. He was 65.
"Andy passed away peacefully at home, surrounded by his family on Monday 23rd November," they said in a joint statement.
Taylor's rock- and funk-inspired riffs drove the British band's dancefloor hits such as "Girls on Film" and "Rio", helping it to define the sound of the 1980s.
Frontman Simon Le Bon was the face of the band, but Taylor's jangling, syncopated guitar lines helped forge its distinctive sound, fusing punk rock and dance influences to make it one of the leaders of the UK "New Romantic" movement.
The band enjoyed huge success in the 1980s, with their popularity in the United States compared to Beatlemania.
They have sold more than 100 million records, had 30 top 40 singles in their native UK and 21 top 40 hits in the US charts.
Best known for timeless hits such as "Rio", and "Hungry Like the Wolf", the band were also one of the first to harness the advent of the music video, to burnish a sophisticated and seductive image.
Taylor was born on the coast near Newcastle, northeast England, on February 16, 1961 and learned the guitar aged 11.
"To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad and the proudest grandfather," his wife, Tracey, said on the Instagram post.
"He was the rock of our family. For all the incredible experiences music brought him, family always came first," she added.
Duran Duran added that Taylor had been there from the beginning of the band's story and had had "an unshakeable belief in what we could become".
"He brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music," they said. "His playing drove the songs that took us around the world."
Taylor joined Duran Duran in 1980, travelling from Tyneside to the band's base in Birmingham, central England, in response to an advert in a music magazine.
The group released its debut album a year later, with first single "Planet Earth" peaking at number 12.
But it was their third single, "Girls on Film", accompanied by a raunchy video featuring topless women mud wrestling, that catapulted Duran Duran to global fame.
