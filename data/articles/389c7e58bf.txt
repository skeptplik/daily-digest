What makes AWS European Sovereign Cloud different from regular AWS regions? What makes it unique, and why it matters for European organizations?
We announced the general availability of the AWS European Sovereign Cloud on January 15, 2026. The AWS European Sovereign Cloud Region is located in the state of Brandenburg, Germany, supported by €7.8 billion investment in infrastructure, talent and skills development.
Everything needed to operate the AWS European Sovereign Cloud is located in the European Union. The talent, the technology, the infrastructure, the leadership.
What you find in the AWS European Sovereign Cloud is the experience you know from our other AWS Regions. The resilience of multiple availability zones, the security of the AWS Nitro system, encryption capabilities, and the full depth of AWS services.
The AWS European Sovereign Cloud also has separate billing and usage metering systems operating entirely within the region. But there is more. The AWS European Sovereign Cloud is built on four fundamental assurances.
The first assurance is enhanced data residency. Customers have always had control over the location of their data in AWS. Your customer content, the data you upload to S3 buckets, stored in RDS databases, and so on, stays within the AWS Region you select. That’s true in every AWS Region globally.
The AWS European Sovereign Cloud goes further. It allows customers to keep all the metadata they create within the EU. Elements like roles, permissions, resource tags, and configurations you use to operate on AWS. This is a meaningful distinction for organizations with broad EU data residency requirements. It’s important to note that sovereignty is a continuum.
Customers can choose to deploy their data to any of our 8 existing AWS European regions to keep data securely in Europe, or they can build on our 6 regions located within EU member states to meet specific EU data residency needs.
The AWS European Sovereign Cloud provides the broadest level of residency assurance for those who require it.
The second assurance is operational autonomy.
The AWS European Sovereign Cloud has no critical dependencies, physical or logical, on any AWS infrastructure outside of the EU. It is more than a region; it is an independent cloud.
Daily operations, technical support, and customer services are provided exclusively by EU residents who are located in EU member states and subject to EU law. Data centers are exclusively managed by EU residents located in the EU.
We are also gradually transitioning the AWS European Sovereign Cloud to be operated exclusively by EU citizens located in the EU. During this transition period we will continue to work with a blended team of EU residents and EU citizens located in the EU.
Its unique design allows it to continue operations indefinitely, even in the event of disruption of communications with the rest of the world.
To support continuity, even in extreme circumstances, authorized AWS employees of the European Sovereign Cloud have independent access to a replica of the source code necessary to maintain services.
That is a level of operational independence that is unique to the AWS European Sovereign Cloud.
The third assurance is European governance.
Who controls AWS European Sovereign Cloud?
AWS has established a dedicated governance structure in Europe. We incorporated a new parent company, AWS European Sovereign Cloud GMBH, in Germany, along with 3 local subsidiaries, one for infrastructure, one for research and development, and one serving as a trust and certificate provider.
The AWS European Sovereign Cloud is led by EU citizens who are required to comply with European laws and act in the best interest of the region.

This board serves as a source of expertise and provides accountability on all aspects related to the sovereignty of the European sovereign cloud. Zero operational control resides outside of the EU. That’s by design.
The fourth assurance is our sovereign reference framework, the AWS European Sovereign Cloud: Sovereign Reference Framework (ESC-SRF). The ESC-SRF is available today in AWS Artifact. It lists sovereignty criteria across multiple domains. Governance independence, operational control, data residency, and technical isolation.
The ESC-SRF was developed by working backwards from our customers’ sovereign use cases.
We incorporated direct customer feedback, EU regulatory requirements across multiple member states, industry leading framework guidance, AWS’s own contractual commitments, and input from our implementation partners.
It’s industry and sector agnostic, written to address fundamental sovereignty needs at the foundational layer of cloud.
The framework builds on core AWS security capabilities, encryption, key management, AWS Nitro-based isolation, and internationally recognized compliance certifications.
On top of that, it adds sovereignty specific measures, independent EU corporate structures.
Dedicated EU trust and certificate services, operations by EU resident personnel, strict residency for customer data and customer created metadata, complete separation from all other AWS regions and incident response operated from within the EU.
Why is the AWS European Sovereign Cloud: Sovereign Reference Framework (ESC-SRF) particularly valuable?
The ESC-SRF serves a dual purpose. First, it’s an assurance model, providing end to end visibility from each Sovereignty criterion through to its implementation in a manner that can be independently validated by a third-party auditor. Customers can use this with internal auditors, external assessors, supervisory authorities, and regulators. It reduces the need for ad hoc evidence requests.
Second, it’s a design reference. Customers and partners can leverage the ESC-SRF as a foundation upon which they build their own complementary sovereignty criteria and controls. You can refer to the framework when shaping your own sovereignty architecture, selecting configurations and defining internal controls. Because it’s sector agnostic, you apply only the criteria relevant to your specific use case. Each criterion is implemented through sovereign controls that are independently validated by a third-party auditor. This provides clear, enforceable sovereignty assurances, not marketing promises.
What compliance certifications does AWS European Sovereign Cloud have?
Now, let me share something that our customers are building on today. In March 2026, just two months after general availability, we announced our first major compliance milestone, SOC 2 and C5 Type 1 attestation reports, along with seven key ISO certifications for the AWS European Sovereign Cloud.
The attestation reports already cover 69 services operating within the AWS European Sovereign cloud. That’s 69 services with independently validated security controls. The SOC 2 report addresses three critical AICPA trust services criteria, security, availability, and confidentiality. It includes internal controls mapped directly to the ESC-SRF.
Independent auditors validated the design and implementation of our controls, addressing EU corporate structures, EU resident personnel operations, strict residency requirements, and complete separation from all other AWS regions.
The C5 attestation, which is the German government backed Cloud Computing Compliance Criteria Catalog introduced by BSI, is one of the most comprehensive cloud security standards in Europe, if not globally.
Our C5 Type 1 report confirms we meet both the basic criteria and the additional criteria. The basic criteria cover fundamental security requirements across more than a dozen domains. The additional criteria address enhanced requirements for handling sensitive data and critical applications, making this attestation particularly valuable for customers with the most stringent sovereignty requirements.
And the seven ISO certifications, ISO 27001 for information security management, ISO 27017 for cloud specific security controls, ISO 27018 for personal data protection and cloud environments, ISO 27701 for privacy information management, ISO 22301 for business continuity and organizational resilience, and ISO 20000-1 for IT service management. And finally, ISO 9001 for quality management. These certifications collectively demonstrate comprehensive operational excellence.
They confirm that the AWS European Sovereign Cloud has been integrated into Frameworks for managing security, privacy, continuity, service delivery, and quality, meeting the highest standards through systematic risk management and continuous improvement, based on certifications and criteria that are well established and highly relevant today.
By securing these foundational certifications early in our implementation, we’re demonstrating that security, transparency, and regulatory alignment have been part of the DNA of the AWS European Sovereign Cloud from day one. And this is just the beginning of our certification portfolio.

What is the failover process if geopolitical events disrupt connectivity?
Here’s how it works. Your primary workloads run in your existing AWS region. The disaster recovery footprint runs in the European sovereign Cloud, pre-provisioned and kept in sync. If you need to fail over, whether due to a regulatory trigger, or a geopolitical event, or an operational disruption, the full environment scales up on demand entirely within the EU.
No data leaves Europe, no dependency on non-EU regions, no shared identity boundaries.
This is a fully isolated partition with validated architecture, published runbooks, and one click activation.
You don’t need to migrate production workloads. You don’t need to learn a new platform.
