UK studio RHSP has revealed its first project in the Baltic region, a mixed-use office building in Lithuania designed as “a landmark that is unmistakably forward-looking”.
Named after the Lithuanian word for paperclip, the seven-storey Sąvaržėlė is located in central Vilnius, between the UNESCO-listed Old Town and the city’s commercial district.
The stacked building has a cantilevered design and houses office space, a conference hall, a restaurant and a cafe across its 19,000-square-metre space.
“At Sąvaržėlė, we wanted to create a workplace that extends into the city – one that welcomes people, encourages collaboration and contributes new public spaces to Vilnius,” RSHP associate director Jack Evans-Newton said.
“Occupying a unique position between the historic centre and the city’s emerging business district, the building brings these two identities together through a calm and contemporary architectural expression,” he continued.
“Clarity of form, generous public spaces and carefully crafted detailing create a landmark that is unmistakably forward-looking, reflecting the ambition of a growing business district while respecting the scale and character of its historic setting.”
Sąvaržėlė has large, adaptable floorplates, designed to create a flexible interior.
The building has a stacked, rectangular shape and was made from prefabricated steel components that were bolted together on site. On the east and west facades, RSHP placed external ribs that the studio said “echo the tones” of Vilnius’ historic architecture.
Sąvaržėlė is arranged around a central glass atrium with a striking yellow-lined elevator. It can be accessed via a number of different entrances that connect to the surrounding streets.
To enhance the building’s environmental performance, RSHP added a double-skin facade. This uses energy-efficient chilled beam cooling and automated natural ventilation to help reduce energy demand.
In addition, Sąvaržėlė has a 2,897-square-metre fully landscaped green roof.
RSHP has also recently designed a 45-storey skyscraper in Shenzhen and a curved-roof railway hub.
