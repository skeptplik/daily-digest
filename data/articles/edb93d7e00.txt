The Hyogo Prefectural Police have arrested four men, including a company executive, on suspicion of holding a poker tournament at a rented venue without the required permit, police said Wednesday.
All four have partially denied the allegations, reportedly saying they did not think a permit was necessary.
According to police, this is the first case in Japan in which organizers of a poker tournament have been arrested on suspicion of violating the Entertainment Business Act.
Businesses that organize poker games are required to obtain permission from the prefectural public safety commission. Even tournaments held at rented venues can be in violation of the law if they run for two or more consecutive days and are deemed commercial operations, such as when entry fees are collected, police said.
The four men are suspected of holding an unauthorized poker tournament at a rented venue in Kobe’s Chuo Ward between July 24 and 26.
A total of 541 people participated in the event, which generated approximately ¥10 million ($63,000) in revenue, over the three days.
The executive — Tomoki Araki, 51, of Kobe — and the other suspects previously held tournaments at venues that had obtained the necessary permits.
Police received an anonymous tip before the July tournament and said they had warned Araki and the others on three separate occasions that their planned activities could violate the law.
