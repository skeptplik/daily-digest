At the 2026 League of Legends World Championship Finals, Riot Games officially unveiled League of Legends: New Dawn. The large-scale update is scheduled for 2027 and will bring changes to the game's client, Summoner's Rift, visuals, and gameplay, marking the beginning of a new chapter for one of the industry's most enduring titles.
New Dawn introduces what Riot described as an "all-encompassing" update to League, touching almost every aspect of its experience. "This is the start of a new era for League, a new journey," said Paul Bellezza, League's executive producer, in New Dawn's first official trailer. Riot is expected to share the full reveal of New Dawn this coming January.

A new look for Summoner's Rift
The announcement came with a trailer shown at Worlds Finals that offered the first glimpse of what League players can expect from New Dawn, and the changes appear to go beyond simple refreshed graphics. The first noticeable change is a redesigned champion selection screen, which looks much closer to the modern interface found in Wild Rift, League's mobile game. The footage also hints at a new or reworked rune system, which seems to have a much bigger impact on the gameplay than ever before.
The trailer also showcased updated minions, a new look for Summoner's Rift, redesigned dragons and Baron Nashor, as well as what seem to be new visual effects for many champion abilities. More interestingly, the footage also hinted at gameplay changes that will change how players approach their matches, including major changes to the map terrain that open new strategic consideration — and not only for junglers.
Since its launch in 2009, League has seen more than a few major changes, including a rebuilt Summoner's Rift in 2014, a new client a couple of years later, and runes and then terrain changes in the following years. Still, nothing ever came close to the scale of what New Dawn promises to be. For now, Riot is keeping the full extent of these gameplay changes to itself, leaving much of New Dawn's scope to be revealed in the coming months.
Leaving the past behind
Riot spent part of 2026 looking back at League's history through League Classic, a mode that allowed players to revisit older versions of both the Summoner's Rift and League's champions. However, nostalgia alone proved difficult to sustain. Just a few weeks after its release, Riot announced that the mode would end its current run in January 2027. The experience could return in a modified format in the future, but it demonstrated that revisiting the past is not necessarily enough to keep a game of League's scale moving forward.
Now, Riot is looking in the opposite direction. 18 years since League first launched, New Dawn represents another attempt at evolving a game whose identity has been shaped as much by its community as by its developers. But whether players embrace the changes or push back against them remains to be seen. After all, it is the playerbase that will ultimately decide whether this new chapter gives League the foundation it needs to thrive for another decade or sends it down a different path.
More information about League of Legends: New Dawn is expected in the coming weeks, with Riot's full presentation scheduled for some time in January 2027.
