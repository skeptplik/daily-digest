In a dramatic, sudden maneuver, Macy’s for the first time ever has postponed its annual Thanksgiving Day parade to this Saturday due to the forecasts for a severe storm in the New York area on Thursday.
For 99 years Macy’s has always staged the parade the morning of Thanksgiving Day in Manhattan. Rescheduling it poses a herculean logistics task, given the thousands of personnel, volunteers, marching bands, and celebrities that must be communicated with and rescheduled, not to mention the television stations that were scheduled to broadcast the event, as well as Santa, who must rearrange his calendar. Broadcasts were scheduled with NBC, Peacock and Telemundo.
However, Macy’s management is intent on staging its biggest and possibly best parade ever in the best possible weather conditions, since it’s the 100th edition of the parade this year. Eleven thousand volunteers and 100 inflatable elements, including the giant character balloons, are set to parade down Manhattan’s west side, with the parade culminating by Macy’s Herald Square flagship.
In an interview earlier this year, Will Coss, vice president and executive producer of Macy’s branded entertainment, told WWD the retailer would be “going all out” to put the parade together. He added that 18 months of labor were required to produce the parade.
	“With severe weather expected on Thanksgiving Day, and out of an abundance of caution, in partnership with the city the 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade will be postponed,” Macy’s stated.
“In close coordination with our city and agency partners, the 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade will move to Saturday, Nov. 28, with an expected step-off at 8:30 a.m. Our team is now working through the complex operational logistics required to bring the parade to life on Saturday, and we will share additional details as they are finalized.” 
Sharon Otterman, Macy’s chief marketing officer, earlier this year stated, “For our 100th parade, we’re creating a celebration of unprecedented scale and will deliver an unforgettable Thanksgiving for millions across the country.”
Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade debuted in 1924 and has been held annually since with the exception of three years during World War II.
Forecast conditions for Thanksgiving Day include heavy rainfall, strong wind gusts, and localized flooding. Macy’s is required to ground the parade balloons if winds are 23 miles an hour or higher, or gusts are 34 miles an hour or higher.
With the parade on Saturday, Macy’s should see massive crowds spilling into its Herald Square flagship shopping holiday gifts after the event. As of Wednesday, Saturday’s forecast called for a low chance of rain, just some clouds, and winds no higher than 14 miles per hour. Macy’s stores are closed on Thanksgiving Day.
New balloons and inflatable elements this year include Bluey by BBC; Buckaroo Bob the Minion by Illumination; Earl the Squirrel by Universal Orlando Resort; Hello Kitty Big Apple by Sanrio; Joe Cool & Woodstock by Peanuts Worldwide; Sorcerer’s Apprentice Mickey by Disney; Underdog by DreamWorks Animation, and a new balloonicle, Fuzzyeti’s Fur Wheel Drive by Levi’s.
Also joining this year’s centennial balloon lineup is Billie Brightpaw, the cheerful bear created by Macy’s Studios. Country singer Megan Moroney and genre-bending singer Teddy Swims were lined up to perform. And new floats in the parade include Dashing All the Way with DoorDash; Hogwarts Holiday with HBO; Mr. Peanut Power Plant with Planters; Purina Dog Chow Salute to Service Dogs with Purina Dog Chow; Road Trip to Parade with Geico, and Macy’s Tony Sarg’s Amazing Marionettes. The late Tony Sarg, a puppet master and once creator of Macy’s holiday windows, is credited with pioneering Macy’s giant inflatable balloons.
Floats will feature iconic characters from past parades, including Angry Birds’ Red, Arthur, Bugs Bunny, Bullwinkle J. Moose, Curious George, Adventure Time’s Finn & Jake, Jimmy Neutron, Popeye & Olive Oyl & Swee’Pea, Pink Panther, Poppy from Trolls, Scooby-Doo, Sonic the Hedgehog, the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles and Wild Thing.
