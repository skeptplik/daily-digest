NEW DELHI
The return of the deposed former Prime Minister of Bangladesh Sheikh Hasina, using a “travel document” to her country and the process of her extradition from India to Bangladesh were two separate issues, India’s Ministry of External Affairs said on Wednesday (November 25, 2026). Speaking at the bi-weekly press briefing, the MEA’s official spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal made the distinction, while expressing concern over the prevailing law and order situation in Bangladesh.
Bangladesh’s request for Ms. Hasina’s extradition, made during the tenure of the interim government under Prof. Mohammed Yunus, is “being examined in line with our established processes”, Mr. Jaiswal said. Dhaka, in recent weeks, has talked about getting Ms. Hasina through an “acceptable prisoner exchange process”. Ms. Hasina had earlier this month requested the High Commission of Bangladesh, through a U.K.-based law firm, to issue her a fresh Bangladesh passport so that she could return home. Mr. Jaiswal said her plans to return to her country were separate from India extraditing her to Bangladesh.
“The case of a citizen willing to return voluntarily to her country on a travel document is a matter separate from the extradition request,” Mr. Jaiswal said, adding that the issue of her return was a matter “between the former Prime Minister and the Bangladesh government”.
Ms. Hasina’s supporters from the Awami League have been agitating in her party’s strongholds in places, including Gopalganj and Narail, where the party’s workers have been targeted by law enforcement authorities. “We understandably are concerned at the incidents of violence against minorities in Bangladesh. We urge the government of Bangladesh to thoroughly investigate these incidents and bring the perpetrators to justice,” Mr. Jaiswal said.
