In short:
The cost to build the Macquarie Point stadium will rise by $100 million to $1.23 billion, but a swag of other expenses will add to that.
The new stadium budget includes whole-of-precinct works, but omits some crucial costings.
A building contract is set to be signed in the coming weeks.
It has become a familiar — verging on cliché — line when discussing anything regarding the Macquarie Point stadium.
The devil is in the detail.
When Premier Jeremy Rockliff revealed the updated cost of the stadium had risen from $1.13 billion to $1.23 billion via his Facebook page this morning, the number was met with a mix of relief and surprise from those who expected a much higher spike.
But here comes that devil.
For the stadium to actually function, it requires broader precinct works at Macquarie Point to be completed.
They are works that sit outside the core construction of the stadium but are vital to its functionality.
Energy. Water. Landscaping. For those things, an extra $132 million has been allocated by the government.
It is true that that infrastructure will serve a broader purpose — TasPorts, and future developments (hotels, restaurants, shops, housing) on the site will all draw on the energy and water upgrades that will happen as part of the stadium development.
But it is also true that for the stadium's lights to turn on and for its toilets to flush, those things must be built and therefore must be included as a cost.
Doing so sees the price lift from $1.23 billion to $1.36 billion.
Then there are the costs of facilitating that extra work, as well as costs already incurred by the Macquarie Point Development Corporation.
That spits out at $183 million.
Now we are at $1.54 billion for the stadium and the precinct works that let it operate.
And that's if everything goes to plan across the life of the build.
One crucial number that's missing from the update is the contingency price, with the government and Macquarie Point Development Corporation opting to keep that number secret ahead of penning a building contract with construction partner BESIX Watpac.
Previous estimates included this figure, with $174 million making up part of the stadium's last cost estimate of $1.13 billion.
That is 14 per cent of the budget.
Applying this number to the new overall cost adds another $215 million to the total, for a total budget of $1.76 billion.
How much contingency the Macquarie Point Development Corporation keeps up its sleeve depends on the contract it signs with BESIX Watpac.
At a recent public accounts committee hearing, department secretary Kathrine Morgan-Wicks spoke of assurances made before the "final investment decision".
"This includes understanding the proposed relationship between MPDC and the lead contractor," she said.
"For example, the contract cost will be higher as risks are transferred to the contractor, or lower if the state accepts risks."
The MPDC cited the war in Iran as a major factor behind the cost increase, as well as Australia's competitive infrastructure pipeline, which includes the upcoming Brisbane Olympics.
In a bid to keep costs low, the MPDC also confirmed it had "value managed" the project by $100 million, mainly through the design process and the process in which the stadium is delivered.
So, let's break down the numbers even further.
Included in the $1.2 billion stadium cost is the core construction of the stadium building, seats, roof, grass and fit-out.
Everything that makes a stadium a stadium.
It also includes the cricket test rig, which was constructed earlier this year, dilapidation surveys, environmental monitoring equipment and bulk excavation works currently occurring at the Macquarie Point site in anticipation of the build.
The $132 million in precinct costs will cover stormwater and water upgrades, power upgrades and landscaping.
A car park has also been included, but MPDC chief executive Anne Beach said it "absolutely wasn't required" for the stadium; rather, it would act as an "enabler" for the precinct.
The $183 million in project delivery costs are the costs to deliver the precinct — It includes paying for consultants, the Macquarie Point Development Corporation staff, incidentals like personal protective equipment, computer software, training, headworks and building insurance.
Not included? The Northern Access Road, which is priced at $78.9 million and, while necessary for the stadium to function, is a long-existing project which will be headed up, and funded by, Building Tasmania.
The updated budget also does not account for interest on the additional borrowings which will be required to cover the increased cost, which will flow through a new "funding facility" being set up for the MPDC.
In other words, the MPDC's borrowing limit will be increased in order for it to pay for the project, and $1.29 billion of the total project cost will currently be borne by the Tasmanian taxpayer through capital and borrowings.
That means the state funding cap of $875 million agreed to by Premier Jeremy Rockliff in exchange for the upper house vote of Bec Thomas is essentially scrapped.
But there does still remain the possibility for more funding from external sources, with the premier today confirming ABC reporting that conversations had begun with the federal government, AFL, cricket and private sector regarding increased financial contributions.
The new budget will be presented to cabinet by November 30 and is expected to be approved, with a construction contract to be signed in the coming weeks.
"We've had our commercial and legal team, as well as our technical team, look at the detail submitted by [BESIX] Watpac," MPDC's Anne Beach said.
"We've undertaken an analysis, and we're confident that we can deliver within that budget," she said.
