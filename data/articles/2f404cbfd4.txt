Reports of artificial intelligence being used to sexually exploit children have risen by more than 8,400% in two years, research has found.
A report by the Edinburgh-based Childlight – Global Child Safety Institute says AI-related child abuse material, including deepfakes using “nudify apps”, had risen from about 4,700 in 2023 to more than 400,000 in 2025.
The images, although fake, can be used to blackmail children to demand money or more images.
The research examined reports of material by tech companies to the US-based organisation the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children.
Prof Debi Fry, the global director of data at Childlight, said the findings showed the “shocking” level of child sexual exploitation.
The research focuses on east Asia and the Pacific, Latin America and the Caribbean, and North America. Previous studies have examined western Europe.
Fry said: “Our research reveals the shocking scale of child sexual exploitation and abuse and how rapidly the threats facing children are evolving, particularly online.
“But the findings also show where governments can make a difference. By bringing together evidence from surveys, police records and other sources, we can build a clearer picture of the risks children face, identify weaknesses in protection systems and help target resources and interventions where they are needed most.”
A report by the Internet Watch Foundation (IWF) last month found AI-generated child sexual abuse material was growing online, with the amount of illegal material investigated this year already exceeding the total for 2025.
IWF analysts have found more photorealistic child sexual abuse material in the first half of 2026 than for the whole of the previous year. The UK-based NGO, which monitors abuse material globally, said it had assessed 6,310 AI images that met the legal definition of child sexual abuse, 40% higher than last year’s total of more than 4,500.
The problem had already risen in 2025, according to the IWF.
Childlight estimates more than 300 million children worldwide are the victims of at least one form of technology-facilitated sexual exploitation or abuse ever year.
It also estimates that about one in four children will be subjected to at least one form of abuse before their 18th birthday. The research also looked at offline sexual abuse reporting.
Almost a third of girls, and more than one in 10 boys were subjected to in-person sexual abuse during childhood in North America, the research found. This includes crimes such as sexual assault, rape and “upskirting”.
The recorded rate of reports and notices involving child sexual abuse material rose by 63% in the US between 2024 and 2025, compared with 18% in Canada.
Fry said: “The speed at which artificial intelligence is changing the threat to children is deeply concerning. These figures show an extraordinary rise in reports of AI-generated child sexual abuse material in just two years.
“While improved detection and reporting may explain some of that increase, the findings underline why governments and technology companies must act urgently to understand and prevent these emerging forms of abuse and remove material rapidly.”
Paul Stanfield, the chief executive of Childlight, said: “Millions of children are being subjected to sexual exploitation and abuse, yet governments are too often working with an incomplete picture of where harm is happening and who is most at risk.
“We cannot effectively prevent abuse or protect children without understanding its true scale and nature. But there is nothing inevitable about child sexual exploitation and abuse. We have the knowledge and tools to make a difference.
“We are calling on governments to recognise this as a preventable public health emergency, invest in better evidence and turn that intelligence into action. With sustained leadership and international collaboration, we can prevent harm, intervene earlier and give millions more children the safe childhoods they deserve.”
