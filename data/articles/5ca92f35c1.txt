Former Liberal MP Sharman Stone has died via voluntary assisted dying after spending much of her political career advocating that people should have autonomy over their bodies.
Stone died aged 75 on Wednesday surrounded by family after a long fight against glioblastoma, an aggressive form of brain cancer. She had a long political career, serving as the minister for workforce participation from 2006-07 and the ambassador for women and girls from 2016-20.
During her time in office, Stone championed contentious medical research and supported access to abortion, and readily – and publicly – disagreed with former leader Tony Abbott on both topics.
“Sharman and I disagreed on ‘life’ issues … but she was always upfront about what she thought and always handled disagreement well,” Abbott told the Sydney Morning Herald after her death.
“That actually made her a good colleague because being straight with people is just about the most important quality anyone can have.”
Stone was one of the first female superintendents in the Victorian Office of Corrections, the first female director of the Victorian Farmers Federation, and the recipient of an economics PhD.
She was elected to federal parliament in 1996, serving through the Howard years in what was then the seat of Murray in northern Victoria.
Nationals MP Sam Birrell, who now holds the same seat Stone once held, paid tribute to his predecessor in a post on Facebook.
“Sharman was an articulate and intelligent fighter for our region, and made significant contributions to parliament and Australian public life in areas such as women’s rights, regional manufacturing and indigenous affairs,” he wrote.
“Sharman was particularly strident in her advocacy for regional communities in relation to the Murray-Darling Basin plan, and she always warned of the dangers of water buybacks to the economies of the regions affected.
“She was never afraid to confront anyone on issues she believed in, even those in her own party.”
Stone was remembered as a strong voice for her local community, right up to her later days.
“She was born and bred in the heart of the electorate she served – a sixth-generation northern Victorian from a farming family,” the opposition leader, Angus Taylor, said after her death.
“She understood the rugged and regenerative nature of the land and what makes regional Australia tick.”
Stone spent two decades in parliament and was characterised by her independence.
“Over all the years, on every day, I have striven to make a difference, to create more opportunities and more choices for the people I love, the people of Murray,” she said in her retirement speech from federal parliament in 2016.
“My Murray electorate is magnificent. Its people are heroic, innovative and resourceful.”
She held several roles during the Howard government, including as parliamentary secretary to the environment and finance ministers.
She also held the shadow environment portfolio after the Liberals’ defeat to Kevin Rudd.
Stone retired from politics in 2016, and served as the Australian ambassador for women and girls until 2020.
Her celebration of life is expected to be held after the 28 November Victorian election.
