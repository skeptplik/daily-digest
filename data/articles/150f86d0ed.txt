WASHINGTON — A new website launched by the Trump administration provides centralized access to more than 92,000 public records from the Department of Health and Human Services and the agencies it oversees — including partially redacted entries from health secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s calendar.
While many of the records were previously available, the health department heralded the launch of the site as a sign of its “radical transparency.”
In June, after a New York Times report referred to him as disengaged with the job, Kennedy said on social media that his calendar was publicly available, though no such calendar had been made available for the media or the public before Tuesday. A number of Freedom of Information Act requests filed by STAT reporters had previously gone unanswered and even unacknowledged and do not appear to be included in the Tuesday release.
Much of the secretary’s partial calendar, which reflects his activities from Feb. 13, 2025, to June 19, 2026, is redacted, including some private calls taken by the secretary. One of his closest aides at the agency, Stefanie Spear, appears to be listed only once in the calendar. References to other notable meetings are not redacted, including one that would ultimately lead to the ouster of a short-lived director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Susan Monarez.
