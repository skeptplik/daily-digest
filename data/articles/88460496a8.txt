PARIS — Ten years after launching its first restaurant in Paris, Loulou Groupe is expanding overseas, with six international openings in the pipeline.
The French hospitality group run by siblings Gilles and Claire Malafosse opened a Loulou restaurant in St. Barths on Friday, marking its first overseas branch — even if the Caribbean island is technically French soil.
That is scheduled to be followed by outposts in Marbella, Spain, in April 2027, and Port Heli in Greece in summer 2028; a London club at the end of 2027, and restaurant openings in Dubai, Miami and São Paulo further down the road.
Many of the new restaurants will be paired with their own boutiques, paving the way for additional collaborations with fashion brands. And Loulou Groupe is hoping to branch out into hotels within the next four years as part of its full lifestyle approach, cofounder and artistic director Claire Malafosse told WWD.
The spate of openings comes two years after Barrière Group acquired a majority stake in Loulou Groupe, pairing a legacy hotel and casino operator with a clutch of trendy eateries. But many of the upcoming projects have been in the works for even longer, Malafosse noted.
“There are a lot of openings coming up at once, but these are projects we’ve been working on for years. It might look like we’re expanding at an incredible pace, but the growth is actually quite measured: we’ve been working on London for six years, and Marbella for six years as well,” she said in an interview at Le Grand Café, the group’s restaurant inside the Grand Palais.
The alliance with Barrière has accelerated Loulou’s rollout in prime holiday destinations, including an outpost at Hôtel Barrière Les Neiges in the ski resort of Courchevel; the St. Barths restaurant on Shell Beach in the capital Gustavia, operated in partnership with the nearby Fouquet’s hotel, and the Miami location, also part of a future Fouquet’s hotel.
It has also ushered in new license agreements with other hotel groups. Loulou Groupe will partner with Four Seasons Hotels and Resorts on the Port Heli project, and with Dubai Holding on its opening at the Burj Al Arab, which is currently closed for renovations.
“We have no exclusivity agreement whatsoever with Barrière. We’re open to partnering with other high-quality hoteliers in destinations that we regard as extraordinary,” Malafosse said.
A Curated Approach
But she insisted that unlike some other hospitality groups, Loulou is not betting on scale to fuel growth. Even with Barrière Group properties, it’s taking a selective approach.
“We won’t open a Loulou in every hotel — that’s absolutely not the goal. We’ll operate only in places we consider exceptional, in a highly discerning way. The ambition is to open in three or four of their hotels, no more, because these are the most premium and charming destinations,” she explained.
“We certainly choose destinations where our customers vacation, such as Saint-Tropez, Monaco, St. Barths and Marbella. Some places, we simply fall in love with. We don’t have a fixed expansion plan; we’re guided largely by instinct. If a place really speaks to us, we’ll consider it,” Malafosse added.
Gilles Malafosse initially partnered with fellow entrepreneur Laurent de Gourcuff on a series of high-profile ventures including Monsieur Bleu, Girafe and Loulou. The two parted ways in 2020 over divergent corporate strategies, with de Gourcuff steering his Paris Society group toward rapid growth with backing from hotel giant Accor.
Claire Malafosse, who quit her career as a lawyer to join her brother in the business, highlighted that theirs is a smaller structure focused on tightly curated locations. Their role model is Le Flandrin, the 16th arrondissement institution that has been in the family for four generations and that has set the tone for their hospitality style.
“We don’t serve haute cuisine; we focus on high-quality ingredients. It’s honest, classic French cooking, delivered consistently, and I think that’s the key. People know they’ll always eat well at our restaurants and receive a warm welcome and attentive service,” she said.
Still, the group aims to log average annual growth of 20 percent and double its earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization between now and 2030. It’s on track to post revenues of 65 million euros this year, up from 60 million euros in 2025 with the same number of restaurants, Malafosse said.
“Given the current climate — knock on wood — we’re among the few really managing to weather the downturn,” she said, referring to the series of widespread anti-government protests that have dampened tourism and consumer spending in France this fall.
A Bigger Vision
Loulou Groupe’s most ambitious project to date is the private members’ club it will open in London at the end of 2027, one year behind schedule.
Malafosse blamed delays in obtaining construction permits for the 25,000-square-foot, Grade II listed Mayfair townhouse that will house 16 Charles Street, but said the company is also tweaking its original concept, which encompasses five floors of salons, bar spaces and restaurants.
“We’re taking our time in London to develop the club concept, which is unfamiliar to us and not really part of French culture. We don’t want to get it wrong. When we open a venue, it’s not to be a flash-in-the-pan. We want to build establishments that become institutions and remain part of our customers’ lives for decades,” she explained.
“We’re thinking about how we can make this club stand out in an extremely competitive market, and what we can offer our clients that they won’t find elsewhere. For example, we’re considering partnerships with auction houses to host events,” Malafosse said.
On a recent visit to New York City, she was “blown away” by Marcel, the restaurant Sotheby’s has opened in the historic Breuer Building on Madison Avenue. “It’s one of the most beautiful restaurants I’ve ever seen, with incredible service,” she marveled.
Opening a brasserie-style restaurant in Manhattan is on her bucket list. “It will happen, but we’re going to take our time and do it right,” she predicted confidently.
Further fashion collaborations are also in the works, following partnerships with brands including Gucci, Burberry, Farm Rio and Bonton, among others. Loulou’s boutiques offer a mix of gifting, resort wear, homewares, accessories and art — and its next frontier is hotels.
“Hospitality is certainly part of our future ambitions. We’d very much like to develop our own hotels and offer a dining experience that differs from what’s currently available,” Malafosse said. “These ideas are already taking shape, although there’s no concrete project yet. It’s certainly something we’d like to do, but opening a hotel requires significant investment, so that will come later.”
