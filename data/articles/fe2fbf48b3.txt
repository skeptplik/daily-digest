The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time brought every great aspect back from the original version — including its iconic boss fights. From fire-breathing dragons to giant spiders to wicked witches, Ocarina of Time features a diverse lineup of boss battles that adds to every dungeon's unique charm, making this one of the best games in The Legend of Zelda series.
Many of the bosses force players to use the items obtained in their respective dungeons in Ocarina of Time. But overall, many of them feature such clever designs and battles that have made exploring the dungeons worthwhile. In fact, the bosses are so great that Nintendo has allowed players to fight them again in the remake’s Hall of Fallen Foes.
No boss in this game is the same, and some are far better than others. While Zelda fans may be familiar with them from the original game, some of the bosses have been given some enhancements in the remake. So now, here are all the bosses in Ocarina of Time ranked from worst to best.
10 Morpha (Water Temple)
Nintendo made the Water Temple in Ocarina of Time much easier and enjoyable for gamers in the remake, but the aquatic boss fight still feels dry. There’s nothing visually noteworthy about Morpha, as it’s essentially a giant blob of water with an amoeba inside it. The player simply has to use the Longshot to pull out Morpha’s core and strike it repeatedly. Though the remake gives Morpha some more dynamic attacks, many can agree that Dark Link was a much more interesting battle in the Water Temple.
9 King Dodongo (Dodongo’s Cavern)
This giant, fire-breathing dinosaur takes over the cavern in which Gorons harvest their rock roast, forcing Link to go in and take it out. Though it is a fearsome presence, King Dodongo loses points for being just a bigger version of the regular Dodongo. At the same time, the boss battle is very straightforward, as Link must repeatedly throw bombs into the beast's mouth before hitting its weak spot. At least it features its own theme song rather than the traditional boss music.
8 Barinade (Lord Jabu-Jabu's Belly)
Lord Jabu-Jabu has some truly weird things in his stomach, from a giant octopus to electric jellyfish to fleshy elevators. But what really takes the cake is the dungeon's boss, Barinade: a giant, tentacled blob that shoots lightning bolts. To defeat it, Link must use the boomerang to sever its tentacles and strike its core, all while avoiding its giant jellyfish and electric blasts.
Barinade is one of the wildest battles in Ocarina of Time and the remake improves the experience by putting it in a dark, neon-lit chamber. It also adds another bit of protection for the boss' weak point, making the fight a more challenging test of the player's skill, timing, and patience.
7 Gohma (Inside the Deku Tree)
Deep inside the Deku Tree, Link encounters his first boss in the form of Gohma. The buildup to this fight is straight out of a horror movie, with Link noticing dirt falling from above before seeing a giant, one-eyed spider crawling above him. The fight itself is pretty simple, as Link must use the Fairy Slingshot to shoot the monster's exposed eye. Given that it's the first boss fight of the game, it doesn't offer a huge challenge. Nevertheless, it serves as a strong preview to the other horrors Link must face throughout Hyrule.
6 Volvagia (Fire Temple)
This ancient lava dragon was revived by Ganondorf to eat the Gorons of Death Mountain. Link faces Volvagia in the heart of the Fire Temple, and their battle is nothing short of glorious. With the Megaton Hammer, Link must break open the dragon's skull as it pops out from the lava pits, turning this boss fight into a fiery game of Whac-A-Mole.
Volvagia poses an especially challenging fight, spewing fire at Link and sending boulders crashing down from the ceiling. At the same time, the game doesn't make this a linear battle, as the player can also use the Fairy Bow to shoot the dragon's exposed skull. Ultimately, this boss fight cranks up the heat with a thrilling environment and concept that allows players to get creative.
5 Bongo Bongo (Shadow Temple)
Despite its silly name, Bongo Bongo is no laughing matter. This phantom shadow beast scorched Kakariko Village after escaping its prison at the Bottom of the Well. After Link chases it into the Shadow Temple, the Hero faces him on top of a giant drum in the middle of a toxic lake.
This boss fight builds an eerie atmosphere with Bongo Bongo’s monstrous moaning and spooky lair. It also forces the player to use their resources wisely in order to come out on top. They must evade and shoot the demon's giant hands, wear the Hover Boots to move steadily across the beating drum, and use the Lenses of Truth to see and strike its head.
4 Phantom Ganon (Forest Temple)
In the first boss as Adult Link, the Hero of Time faces Phantom Ganon, an evil spirit that resembles the Demon King. The boss of the Forest Temple cleverly hides inside multiple paintings in its chamber, forcing Link to shoot the right Phantom Ganon with the Fairy Bow.
But after a few hits, the boss changes tactics, challenging Link to a game of Dead Man’s Volley, in which he must use his sword to knock an energy ball back at him. It’s a thrilling and inventive boss battle that culminates in a haunting cutscene where the real Ganondorf threatens Link, building up hype for the game’s final boss.
3 Twinrova )Spirit Temple)
The surrogate mothers of Ganondorf, Komue and Kotake, known collectively as Twinrova, take control of the Spirit Temple after Young Link retrieves the Silver Gauntlets. Seven years later, after making Link fight one more Iron Knuckle, the two witches grow in size and battle him using their powers over fire and ice.
The boss fight features an incredibly cool concept, in which Link deflects magic lasers back at Twinrova using the Mirror Shield. Also, unlike most of the game’s bosses, Twinrova actually transforms, with both witches fusing into one giant, superpowered sorceress. It feels especially rewarding taking down Twinrova after seeing them brainwash Nabooru for seven years. The remake also adds another intrguing layer to this fight by having the witches spare Young Link beforehand, thinking he wouldn’t be worth killing, making their defeat a result of their own arrogance.
2 Ganondorf (Ganondorf’s Castle)
After lowering the barrier around Ganondorf’s Tower and battling several monsters, Link comes face-to-face with the Demon King himself at the top of his castle. However, Ganondorf's magic prevents Navi from reading his weakness, forcing the player to figure it out for themselves. Ultimately, this fight makes the player use what they learned fighting Phantom Ganon and deflect Ganondorf’s magic back at him, all before shooting him with Light Arrows.
After everything Ganondorf made Link and his friends endure throughout the story, the game pays off this build up by letting the player give the villain an incredible beatdown. The remake heightens the challenge by having Ganondorf remove more portions of the floor during the fight. It seems as though Nintendo knew many veteran Zelda players would be playing this game and wanted to make things more exciting for them. And boy, did it work.
1 Ganon (Ganondorf’s Castle)
Ocarina of Time saved the best for last. After Link and Zelda escape Ganondorf’s Castle before its collapse, the duo see the Demon King has survived the wreckage. Then, in a blind rage, the villain transforms into his final form, a giant, hulking pig demon with two massive swords.
The final boss is perfect based on atmosphere alone. The game fills the player with incredible dread and excitement as Link fights Ganon in the ruins of his castle, all while a dark storm hangs over them. The player can feel the weight of Hyrule on their shoulders as it seems like they're fighting at the end of the world.
The remake makes some clever deviations from the original game by giving Ganon new weak points and new attacks during the second phase. It also features more gripping cinematics that give Zelda a more active role during the battle. Overall, the remake's final boss delivers a much more epic climax to Link and Zelda's war with Ganon.
