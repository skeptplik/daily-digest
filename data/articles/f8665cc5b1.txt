A Peruvian call center operator was sentenced to eight years in prison for stealing millions of dollars from over 30,000 Spanish-speaking US residents in a years-long government-impersonation fraud scheme.
45-year-old Carla Magaly Alcedo Mendoza of Lima, Peru, pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit mail and wire fraud after being charged in an 18-count indictment following her March 2023 arrest and April 2025 extradition to the United States.
According to court documents, Alcedo managed and operated Peruvian call centers linked to multiple large-scale fraud and extortion schemes between January 2013 and December 2018.
The scammers first contacted Spanish-speaking Americans, claiming they qualified for free services or products. However, they later impersonated US government officials and lawyers and told the victims they were contractually obligated to pay large sums of money to receive them.
Together with her accomplices, she defrauded and extorted thousands of Americans by falsely threatening them with court action, arrest, and immigration consequences in unsolicited phone calls targeting Spanish-speaking Americans.
"The scheme spanned several years and thousands of victims made payments to Alcedo. Collectively, the scheme was responsible for defrauding more than 30,000 Spanish-speaking residents of the United States out of $15 million," the Department of Justice said.
"These criminals preyed on hardworking Americans, using fear and intimidation to steal millions of dollars. They deliberately targeted Spanish-speaking communities, impersonating government officials and threatening innocent people with arrest and legal consequences," added Acting US Attorney Yara Lorenzo Klukas.
Alcedo was the tenth defendant extradited from Peru and sentenced in this scheme, with ten other Peruvian call center owner-operators and four distribution center owner-operators involved in the scheme convicted so far.
This follows a June 2026 warning from the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) that Americans have lost $3.5 billion to impersonation scams in 2025, with reported losses nearly tripling since 2020 and approximately $920 million stolen by government impersonators.
In April, the FBI also warned in its 2025 Internet Crime Report that US victims lost nearly $21 billion to cyber-enabled crimes throughout last year.
