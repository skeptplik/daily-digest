Pilar Sabaj came halfway around the world to go straight to the source.
The head of beauty at Mercado Libre, Latin America’s largest e-commerce platform, arrived at Beauty World Dubai in October with teams from Mexico and Brazil and a group of the company’s sellers, intent on meeting the Arabian fragrance houses their shoppers have turned into a social media phenomenon. The team closed negotiations on the show floor, she says, and those brands will reach Latin America within weeks.
	“Working with brand owners is the best guarantee of authentic products and consistent supply for our customers,” Sabaj said in an interview after the show. Dubai, she noted, is “the origin of a great many of the brands our consumers are asking for.”
It is exactly the connection Beauty World Dubai exists to make. A buyer from one part of the world meeting a brand from another, and bringing it to consumers who could never find it before.
Few places are better positioned to make those connections. A natural crossroads, Dubai sits within a few hours’ flight of Europe, Africa and South Asia and its airport connects it to more than 140 destinations. At Beauty World Dubai’s 30th edition, the first under its new name, that geography was on full display — Latin American retailers sourcing Arabian perfume, Korean brands courting Middle East partners and Harrods coming from London to understand their shopper even better. “When this show began 30 years ago, the industry came here to sell. Today, it comes to discover, to invest and to learn,” says Ravi Ramchandani, the show’s event director.
The show’s own data charts that shift. The U.S. and U.K. broke into its top 10 visitor markets for the first time in 2024, after more than a decade of tracking. On the other side of the aisle, UAE exhibitors more than doubled, from 160 in 2019 to 351 in 2025.
	Mia Collins, Harrods’ buying director for beauty, sees it from London. “In days gone by, certain ingredients, certain accords or captives were classified as Middle Eastern,” she said during an exclusive interview at the show. “That now, for me, is entirely irrelevant. It may have first come to the fore in the GCC. Now those are globally recognized.” The client buying into them, she added, “is absolutely international.”
The order books bear that out. Over three days from Oct. 6 to 8, exhibitors reported 51,614 deals and agreements valued at $1.19 billion, according to GRS, the independent research firm the organizer commissioned to track deals on the floor. One South American buyer alone placed orders worth close to $60 million with two of the region’s biggest fragrance houses, according to Ramchandani.
	Unusually, momentum built through the week: 73 percent of exhibitors reported a deal on Day One and 90 percent on days two and three, with the final day the most valuable of the show. “The last day of a trade show is usually the quietest. Ours was the biggest for deals,” said Ramchandani. 
Behind those deals sits the world’s fastest-growing beauty region. Beauty and personal care sales in the Middle East and Africa rose 16 percent in 2025 to $45.2 billion, according to Euromonitor International, against 6 percent globally, to $630.6 billion. Stripping out inflation and currency swings, the region still grew 5 percent, against 2 percent worldwide. Euromonitor expects it to nearly double, from $52 billion in 2026 to $93 billion by 2030.
Star Power, Serious Buyers
When Ramchandani took over in December 2020, in a year the show did not take place, he inherited a strong but predictable regional fair. “A lot of shows around the world are on people’s KPIs, so they attend. They arrive, do their meetings and can’t wait to get home,” he said. “I wanted people to want to be here.” His bet was content: conference stages, masterclasses and education. At this edition, one brand weighing a future stand asked his team, “Can you make it four days?”
This year tested that approach. With the regional conflict weighing on travel and paid tickets introduced for the first time, visits fell 27 percent to 62,130 and the show drew 1,300 exhibitors from 56 countries, 239 of them first-timers. But visitors with senior management titles rose 25 percent, and final decision-makers jumped 178 percent. Some 42 percent came from outside the UAE, with Brazil, the U.S., the U.K., France, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Oman among the leading markets.
	“The easy option was to cut back and just deliver a show. We didn’t,” Ramchandani said. “Every edition has to be earned, and it was our 30th, so we invested heavily in content.”
The roster made the case. On the Next in Fragrance stage, Louis Vuitton master perfumer Jacques Cavallier-Belletrud and Aurélien Guichard, master perfumer at Takasago and founder of Matière Première, spoke to the region’s growing pull on global perfumery. Givaudan’s Jenine Guerriero closed the conference with predictions for the year ahead. Collins addressed the future of luxury retail at Next in Beauty.
In The Makeup Studio, Patrick Ta’s red-carpet masterclass was mobbed and Mary Phillips, makeup artist to Kendall Jenner and founder of M.Ph, taught alongside Josh Lamonaca and Maggie Seeman. On the BeautyLive stage, Dimitris Giannetos ran a two-hour masterclass and the Spanish World Champions of Hair, Jorge X, Rafael Bueno and Carlos Valiente, closed the show.
	The Scent Trail
If makeup drew the crowds, fragrance drove the deals. Euromonitor expects the category to deliver a third of the region’s absolute value gains in beauty and personal care this year. At the show, it accounts for less than a quarter of exhibitors but about a third of projected deal value and the fragrance halls filled so fast that the organizer built a temporary one, Za’abeel Hall 7, rather than turn brands away. Close to 100 ingredient houses, including Givaudan, Dsm-firmenich, Symrise and Takasago, now exhibit alongside the Gulf brands that buy from them.
The people who compose the world’s perfumes agree. “I think the way people fragrance themselves in the Middle East is influencing the entire world today,” Guichard told WWD after his talk.
	The appeal of brands like Lattafa, Armaf and Afnan that have gone viral with Latin American shoppers, is “an excellent balance between quality and price, intense and long-lasting scent profiles, and distinctive, eye-catching presentation,” says Sabaj. Coming to Beauty World and dealing directly with brand owners secures volume, early access to launches and full catalogs. “Fragrance is the starting point, but the strategic opportunity is much broader,” she said about the show. “Together here we can grow beauty as a whole, from fragrance to skin care and hair care.”
Built in the Gulf
The region’s beauty exports go beyond fragrance, and Huda Kattan, named Icon of the Year at this year’s Beauty World Dubai Awards, proved it more than a decade ago. “My love of beauty grew into something far bigger than I could have imagined, and I’m thankful to the region for being my home and the place where I could make a difference in beauty,” Kattan said while accepting the honor via video from Paris. A new generation is following. Dubai-born Aïza was named the Homegrown Brand of the Year.
	“Every one of them gives international buyers another reason to come, the way Arabian fragrance does today,” Ramchandani said.
Harrods has backed the regional pipeline for years. The store was Huda Beauty’s first U.K. location, following its launch at Sephora in Dubai, and carries regional fragrance houses including Amouage, Widian and Kayali. Collins calls Gulf clients “an exceptionally important part of our client portfolio, and that’s not a new thing.” What sets Beauty World Dubai apart, she added, is “the proliferation of Middle Eastern-born brands and distributors.”
The show’s pull also runs strongly toward South Asia. One of the edition’s biggest moments came on opening day, when Bollywood superstar Salman Khan arrived to launch Kenz de Rasasi, the new luxury house from Dubai’s Rasasi Perfumes, and hundreds of fans mobbed the aisles to catch a glimpse.
Dubai-based makeup artist Vimi Joshi, whose namesake brand Ulta stocks across its Middle East locations, led a Diwali-themed makeup masterclass. “Dubai celebrates diversity, it celebrates inclusivity,” she said. “In terms of makeup artistry, I do want to hold the South Asian flag, and yet we are a global brand.”
	Collins says the South Asian shopper “for decades upon decades one of the cornerstones” of the London market, is part of the draw to be at Beauty World. “To observe how that client is moving around and is responding to things in Dubai and in the Emirates is always profoundly useful,” she said.
	Imported categories are maturing, too. Euromonitor names an “Asian wave” among its top five beauty trends for 2026 and places K-beauty in its growth phase in the Middle East. Dubai based chain XBeauty has built a business on that wave. The 11 mall-based stores across the UAE centers its edit on Asian beauty. Pranay Sethi, product director of its beauty division, said Asian brands now come to the Gulf with bigger ambitions. “Historically, many brands approached the Middle East primarily as an export opportunity,” he said. “The conversation is shifting from ‘Who can distribute our products?’ to ‘Who can help us build our brand?’” Gulf shoppers now arrive asking about niacinamide, peptides and PDRN, he added, and the climate makes Asian sun care, with its lightweight textures and minimal white cast, one of the region’s most promising categories.
In 2027, the show will leave Dubai World Trade Centre, its home for three decades, for Dubai Exhibition Centre at Expo City Dubai, site of the 2020 World Expo. Within days of this year’s close, the organizer had booking requests for more than a third of the planned space, more than half of it for fragrance. Niche perfume gets its own event, Notes Dubai, in January.
Ramchandani wants the show ahead of the next trend, and longevity tops his list. A new section launching in 2027 will be strict about what gets in. “The bar will be evidence: clinical data, published studies and results you can measure,” he said.
	By the 35th edition, he wants Beauty World Dubai to be the world’s top beauty show. “Not the biggest. The best for business, for education and for experiences,” he said. “If you’re in beauty and you’re not in Dubai yet, are you really in this space?”
