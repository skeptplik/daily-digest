Lanterns star Aaron Pierre had already reprised his role as Green Lantern John Stewart for the 2027 film Man of Tomorrow when the news broke that DC Studios bosses James Gunn and Peter Safran had been fired by new parent company Skydance. Whether his appearance will remain in the final film now that there’s a new regime in charge of DC, Pierre says, “I’m very at peace. What will be will be.”
In a podcast chat with IndieWire recorded the same day the news about Gunn and Safran broke, Pierre says, “Well, I actually only just found out the news prior to logging on so I haven’t very much time at all to process the enormity of that news. But what I can say is that my relationship with James and my relationship with Peter is a beautiful relationship and I’ve really loved every moment collaborating with them and working with them and getting to know them. And I’m excited to, beyond this point, in a different context, collaborate with them again and I’m also excited to see what happens next for DC.”
The Superman sequel Man of Tomorrow was in post-production when Gunn and Safran were removed from their posts. It didn’t take long for the online rumor mill to flood the internet with claims that Skydance was going to remove many of the cameos from Man of Tomorrow, namely Supergirl and Blue Beetle. John Stewart is among the DC heroes set to appear in the film.
Gunn, though, shot down all such claims of studio meddling, stating that no one at Skydance has even seen the film yet. "There are a lot of untrue rumors going around, but one of them is that Skydance has seen Man of Tomorrow and that all sorts of sweeping changes are being made," Gunn posted. "No one from Skydance has even seen the movie - the only people that have are Peter Safran and a very small trusted group of friends and family. So any stories around this narrative are false."
It remains unclear what DC Studios’ future looks like until Jeremy Latcham, the incoming President and Chief Creative Officer of DC Studios, takes his post on December 1. For his part, Pierre is remaining zen about what’s in store for Man of Tomorrow and DC’s future. (The status of Lanterns Season 2 also remains up in the air.)
“I think for me, you know, we did the film and we really loved every moment of doing the film and what happens beyond this point is not my decision. So I’m very at peace,” Pierre tells IndieWire.
“What will be will be. But what I do know is that those friendships will last beyond this point and also those artistic creative, working collaboration relationships will live beyond this point as well. And as I say, I’m excited to see where DC goes from here.”
Until Latcham says otherwise, fans can only suspect things are back to square one again lest they heed some fans’ advice not to reboot the DCU again. If that’s it for Gunn and Safran’s “Gods and Monsters” saga then why should fans be invested in Man of Tomorrow if it no longer matters? It will likely just collapse at the box office like The Flash did after Gunn ended the DCEU.
