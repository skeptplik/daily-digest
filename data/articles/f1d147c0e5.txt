Iranian journalist, Oscar-nominated screenwriter Mehdi Mahmoudian arrested in Tehran
Mahmoudian reported on 2 jailed protesters at risk of execution day before arrest
Rights groups are calling for the release of freelance journalist and Oscar-nominated screenwriter Mehdi Mahmoudian after Iranian authorities arrested him at his Tehran home on Wednesday, a day after he reported on an execution notice of two jailed protesters in Iran.
Hosein Razzagh, a friend of Mahmoudian's and an Iranian activist based in Europe, told The Associated Press on Thursday that Mahmoudian was taken by security forces from his home.
The Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ) said in a statement on Thursday that a source close to the journalist told the organization that Mahmoudian's home was searched and his electronic devices were confiscated before he was detained. The source spoke to CPJ on condition of anonymity for fear of reprisal, it said.
A message posted on Mahmoudian's Telegram channel later on Thursday said he had been charged with anti-government "propaganda against the regime" and "spreading false information." His detention was also reported on Wednesday by reformist news outlet Emtedad.
A day before his arrest, Mahmoudian published an execution notice on his channel that two protesters jailed after the 2022 nationwide "Woman, Life, Freedom" protests were at risk of execution within 24 hours.
Mahmoudian posted later that day that according to a lawyer and family, the sentences had not been carried out.
The 2022 protests were sparked by the death of 22-year-old Mahsa Amini after she was arrested by Iran's morality police over allegedly not wearing her headscarf correctly. Amnesty International said in a September report that authorities committed crimes against humanity in the monthslong crackdown on the protests that left hundreds of people dead and thousands detained.
Mahmoudian, who co-wrote the 2025 Iranian film It Was Just an Accident, which won the Palme d’Or at Cannes, has campaigned openly on behalf of jailed protesters and those at risk of execution in Iran, Razzagh said, adding that Iranian authorities "are extremely sensitive about execution cases."
There has been no official statement or public comment from Iranian authorities on his arrest.
The Oslo-based Iran Human Rights group has said at least 15 protesters have been executed in connection with the protests over Amini, with two of the executions being carried out in 2026.
Mahmoudian subjected to severe torture
During a brief telephone call with his family on Thursday, Mahmoudian said he had been transferred to Greater Tehran Prison, just outside of Iran's capital. He said that security forces had severely beaten him during his arrest, leaving visible injuries, the source told CPJ.
Reporters Without Borders (RSF) said Mahmoudian was also reportedly subjected to severe torture in detention, to the point of losing consciousness, according to confirmed reports it received.
"Journalism saves lives, and Mehdi Mahmoudian’s courage deserves to be celebrated. Instead, the journalist has been arrested, imprisoned and reportedly tortured by the very authorities he exposed," said Jonathan Dagher, head of RSF Middle East, in a statement on Thursday.
"RSF stands in full solidarity with the renowned journalist and filmmaker and calls for his immediate release. Publishing information in the public interest must never be treated as a crime.”
In recent months, the Iranian regime has resumed its wave of arrests targeting media professionals, RSF said.

Razzagh told The Associated Press he last spoke with Mahmoudian by phone roughly between 2 to 3 a.m. on Wednesday about a death penalty case linked to the 2022 protests.
He said that Mahmoudian later didn't answer his phone, and that witnesses reported seeing more than a dozen armed agents raid his home.
The screenwriter and journalist has been repeatedly imprisoned for his work.
Mahmoudian was arrested earlier this year on Jan. 31 and subsequently released on Feb. 17, shortly before the Academy Awards, according to the CPJ.
His detention comes on the heels of Tehran's sentencing on Oct. 26 of his colleague and acclaimed director Jafar Panahi to five years and one month in prison. The Tehran court also ordered the confiscation of Panahi’s “movable and immovable assets."
Director Jafar Panahi poses on the red carpet at the Academy Awards in Hollywood on March 15. (Daniel Cole/Reuters)
Panahi, who directed and co-wrote It Was Just an Accident with Mahmoudian, returned to Iran in March after he had been previously convicted of “propaganda activities against the system.” Panahi has not been detained at this time, but his current whereabouts are unclear.
Panahi is among the most celebrated of contemporary Iranian directors and has continued making movies despite repeatedly being imprisoned, banned from travelling and put under house arrest by Iranian authorities over the past 20 years.
Mahmoudian and Panahi were among 17 Iranian activists, filmmakers and lawyers who signed a public statement issued on Jan. 28 condemning Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and the violent crackdown on protesters following mass anti-government protests in January.
