GL.iNet has warned customers that a threat actor exploited a vulnerability in its cloud-based remote management service to deliver malware to some routers and KVM devices.
China-based networking hardware maker GL.iNet is well known for its pocket-sized travel routers and Comet KVM devices. The devices can be managed remotely through its GoodCloud platform.
The vendor discovered the remote management service vulnerability leveraged by the attacker on November 20 and has since patched it.
However, it noted that the fix doesn’t remove the malware from infected devices — customers need to install a separate “security firmware” for that. GL.iNet recommends installing the security firmware on all devices, even those with no signs of infection.
It remains unclear who is behind the attack and how many devices were compromised.
It’s also unclear whether the attackers reached the computers connected to compromised KVM devices.
“As a precaution, check for unfamiliar programs, browser extensions or user accounts; unexpected startup items or scheduled tasks; and unusual settings changes or network activity. Run a full scan with up-to-date security software,” the company recommends for KVM devices.
Security and networking firm RemoteToHome Consulting, which manages a large number of GL.iNet routers, has been investigating the incident. Based on its code analysis, the attack worked because the GoodCloud software on the routers ran commands from cloud messages without checking them. GL.iNet has not confirmed this information.
RemoteToHome reported that most of the nearly 200 routers it checked appeared to be impacted, either by this new attack or an older backdoor that appears to date back to September 2025. RemoteToHome says it doesn’t know how that older backdoor was installed.
SecurityWeek has reached out to GL.iNet for additional information and will update this article if it responds.
