Turns out that despite much evidence to the contrary, governments that commit atrocities are sometimes held to account for their crimes.
The International Court of Justice last week found Myanmar responsible for genocide against the Rohingya, in a landmark judgment that will reverberate far beyond the country’s borders. It is the first time the U.N.’s top legal body has held a state responsible for the crime and the findings will be dissected line-by-line regarding what it could mean for other proceedings, such as South Africa’s 2023 case before the court that accuses Israel of genocide in Gaza — an allegation Israel rejects.
The judgment detailed the military’s horrific crimes against the mostly Muslim minority, from coordinated hate speech, widespread killings, mass-scale gang rapes, forcible displacement, systemic burning of villages, the separation of children from their families and the imposition of measures to prevent births within the group. It will no doubt lend considerable support to moves aimed at preventing ongoing violations and to secure reparations for victims.
