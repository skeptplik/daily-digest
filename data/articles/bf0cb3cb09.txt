A Palestinian man who worked for the EU in Gaza has told of a “devastating” struggle to survive after he was made redundant by bosses in Brussels.
He was let go last year after being evacuated to Egypt but his redundancy payment was made to an account to which he has no access. “My family’s situation has become increasingly desperate,” said Mohammad Baraka, a former staffer at the EU border assistance mission (EUBam).
“Both of my daughters have suffered severe psychological distress as a result of the war, displacement and the prolonged uncertainty surrounding our future.”
The 220,000 Israeli shekels (£52,000) payment for almost 20 years’ service was made on 5 August last year to the Bank of Palestine but access to the money has been blocked, a risk he was aware of given the relatively large sum being transferred.
He said he wrote on several occasions to the European Commission, asking it not to make the payment into that bank because of a known risk that a large sum of money could be misconstrued as war funds. “I am now in the devastating position of having money in my name that I cannot access, while my family is experiencing severe financial hardship,” said Baraka.
Instead he had asked for the payment to go to a new account in an Abu Dhabi bank, but that never materialised.
Baraka was evacuated in 2023 after his house in Gaza was bombed and family members killed. The EU tried to relocate him to alternative offices in the West Bank but travel for Palestinians in Gaza was severely restricted. After a hasty three-day concession agreed with the Egyptian government, Baraka was able to leave for Cairo.
As the conflict continued, Baraka was initially able to work remotely from Cairo as an EU employee and had the comfort of a salary, private healthcare and access to schools for his two children. But when Brussels decided to close its EUBam office in Rafah, he was let go, leading to what he says have been traumatic consequences for himself and his wife and two daughters.
He says the evacuation to Egypt with no plan for onward travel to an EU nation or a diplomatically secured place in another country has rendered him stateless, with no residency rights and no right to healthcare or schooling for his seriously ill wife and two daughters, both of whom he says are suffering from the trauma of war.
One of his daughters has an acute mental health condition and Baraka says he cannot pay for her treatment. “I am not asking for charity or special treatment. I am asking for access to my own money, and for accountability over a decision that could have been avoided,” said Baraka.
“I had specifically asked the [EU] mission to transfer my money to my new bank account in Egypt to avoid precisely this situation,” he said.
“If the EU argues that it fulfilled its obligations simply because it transferred the settlement, I believe the legitimate question is whether an employer can consider its responsibility discharged when it knowingly disregards an employee’s repeated warnings about the destination of such a substantial payment.”
Last year he wrote to the European Commission president, Ursula von der Leyen, pleading with her to reverse what he called his “unjust dismissal” and asked her to restore his employment, even temporarily, “until a dignified and legal solution is arranged”.
He also wrote to the European ombudsman to ask them to open an investigation but they replied last week to say they had no scope for an inquiry. “We fully appreciate that you and your family are in an immensely difficult position. The hardship you are experiencing as a result of the war and your displacement are clear from your complaint,” wrote Tina Nilsson, the head of inquiries.
“I realise that this conclusion will be disappointing. I nevertheless hope you can understand why, based on the information available, there are insufficient grounds for the ombudsman to intervene further.”
He is now suing the commission for alleged breach of Belgian employment law but the case could take years to be heard.
Without the “laissez-passer” document that allowed him to be evacuated from Gaza, he cannot leave Egypt, he says, putting him in an impossible position.
A spokesperson for the commission said: “Due to the ongoing related court case, we cannot comment on this further.”
