For years, the Edgar Thomson steel mill stood on the outskirts of Pittsburgh as a rusting symbol of faded American industrial power. Two years ago, the plant, opened by Andrew Carnegie in 1875, appeared to be on the brink of closure, threatening to take hundreds of jobs with it.
Today, however, the mill is being jolted back to life by an unlikely savior. Following a fraught $14.9 billion takeover of U.S. Steel by Japan’s Nippon Steel, a squad of translation app-wielding Japanese engineers has descended on the plant, armed with a multibillion-dollar mandate to drag the aging facility into the 21st century.
In the Mon Valley, an industrial region winding along the Monongahela River in southwestern Pennsylvania, land at the Edgar Thomson site is being cleared for a new steel rolling mill the length of five football fields. Inside the existing facilities, equipment is being upgraded, while engineers dispatched by Nippon Steel are introducing new and more efficient Japanese steelmaking techniques.
