United Airlines said it has resolved a "widespread system outage" that affected hundreds of departing flights on Monday.
Flight tracker FlightAware said 418 United flights were delayed on Monday afternoon.
The issue comes three days after a malfunction in the Federal Aviation Administration's network grounded flights across the US on Friday.
What did United Airlines say about Monday's tech issues?
United Airlines said there was "no indication" that Monday's outage was the result of a cyberattack.
There was also no indication that the issues affecting United Airlines on Monday were related to Friday's nationwide shutdown.
United Airlines said later on Monday that it had "resolved a technology issue that delayed flight departures."
The airline added that airborne flights, as well as those operated by its regional brand United Express, were not affected.
The disruption on Friday and Monday highlights how vulnerable air travel is to tech failures.
Disruption follows other recent major tech meltdowns
United Airlines' computer problem comes amid other recent similar high-profile technology meltdowns, including Friday's nationwide grounding.
Thousands of people in the US said they were unable to access their online banking accounts at several large US banks after JPMorgan Chase reported a technical issue.
On Thursday, Microsoft saw a major disruption to its services, including its Outlook email and Teams messenger.
The tech company said Saturday that its services had been fully restored.
