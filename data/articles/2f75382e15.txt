On Monday morning, Jensen Huang walked into Nvidia’s Santa Clara headquarters to rapturous applause from employees lining the walkways above him. Huang was visibly moved as one colleague threw themselves into his arms for a hug.
The spectacular reinstatement of the chief executive of Nvidia, less than two weeks after he resigned in a scandal over an affair with a customer and suspected Chinese spy—as well as a second reported affair with a subordinate—caught even the company’s most senior executives by surprise.
The abrupt reversal by Nvidia’s board was spurred by President Donald Trump, who applied pressure to the board over the weekend after watching what was once America’s most valuable company as well as the wider tech sector haemorrhage value last week.
It was the culmination of frenetic lobbying by a “war room” of Huang’s Silicon Valley allies, who pulled in Trump and vice-president JD Vance, according to people familiar with the events. They argued the Nvidia founder was personally integral to the health of the US AI industry, and therefore the stock market and entire economy.
“Great men make great mistakes. But nobody has ever been like this, not Elon Musk, not anyone. All the success we’re enjoying, all the tech gains, that’s all down to one guy,” said an ally of Huang.
But one of the most remarkable comebacks in corporate history, which called to mind Sam Altman’s brief ousting at OpenAI, still leaves the semiconductor giant at the heart of the AI boom in treacherous waters.
Nvidia and Huang face probes by the FBI and SEC, congressional investigations, and difficult questions over governance and Huang’s personal connections to a suspected Chinese spy allegedly involved in chip smuggling.
Even after gaining nearly $1 trillion in market value on the news of Huang’s return on Monday, Nvidia is worth about a fifth less than it was a month ago.
