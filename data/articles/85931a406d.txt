The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3 opened with a frightening scene in which Sauron (Charlie Vickers) forges a magical ring. It was a deliberate fakeout (the Orc he tested it on burst into flames) but also a chilling reminder of the inevitable. Sauron is eventually going to forge the One Ring and come into his full power as the Dark Lord of Mordor. But he can’t seem to get it right — yet.
Sauron has spent much of this season trying to uncover a secret he believes Celebrimbor (Charles Edwards) took to his grave. Episodes 5 and 6, released Wednesday on Prime Video, finally bring the missing pieces together and point toward an origin for the One Ring that diverges significantly from J.R.R. Tolkien’s canon. Here’s what The Rings of Power season 3, episode 6’s ending reveals about Sauron’s plan, and how it changes the One Ring’s canonical creation.
This story contains spoilers for The Rings of Power season 3, episodes 5 and 6.
What secret does Sauron learn from Celeborn?
Earlier this season, the Orc Marnukh (Adam Young) led Arondir (Ismael Cruz Córdova) and Galadriel (Morfydd Clark) to the fortress of Gundabad looking for the last of the elven smiths, mainly because Marnukh had exposed Sauron’s plan to forge the One Ring. Sauron believed the smith knew some secret of the craft that would allow him to finally achieve it. That smith, however, had died by the time Galadriel or Sauron arrived. Galadriel’s long-lost husband Celeborn (Jamie Campbell Bower) had spent years in a nearby cell. Did the smith reveal any secrets while Celeborn was imprisoned? That’s a question Sauron is willing to bet on — and torture for.
After Galadriel and Celeborn escaped Gundabad at the end of episode 4, they get some time alone together as they run from Sauron’s forces. In a moment of desperation as episode 5 wraps up, Celeborn surrenders himself to protect Galadriel. Unfortunately, it seems like it’s Celeborn he wants anyway. Sauron resorts to all manner of torture to get the information out of Celeborn. The worst of it is a mechanism that presses down on Celeborn’s entire body as the Orcs keep piling on more weight. For a while, it seems like Celeborn doesn’t know anything and might wind up dead.
Meanwhile, Galadriel encounters Theo, who’s spent much of this season as one of Sauron’s Nazgnagôl but is having second thoughts. With Galadriel’s help, he’s able to remove his ring. They then travel together in the hopes of rescuing Celeborn and destroying Theo’s ring.
Toward the very end of episode 6, we see an Orc head roll into view just outside of Celeborn’s new cell, and Galadriel rushes in to save her husband. A bit delirious, he proudly claims he didn’t reveal any of the secrets he knows to Sauron, specifically about the dagger that Galadriel inherited from her dead brother, Finrod. “Gold and silver from Valinor is the key,” he says. Back in season 1, Celebrimbor melted down the dagger — which was forged in Valinor — as the raw material for the three Elven Rings.
Galadriel kisses Celeborn, but as the camera pans, she becomes Sauron. All that torture was just the warm-up, and this bit of shapeshifting trickery finally gets Sauron the information he needs.
Why does Sauron need Celebrimbor’s hammer?
Sauron quickly realizes that the hammer he took from Celebrimbor also contains gold and silver from Valinor, so he’s already got the raw material he needs to forge the One Ring. But there’s a problem: The hammer was forged by Fëanor, the legendary Elven craftsman who used it to create the Silmarils. (Those three jewels preserved the light of the Two Trees of Valinor, and the struggle over their possession drives much of Tolkien’s The Silmarillion.)
Celebrimbor’s apparition tells Sauron that no forge can melt the hammer, and even dragon fire would not stain it. The episode’s closing moments suggest the obvious answer as Mount Doom rumbles in the distance. Seemingly the only way to melt the hammer is in the fires of Mount Doom, which itself is said to be a sort of wound in the world created by the first Dark Lord.
It now makes a lot more sense why Sauron keeps Celebrimbor’s body in Sauron’s throne room and why the dead elf seems to be haunting the new Dark Lord. Celebrimbor is, in fact, Fëanor’s only grandchild, which explains why he too was a renowned smith who had the hammer in his possession to begin with.
How is the One Ring’s creation different in Tolkien’s books?
Fëanor certainly used some kind of hammer when crafting the Silmarils, but this specific tool is a novel invention unique to Rings of Power. Tolkien largely leaves the finer details of the One Ring’s creation a mystery. We know that Sauron poured a great deal of his essence into the creation of the One Ring using dark magic, and that he did it within the Crack of Doom, a volcanic fissure deep within Mount Doom. Tolkien never bothers to explain the raw materials or any other specific details.
Rings of Power has also made many changes to the order of events. In Tolkien’s chronology, Sauron forges the One Ring around the year 1600 of the Second Age. When he puts it on, the Elves perceive his deception and remove their rings. The destruction of Eregion and Celebrimbor’s death happen in 1697, almost a century later. Once Sauron takes the Nine Rings, he distributes them to kings of Men who are gradually corrupted into full-on Nazgûl over the course of several centuries. The first recorded sighting of a Ringwraith wasn’t until 2251. Like the hammer, the Nazgnagôl Ring-servants are unique to The Rings of Power.
In the show, Sauron is still struggling to make the One Ring five years after Celebrimbor’s death and the fall of Eregion. These changes also set up a bitter parallel with season 1. Galadriel surrendered her brother’s dagger so Celebrimbor could make something that might preserve the Elves’ future in Middle-earth. Sauron is now poised to destroy another cherished Elven heirloom to bring Middle-earth under his control.
