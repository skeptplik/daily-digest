In short:
Adelaide's Taylah Lessue received abuse on social media after losing at the City of Playford Tennis International.
The 18-year-old was ill during the match, which was live-streamed.
What's next?
Police are investigating "inappropriate and offensive messages".
An 18-year-old player has been subjected to death threats and graphic abuse after losing her tennis match during an Adelaide international tournament.
Adelaide's Taylah Lessue was competing during the City of Playford Tennis International on Sunday, winning the first set 6-4 before succumbing to an ongoing illness and losing 6-4, 0-6, 0-6.
According to her mother, Rachelle Lessue, the singles player had been bed-ridden for three weeks with glandular fever before giving the tournament "everything she had".
"Only eight days ago she was in hospital on a drip and lost 6 kilograms in a week unable to eat or swallow anything," Ms Lessue wrote in a social media post.
"But what breaks my heart is, 15 minutes after walking off court, the disgusting, hurtful and sick messages she received on social media."
Lessue is number 647 in the ITF World Tennis Junior Girls Singles rankings and her match, which was streamed live, had apparently been subject to betting.
She received a torrent of violent, sexually explicit death threats shortly after her loss, even as she recovered from the "fatigue, chest tightness and muscle spasms" her mother said had affected her performance.
"These people have no idea what Tay has battled the last few weeks, and the huge amount of courage and fight she showed to play today," Ms Lessue said.
The abuse started as Lessue stood courtside after the match with City of Playford Mayor Glenn Docherty, who described it as "too graphic" to repeat.
"I know that she's a pretty resilient young lady, but she should not be putting up with this type of abuse," he told 891 ABC Adelaide.
"It's just absolutely scary.
"To see the content of those messages … I'm sickened by it.
"Hopefully this person or persons can be unmasked and can be dealt with appropriately by law enforcement."
Police said they were investigating after a victim received messages of an inappropriate and offensive nature on social media.
"Northern District Police are investigating and will determine if there is evidence to sustain any criminal charges," a spokesperson said.
Tennis Australia responds
Tennis Australia head of integrity Dan Stuk said it was disgraceful that people would go online and "target individuals that they don't even know simply because of their sporting performance".
He said social media was an "overwhelmingly positive space" that gave a lot of "support and love for a number of athletes".
"But there are small numbers of abuse taking place and what we're finding is that it has, as you would imagine, very deeply personal impacts and it can have a real effect on the athletes."
Mr Stuk said the abuse could be targeted at anyone from experienced players to junior athletes, and was "circumstance-specific".
"I would say that online abuse can come from a number of different areas and it can be betting-related, but it can also be related to any other aspect of society,"
he said.
"Our job at Tennis Australia is to be ready to respond to that, regardless of the abuse.
He said that included educating players about social media use and their privacy settings, how to manage and report any online abuse, and options to monitor and moderate online content with a third-party provider so "they don't even see this in the first place".
It also included providing support for players and family in response to abusive content.
Ms Lessue said tournament doctors examined her daughter after the match and she had been ruled out of any competition for the next seven to 10 days.
"But the way she conducted herself and fought until the end, when most other people would be bedridden or retired mid-match, fills me with immense pride," she said.
"While it's a loss on the scoreboard, it's another step in her journey and she learned many valuable lessons.
"Above all else, she can hold her head high knowing she fought her heart out and gave the match everything she had."
