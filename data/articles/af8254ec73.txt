Moscow, Kyiv exchange overnight attacks on energy infrastructure
Kyiv and Moscow both assessed damage to energy infrastructure on Wednesday morning as both sides continued to inflict damage on one another amid Russia's over four-and-a-half-year attempted invasion of neighboring Ukraine.
Russia again focused its attacks on Ukraine's energy structure in an attempt to break the will of the country's civilian population as the bitter cold of winter sets in.
Moscow's troops attacked a thermal energy power plant, shutting it down; as well as targeting a gas production facility in Poltawa, where ensuing fires also shut down operations.
Ukraine's acting energy minister said that power outages were reported in five regions as a result of the Russian attacks.
In Russia's Samara region, Ukrainian drones hit at least two oil refineries, killing one person, sparking large fires and and causing energy blackouts.
France, Italy offer different takes on Ukraine's actions in Libya
Kyiv's attacks on Russian ships in and off the coast of Libya over the past couple of days have ruffled feathers among Ukraine's allies, with France and Italy, for instance, coming down on entirely different sides of the issue.
French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said Kyiv was justified in its actions because it has an "absolute right to self-defense." Libya on Wednesday announced that two of its soldiers had died from injuries sustained during a weekend drone attack that sank a Russian warship.
Speaking with French broadcaster TF1, Barrot said, "For nearly five years now, [Russian President] Vladimir Putin has not only been bombing Ukraine, but has also been constantly engaging in acts of aggression and harassing Europeans in an attempt to undermine our unity."
Barrot said Russia alone is to blame for the war and its expansion, and then asked, "What would we do if we were being bombarded day and night by an enemy — an invader?"
Italy's Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani on Wednesday criticized Kyiv's actions, saying, "It was a mistake to violate Libya's sovereignty" and warned against further similar activities in other regions.
Speaking with Italy's Corriere della Sera newspaper ‌on Wednesday, Tajani said, "Italy was absolutely unaware of Kyiv's intentions to strike in Libyan territorial waters, even inside a port."
Tajani said he had spoken with Ukraine Foreign Minister Andrii Sybihato and conveyed Italy's concern over the matter, adding that Rome would continue its staunch support for Ukraine and its sovereignty while at the same time seeking to keep the conflict from spreading.

Merz called out those wanting to curtail support as either "failing to grasp" the fact that "Russia wants more than Ukraine" or "pursuing entirely different aims with regard to our country's freedom, openness and liberalism."
