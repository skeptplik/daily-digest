Developer Playground Games had to look beyond Lionhead's original run of Fable games when rebooting the classic fantasy series, and it's all thanks to games like The Witcher 3, which changed what many players expect from their big RPGs and set a new "gold standard."
The upcoming Fable reboot is finally ending its decade-long development journey soon. Playground Games had to set up an entirely new team to work on its fairytale revival, so Fable's production wasn't quite as smooth sailing as the studio's consistently excellent Forza Horizon series, for example.
Asked about what's changed during that time, in an interview with GamesIndustry.biz, studio founder and general manager Ralph Fulton named technological advancements as a biggie, but the impact of other games was perhaps even greater.
"In 2015, The Witcher 3 redefined what an open-world action RPG looks like and became the gold standard," Fulton said. "And after that bar was raised, the Assassin's Creed franchise changed into this more modern iteration with Origins, Odyssey, and Valhalla, and became like a quasi-RPG."
"All of these games continue to reset player expectations," he continued. Millions of players suddenly had certain expectations about what their big-budget, open-world role-playing games should look like. So, Playground couldn't pull inspiration from Fable and Fable alone.
"I think looking only to the original Fable trilogy for inspiration would leave us lacking in terms of what the modern audience, the modern marketplace, expects," Fulton added.
Still, Playground's Fable is drawing most heavily from Lionhead's Fable. Fulton specifically mentions the game's signature British wit and in-flux morality system as examples. Then there's also the spontaneous life sim elements that "none of our competitors do," according to Fulton. "It's a totally Fable thing, the social simulation and economic simulation. Okay, there are games that have economic simulations, but they're not in [our] RPG category. Fable is unique in that it's almost like a life sim in terms of genre."
Raising four separate families and becoming the entire map's benevolent landlord was one of the funnest parts of Fable back in the day, alongside those sometimes silly characters. Seeing next year's Fable lean into those systemic choices, and having Albion's NPCs react to them more believably, could give this one a very long shelf life.
