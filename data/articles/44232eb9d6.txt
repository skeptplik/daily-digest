Carney selects new ambassador to China, plans to announce 'in very short order'
Canada without a permanent ambassador in Beijing since April
Canadians will know the name of their new ambassador to China "in very short order," said Prime Minister Mark Carney on Tuesday.
The position has been unfilled for six months.
"It's a little longer than we would have liked," Carney said at a news conference in Montreal. "We wanted to get the right person and we have found that right person."
He said the standard formal diplomatic process is underway to have China, the host country, approve the proposed candidate.
Jennifer May served as Canada's last ambassador to China, stepping down from her role in April to become Carney's foreign and defence policy adviser in the Privy Council Office.
She was Canada's first female ambassador to the People's Republic of China, and had been appointed to the role in September 2022.
"As I leave Beijing, I do so with deep gratitude for the experiences, partnerships and friendships built here, and with anticipation for the challenges ahead," May said in a LinkedIn post earlier this year.
"I am proud of the work we've done to navigate complex realities, advance Canada's interests, support Canadian businesses and deliver new priorities under a new government."
Carney in China next week
The announcement comes ahead of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation's (APEC) leaders' summit in China next week, which Carney is expected to attend from Nov. 18 to 19.
There, Carney says he and Chinese President Xi Jinping will discuss several topics including global security and the two countries' economic partnership, which he described as "something that is deepening, but has clear guardrails in terms of the areas in which we're involved."
"The advantage of these meetings is that you can raise issues directly and frankly and I will be raising the importance with which we attach to the non-interference in our affairs by China and other countries," Carney said.
