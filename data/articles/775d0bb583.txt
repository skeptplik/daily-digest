As World of Warcraft: Forever players dived into the game, they quickly discovered a specific weapon enchant that took area of effect spells to a whole other level. Sadly, with a sudden hotfix last night, this particular use case for the enchant has been removed.
The enchantment in question was Revelation - a new addition to the game included as part of the new trade good reputations. What it does is simple: when you fail to deal a critical strike with a spell, the next spell hit is guaranteed to do so. What made this particularly powerful is that the buff also worked with continuous area of effect spells, meaning an early instance of the spell not crit-ing would result in the remaining damage hits all dealing critical damage.
So for example, let's say you're a druid casting the Hurricane spell. This is a channel spell with a total of 10 seconds of cast time. Every second, it deals damage. If the first instance of damage at the one second mark doesn't crit, the remaining nine instances of damage would all crit. This resulted in absurd amounts of damage, and made players of druids, warlocks, and mages very happy.
While strong enchantments are often pricey, this was notably cheap, due to the low-cost of resources used to create it and the low requirements to learn it. As such, people were spending a handful of gold for an early game enchantment that was being actively used all the way into the end game.
It was admittedly a tad too strong, and the effect triggered on AOE spells was almost certainly an unintended effect of the enchantment. Last night, the fun came to an end. Now, the effect is used up as intended in long-casted spells. So going back to the example given, if the first instance of damage on Hurricane doesn't crit, then the second one still will. However, this will use up the effect, and the third instance of damage will revert to regular ol' damage numbers. It also seems as though it Revelation can't trigger more than once during a single channeled spell.
This is the latest in a string of ongoing quickfire bug fixes and game adjustments being made to World of Warcraft: Forever. The devs have been continuously updating the game in an attempt to curb certain gold farms, following the discovery of a particularly lucrative Blackmaw Hold gold exploit. The dev team has expressed some interesting ideas for the game longterm, including a desire to kill best-in-slot gear.
