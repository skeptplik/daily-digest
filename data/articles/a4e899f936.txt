3 bodies found in home of Krakow shooting suspect, Polish PM says, after 6 killed, 16 injured in city square
Defence minister condemns 'act of terror'; interior minister said gunman held 'ultranationalistic' views
Six people were killed and 16 wounded after a gunman opened fire Saturday afternoon in a busy market square in Krakow, authorities said, in what Poland's defence minister called an "act of terror."
Another three people were later found dead inside the home of the suspect and were likely close family members, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said late Saturday. He called the discovery of the bodies of two women and one man "the next acts of this tragedy."
The suspect opened fire with an automatic weapon in the city's Main Market Square, a major tourist destination.
The 25-year-old attacker drove to multiple spots in the square and opened fire at each stop with an automatic weapon, authorities said. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said the man, whose name hasn't been released, held "very radical" and "ultranationalistic" views.
"Everything indicates that he was fascinated by far-right ideologies," Kierwiński said at a news conference.
Four women and two men were killed. At least three of the women were Polish, and one of the dead was a U.K. citizen, authorities said at a news conference. Police were still trying to identify the other two people killed hours after the attack.
Two police officers were among the injured, including one whose injuries were serious, Kierwiński said. Also injured in the attack were five non-Poles, police commander Marek Boroń said.

Authorities said a total of 13 people were hospitalized in serious condition, though that figure may include the gunman, who underwent surgery after the attack.
It's unclear how many of the injured were shot. Two of them were treated and released within hours of the mass shooting.
Polish Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz called the shooting an "act of terror" on the people of Krakow.
In a statement issued Saturday evening, Global Affairs Canada said that it is not aware of any Canadian citizens who are injured, and that "Canada is deeply saddened by the devastating shooting."
Police found several weapons in gunman's car
The perpetrator was shot and wounded during a gunfight with officers a short distance away from the initial violence, police said. The death toll earlier stood at five, but the Malopolska regional government updated it to six in a post on X.
Katarzyna Cisło, a spokesperson for the Krakow police, said the suspect was wearing military-style fatigues and a bulletproof vest and used a military-type long gun in the attack. Police said they found many other weapons, as well as ammunition, in his car. He kept firing at police even after being shot himself, she said.
The attacker engaged in a "wild ride" around the square, Cisło said, which is why people were wounded at several locations. Videos posted on social media by witnesses show people running away from the square to take cover on small streets nearby.
Cisło added that the gunman acted alone and that the public is safe.
Krakow mayor Łukasz Gibała said the city will be in official mourning until Wednesday. He vowed that Krakow would remain open to visitors despite the tragedy.
"The square will belong to us again," Gibała said in a video posted to X.
Emergency personnel speak to a person at the site of the shooting near Krakow's Main Market Square on Saturday. (Jakub Porzycki/Reuters)
It was not immediately clear whether the firearm used in Saturday's attack, or the guns found in the suspect's car, were legal. About 411,000 people hold gun permits in Poland, which has a population of 37 million, and interest in gun ownership has been rising.
Mass shootings are uncommon in Poland. However, in mid-October three attacks at schools in the central province of Mazovia took place within four days, leaving one person dead and many wounded, including children.
Bodies found at gunman's house
Tusk, the Polish prime minister, said that the bodies of two women and one man had been found at the shooter's home in Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski, just under 200 kilometres north of Krakow.
"There are strong indications that they are members of his immediate family," Tusk told a crisis meeting.
He said a drone had been used to look inside the gunman's home due to potential security threats.
"We know that a significant amount of weaponry may be inside the house," he said.
Local media in Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski reported that residents had been evacuated from the building where the gunman lived and that counterterrorism officers had entered the building.
Speaking along with the prime minister, Tomasz Siemoniak, the minister co-ordinating special services, said Poland's Internal Security Agency (ABW) and Military Counterintelligence Service (SKW) are supporting the investigation.
Police officers stand guard at the site of the deadly shooting. (Jakub Porzycki/Reuters)
Images from the scene of the shooting in Krakow showed police officers and ambulances covering the area, and a Polish Medical Air Rescue helicopter also landed in the square.
A tram driver reported at about 3 p.m. local time that his vehicle was shot at from a man in a moving car while passing nearby the main square, the Krakow municipal transport company said. The window to the tram driver's cabin was shattered.
An estimated 15 million tourists a year visit Krakow, the majority passing through the Main Market Square. The country's second-largest city, Krakow is about 250 kilometres south of Warsaw, the capital.
