1866: The breakthrough that powered the future
The year 1866 marked a decisive turning point, not only in the broader arc of industrial transformation but specifically in the realm of electric motion. At a time when Europe was entering a new phase of industrial transformation, Werner von Siemens pioneered the dynamo-electric principle – a breakthrough that enabled electric machines to generate their own magnetic field through self-excitation. This innovation transformed electric machines from laboratory curiosities into practical power sources, enabling the widespread electrification of industry and infrastructure that defined the late 19th century.
This electric motor from Innomotics was installed in the early 1900s at the historic Tsingtao Brewery in China and supported production processes for more than 90 years, operating continuously under industrial conditions.Innomotics
The principle itself is elegantly simple: an iron core generates a stable magnetic field; a rotating coil is placed within this field; movement in the magnetic field induces an electric current; part of this current strengthens the magnetic field; and electrical energy is continuously converted into motion. This self-reinforcing cycle made efficient and reliable electric motion possible – and it remains the foundation of modern motors today.
One principle. Millions of motors.
More than 160 years later, the same fundamental idea continues to power industrial applications around the world. Millions of motors operate reliably under demanding conditions, keeping production running across generations. The dynamo-electric principle made it possible for electric machines to convert electrical energy into motion (and motion back into electrical energy), creating the technological foundation for modern electric motors and drive systems.

Award-winning innovations
Continuity, however, does not mean stagnation. Sixteen decades after the original breakthrough, innovations continue to emerge that build upon this proven foundation. Two recent developments – the Active Vibration Control System (AVCS) and oil-lubricated rolling bearings (OLRB) – carry this spirit of innovation and ingenuity forward, a commitment that has been recognized with the German Innovation Award.

AVCS helps optimize high-voltage motor performance by actively decoupling motors from their foundations to reduce vibration issues. OLRB helps prevent rolling bearing failures from the start with continuous lubrication, supporting higher availability, less downtime, and long-term cost savings. These innovations demonstrate how the basic principle of 1866 can be enhanced through modern engineering to meet contemporary demands for reliability and efficiency.
