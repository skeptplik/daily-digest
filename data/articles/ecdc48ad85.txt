Starbucks has tapped advisers to prepare for a potential sale of its Japan business for several billion dollars, according to people familiar with the matter.
The U.S. coffee chain is working with banks including Citigroup and Morgan Stanley and has started sounding out interest from some strategic and financial investors, the people said, asking not to be identified because the information isn’t public. A formal process might start as soon as this year, they said.
Starbucks has about 2,000 coffeehouses in Japan, making it one of its biggest markets. And while Starbucks doesn’t break out standalone revenue for Japan, combined with Canada and the U.K., the three accounted for about 72% of net revenues generated outside the U.S. and China in fiscal 2026.
Deliberations about the business are ongoing and might not result in a sale, the people said.
A Starbucks spokesperson said the company continually assesses the best structure for its customers and creating value for shareholders. The chain has been in Japan more than 30 years and has a strong business in the country, the spokesperson said. Citi and Morgan Stanley declined to comment.
It was reported in June that Starbucks was considering options for the Japanese business and had held preliminary talks with banks.
There are thousands of Starbucks stores in China, but the Seattle-based chain sold a majority stake in its business in the country earlier this year to private equity firm Boyu Capital. Starbucks opened in Beijing in 1999 and spread at a rapid pace, but more recently came under pressure from local rivals such as Luckin Coffee offering cheaper, customized drinks.
Boyu’s familiarity with the local market was part of the reason for its success in beating other bidders for the Chinese business, it was reported at the time. Last month, Starbucks said customers would have to make a purchase to sit in its cafes in China, following a similar move in the U.S.
In Japan, other prominent coffee-shop chains include Doutor, Komeda and Tully’s.
Unlike China, where local competition led to a rethink of Starbucks’ operating model, a stake sale in Japan might help unlock value for the company, according to Bloomberg Intelligence analyst Catherine Lim.
“Bringing in a strategic partner with local market expertise could accelerate growth while freeing up capital for Starbucks’ U.S. turnaround,” Lim said. “The key is finding a partner that adds more than just capital without diluting the brand’s premium positioning.”
Starbucks’ sales in the U.S. rose faster than expected last quarter as store and service upgrades, targeted marketing and revamped menu attracted customers. However, the international business weighed on overall results.
