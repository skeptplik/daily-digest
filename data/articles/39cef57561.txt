The latest iteration of the COP 31 draft cover text has eliminated references to fossil fuel phase-out and net-zero dates but has also deleted references to a firm $120 billion adaptation-finance target that developing countries have been asking for as “legally binding financial obligations” on developed countries. This suggests that the most ambitious demands of both the developed and developing country blocs have been eliminated.
A cover text is an overarching political decision that frames the outcome of a climate conference rather than settling any single agenda item. It is usually finalised last, and like every outcome it needs the consensus of all Parties. The Presidency has said earlier drafts were not meant to reflect consensus.
The COP31 Presidency’s revised draft cover decision, dated November 19, has 80 paragraphs, down from 144 in the November 16 version. The summit officially ends on November 20, though negotiations frequently overshoot into the weekend.
A major concern raised by developing countries, of phasing out unilateral trade measures such as the CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism) by 2027, that was present in the earlier draft has also been eliminated.
However, a widely expected outcome of raising electricity’s share in final energy consumption to 35% by 2035, scaling up the use of carbon capture utilisation technologies and making early warning systems universal by 2027, continue to find mention in the text. A broad commitment to triple adaptation finance also remains.
“The draft cover text weaves the 35% electricity in final energy consumption by 2035, alongside tripling renewable energy capacity and doubling the rate of energy efficiency improvement by 2030,” Ajay Mathur, India’s lead negotiator at Paris COP and former DG, International Solar Alliance, said in a statement. The electricity use in India went up by 13% in 2025 due to the Hormuz crisis alone. It will be an ambitious goal for India to achieve 35% electrification by 2035, but due to the increasing amount of renewable energy in the Indian energy mix, we will be near the 32-34% mark by 2035.”
“We’re down to the wire at COP31, and the latest texts are alarmingly far from ambitious or equitable,” said Rachel Cleetus, Climate Program director at the Union of Concerned Scientists (UCS) in a statement. “Rich nations, most notably the EU nations, are still holding a harsh line against committing to tripling adaptation finance to at least $120 billion annually by 2035. Instead of reaching agreement on urgently needed climate finance, some countries seem willing to push negotiations toward failure.”
