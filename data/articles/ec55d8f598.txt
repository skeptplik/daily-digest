Today’s puzzle is about the hidden mathematical pattern behind a much-loved video game.
Consider a simplified game of Super Mario. He moves from platform to platform. He can either step forward 1 space, to the next platform, or jump forward 2 spaces, to the platform after that.
He starts on the first platform. How many ways are there for Mario to land on the last platform if the level has:
- 3 platforms?
- 4 platforms?
- 5 platforms?
- 10 platforms?
And what has this got to do with Leonardo da Vinci?
The point here is not to do too much calculation. Spot the pattern, and you should be able to easily calculate how many ways to cross any reasonable number of platforms.

Today’s puzzle was suggested by Dr Tom Crawford, who is an Oxford don, a successful maths Youtuber, and a lover of science fiction and fantasy. His new book How Many Pikachus Would it Take to Power a Lightbulb? asks entertaining questions about fantasy worlds and answers them with proper maths. Such as using the Navier-Stokes equations to reveal the aerodymics of Quidditch broomsticks, working out the half-life of the Incredible Hulk and calculating who is richer, Sonic the Hedgehog or Super Mario, based on the gold they have collected.
