Last week’s U.S. midterm congressional elections have highlighted just how strong opposition to data center construction has become — and Japan is unlikely to be immune to a similar public backlash amid legal loopholes and a recent rush to build artificial intelligence infrastructure.
U.S. voters clearly expressed skepticism about energy-guzzling data centers and their impact on electricity prices. Recent polls by energy news platform Heatmap Pro and the Associated Press both showed that more than 70% of respondents opposed building new data centers in their areas, contributing to a “blue tsunami” of Democratic victories.
Tetsuharu Oba, a professor of urban planning at Kyoto University who researches data center issues, says Japan should move quickly to find solutions for growing friction between residents and data center developers.
