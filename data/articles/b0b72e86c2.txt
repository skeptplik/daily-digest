Russia attacks Zelensky's hometown, Ukraine reaffirms wish to join NATO
A Russian ballistic missile attack on President Volodymyr Zelensky’s hometown in central Ukraine on Saturday killed two people and wounded dozens. Hours later, Zelensky reiterated his country's determination to join NATO, following proposals to provide Kyiv with security guarantees similar to those of the military alliance.

A Russian strike on the center of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky’s hometown, Kryvyi Rih, killed two people and wounded 60 others, local officials said Saturday.
Meanwhile, a Russian cargo ship bound for Kaliningrad caught fire in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday after it was hit by drones, Finnish and Russian officials said. It was not clear who struck the ship not far from the Russian border. The Finnish prime minister warned that the war was spilling into the region.
A 7-year-old boy and a 20-year-old woman were killed in the attack Friday night on Kryvyi Rih in central Ukraine, Oleksandr Vilkul, the head of the city administration, wrote on Telegram. Oleksandr Hanzha, head of the regional military administration, said 60 people were wounded, including six children.
The strike caused four fires and damaged about 25 apartment buildings, a hospital, schools and an industrial facility, as well as businesses and vehicles, Vilkul said.
Video footage published by the regional branch of the Ukrainian State Emergency Service appeared to show further strikes while ambulance and firefighting crews were responding at the scene, as fires burned around them.
“First, two ballistic missiles. And then, when the emergency and rescue operation began, three more rockets hit doctors, rescuers and utility workers,” Vilkul wrote on Telegram.
The attack added to uncertainty about the prospects for ongoing talks brokered by Washington. It also came hours after two Russian missiles and a suspected drone came down in Poland, in what its prime minister said was likely a provocation timed to coincide with European military exercises in his country.
In the unprecedented two-week military exercise that began November 9 in southeastern Poland, forces are preparing for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine aimed at helping to stabilize the country and rebuild its military. European countries previously have expressed alarm at what they deem to be Russia’s attempts to destabilize the continent.
NATO will defend every inch of Polish territory, Rutte says
NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte, speaking to reporters in Warsaw on Saturday, called the airspace violation “dangerous” and said all allies stood “in full solidarity with Poland.”
“NATO will defend every inch of Polish territory,” Rutte said. He said the alliance functioned “as it should” on Friday morning, when NATO jets supported Polish F-16s scrambled as Russian objects approached Polish airspace. NATO’s top military commander was assessing the alliance’s defenses and would make adjustments if needed, Rutte said.
The head of NATO forces in Europe, General Alexus Grynkewich, would do everything necessary to identify any threats to defense and deterrence capabilities and would implement appropriate adjustments if needed, Rutte said.
On Monday, a meeting of NATO's North Atlantic Council will be held in Brussels to discuss the violation of Polish airspace by Russian missiles, PAP also reported on Saturday.
"In the near future, we will receive strong support from our NATO allies to bolster the protection of our eastern border," Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz wrote on X on Saturday, commenting on the meeting of the NATO body.
"I've been in contact today with general Grynkewich, to coordinate the details of these measures."
Zelensky reaffirms Ukraine's wish to join NATO
Zelensky on Saturday reiterated his country's determination to join NATO, following proposals to provide Kyiv with security guarantees similar to those of the military alliance.
"Of course Ukraine very much wants to be in NATO," Zelensky told reporters, judging that membership of the alliance was one of the "most obvious security guarantees" for his country.
The issue of Ukraine joining NATO, ruled out by Washington since 2025, resurfaced in recent days during fresh talks to end the war nearly five years after Russia's invasion.
At a meeting with Kyiv's allies in Berlin on Thursday, US envoy Jared Kushner said Washington could be ready to grant Ukraine a security guarantee similar to that provided under Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty.
That clause -- the cornerstone of NATO -- states that an attack on one member is considered an attack on all allies.
Putin 'doesn’t want to stop the war'
At a meeting with UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting in Kyiv, Zelensky said Saturday that Ukraine’s repeated attacks on the Kerch Bridge linking occupied Crimea with the Russian mainland were a “fair response” to scores of Russian attacks on Ukrainian bridges. He said that Russia President Vladimir Putin had "attacked Ukrainian bridges 109 times.”
“Not the number of missiles — the number of attacks,” he said. “This is important, that’s why it’s our absolutely fair response.”
The Ukrainian leader said Putin “doesn’t want to stop the war” and is looking to escalate. He also rejected Putin’s claim that the Kerch bridge is used exclusively by civilian traffic.
“If he can use internet, he can use it and see tanks, armored vehicles, artillery,” the Ukrainian leader said.
Streeting called the airspace violations in Poland “an outrageous provocation” and urged allies to respond by stepping up support for Ukraine. “The more they try and deter Europe from supporting Ukraine, the more we will step up,” he said.
The drone incursion in Poland stoked tensions between Europe and Moscow just as the U.S. tries to add momentum to talks it is mediating on ending the war that began with Russia’s all-out invasion of Ukraine nearly five years ago.
US President Donald Trump discussed the war with Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping at a summit in China on Thursday, according to his envoy Steve Witkoff. Trump called Putin his “friend” during the meeting and continued to tout their relationship at a White House news conference Friday.
Asked about Trump’s remarks in an interview with CNN, Zelensky called Putin “a killer.”
“For us, he is an enemy. And of course, for us, he is a killer,” Zelensky said Friday. He warned that describing the Russian leader in friendly terms could mean “he doesn’t feel enough pressure.”
Elsewhere in Ukraine, 11 people, including two children, were wounded when a Russian drone attacked a five-story apartment building in the Kharkiv region, Ukraine’s State Emergency Service said Saturday. One person was wounded in a Russian attack on the capital, Kyiv, according to the service.
In Russia, local officials said Saturday that Ukrainian drone attacks wounded three people in the Bryansk and Belgorod border regions.
