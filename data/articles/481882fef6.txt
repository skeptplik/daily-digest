Nagoya – Animated movies are big business in Japan. Films like “Demon Slayer” and “Chiikawa” have helped bring audiences back to pre-COVID levels while cinemas in other countries are struggling to do the same. But aside from the occasional Disney-Pixar hit, Japanese viewers are largely hooked on domestic product.
Introducing those audiences to independent animation from outside Japan is the remit of the Aichi Nagoya International Animation Film Festival (ANIAFF), which ran from Nov. 13 to 18.
The festival’s top prize, the Gold Shachi, was awarded to Chinese artist Sun Xun’s “Magic Atlas,” which was created over a period of 10 years using traditional artistic techniques like woodblock printing. The film won the Special Jury Prize at the Tokyo International Film Festival earlier this month.
