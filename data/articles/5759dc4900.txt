Why Indonesia’s death penalty lives on, even as executions dwindle
A report on the high number of convicts on death row in Indonesia has prompted legal experts to urge judges to focus on rehabilitation
A new report on the persistent use of capital punishment in Indonesia’s courts has raised questions about the rulings despite an unofficial moratorium on executions.
There were 639 people on death row in Indonesia as of April, an increase from 596 as of August 2025, according to a report by the Indonesian Commission for the Disappeared and Victims of Violence (KontraS) published in October.
