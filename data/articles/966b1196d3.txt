Norwegian low-cost carrier Norse Atlantic, hit by surging jet fuel prices due to the war against Iran, said on Thursday that it was teetering on the verge of bankruptcy as it cancelled all flights for the day.
Founded in 2021 and specialising in long-distance flights, Norse had already warned on Wednesday that its financial situation was "critical", sending Norse shares plunging 85%.
"If a short-term financing solution is not secured urgently, it is the board's intention to file for bankruptcy," the company said in a filing to the Oslo stock exchange.
"There can be no assurance that a viable solution will be found," it added.
All of Thursday's flights have been cancelled "pending clarification as to whether a viable financing solution can be established".
Norse said it was "in discussion with investors regarding a possible takeover that would include a financing solution", without providing any details about their identities.
Its shares fell further in early trading on Thursday before rebounding into positive territory, but at 0.04 kroner each, the airline now has a market value of just $9.7 million.
The airline's problems are not new but have worsened following the sharp increase in jet fuel prices resulting from the war in the Middle East.
Spanish airline Plus Ultra announced on Tuesday that it was ceasing operations, and Latvian carrier Air Baltic filed for US Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in September.
