Demand for penetration testing keeps rising, be it due to DORA threat-led testing, NIS2, or customer security questionnaires. However, supply often cannot keep up.
Experienced testers are scarce, take months to hire and train, and every one of them spends part of each engagement on work that is not testing. For a company that sells hours, that unbillable part is the margin.
This article shows how to measure it, which parts AI can take over, and how to do that without sending client vulnerabilities to someone else's servers.
Step 1: Measure where an engagement's hours go
Most companies track billable testing time and lack a number for the rest. If you tag the last ten engagements by phase, the picture usually changes:
Two ratios matter: documentation plus review as a share of testing time, and calendar days from last testing day to report delivery. The first sums up to the hidden cost, but only the second is what clients feel.
Step 2: Decide what AI should and should not do
The tasks that suit a language model are the ones where the tester already knows the answer and needs it written down:
- Drafting finding text from tester notes, using your own vulnerability library as the template, so tone and structure stay yours.
- Normalizing and de-duplicating scanner output from Burp, Nessus, Nuclei and similar tools into one set of draft findings.
- Consistency review: missing remediation, severity that does not match the CVSS vector, the same issue worded three ways.
- Executive summary drafts, built from the validated findings and then edited.
It will be helpful to decide beforehand in what capacity to keep humans on severity decisions, exploit validation and anything the client will rely on as a statement of fact.
A model that confidently describes and is set to run an exploit chain that nobody verified can be is worse than no help.

Step 3: A reference workflow
- Import scanner output and tester notes into one project as draft findings.
- Pull in matching entries from your reusable vulnerability library.
- Let the model rewrite each draft in your house style. The tester edits and approves.
- Attach evidence to the finding at the time of capture.
- Senior reviewer works through comments on the report itself, not email.
- Export the branded report (DOCX or PDF) or deliver it through a client portal.
- Track remediation and retest status on the same findings.
If an AI agent is part of the process, give it a defined interface rather than a free hand. A typed tool interface (for example MCP) lets an agent create findings or fetch a project while permissions, audit and the data boundary stay in the platform.
Three numbers can be tracked:
- Documentation-plus-review hours as a percentage of testing hours.
- Days from last testing day to delivery.
- Rework rounds per report.
If the first two fall and the third does not rise, the change is working.
