UnitedHealth Group has sued the federal government over its lowered Medicare Advantage star ratings for next year, further plunging the program’s ratings system into disarray.
UnitedHealth marks at least the 15th Medicare Advantage plan to file a lawsuit against the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services for how the agency calculated 2027 star ratings. UnitedHealth is also the largest Medicare Advantage insurer, covering more than 9 million people. Medicare’s annual enrollment period ends Dec. 7.
The government rates Medicare Advantage plans on a scale from one to five stars, based on health screens, customer satisfaction, and other measures that get mixed into a complicated formula. Those with at least four stars earn bonus money. The insurers argue in their lawsuits that the government’s measurement is inconsistent and is inappropriately dropping their scores below the bonus thresholds.
