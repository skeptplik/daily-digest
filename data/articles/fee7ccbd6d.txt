Private BitTorrent trackers emerged soon after BitTorrent itself was created 25 years ago. This was at the height of the file-sharing wars, when Hollywood and the major record labels were taking one peer-to-peer network operator after another to court. Entertainment industry lawsuits resulted in the shutdown of Napster in 2001, Audiogalaxy in 2002, and Kazaa in 2006.
Those closures drove millions of file sharers to embrace BitTorrent, which in turn put torrent sites into the crosshairs of rightsholders and their lawyers. Suprnova, a public torrent site frequented by millions of users, shut down amid legal pressure at the end of 2004. And in 2006, authorities raided The Pirate Bay.
Some torrent site admins responded to the increasing legal pressure by simply pulling up a drawbridge to their piracy communities. Instead of letting just anyone download torrents, they required users to register, and in some cases only admitted new users that had gotten an invite from a trusted community member.
Nobody knows exactly how many private trackers are currently up and running, or how many people use them for their pirated media fix. But while BitTorrent piracy as a whole has declined since the emergence of Netflix, Spotify, and other paid streaming services, private trackers are still going strong.
