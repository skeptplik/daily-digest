Competitive handbag throwing and high-heel skipping were once acts of political defiance in Perth.
Both disciplines featured in what was originally known as the city's 'Gay Olympics', which ran annually from 1979 to 1999 to raise money for gay and lesbian charities.
They event also pushed for law reform at a time when consensual homosexual sex between men was still illegal in Western Australia; the decriminalisation of which only began in 1990.
Volunteers at the West Pride Archives have been sifting the event's history ahead of Perth hosting the 2030 International Gay Games.
Jo Darbyshire, co-convenor of West Pride Archives said Perth's original games had a strong political edge.
"Being visible out in the community and not being shamed was a huge element to (them)," she said.
"We were still fighting for gay law reform, for gay men in particular to be able to have sex with other men without being harassed by the police or charged or considered dirty and deviant."
The games galvanised the community, but fundraising was also a key part with money going towards things including the telephone bill for the Gay Lesbian Counselling Service.
"That sort of power over helping our own communities enabled us to have the courage to really push for gay law reform and equality in the law," she said.
"We refused to just roll over and die."
Tim Grant photographed the event in the 1980s, and said it was a unique chance to document his community, as it was forbidden to take photos inside bars and clubs at the time.
"It was safe and one the few large-scale spectaculars that my local community could ever have at the time," he said.
"I felt I had no restraints on what I could photograph on the day, as I was a known face and just a part of the scenery."
International Gay Games
Ray Currell, 78, said the local event was "very important" for the community.
"It was great, we had high-heel skip, the handbag throw and then there were some serious events," he said,
"[The games] meant freedom. I've always been gay, I've never hidden the fact."
Currell also helped organise the first Perth team of around 30 members to participate in the New York Gay Games in 1994, which coincided with the 25th anniversary of the Stonewall Riots.
The Gay Games do not require any qualifications for participants.
"As long as you could afford to get there and compete, they let you," Currell said.
Held every four years, Currell competed in five Gay Games and said he would still compete if his body allowed him.
Perth to hold 2030 Gay Games
Perth will host the international Gay Games for the first time in 2030, which is expected to attract thousands of people.
At the 2026 Valencia Gay Games, more than 50 Western Australia participants formed the Australian contingent.
It is expected to be far larger when it's Perth's turn to host.
Sophie Herbert, volunteer at West Pride Archives, said it was "heartwarming and inspiring" to discover the light side of Perth's LGBTQIA+ history.
"They were very brave in the way that it was just people existing and going out and having fun — but just by doing that was an act of protest in itself," she said.
"Although there was so much awful stuff happening at that time, people were resisting in their own way and that's really inspiring."
She said she hopes the Gay Games sparks the local event to be resurrected and encouraged people to donate their photos and medals to the West Pride Archives ahead of the 2030 Games.
