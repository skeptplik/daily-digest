Sales of filling foods such as porridge oats that mimic the effect of taking weight-loss medication are booming on the back of social media hype billing them as “nature’s Ozempic” or “oatzempic”.
Sales of Waitrose Scottish Oats are up 124% this year, according to the retailer’s annual food and drink report, which dissects the biggest trends sweeping the grocery aisles. A poll conducted for the report revealed that 64% of shoppers are “actively choosing to eat foods like oats that keep us feeling fuller for longer”.
“GLP-1s are reshaping the entire food and drink landscape, even for people who aren’t on the medication,” said Dr Joanne Lunn, Waitrose’s head of health and nutrition. “As appetite patterns change, customers’ baskets are shifting towards smaller portions, nutrient density and high-satiety (feeling full) foods.”
There are now more homes with GLP-1 users than contain vegetarians and pescatarians combined in Great Britain, according to a recent survey by Worldpanel by Numerator. About 2.6 million adults were using weight-loss medication in August, a rise of about 700,000 people since February, it said.
But those taking the medication have a big influence on what is bought in the supermarket as nearly eight in 10 GLP-1 users are also the main grocery shopper for their household, according to the report.
Just over 4,000 customers were polled for the report. Of the respondents who were taking weight-loss medication, 88% said they were shopping differently as a result. More than half were eating more protein while a third were drinking less alcohol. One in five had started taking more supplements while a similar number ate more fish.
The terms “nature’s Ozempic” and “Oatzempic” refer to a wave of social media trends promoting everyday alternatives to prescription weight-loss jabs. Foods on this list include pulses, seeds, nuts, and tinned fish, the retailer said, but are also good options for those taking the appetite-suppressing medications and who want to eat a balanced diet.
As part of this, Waitrose says it is seeing “fibre-maths” enter everyday home cooking, with people using salad toppers, seeds, and cereal stir-ins to stack extra nutrients on to ordinary meals.
Porridge is the unlikely poster child for the trend, with the budget-friendly cereal billed as a “standout wellness staple” in the report. Recipe creators are experimenting with everything from miso versions to sour cherry and pistachio butter toppings.
“We’re seeing a growing shift towards savoury porridge,” said Lunn, who suggests swapping sweet toppings for chilli oil, bone broth and roasted veg is the “ultimate hack for a low-sugar, high-satiety boost”.
The report also highlights the food trends that will shape what appears on supermarket shelves in the future. You may already have heard about switter (sweet-bitter) and fricy (spicy fruit), but the flavour mashup of 2027 is set to be “smugar” which is sugar with a burnt makeover.
Waitrose says you can expect to see this smoky-sweet portmanteau flavour turn up in bakeries, barbecue ranges and as bitter-caramel finishes on desserts.
