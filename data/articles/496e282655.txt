U.S. flights no longer grounded as FAA lifts order that caused thousands of delays and cancellations
The Latest
- The U.S. Federal Aviation Administration has lifted a nationwide ground stop order that brought flights across the United States to a standstill.
- Thousands of flights were cancelled or delayed after the country's aviation agency ordered planes to be grounded due to an air traffic control system issue.
- The grounding of flights caused chaos at U.S. airports and confusion and uncertainty in other countries. Air Canada said it cancelled flights to and from the United States for "the remainder of the evening."
- The ripple effect of the FAA's order, which was in effect for more than four hours, will likely last many days as airlines work through the backlog of delays.
Updates
- November 14 BREAKING: Ground stop order lifted Mike Crawley The FAA has lifted its extraordinary order that grounded thousands of flights across the U.S. "At 8:15 p.m. tonight we've opened up the airspace," U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy told a briefing at the White House. Duffy said it will take some time before air travel returns to normal schedules.
- Mullin says no connection with Microsoft outage Mike Crawley Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin kicked off the briefing by denying the air traffic shutdown had anything to do with the Microsoft outage of the past two days. "I know a lot of people are making that jump," Mullin said. "What we've seen, there is actually no cyberattack."
- Why some U.S. planes are taking off Mike Crawley Video feeds from a few U.S. airports are showing the occasional plane taking off despite the FAA's nationwide ground-stop order. You might be wondering why. These are planes departing coastal airports with flight routes taking them over the Atlantic and Pacific oceans. Such flights don't fall under the scope of the 22 regional air traffic control centres that have been hit by the communications outage that triggered the ground stop. These centres manage flights at higher altitudes as they cross the continental U.S. Flights have been allowed to take off for European destinations from such airports as Washington's Dulles International, and for Pacific Rim destinations from San Francisco and other West Coast airports.
- Air Canada cancels Friday flights to and from U.S. Geoff Nixon Air Canada, citing the ongoing FAA ground stop, says it's cancelling flights both to and from the United States for "the remainder of the evening." In a Friday evening post on X, the airline is also advising that "flights between Canada, the Caribbean and South America that transit U.S. airspace may also be impacted."
- Disappointment at U.S. departures in Vancouver Troy Charles I'm here at Vancouver International Airport as passengers in the U.S. departures wing struggle to sort out travel plans. The big board here shows 22 flights bound for the United States, as of 5:45 p.m. PT, eight of those flights are cancelled and seven are delayed. I spoke with a U-15 B.C. Lacrosse team that had an afternoon flight set for California to play in a Lacrosse tournament. Right before boarding the call came down that their flight was cancelled. The coach of the team says they are out of options and have had to pull out of the tournament. Another Vancouver resident had boarded a plane bound for Las Vegas for his birthday weekend when the captain came over the intercom informing passengers of the nationwide ground stop in the United States. Plenty of long faces and frustrated passengers here as they wait to find out if and when they may reach their destination.
- Update coming from the White House Mike Crawley Two members of U.S. President Donald Trump's cabinet are expected in the White House press room at any moment. The media have been told to gather for a briefing from Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin.
- Nearly 5,000 flights cancelled: FlightAware Mike Crawley The number of flights cancelled across the U.S. has hit 4,777, according to the FlightAware tracking website. There are also more than 9,800 delays reported.
- The ripple effects are only just beginning Mike Crawley It's only been around 3½ hours since the nationwide ground stop was issued and more than 3,000 flights have already been cancelled. With so many aircraft grounded, some U.S. airports are quickly headed toward a saturation point where they're running out of gates for newly arrived planes. Empty planes can be moved to parking spots on the tarmac away from the terminals, but it still poses a logistical puzzle that can only add to delays. Whenever the ground-stop order ends, airlines will face big challenges in resuming their regular schedules. That's because thousands of planes and airline crews will be out of place, along with hundreds of thousands of passengers in need of flights. Airline operations teams will have the complex task of sorting out how to get those planes and their passengers to their next destinations, while abiding by rules of pilots getting adequate rest. It means it could be days before air travel around and into the U.S. returns to any semblance of normal.
- Gander, N.L., on standby to help stranded passengers as it has before Rhianna Schmunk The airport in Gander, N.L., is one of the Canadian airports on standby for American flights looking for a place to land, just as it was more than 25 years ago. Gander and other nearby towns in Newfoundland and Labrador helped at least 6,000 stranded travellers on 38 commercial flights diverted to Canada after the terrorist attacks on 9/11. With their town's population having doubled, Newfoundlanders stepped up and offered clothing, food and their homes for two days before flights resumed.
- A waiting game for Mexico-bound Canadian tourist Geoff Nixon Alison Scott is sitting on a taxiway at an airport in Mexico, waiting, along with fellow passengers, to get to where they want to go. Scott, her husband, and a friend travelling with them, touched down at the airport in Cabo on Friday afternoon. But all the passengers on board have been unable to disembark so far. "Right after we landed, we got onto the taxiway and we stopped," Scott, a resident of Sidney, B.C., told CBC News. The captain then told passengers they were in "a holding pattern," stemming from the problems in the U.S., where planes are not departing as a result of the ground stop. That situation has left planes unable to depart Mexico, and accompanying delays. "There's five planes in front of us [on the taxiway]," Scott said.
