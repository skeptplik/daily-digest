'I'm not a hero,' says Arizona man who found lost toddler alone in the desert
Police, dogs and drones scoured area for 2-year-old to no avail. Then Jay Stewart found her in a bush
Jay Stewart has a lot of precious memories, but he says nothing compares to the moment he found two-year-old Vella Campillo alone in the cold, dark Arizona desert, wearing nothing but a diaper.
Police are hailing Stewart as a hero after he found the missing toddler who wandered away from her backyard last week, sparking a massive search-and-rescue operation in the rural community of Eloy, Ariz.
"I am so grateful to have experienced that life event, because there's no other feeling like it in the world," Stewart told As It Happens host Nil Köksal.  
"That's No. 1 in my life, the most emotional moment I've ever had."
Vella was unharmed and has been safely reunited with her parents, police said.
Vella was reported missing around 6 p.m., local time, on Nov. 17, according to the Pinal County Sheriff's Office.
Her parents, Joseph Campillo and Deanna Fritz, told Fox News affiliate KSAZ that she'd been playing in the backyard with her siblings under the care of another family member when she wandered off.
Officials from several police and fire departments spent hours scouring the area along with dozens of volunteers, two helicopters, K-9 units and heat-detecting drones, all to no avail.
Then, just after midnight, Stewart called Vella's name and saw her pop out of a tiny patch of plants in the desert.
"I spun around, and she jumped back down into this little bush … the size of, like, four dinner plates put together," he said. "She almost looked like she was swimming. Her little arms were moving around like she was treading water."
Pitch black and freezing cold
Stewart and his wife had been looking for Vella ever since the emergency alert hit their phones notifying people of a missing child in their area.
They spent two hours searching along the side of the road, he says, before heading home to check the news and bundle up in warmer clothes as the sun went down and the cold set in.
"The desert is kind of like being in a mine. It could be pitch black where you can't see anything. And that's what it was like," he said.
On their second try, they drove into the desert behind Vella's home on their ATV.
"I've lived out there 20 years and I fly over it all the time in our helicopter. So I know it like the back of my hand," he said. "It's my backyard."
Pinal County Sheriff Ross Teeple, right, awarded Stewart a plaque and a badge the day after he found a missing toddler near Eloy, Ariz. (Pinal County Sheriff’s Office)
Stewart says he'd separated from his wife as he wandered about a mile down a ditch calling Vella's name. He heard a yell, then saw her pop up and back down again.
He reached down, plucked her into his arms, unzipped his jacket and tucked her inside.
"She whimpered for about 15 seconds when I picked her up, and then once I snuggled her to me and got her, she seemed pretty happy," he said. "She warmed up pretty quick."
Stewart says she didn't make a peep until he dropped his phone while talking to a 911 dispatcher.
"And she went, 'Phone!' I started laughing. And I'm like, 'Yeah, baby, phone!' " he said. "It chokes me up. I can hear that little voice."

Within 10 minutes, he says, police and other searchers were on the scene.
Body cam footage released by the Pinal County Sheriff's Office shows Stewart holding the toddler on his hip. By that point, she was wrapped in a blanket provided by another searcher and chattering away happily in baby talk.
When the officer took Vella to check her over, the little girl clutched onto Stewart.
"She wouldn't let go of me," he said.
'The very best of our community'
At a news conference on Nov. 18, Sheriff Ross Teeple awarded Stewart a plaque and a badge for his community service.
"Your selflessness and willingness to get involved amplify the very best of our community," Teeple said. "Your actions remind us that every citizen has the power to make a meaningful difference and that sometimes, the greatest acts of service come from simply choosing to help."
But Stewart says he was just one of dozens of volunteers who showed up for Vella, and he happened to get lucky.
"I'm not a hero. I'm a searcher, just like everyone else was," he said.
He called the rescue the most emotional moment of his life, followed by the birth of his children and the day he married his wife.
"The reason I put it ahead of my kids is because I knew where my kids were. They weren't lost. I was holding them," he said. "But to find something that's lost like that, helpless and lost? I can only imagine what the parents were going through."
