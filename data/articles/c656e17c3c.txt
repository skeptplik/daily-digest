This concludes our live coverage of the second Trump administration for the day. Here are the latest developments:
Donald Trump defended his commerce secretary Howard Lutnick today, after he came under fire for allegedly slow-walking export restrictions on Nvidia chips to China. This comes after treasury secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC last week that he has been pushing “for months” to add Megaspeed, the data center company led by an alleged Chinese spy who was reportedly in a relationship with ousted Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, to the US trade blacklist.
Democrat Mary Peltola is leading Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan in the nail-biting race for US Senate in Alaska. With 97% of the ballots counted, Peltola – a former congresswoman – has 49.9% of the vote and only needs more than 50% to win. At present, Sullivan only has 46% of the vote.
A third US aircraft carrier arrived in the Middle East. The USS Theodore Roosevelt has now been deployed to the “area of responsibility”, US Central Command (Centcom) said on Monday. Until April this year, the presence of three warships in the region hadn’t been seen since 2003 during the Iraq war.
Trump blasted the new leadership of CBS News’ 60 Minutes, which was overhauled under the network’s conservative editor-in-chief Bari Weiss. In a social media post today, the president was particularly critical of a report on the more than 800 American service members wounded and 23 killed in the war in Iran that 60 Minutes aired on Sunday.
Trump also said today that he would “not mind” seeing Jensen Huang reinstated as the chief executive officer of Nvidia. The president says the ex-CEO has learned a lesson, and denied that national security has been put at risk.
The president also continued to shift responsibility to the Biden administration for the nationwide ground stop over the weekend. Trump said that he needed more funding to overhaul the outdated telecommunication system responsible for the disruptions.
The Trump administration has warned Elizabeth Warren, the senior Democratic senator expected to chair the powerful Senate banking committee, that she could face “significant legal consequences” if she fails to preserve records related to investigations into Donald Trump. The warning mirrors other letters justice department officials have sent to Democratic lawmakers who have indicated they will investigate the Trump administration when the party becomes the majority in Congress next year.

The hearing concerned a May 2024 ruling that found Trump guilty on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records to obscure a $130,000 payment to adult film star Stormy Daniels.

Donald Trump’s approval rating has held steady at 32% since mid-October, when the president’s rating reached a career low of 31%, according to a new Reuters/Ipsos poll.
The poll also found that Chuck Schumer, the Democrats longtime leader in the Senate, is losing popularity. Of the polls Democratic respondents, 43% viewed Schumer unfavorably compared to 34% who viewed him favorably. In February, 37% of self-identified Democrats viewed Schumer unfavorably compared to 38% who viewed him favorably.
The Nvidia board is not weighing allowing Jensen Huang to return as CEO, Reuters reports, citing three people familiar with the matter.
Although Donald Trump and Elon Musk have thrown their weight behind the ousted tech executive, “The Nvidia board is not considering bringing Jensen back,” one of the people familiar with the matter said.
A federal appeals court heard arguments today in Donald Trump’s hush money case, as the president tries to see the case shifted to federal court where he could have it dismissed on grounds of immunity.
The hearing concerned a May 2024 ruling that found Trump guilty on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records to obscure a $130,000 payment to adult film star Stormy Daniels.
Earlier this month, Trump appealed the ruling in state court. But in an effort to see the charges dismissed his legal team also took the case to federal court. Today’s hearing was before a three-judge panel of the 2nd US Circuit Court of Appeals.
It’s unclear how likely the president is to be successful. Trump asked the 2nd Circuit to step in after a federal judge in August ruled against his bid to see the case transferred to federal court for a third time.
The state department is offering up to $20m in rewards for information leading to the arrest of two Guatemalan gang leaders.
The Trump administration announced that it will pay up to $10m each for information about Axel Bladimir Montejo Saenz, also known as “Mosh”, and Walfre Donaldo Calderón-Calderón, also known as “El Teniente Jr”. The two have been indicted on charges of cocaine smuggling and have allegedly worked with a Mexican cartel that the Trump administration designated a “foreign terrorist organization” last year.

California has sued OpenAI over a July cybersecurity incident where the artificial intelligence system hacked into the company Hugging Face.
In the lawsuit, filed in San Francisco Superior Court today, California attorney general Rob Bonta alleges that OpenAI misled and endandgered the public by knowingly developing advanced AI models without necessary safeguards, and called the AI company a “public nuisance” under state law. The lawsuit asks a judge to order the company to change its business practices.
An OpenAI spokesperson told the Associated Press that the lawsuit appears to single out their company and added, “Since the Hugging Face incident, we’ve undertaken an extensive and transparent effort to understand and address misaligned model activity.”
In the lawsuit, Bonta wrote that “the true extent of the company’s legal violations and the risk of future harm is unknown due to OpenAI‘s lack of transparency and misrepresentations.”
