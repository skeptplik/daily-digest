Consultants and specialist doctors have begun a 24-hour strike as part of an ongoing dispute over pay.
It follows a separate 24-hour strike on Friday in which resident doctors took industrial action as part of the same dispute.
The British Medical Association (BMA) rejected a recommended 3.5% pay rise and wants to restore the value of doctors' pay to 2008 levels.
Health Minister Robbie Butler said he had accepted the recommended rise but could not implement it without sufficient funding for his department.
Niall Corrigan, a consultant psychiatrist in the Belfast Trust, was among those on the picket line outside the Ulster Hospital.
He said they were on strike "to advocate for the health service", which he said had been "under-resourced" in recent years.
He said he sympathised with patients who have appointments during the strike but if they did not take action then there would be "continuing degradation of our health service".
Leanne Davison also works in the Belfast Trust as an associate specialist doctor in ICU.
She said doctors in Northern Ireland were "quite rightly feeling aggrieved that their colleagues across the UK have already had uplift in their pay awards".
"Most public and private sector workers are now back in line with inflation, yet doctors, nurses and hospital staff are not," she said.
She added that her colleagues could not "continue to watch the health service disintegrate around us".
"We can no longer accept the moral injury of trying to deliver the care that we want to deliver in third world conditions, delivering care to patients in corridors," she said.
The strike, which began at 07:00 GMT, has taken the form of Christmas Day cover meaning full emergency cover will remain in place.
The health minister said his door remained open.
"But, to be clear, going beyond the 3.5% as recommended by the independent pay review body would have significant repercussions for nurses, teachers, police officers and indeed the entire public sector workforce," he added.
The Western and South Eastern health trusts have asked patients with appointments on Monday to attend as planned, unless their trust tells them otherwise.
The South Eastern Trust said the urgent care centre at Lagan Valley Hospital would be closed on Monday, and that postponed appointments and procedures would be rescheduled as soon as possible.
Dr David Farren, chair of the BMA's Northern Ireland consultants committee, told Good Morning Ulster that doctors were not being valued in Northern Ireland.
"We have given the trusts a six week notice period, which is six times longer than the legal requirement, so they can plan for that," he said.
"We met with the minster on 1 October, any sort of meaningful engagement could've halted these strikes, after that meeting we heard nothing.
"No doctor wishes to strike, we are incredibly sorry that it has come to this."
He said full emergency cover would remain in place, but "hospital appointments, routine investigations, routine operating lists" would be affected.
Dr Farren said the official advice was that "if you have not been notified, you go ahead to your appointment.
"However don't be surprised if your appointment doesn't happen when you get there," he added.
The health minister said Monday's strike, paired with Friday's would have "real consequences for patients, waiting lists and service delivery, with around 1,694 patients expected to be impacted".
He also warned there would be further consequences for patients "as we head into the busy winter period due to the knock-on effect of the strikes as we reschedule cancelled activity".
