Consultants and specialist doctors have begun a 24-hour strike as part of an ongoing dispute over pay.
It follows a separate 24-hour strike on Friday in which resident doctors took industrial action as part of the same dispute.
The British Medical Association (BMA) rejected a recommended 3.5% pay rise and wants to restore the value of doctors' pay to 2008 levels.
Health Minister Robbie Butler said he had accepted the recommended rise but could not implement it without sufficient funding for his department.
The strike, which will begin on at 07:00 GMT, will take action in the form of Christmas Day cover meaning full emergency cover will remain in place.
Butler said: "My door remains open but, to be clear, going beyond the 3.5% as recommended by the independent pay review body would have significant repercussions for nurses, teachers, police officers and indeed the entire public sector workforce."
Dr David Farren, chair of of the British Medical Association's (BMA) Northern Ireland consultants committee told Good Morning Ulster that doctors are "not being valued in northern Ireland".
"We have given the trusts a six week notice period, which is six times longer than the legal requirement, so they can plan for that.
"No doctor wishes to strike, we are incredibly sorry that it has come to this."
He advised the public that "full emergency cover will remain in place."
What will be affected however will be "hospital appointments, routine investigations, routine operating lists", he said.
Dr Farren added that the official advice is that "if you have not been notified, you go ahead to your appointment.
"However don't be surprised if your appointment doesn't happen when you get there."
He added that the BMA gave the health trusts "a six week notice period, which is six times longer than the legal requirement, so they can plan for that."
"We met with the minster on 1 October, any sort of meaningful engagement could've halted these strikes, after that meeting we heard nothing."
The health minister said Monday's strike, paired with Friday's will have "real consequences for patients, waiting lists and service delivery, with around 1,694 patients expected to be impacted".
He also warned that "there will be further consequences for patients as we head into the busy winter period due to the knock-on effect of the strikes as we reschedule cancelled activity".
