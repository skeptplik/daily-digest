Nvidia and its chief executive, Jensen Huang, are facing a proposed class-action lawsuit that accuses them of misleading shareholders by failing to disclose Mr. Huang’s alleged relationship with Alice Huang, who has been linked to Chinese intelligence.
The complaint, filed in a U.S. federal court in San Jose, California, on November 9, 2026, alleges that Nvidia and Mr. Huang concealed the relationship and the national security concerns surrounding it. Shareholders contend that the alleged omission exposed the company to reputational and business risks.
Who is Jensen Huang?
Sixty-three-year-old Taiwan-born Mr. Huang has led U.S. chipmaker Nvidia for more than three decades, transforming it into what is arguably the world’s most influential technology company. He is now at the centre of a controversy stemming from his alleged relationship with Ms. Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based firm Megaspeed and an alleged Chinese spy.
Mr. Huang was born on February 17, 1963, in Taiwan, the second son of a chemical engineer and a schoolteacher. In 1968, his family moved to Thailand before relocating to the United States amid the Vietnam War, according to Graphics News. He graduated in electrical engineering from Oregon State University and later obtained a master’s degree from Stanford University, according to his Linkedin profile.
Jensen Huang timeline (according to Graphics News)
- February 17, 1963: Jensen Huang is born in Taiwan, the second son of a chemical engineer and a schoolteacher.
- 1968: His family moves to Thailand before relocating to the United States during the Vietnam War.
- 1970s: He spends part of his childhood at a boarding school in Kentucky before moving to Oregon, where he develops an interest in computing.
- 1984: Graduates with a degree in electrical engineering from Oregon State University.
- 1984-1992: Completes a master’s degree at Stanford University.
- 1993: Co-founds Nvidia with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. He becomes chief executive officer as the company enters the computer gaming market with an initial capital of $40,000.
- 1999: Nvidia goes public. The launch of the GeForce 256, promoted as the world’s first GPU, establishes the company as a major force in the technology industry.
- 2006: Under Mr. Huang’s leadership, Nvidia introduces CUDA technology, enabling graphics processors to handle complex computing tasks beyond gaming and helping lay the groundwork for the AI revolution.
- 2022: The launch of ChatGPT triggers a sharp rise in demand for advanced AI processors.
- 2024: The boom in generative AI drives Nvidia’s rapid expansion, with Mr. Huang’s net worth surpassing $100 billion.
Spy scandal timeline
- 2024-2025: Jensen Huang and Alice Huang are reportedly seen together at several industry events in Taipei.
- 2026: U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio sends a memo warning Trump administration officials about the Nvidia chief executive’s relationship with Alice Huang.
- October 29, 2026: The New York Times reports that U.S. authorities investigated Alice Huang over suspicions that she passed intelligence to Beijing and helped circumvent U.S. restrictions on advanced AI chips.
- November 5, 2026: Senior U.S. lawmakers seek a closed-door briefing from Jensen Huang over potential national security concerns.
- November 8, 2026: Mr. Huang admits he was “unfaithful” to his wife. Nvidia announces an independent investigation into the espionage allegations.
- November 9, 2026: Nvidia shares fall by more than 4%. U.S. President Donald Trump says the FBI is investigating the controversy involving Mr. Huang and Alice Huang.
The Jensen-Alice saga
Mr. Huang admitted to being unfaithful to his wife but did not disclose details about the alleged relationship with Ms. Alice Huang. The Nvidia chief executive said he was “deeply sorry” for the affair. “I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did,” he said in a statement posted on Nvidia’s website.
The New York Times reported that Ms. Alice Huang is a Chinese citizen allegedly recruited by Beijing to gather intelligence on Mr. Huang.
Mr. Huang also sent a message to Nvidia employees on November 8, 2026, acknowledging the affair and apologising to both his family and company staff. While expressing regret for his actions, he did not address allegations that Alice Huang was a suspected spy.
The Times reported last month that U.S. intelligence agencies had concluded that Mr. Huang had developed a close relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese citizen and chip buyer, and alleged that she was providing information directly to Beijing.
Nvidia announced a probe into the matter on November 8, 2026. Independent directors have engaged external legal counsel to examine the circumstances surrounding the relationship. Nvidia’s board said it had “no reason to believe” that either national security or Nvidia’s proprietary information had been compromised, and that Mr. Huang would remain in charge while cooperating with the investigation.
