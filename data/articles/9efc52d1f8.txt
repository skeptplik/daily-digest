YouTube is preparing to pay $3 billion to thousands of school districts to settle claims that its “addictive design” harmed kids, according to Bloomberg. Sources tell the publication that YouTube reached the agreement “in principle,” and that the deal would also include new safeguards for teens.
YouTube is reportedly preparing to settle social media addiction claims for $3 billion
The potential settlement would also require YouTube to introduce new safeguards for teens, Bloomberg reports.
The settlement would allow Google to avoid a wave of lawsuits filed by US school districts that accuse YouTube, Meta, Snap, and TikTok of negatively impacting the mental health of teen users. The first “bellwether” trial of these federal multidistrict litigation (MDL) cases was supposed to start in Kentucky in June, but all four companies settled for a total of $27 million before it went to court. The next trial is set for February 2027 before an Oakland, California, judge, with Meta, Snap, and TikTok as defendants.
Google didn’t immediately respond to The Verge’s request for comment.
In August, Meta agreed to pay $17.1 billion over 10 years to settle lawsuits filed by a group of 47 states, along with several districts and territories. It also agreed to implement stricter safeguards for teens, but is pushing TikTok and YouTube to do the same. As part of the agreement, Meta will only shell out the remaining $5.3 billion of its $17.1 billion settlement if YouTube and TikTok roll out similar changes and pay matching amounts. In March, both YouTube and Meta were found negligent in a separate legal battle over social media addiction.
