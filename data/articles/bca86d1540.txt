REC Equine Specialists said So You Are had been euthanised before being collected by a "contracted provider" and taken to a "licensed landfill." The practice has apologised and said the horse should have been "promptly and respectfully buried."
A dead Australian racehorse has been found dumped in an industrial waste facility in Sydney, two weeks after finishing eighth in the prestigious Melbourne Cup.
The body of So You Are was found by a member of the public, who posted a video of the discovery on social media.
"That can't be normal. Does anyone know if that's legal?" Gregg Jowett said in the footage. "I'm disgusted by that."
Trainer Bjorn Baker announced on Monday that the thoroughbred had died following a "severe bout of colic."
"He was rushed to a specialist equine hospital for emergency veterinary treatment, but sadly could not be saved."
Baker posted a later statement saying he was "deeply distressed by the handling of So You Are's remains."
"He was a much-loved horse, and he deserved to be treated with dignity and respect after his death," he said, adding that he understood that the REC Equine Specialists veterinary practice had launched an investigation into the incident.
REC has apologised for the "distress" caused by the footage and said So You Are should have been "promptly and respectfully buried."
The practice said the horse had been euthanised under anaesthesia before being collected by a "contracted provider" and taken to a "licensed landfill."
"What happened was unacceptable and disrespectful to So You Are and to everyone who cared for him. We are deeply sorry," it added.
The company said it had stopped using the provider "effective immediately."
Racing NSW, the regulating body in the state of New South Wales, said that under NSW Department of Primary Industries guidelines, "a licensed landfill is the preferred option for animals who have died via euthanasia due to the chemicals involved in the process," per Australian media.
It added that a process was now underway to "determine what steps can be taken to ensure this cannot happen again."
