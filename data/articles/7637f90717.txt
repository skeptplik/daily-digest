Truck acid leak prompts alarm, school closures in Krefeld
Residents in the western city of Krefeld woke to warning sirens in the early hours after a truck accident nearby caused a nitric acid leak.
About 200 firefighters worked at the scene for most of the morning to contain the leak.
The truck was reportedly carrying 23 metric tons of highly corrosive acid when it crashed and overturned at around 3 a.m. local time (0200 GMT) on Highway 57.
Authorities had warned of an "extreme danger" within a 1-kilometer (0.6-mile) radius of the site and urged residents in that area to stay indoors and keep their windows closed.
That warning was later downgraded.
"Most of the spilled nitric acid has now been contained," emergency responders reported. "Currently, about one metric ton of the hazardous substance remains in a pool on the street."
Authorities said about 10 metric tons of acid likely leaked from the vehicle.
The truck driver was injured and taken to a hospital for treatment.
Four schools and two daycare centers were closed on Friday as a precaution.
Highway 57 was also temporarily closed, and train services between Duisburg and Krefeld were suspended.
Water use by German data centers could double by 2030: study
The expansion of data centers in Germany as a result of the AI boom could massively strain local water resources, according to a study by an environmental research group.
The report by Öko-Institut, commissioned by Greenpeace, found that data centers in Germany currently consume around 13 billion liters (3.4 billion gallons) of water for cooling per year.
It said this demand could more than double to 27 billion liters per year by 2030 as a result of new mega data centers. That is on par with the drinking water needs of a city of around 580,000 people.
Data centers house hundreds of thousands of servers that consume a significant amount of energy and water, depending on the cooling technology.
"The trend toward ever-larger facilities with their enormous water consumption is exacerbating local water stress and triggering conflicts over water use," Greenpeace digital expert Linda Klapdor said.
According to Greenpeace, nearly 30 large data centers with more than 100 megawatts of server capacity are planned in Germany, with an annual water demand of 200 million to 930 million liters, depending on the cooling technology used.
Klapdor called for data centers in water-scarce areas to only go online if they use closed-loop water systems, which can reduce a data center's continuous water consumption to zero.
About one-third of Germany's roughly 1,200 data center operators currently use evaporative cooling systems during the summer months, Greenpeace said, meaning potable water used in these systems escapes into the atmosphere as vapor and is no longer available to the local water cycle.
"People who live near a data center are right to worry about their quality of life, drying-up wells, and rising water and electricity costs," Klapdor said.
She added that operators were only partially meeting obligations to report water consumption, and she called for more transparency, especially concerning the water use of planned mega data centers.
Bundestag backs 5-year prison term for using date-rape drugs
Lawmakers in Germany's lower house of parliament, the Bundestag, have approved a proposal to toughen penalties for the use of date-rape drugs.
The legislation proposes classifying the drugs as "dangerous substances" alongside weapons. It also envisages increasing the minimum jail time for crimes involving the substances, such as rape or robbery, from three to five years.
The changes passed with the votes of the conservative CDU/CSU, the center-left SPD, the Greens, and The Left. The far-right AfD abstained.
The proposed legislation now goes to the upper house, the Bundesrat.
GHB (gamma-hydroxybutyrate) or GBL (gamma-butyrolactone) are often used by perpetrators to incapacitate their victims, who may experience dizziness, loss of consciousness or memory loss. These colorless, odorless substances can be slipped into drinks at clubs or bars, or even administered in the victim's own home.
Sonja Eichwede, deputy chair of the center-left Social Democrats' parliamentary group, told the AFP news agency that such drugs often "put women in mortal danger and violate their sexual autonomy."
If passed, the law "will strengthen the protection of women and ensure that such crimes are finally punished in a manner commensurate with their particular severity," she added.
Four alleged 'Kingdom of Germany' leaders on trial
Four alleged ringleaders of the banned far-right "Kingdom of Germany" (KRD) are expected to appear before the Düsseldorf Higher Regional Court this morning.
The group is part of a conspiracy theorist movement that rejects the legitimacy of the modern German republic.
Authorities banned the KRD in May 2025 after monitoring its activities for more than a decade. Since then, the four defendants, including the group's so-called "King" Peter Fitzek, have been held in pre-trial detention.
They have been charged with membership in a criminal organization and other offenses, including document forgery and tax evasion amounting to millions of euros.
The Federal Prosecutor's Office accuses Fitzek and his co-defendants of seeking to replace the German republic with their own system. They are alleged to have set up "pseudo-state structures" such as a banking and insurance system, as well as a "registration office" that issued fictitious identity documents.
At the time the group was banned, hundreds of police officers carried out raids on 15 properties across Germany. They seized "further evidence of the association's anti-constitutional goals and activities," according to the Interior Ministry.
Authorities said the group had acquired land through donations with the aim of reducing the territory of Germany.
According to the prosecutor's office, they sought to expand their purported territory to the borders of the German Empire of 1871.
The group is estimated to have had around 1,000 followers at the time it was banned.
