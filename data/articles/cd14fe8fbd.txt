EU proposes €45 billion additional support for Ukraine in 2027
The European Commission has proposed a further €45 billion ($49.3 billion) in additional financial support to Ukraine in 2027.
"For 2026, Ukraine's needs are covered. And today we are proposing a further €45 billion to cover two thirds of its needs for 2027," European Commission chief Ursula von der Leyen said.
"Together, with our international partners, we will strengthen Ukraine. For as long as it takes to achieve a just and lasting peace," von der Leyen said.
According to the Commission, the proposed support would have up to €25 billion allocated to military support and up to €20 billion as general budget support in Ukraine.
The EU and member states have provided nearly €238 billion in support to Ukraine since Russia's full-scale invasion began in February 2022.
Germany calls for Russia to be held accountable after chemical weapons probe
Germany's Foreign Office has called for Russia to be held accountable after a Ukrainian investigation concluded that Russia had used CS gas on Ukrainian forces.
"Russia continues its relentless war against Ukraine and commits grave breaches of the Chemical Weapons Convention," the Foreign Office said in a post on X.
"A Ukrainian investigation has found clear evidence of the use of prohibited chemicals. Russia needs to be held accountable," the statement read.
The Ukrainian probe was conducted with the help of the global chemical weapons watchdog, the Organization for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW).
The organization confirmed in 2024 that CS gas, which is banned on the battlefield, was found at several sites in Ukraine.
The new investigation now links the weapons used to the Russian military.
Ukraine focused on an incident from September 2024, in which Russian forces attacked a military post in central Ukraine.
Investigators found a video posted to a pro-Russian Telegram channel, which they say showed a grenade filled with tear gas being dropped from a drone onto Ukrainian forces.
Russia is party to the Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC), having signed it in 1993 and ratified it in 1997.
Use of riot control agents on the battlefield is prohibited under the CWC.

Europe must be part of Ukraine peace talks — Wadephul
Germany's top diplomat is insisting that Europe should be involved in negotiations to end the war between Russia and Ukraine.
"I expect, and have no reason to doubt, that talks on Ukraine's future will be held between Russia and Ukraine with Europe's participation," Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said during a meeting with his Azerbaijani counterpart, Jeyhun Bayramov, in the capital Baku.
Failing that, it would not be possible to achieve a sustainable solution that guarantees "that a new conflict does not emerge after this war, which we hope will end as soon as possible," Wadephul warned.
The comments come ahead of a trilateral meeting between the leaders of the United States, Russia and China on Thursday.
Wadephul did welcome the planned trilateral talks in Shenzhen between US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.
"Every conversation that takes place is a good conversation, I say as a foreign policy expert," he said.

The Kerch Bridge appears heavily damaged after a major Ukrainian strike. Footage released by President Volodymyr Zelenskyy shows drones and missiles hitting the key link between Russia and occupied Crimea.
Reports of two industrial facilities struck in Russia's Krasnodar region
Ukraine has reportedly struck two industrial targets in Russia's Krasnodar region.
Media outlets in Ukraine are reporting that oil refineries have been struck, although this cannot be independently verified.
Telegram channels in the Krasnodar region warned of overnight drone strikes but it was not immediately clear what had been hit and whether damage had been caused.
Ukrainian online publication The Kyiv Independent reported that the Ilsky and Afipsky oil refineries had been hit and cited Telegram channel Exilenova, a pro-Ukrainian account that tracks developments in the ongoing war with the information.
The two refineries are the largest in the region and have previously been targeted by drone strikes.
The reported strikes come a day after Ukraine hit the Kerch bridge, linking occupied Crimea to Russia, putting the road and railway out of action.
The Ukrainian General Staff said an arch and part of the bridge's road section collapsed into the water, severing the link for vehicular traffic, while the railway line was still standing but with a damaged arch.
