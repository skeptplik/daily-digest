9-month-old dies of hypothermia in Gaza as territory heads into winter season
Ahmed Arqiq, 37, says his son Kinan froze to death overnight
Ahmed Arqiq walked through a crowd of mourners, carrying the lifeless body of his nine-month-old baby, Kinan, wrapped in a white blanket. With every laboured step he took, passersby gave him condolences. He quietly whispered prayers over his son’s body.
The prior morning at 4 a.m., the 37-year-old said he rushed his son to the Kuwait Specialty Hospital in southern Gaza's Khan Younis, but it was too late.
“He was wrapped up, [but] his head, his clothes, his pants … all of them were soaked in water,” Arqiq told CBC News freelance videographer Mohamed El Saife, holding up the infant's death certificate as he recounted the night’s events.
Earlier this month, heavy rains pummelled parts of Gaza, causing flooding and damaging many shelters. One storm led to the collapse of a sand embankment, killing five people.
Hundreds of people have been living in makeshift tent cities for years, including the Arqiqs, who were displaced from their home in 2023 when Israel's war in Gaza began.
As heavy rains hit the Gaza Strip in the week leading up to the infant's death, many parts of the enclave flooded, leaving people stuck in metres of water with their belongings drenched. For the Arqiq family, that meant the baby's clothing and blankets were soaked and they were unable to fully dry him.
Kinan, the youngest of three children, eventually died of hypothermia — the first death from the condition this season in Gaza.
“My son died from the cold,” Arqiq said. “His mother wanted to nurse him and she found him dead.”
'He was living in very bad conditions'
Dr. Jamal Al-Hams, medical director at the Kuwaiti Hospital, said the infant was dead on arrival. He said the baby had no other underlying medical conditions.
“The cause of death was due to … to the severe, extremely cold weather he was exposed to in his tent,” he said. “He was living in very bad conditions.”
Temperatures in Gaza tend to fall overnight in the winter, where they can hover around eight to 10 C or even as low as seven degrees.
In a press release published on Tuesday, Shaun Hughes, UN World Food Programme country director for Palestine, said Gaza was facing a “deepening” shelter, sanitation and food security crisis.
A displaced Palestinian man stands in a partially flooded tent camp during a rainy day in Gaza City on Nov. 7. (Dawoud Abu Alkas/Reuters)
“Winter rains flood makeshift shelters, destroy scarce food supplies and leave displaced families struggling to stay warm and fed,” it reads.
A UN report published in February 2026 stated that at least 11 children had reportedly died of hypothermia that winter. It said the situation “underscores the deadly impact of exposure to cold conditions without adequate shelter.”
In January 2025, the UN reported eight newborns and 74 children died of hypothermia in the prior month alone.
The war began on Oct. 7, 2023, after a Hamas-led attack on Israel killed some 1,200 people, by Israeli tallies, with another some 250 taken hostage. Since then, more than 74,000 Palestinians in Gaza have been killed, according to the Gaza Health Ministry.
Aid still needed, orgs say
A year into the fragile ceasefire between Israel and Hamas, the flow of aid has improved, but the demand still outstrips supply, humanitarian groups say.
“When you’re in Gaza, it does not feel like a ceasefire,” Hughes said.
The Israeli military still controls about 70 per cent of the Gaza Strip, he said, making it difficult for Palestinians to grow their own food.
“Fishing in the sea is prohibited and also life-threatening," he said, due to risk of gunfire. "Unemployment is over 80 per cent, so without humanitarian assistance, people have little or no alternate means of survival."
A Palestinian lights a fire outside his tent at a tent camp set up inside a school, in Khan Younis, on Nov. 18. (Ramadan Abed/Reuters)
More than 93,000 tents and 8,600 trucks with winter supplies have entered Gaza as part of the Israeli military's winterization plan for the region, the Co-ordination of Government Activities in the Territories subunit posted on X last week.
But many aid groups say it’s still not enough.
"Without proper shelter, without critical relief items, then hypothermia deaths will occur," Karl Baker, head of the UN International Organization for Migration's Gaza programme, said on Tuesday, adding that children were particularly vulnerable, since they lose body heat more quickly.
He said the supply of aid has been hindered by lengthy Israeli security checks and crossing closures.
"Gaza is turning into one large IDP camp [internally displaced persons]. It ranks as one of the worst I've walked through," he said.

Back at the Kuwaiti Hospital, Arqiq carried his son to the hospital's courtyard, where a funeral prayer was held. The little shrouded body lay on a coat on the ground in front of rows of family, friends and passersby lined up to pray before the body was carried to its grave.
“He was like a flower,” Arqiq said, tears rolling down his cheeks.
