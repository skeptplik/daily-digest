Gaza’s health ministry has issued an epidemic alert after a sharp rise in meningitis infections, with more than 270 new cases reported in October.
Dr Nidal Ghoneim, a senior official at the ministry, said the spread of the disease posed a threat to the lives of hundreds of thousands of people, including children.
Health organisations attribute the rise in infections to the destruction of infrastructure, overcrowded shelters and the contamination of drinking water with sewage – a crisis made worse by winter flooding and restrictions on humanitarian aid. Much of Gaza’s drinking water previously came from desalination plants and three Israeli-controlled pipelines, which were cut off at the start of the war and have only partially reopened.
Ghoneim told a news conference that 272 meningitis infections had been reported in the territory in October, more than eight times the normal monthly rate of 30 to 50 cases. Fifteen Palestinians have died of meningitis since the beginning of this year, he said.
“We hold the [Israeli] occupation fully and directly responsible for the epidemic outbreak due to its prevention of the entry of maintenance spare parts, sterilisation materials and fuel,” the health ministry said.
The UN’s humanitarian agency (Ocha) had previously reported 413 suspected cases of meningitis in Gaza between 27 September and 31 October, including 294 viral and 119 bacterial cases. About 68% were recorded in Gaza City.
Ocha said the increase in cases could be attributed to crowded tents and schools, the deterioration of shelter and sanitation conditions and exposure to unsafe water.
Gaza’s water crisis is severe. Before the war, 80% of the territory’s supply came from an aquifer that is now contaminated by seawater and wastewater, making it unfit for human consumption.
For many, this means a stark choice between drinking an extra cup of water or saving it to wash their hands and prevent disease. Acute diarrhoea is among the leading causes of illness in the territory. About 15,000 cubic metres a day of untreated waste seeps into the groundwater from soak-away pits, particularly in the informal camps in al-Mawasi.
The World Health Organization said in September that microbial contamination in water samples had risen from less than 6% in January to nearly 20% in August.
In addition to disease outbreaks and shortages of drinking water and basic necessities, Palestinians continue to face attacks and ceasefire violations by the Israeli military, which has intensified its strikes in recent days.
Soldiers crossed into Hamas-controlled central Gaza last Wednesday in the first ground operation across the “yellow line” boundary since a fragile ceasefire was agreed in October last year, forcing dozens of displaced Palestinians to flee their tents.
Four people were killed and seven wounded in Gaza over the past 24 hours, according to the health ministry, bringing the death toll since the ceasefire took effect to 1,679.
More than 74,000 Palestinians have been killed and more than 176,000 wounded since the beginning of the war in October 2023, according to Gaza’s health authorities.
