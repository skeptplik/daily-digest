London – Wayne Rooney has revealed that he contemplated suicide even as he flourished on the pitch for Manchester United when the Premier League club was at the height of its powers.
The former England forward, 41, said his mental health was suffering even though he was newly married and a father to his eldest child, Kai.
He married Coleen in 2008 and his first child was born the following year.
Rooney was part of a powerful squad assembled by Alex Ferguson that won multiple Premier League titles and the Champions League.
The striker, who would spend two days at a time drinking at home, told the “Life At The Midpoint with Gabby Logan” podcast that he considered taking his own life when he was at his lowest ebb.
“I can look back and say I was selfish, but I knew I was going through something which I was really struggling to deal with,” he said.
“Then I didn’t want Kai seeing me like that, so that’s why I’d hide away as well.
“I got to a place where you’re contemplating suicide. You just didn’t want to be around anyone. I didn’t want to see people in the streets. I didn’t want to go into shops.
“I was always comfortable on a football pitch, but everything else (I) really struggled with it.”
Rooney, United’s all-time record goalscorer with 253 goals, said the issues he faced occurred at the height of his Red Devils career.
“You don’t want to show any weakness, so you try to mask it,” he added.
“Even though I knew the position I was in, it was difficult to actually admit that I was in that position because I felt like you feel less of a man if you like for doing that.
“I was speaking to a therapist to try and figure out what was up with me because when I look back I was probably at the height of my career, I’ve just had a young son, I’ve just been married. It should be the happiest point in my life and it wasn’t.
“It was probably the lowest point because of the selfish acts I was taking.”
Rooney, who was England’s record goalscorer before Harry Kane overtook him, credits Coleen with helping him turn the tide.
“Coleen helped me massively. She could have walked away, it was that bad, but she stuck by me and helped me get through it,” he said.
After Rooney left United in 2017 he had spells with Everton, DC United and Derby before embarking on a managerial career.
His last job was at Plymouth, which he left in December 2024.
