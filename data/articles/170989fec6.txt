GTA 6 has been on sale for a week, and remained the dominant game and talking point across the games industry – supported, admittedly, by a quiet news week in America due to the Thanksgiving holiday. Beyond its record-breaking day-one revenue haul, it's now possible to start judging its impact on the wider industry, and consider if the anticipation – and in some cases, anxiety – was justified.
GamesIndustry.biz spoke to a variety of analysts, data firms and other industry figures, along with the publishers brave enough to launch against it, to find out if Rockstar's opus was as all-consuming as it appeared.
The competitor impact
The well-documented decision of other publishers to avoid releasing their games against GTA means no head-to-head console comparisons, although the game pulled players away from live-service titles: Call of Duty and Fortnite DAUs fell 46% and 25% respectively across console and Steam, according to Sensor Tower data.
Steam continued more or less unmoved, according to GameDiscoverCo's Simon Carless. "There was no obvious/immediate concurrent player drainage across Steam as a whole when GTA 6 launched," he says, and the firm's data suggests that some titles launched against GTA to reasonable success. "There was nothing with more than ~300,000 Steam wishlists launching during the week. Nonetheless, inexpensive volleyball game Just Volleyball sold >200k units and idler Melvor Idle 2 hit >100k, according to GameDiscoverCo estimates. So: nothing huge, but there's plenty of PC game players who don't have consoles and so aren't playing Grand Theft Auto 6."
Devolver's Volvy's Adventure: Reslimed, which with characteristic Devolver flair launched directly against GTA on the 19th, "exceeded expectations because we never really had any to begin with," says Brand Strategist Robbie Paterson. "The fact that thousands of people showed up to support the game on launch day based on little more than some creative marketing shows there's always an audience for what you're doing, even if it's modest."
"I don't think GTA's release affected our sales, to be honest," says Blazej Zywiczynski of Fairy Mount, who launched Flame, Forest and Flood on the 19th. "We had some fun with the date, a lot of people said that they'll support the game because of that move. Could the sales have been better? Absolutely, but that's unlikely to be because of other titles."
Solo dev Tomas Sala is even more upbeat about launching the multiplayer update to his crafting game Shipshaper, which arrived the same day and delivered "the best release/sale so far," with a sales curve that's steadily improved thereafter despite GTA's arrival. "Whether it could have gone better, nobody can really say, but judging by the buzz around the game, the influx of community and playership, my momentum is up not down."
The pricing impact
That record-breaking revenue haul is driven by the game's much-discussed $80 price point, but chiefly by strong uptake for the $100 Ultimate Edition, which accounted for "most" of the pre-orders, according to industry analyst Joost van Dreunen. That success will be highly, and dangerously, alluring to other publishers looking to juice revenues in an increasingly challenging market.
"Charging higher prices for a title that doesn't earn it is a quick way to bring down a publishing empire"
Joost van Dreunen
"Unable to materially grow their audience, publishers have started increasing prices. With increasingly unaffordable – or worse, unavailable – hardware, people have a bit more to spend on games instead," says van Dreunen. "Top-tier titles can command a premium, as in most other consumer-facing markets. Conversely, charging higher prices for a title that doesn't earn it is a quick way to bring down a publishing empire."
The media impact
GTA 6 delivered handsomely for games media outlets, who were looking to it to drive traffic and advertising revenue after a year of diminishing referrals from Google to all media properties. On launch day the traffic to IGN – owned by GamesIndustry.biz parent IGN Entertainment – spiked by 30% over the average, rising to 51% over the subsequent weekend. The launch coverage broke internal records by generating 100m views, the firm said, and by November 23 it had delivered over 400m views across IGN's platforms.
The official GTA 6 strategy guide, which the site produced in partnership with Rockstar, saw very strong performance. The firm said it saw "4.5x more the traffic than 2026's next biggest guide" over its first four days, with extensive use of the interactive map (the most-favourited thing was basketball hoops; the most-removed favourite was bus stops). Other sites reported strong traffic: PlayStation site Push Square saw a 49% WoW traffic growth, and Eurogamer's editorial director Chris Tapsell said the site's editorial traffic for launch week was up 68% over the previous week, while its GTA 6 review saw the highest traffic of the year "by some margin."
Streaming audiences rose. Twitch mobile app downloads and DAUs were more than 80% up day-over-day, according to Karl Kontus of Sensor Tower. Total Twitch watch time rose 61% compared with the two days before launch, according to Stream Charts, and the platform hit a concurrent peak of 5.1m viewers, its highest since creator-based boxing event La Velada del Año 2025.
That audience was there for GTA: over those first two days it drew more watch time than any game in history did in its entire launch week, Stream Charts said. World of Warcraft and Counter-Strike lost about 31% of their Twitch watch time over the same two-day period; Fortnite watch time on YouTube dropped 60%. Data firm Screen Engine said that GTA 6 drew a record 31.4% of all gaming attention on Twitch, YouTube, and Google search, more than three times the previous record set by Fortnite in 2019 and ten times the figure of a typical major release. Its reach pushed audience share for live-service games down from 57% to 42.4% in the launch week, the first time the firm had seen the figure drop below 50% since it launched in 2019.
The wider market impact
It's likely the game's success will cast a shadow over upcoming releases, when they arrive. "Despite the spectacular initial result, GTA 6 did not launch into a vacuum," says van Dreunen. "Its financial success means that titles elsewhere will sell fewer copies and get less playtime." The game's unicorn status is shared with its marketing campaign, which drove a level of mindshare that no other publisher could hope to match. "What's remarkable is how effective Take-Two has been not just in game development, but also in putting together a global marketing campaign unlike anything we've seen. That's not something I'd expect everyone else to be able to replicate immediately."
The longer view suggests better news. While the console install base remains limited thanks to the elevated prices, the GTA launch nevertheless leaves it larger than before. "The scale and pent-up demand for GTA 6 means that the addressable audience will have expanded in anticipation of launch," says Piers Harding-Rolls of Ampere Analysis. "This bodes well for other publishers and the console platforms as a whole."
