Treasury's FinCEN takes systems offline after cybersecurity incident
The Treasury Department has taken some systems offline after a cyberattack, according to a notice posted Tuesday evening.
In a notice posted at the top of the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) E-Filing System website, the department said its Financial Crimes Enforcement Network — commonly known as FinCEN — is “investigating a cybersecurity incident.”
The incident “resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems,” according to the banner.
“As a proactive measure, FinCEN has taken some systems offline. The BSA E-Filing website remains operational; however, some services may be temporarily unavailable,” the agency said.
“FinCEN is working diligently to return all systems to normal operations as soon as possible.”
The incident was first reported by The Verge. FinCEN said it is extending deadlines for filers affected by the outages. It urged financial institutions to call a hotlin if they need to send suspicious activity reports to law enforcement.
The Treasury Department and FinCEN did not respond to requests for comment.
No hackers have taken credit for the attack, but several Chinese state-backed groups were accused of breaching the Treasury Department multiple times in 2024 and 2025.
Twelve Chinese nationals, including two officers with China’s Ministry of Public Security (MPS), were charged for the 2024 Treasury breach — which caused outrage across the federal government because the hackers accessed computers used by then-Secretary Janet Yellen and then-Deputy Secretary Adewale Adeyemo.
The FinCEN incident capped a whirlwind Tuesday in which the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) and at least two other spy agencies reportedly suffered their own breaches that exposed unclassified information.
The New York Times and NextGov reported that the hackers involved in the CIA breaches allegedly used Artex, a Chinese-made platform that can take any large language model and automate multiple phases of cyber intrusions.
The cyberattacks emerged just days after the FBI said it is investigating its own breach revolving around a critical fugitive database used by U.S. law enforcement agencies.
U.S. federal agencies have been battered by hackers this year. The departments of Defense, Transportation, Justice and Agriculture have all dealt with outages or data breaches caused by cyberattacks. The federal bankruptcy courts were impacted by another cyberattack last month.
