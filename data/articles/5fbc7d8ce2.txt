In the first public statement since his resignation, Nvidia founder Jensen Huang signaled his willingness to return as CEO if the board decides it is in the company’s best interest.
Huang’s statement, posted in full on X, reads as follows:
Over the past week, I’ve been overwhelmed by the love and support of friends, industry colleagues, and people I’ve never met. Thank you to everyone who has shown kindness to my family and me. It means the world to me.
I came to the United States 53 years ago, and becoming an American citizen is one of the greatest gifts and proudest moments of my life. NVIDIA has been my life’s work. Building it into the company it is today alongside my colleagues has been a privilege and an honor. Nowhere but in America could a company like NVIDIA be possible. I love our country, and I love NVIDIA. 
I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life. I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation. 
Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO. Many continue to reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics. NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company. If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO. 
Thank you again to everyone who has shared their good wishes. I am profoundly humbled by your support. To my family: I love you, and I will spend the rest of my life being worthy of yours.
Jensen
Huang resigned after reports of multiple affairs, including with a direct report and another with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, who is suspected of working with Chinese intelligence services. Nvidia’s chip exports have faced intense scrutiny since the revelations, and Huang is scheduled to testify before Congress on December 1.
In Huang’s absence, Nvidia has formed a new executive committee to oversee export control compliance.
Still, many investors and employees associated with Nvidia have called for Huang to return as CEO, at least until an investigation can provide clearer evidence of guilt. An open letter circulated earlier this week attracted the signatures of more than 10,000 current and former Nvidia employees, as well as prominent tech figures like Elon Musk, Amjad Masad, and Travis Kalanick.
“We are concerned that Jensen was removed without meaningful due process,” the letter reads. “If the Board believes his continued absence is necessary, provide a specific, evidence-based explanation of why it serves NVIDIA’s best interests.”
Still, many see such a return as unlikely. On Monday, an unnamed source on Nvidia’s board told The Washington Post there were no plans to reinstate Huang. There’s also significant resistance to the idea in Congress, where Huang still faces significant questions about his role in chip exports to China-linked firms.
Reached by Politico, one senator called Huang’s reinstatement “one of the worst ideas I’ve heard today.”
