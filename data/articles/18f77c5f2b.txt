A bikie and former CFMEU member says he can’t recall why union representatives were pushing hard to have him hired on a construction project.
Bandidos member Joel Leavitt gave evidence to the royal commission into alleged corruption across Victoria’s construction sector on Tuesday.
Gravelly voiced, Leavitt confirmed he had acted as a health and safety representative on building sites for the Construction, Forestry and Maritime Employees Union, but denied he was placed there by the union.
“You can’t get on the site by the CFMEU,” he told the commission.
“You get on a site by getting told to come on site by the builder.”
The currently unemployed Bandidos member and former member of another bikie gang, the Rebels, worked as a labourer on a construction project managed by firm Laing O’Rourke in Cranbourne, southeast of Melbourne, from March to August of 2021.
However, messages between Leavitt and former CFMEU organiser Gerry McQuaid suggested some resistance by the builder to hire Leavitt, who at that time was publicly known to be associated with outlaw motorcycle gangs.
In one message McQuaid wrote, “We’re pushing like fuck but I think they will drag it out for another week or two.”
Counsel assisting Chris Carr SC asked, “What does it mean when the CFMEU is pushing like fuck?”
“Speaking to the builder,” Leavitt replied.
Commissioner Chris Kourakis AC suggested McQuaid may have been pushing for Leavitt to get work on the Laing O’Rourke job for various reasons, including that he had a use for him there.
“That’s their job as organisers – to help get members’ jobs,” Mr Leavitt replied.
After admitting to having a criminal history including riot, affray and assaulting police, Leavitt said he couldn’t think of a reason why the company wouldn’t want him on the site.
“Back then your name was associated with some notoriety,” Carr suggested.
“Maybe yes,” Leavitt replied.
The first two weeks of the commission’s hearings centre on a two-day June 2022 standoff between the CFMEU and construction management on the Gap Road level crossing removal at Sunbury, northwest of Melbourne.
The union forced work to halt on the project during a time highly sensitive round-the-clock work stage during which the rail line was temporarily closed at great cost to the project and disruption to the area.
CFMEU delegates occupied the site under the protection of having issued dozens of safety notices, which construction bosses argued were disingenuous and purely intended to give the union unassailable leverage.
On the second day of the stoppage construction bosses met with union representatives Joe Myles and Paul Tzimas at the union’s headquarters and agreed to demands including that union-aligned labour, traffic management and health and safety representation were installed on site.
