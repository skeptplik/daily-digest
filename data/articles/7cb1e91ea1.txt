Euronews' Europe Tonight welcomes Theresa Fallon, founder and director of the Centre for Russia Europe Asia Studies, to discuss the APEC summit with presenter Mared Gwyn-Jones, including comparisons with the Yalta Conference and the possibility of leaders redrawing global spheres of influence.
Historic talks between the leaders of China, the US and Russia concluded earlier today without a major breakthrough. China has published what it describes as a “trilateral peace declaration”, in which the three countries commit to reforming the UN and increasing the production and supply of oil and gas.
US President Donald Trump also praised his “great relationship” with Russian President Vladimir Putin after both leaders held their first face-to-face talks since they met in Alaska last year.
On his way back from China, Trump called into a separate meeting of Ukraine’s European allies in Berlin, where he questioned Europe’s decision to continue funding Ukraine. However, the US President has assured that he will not make decisions without consulting Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.
Euronews' evening show Europe Tonight welcomed Theresa Fallon, founder and director of the Centre for Russia Europe Asia Studies, to discuss the topic in depth alongside our presenter Mared Gwyn-Jones. Among the questions raised were whether the Yalta Conference – where the Soviets did walk away with a good deal – is a fair comparison to the recent talks, and whether these leaders could redraw spheres of influence.
