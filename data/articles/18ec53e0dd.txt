'Despicable betrayal': Democrats stunned after Michigan congresswoman endorses Republican for Senate
Haley Stevens backs Mike Rogers after she lost party primary to Abdul El-Sayed
Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed's former opponent in the U.S. Senate primary, Haley Stevens, stunned party officials Sunday by endorsing Republican Mike Rogers after Democratic tensions boiled over days before the election that could determine the balance of power on Capitol Hill.
Stevens endorsed Rogers after attending a Detroit church service with him. The remarkable show of support for the President Donald Trump-backed candidate in the tightly contested race comes after she was uninvited to a joint event the same day with El-Sayed, who defeated the congresswoman by a slim margin in the August primary.
"Our Democratic nominee for Senate said I was no longer welcome in his campaign and that I should stay away," Stevens said in a video announcing the endorsement, which led her top aide to quit. "And he's right. And that's why I am endorsing Mike Rogers for the United States Senate today."
El-Sayed dismissed the endorsement when asked about it by reporters later Sunday, saying he's focused on issues voters care about, such as the high cost of living.
"At the end of the day I actually don't think about Haley Stevens," he said, adding later: "None of this matters."
People in Grand Rapids, Mich., watch a televised debate between El-Sayed and Stevens on July 7. (Kristen Norman/The Associated Press)
Tensions between the two Democrats have festered since the primary. Stevens is a staunch supporter of Israel, while El-Sayed is a fierce critic of the U.S. ally. He has also been criticized by some in the community for past remarks regarding a Michigan synagogue attack and for appearances with controversial streamer Hasan Piker.
El-Sayed, a former Michigan public health official, is in a tight race with Rogers, an ex-congressman, for the seat held by Democrat Gary Peters, who is retiring.
Keeping Michigan in Democratic hands is vital to the party's effort to win a majority for the final two years of Trump's second term.
Democrats had hoped Sunday would offer a show of unity as they worked to boost turnout in the Detroit area, where concerns have lingered about enthusiasm among Black and Jewish voters in key Democratic strongholds.
Instead, Stevens swayed to gospel music alongside Rogers in the pews at Perfecting Church, while El-Sayed was a few miles away at a different house of worship campaigning alongside prominent Democrats, including South Carolina Rep. Jim Clyburn. The Associated Press attended both services as the dramatic split-screen unfolded.
'Despicable betrayal'
Plans for Stevens to join El-Sayed and Clyburn unravelled after a dispute over Israel and an outside group's campaign advertisement escalated into a public exchange of criticism between the rivals.
After the church service north of Detroit, where Stevens and fiancé Jason Turkish worshipped with Rogers and his wife, Kristi, the congresswoman told reporters, "I'm not turning my back on Democrats. It is this one vote."
Rogers, standing beside her, said he was "honoured" by her support.
Their appearance stunned Democrats, coming days after Stevens publicly reaffirmed she would vote for her party's nominee.
The state party chair, Curtis Hertel, said Stevens' endorsement "shows that she has abandoned everything she claims to care about."
"We reject the attempts by Stevens and bad-faith actors to divide our party and undermine our momentum," Hertel wrote, and won't let such 11th-hour "distractions" deter the campaign.
Hertel later wrote that Stevens "is not a current member of the Michigan Democratic Party, nor will she be in the future."
Democratic Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, who supported Stevens in the primary, denounced her endorsement of Rogers.
"This is a despicable betrayal from Rep. Stevens," Schumer wrote in a social media post.
Persona non grata
Among the Democrats who joined El-Sayed at church were U.S. Rep. Debbie Dingell of Michigan, U.S. Sen. Angela Alsobrooks of Maryland and state Sen. Mallory McMorrow, who dropped out of the Senate primary before voting began.
Dingell, a senior Democratic figure in the state who helped organize the planned joint appearance in hopes of bringing together Democrats in the final days before Tuesday's vote, said she asked Stevens not to attend and that Stevens told her she would abide by that.
"I'm the one who pulled it because it had become way too distracting," Dingell said in a telephone interview before Stevens appeared with Rogers.
"This is not how I was going to spend my weekend before the election, to be perfectly frank."
A voter arrives to cast their ballot during early voting for the midterm elections in Pontiac, Mich., on Saturday. (Brian Snyder/Reuters)
Dingell and other Democrats insist the party is united behind El-Sayed. Early voting has been underway since Oct. 24, before the latest tensions resurfaced, complicating efforts to deliver a unified closing message in crucial areas such as Detroit, where turnout could prove decisive.
As of Friday, more than 1.8 million people had already voted in Michigan. A total of 4.5 million ballots were cast in the 2022 midterm election, a record for a nonpresidential year.
Stevens defeated El-Sayed in Wayne County, which includes Detroit, and neighbouring Oakland County, in the primary, drawing support from Black and Jewish voters who are important parts of the Democratic coalition.
Bitter party primary
The Democratic primary was among the most bitter this year. Each candidate accused the other of making personal attacks. Tens of millions of dollars in outside spending poured into the race, including from the American Israel Public Affairs Committee's affiliated super PAC, which backed Stevens. El-Sayed took particular offence to an outside group's ad accusing him of disrespecting women.
Although Stevens pledged after the primary to help El-Sayed however she could, the two have not appeared together publicly since a unity breakfast two days after his summer win.
Last week Stevens' fiancé told the Detroit News that he would vote for Rogers. Stevens later appeared on CNN to reaffirm her support for El-Sayed but urged his campaign to "shut out the hate and call it out."
But on Friday, after The Associated Press reported on the planned joint appearance, Stevens released a lengthy statement calling on El-Sayed to condemn an ad by an independent left-wing group that linked U.S. support for Israel to high grocery prices.
"Abdul promises to get money out of politics," Stevens wrote. "He can begin by getting Israel out of his explanation for everything Michigan cannot afford."
El-Sayed responded: "So while Congresswoman Stevens seems intent on relitigating a primary that ended three months ago, let's all focus on the task at hand."
He made clear the ad was not made by his campaign and said, "I do not agree with the contents of that ad."
Trump hails 'bravery' by Stevens
Trump praised Stevens in a Truth Social post, saying she was "a Highly Respected Democrat" and that her support from Black and Jewish voters in Michigan is "virtually unprecedented."
"Haley said what few people are willing to say. She showed great and almost unprecedented loyalty to the Great People of Michigan with what she did," Trump wrote. "It's a thing called bravery, and few people have it like just displayed by Congresswoman Haley Stevens!"
A spokesperson for the Republican National Committee, Hunter Lovell, said in a statement that Democrats who feel abandoned by El-Sayed "have a home" with Rogers.
"When Congresswoman Haley Stevens, who ran against Abdul El-Sayed and knows him better than anyone, stands with Mike Rogers, Michigan should take notice," he said.
