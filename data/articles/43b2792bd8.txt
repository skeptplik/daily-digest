US military maintained a "strict" blockade of Iranian ports by firing at a ship that violated the naval containment on Thursday.
An American aircraft fired at a Palau-flagged ship violating Washington's blockade of Iranian ports on Thursday.
The US military announced on Friday it had struck the Pacific Archer in its stern with "precision munitions" while the ship was attempting to run the blockade.
"The ship's propulsion was ultimately disabled, and the civilian crew was unharmed," the US statement said.
The Pacific Archer had left the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas in the Strait of Hormuz to be rapidly intercepted in the Gulf of Oman, just after the strait, according to the statement.
The US forces' Central Command said it "continues to strictly enforce the blockade," which allowed them to disable six ships and redirect 158 others.
Hormuz remains closed
This US blockade has been in action since mid-July following the collapse of a short-lived agreement with Iran.
On its side, Iran has maintained its own blockade on ships transiting through the Strait of Hormuz. In recent weeks, the country's forces have signalled multiple ships catching fire while attempting to use routes that Tehran considers off-limits.
On Monday, Iranian foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei vowed to use all means at the country's disposal to counter the US blockade.
Diplomatic efforts attempting to resolve the crisis — which broke out in February with US and Israeli strikes on Iran — have remained stalled.
Iran has said that Hormuz could reopen within two days if the US agreed to its demands, which include the lifting of the blockade.
