Ayesha Curry brought Saint Laurent‘s interpretation of the clear heels trend to the “Today” show on Tuesday as part of an all-green ensemble from the French maison. Curry went on the daytime show to talk about the Roku film “Second Chance Holiday,” which marks her first leading role in a movie.
To ground her ensemble, Curry showcased the Sibyl slingback pumps from Saint Laurent, a translucent style made from TPU and patent leather. The pump, marked at a price of $3,600, stands out with a crystal jewel embellishment on the vamp. The style also features a distinct pointed cap toe, a satin outsole, and lacquer-and-plexiglas spliced stiletto heel embedded with a Saint Laurent-engraved spike. Curry modeled the amber green and kaki iteration of the footwear style.

The pumps served as a tonal complement to another piece from Saint Laurent, the Lavallière-cuff minidress in lace. The light green dress features semi-sheer lace, buttons down the front, scalloped trim and long sleeves with lavallières cuffs — aka bows tied around the wrists. Curry completed her look with dangling earrings and a hairstyle of long, loose waves.
Curry, founder of the beauty line Sweet July Skin, was recently a speaker at the 2026 WWD x FN x Beauty Inc Women in Power event in September. Among many topics, she offered insight into her strategy behind pursuing business opportunities.
“I learned very quickly the motto of quality over quantity,” Curry said. “[Now], I truly come from a perspective of looking at, one: does this fill my cup? Two: does it fill a white space within whatever market I’m trying to enter? Three: Is it going to make an impact? Is it going to make a difference?”
The fashion industry continues to reinvent clear heels through material use, silhouette and a variety of other design features. Sometimes, the clear accent comes in through an unexpected element. Take, for instance, the unique shoes recently worn by Rosalía — the Junko Shimada spring 2009 “I Feel Love” ballet platform heels, featuring a clear wedge heel. Other times, the entire shoe is essentially clear, like the Saint Laurent’s men’s spring 2027 dress shoes — which also speak to a crossover for the trend traditionally associated with the women’s market.
