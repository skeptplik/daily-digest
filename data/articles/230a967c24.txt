McDonald's Japan, the fast-food giant's Japanese subsidiary, has disclosed a data breach after attackers accessed a database containing sensitive information for users of the company's official smartphone app.
Founded in 1940, McDonald's is now a multinational fast-food chain that operates more than 40,000 restaurants in more than 100 countries and serves tens of millions of customers daily.
In a Friday statement, the company's Japanese subsidiary confirmed that threat actors gained access to its official app's database and said the exposed information includes the users' product purchase history, IP addresses, mobile device location data, and customer management IDs (which cannot be used to identify the customers' email addresses or member IDs).
McDonald's Japan also added that the exposed data doesn't include the affected customers' names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, or credit card information.
While it has yet to reveal when it detected the attack or how many individuals had their data stolen, the company said a follow-up investigation found no service disruption or misuse of official apps as a result of the incident.
"We are working to invalidate any credentials used for unauthorized access, investigate the relevant systems, and report to the Personal Information Protection Commission," it said. "In addition, we will strive to strengthen security measures and prevent recurrence."
This isn't the first data breach to affect McDonald's in recent years, with the fast food giant disclosing a breach in June 2021 after attackers breached its systems and stole information belonging to employees and customers from the US, South Korea, and Taiwan.
In 2017, McDonald's also patched a cross-site scripting (XSS) security flaw that affected its official website and exposed customers' plain-text passwords.
Japanese companies under attack
Last month, Burger King Japan also disclosed a major data breach after attackers stole 11.18 million customer records that were later leaked online.
The two incidents affecting the two Japanese fast food chains add to a large wave of cyberattacks impacting high-profile Japanese companies since the start of the year, including Japan Airlines, entertainment system maker Daiichi Kosho, cloud and digital infrastructure firm IDC Frontier, Japanese publishing giant Nikkei, cloud and data center service provider Sakura Internet, taxi operator Nihon Kotsu, telecommunications giant KDDI, and many others.
Macnica researcher Yutaka Sejiyama has logged 288 cybersecurity incidents affecting Japanese organizations involving personal information theft or exposure since the start of the year.
Sejiyama found that attackers are now probing websites and APIs for access-control, configuration, and authentication weaknesses, and exploiting known (n-day) vulnerabilities to find other victims.
