The number of babies born to Japanese nationals in Japan in the first half of 2026 rose 1,379 from a year before to 320,458, marking the first increase in 11 years, health ministry data have shown.
The rise may have reflected an increase in the number of marriages in 2024 after years of decline during the COVID-19 pandemic.
However, the number of marriages in January-June this year declined 708 to 229,458. The preliminary number of births up to September, including Japanese nationals born abroad and foreign nationals born in Japan, fell 0.3% to 523,304.
“We can’t see whether the full-year number will rise or fall,” a ministry official said.
By prefecture, Tokyo marked the largest number of births in the first half at 41,745, up 1,864 from a year earlier. Osaka came next with 25,431, up 292, followed by Kanagawa with 23,961, up 122, Aichi with 21,325, up 392, Saitama with 18,489, up 88, Chiba with 15,946, up 121, and Fukuoka with 15,294, up 308.
The number grew in 24 of the country’s 47 prefectures. Rises were seen especially in urban areas.
Meanwhile, Tottori posted the lowest figure at 1,383, up seven, followed by Akita with 1,402, down 45, Kochi with 1,454, down 39, Shimane with 1,548, down 43, and Tokushima with 1,710, down six. Ishikawa, Niigata and Tochigi showed large declines of 416, 330 and 258, respectively.
The annual number of births in Japan has been on a downward trend since 1975. It fell below 1 million in 2016 and below 700,000 in 2024. In 2025, the number was 671,270, hitting a record low for the 10th consecutive year.
The decrease in births is progressing about 15 years faster than the forecasts of the National Institute of Population and Social Security Research. The government is trying to curb the decline by implementing measures such as expanding child allowances.
