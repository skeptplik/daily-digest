Israeli strike on a Gaza hospital kills 6 people, including surgeon, health authorities say
Nasser Hospital in the city of Khan Younis is one of the few functioning medical facilities left in Gaza
An Israeli airstrike on southern Gaza's main hospital overnight killed at least six Palestinians, including a surgeon and a teenage boy, medics said, the latest Israeli attack to undermine a year-old ceasefire in the war-shattered territory.
The strike hit a building containing surgery wards at Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis. Israel's military said it targeted a senior Hamas commander and accused the militants of using the hospital complex to plan attacks, which the group denied.
Medics and witnesses said two missiles crashed through the roof of the five-storey building, causing extensive damage. The surgery department, which serves half of Gaza's two million people, was knocked out of service. At least 20 others were wounded.
U.S. President Donald Trump’s Board of Peace, which oversees the ceasefire agreement and a broader peace plan, called on both Israel and Hamas to honour the deal, in a rare response to an individual attack.
Ayman Abu Obeid, a doctor who was injured in the attack, said he was on an overnight shift in the hospital when an explosion ripped through the room where he and others were sleeping.
"All of a sudden, the world turned upside down. It went dark all at once," he said from a hospital bed. Near him, piles of mangled furniture and medical equipment could be seen covered in dust from blown-out concrete. Torn wires hung down from the ceiling.
Among those killed was Rezik Abushira, a surgeon who was featured in a 2026 documentary, American Doctor, working alongside three visiting U.S. doctors who help provide medical care for Palestinians in Gaza. The dead also included a 13-year-old boy, according to Gaza's Education Ministry.
Commenting on the vehicle strike, the Israeli military said it hit a Hamas militant, without giving further details. It did not comment on the two other incidents.
Nasser Medical Complex director Dr. Ahmed Al-Farra said that the strike occurred without any prior warning or evacuation notice. The Israeli military claimed it had held "warning conversations" with Gaza health officials before Thursday's strike regarding militant use of hospitals. It said it made clear that "if the activity was not halted, it would act to remove the threats posed by it."
"What sin did the hospital that is treating children, the elderly and women commit to get targeted?" Khalil Shaheen told CBC News outside of Nasser Hospital.
"All the countries in the world are watching us. This is wrong. The hospital is full of sick people," Maryam Abu Hatab told CBC News on Thursday. "We're afraid to even come to the hospital now."
A projectile lies next to remains from another projectile inside Nasser Hospital following an Israeli airstrike in Khan Younis, in the southern Gaza Strip on Thursday. (Ramadan Abed/Reuters)
Since the start of the Gaza war, Israel's military has struck the Nasser Hospital complex at least seven times, Al-Farra and other hospital officials say.
"This will not stop. They will continue targeting Nasser Medical Complex," Al-Farra said at a news conference Thursday, adding that Gaza's already devastated health-care system is now in a much more dire situation as a result of the attack.
Al-Farra said only one of two projectiles targeting the hospital had detonated, adding that if the second one had also gone off, hospital officials would be dealing with a "massacre."
Meanwhile, four more Palestinians were killed later on Thursday in separate incidents. Two were killed and several were wounded in an Israeli airstrike on a car in Gaza City, and one other person was killed by Israeli gunfire in central Gaza's Bureij refugee camp, medics said. A fourth person was killed in an airstrike near a cemetery in Jabalia, north of the enclave, taking Thursday's death toll to at least 10.
Attacking hospitals a war crime under international law
Hospitals are protected under international humanitarian law and attacking them is a war crime.
Medical facilities can lose that protected status if they are used for military purposes "to commit acts harmful to the enemy." But the Geneva Conventions, passed in 1949, say there must be a warning with a reasonable period of time before any attack, and that the attack must be proportional to the military threat and take care to avoid civilian casualties.
Medical organizations and rights groups also have criticized Israel's restrictions on medical aid and equipment entering Gaza as a violation of international law. The Israeli military says it allows aid to enter Gaza but bars some "dual use" items it asserts could be used for military purposes.
"Gaza's health facilities are lifelines for its people. They must be protected. They must never be militarized or attacked," said World Health Organization chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus in a statement.

Francesca Albanese, the UN special rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territories, says Israel's attack on Nasser hospital is going to be a "blow," to Gaza's health infrastructure.
"Nasser hospital is one of the last functioning parts … of the devastated health system in Gaza," Albanese told CBC News from Tunis on Thursday.
"We have proof for three years that Israel has intentionally, systematically — and in a widespread fashion destroyed the health infrastructure so i wouldn't assume that this is because Hamas is there."
In a statement, Israel's military said it targeted a person named Muhammad Abu Ismail, describing him as "the commander of Hamas' Khan Younis Brigade." The military said he and other militants "were hiding inside the Nasser Hospital."
The military accused the militants of "turning the Nasser Hospital compound into a headquarters and operating from there."
Hamas rejected the military's claim that it had used the hospital as a base for operations. It did not immediately provide comment on whether a leader from the group had been targeted, injured or killed in the strike.
In August 2025, the hospital was hit by a double-tap Israeli attack that killed at least 20 people, including journalists and health workers.
An October 2025 ceasefire halted major fighting but has failed to halt Israeli attacks or secure Hamas's disarmament. At least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire went into effect, according to Gaza's Health Ministry. The Israeli military says five Israeli soldiers have been killed during the same period.
