After the United States, China and Russia held an unprecedented trilateral summit on Thursday, Japan is concerned that the trilateral framework may become a regular occurrence.
On the heels of the summit between U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, China, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi held telephone talks with Trump to affirm close cooperation between Japan and the United States.
At a news conference in Tokyo on Friday, Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara stressed, “We’ll communicate closely with our allies and like-minded nations and work to maintain and strengthen a free and open international order.”
Japan is eager to maintain the existing international order, including by strengthening the rule of law.
In a joint peace declaration released after the summit, the U.S., Chinese and Russian leaders confirmed their commitment to “maintaining strategic stability and international security.” A Japanese government official said, “We were relieved that nothing major was decided at the summit.”
Still, there are concerns in Japan that the three major powers may hold summits on a regular basis. A senior Foreign Ministry official said, “That all depends on how serious Trump is (about the three-way framework).”
At a U.S.-China summit in September, Trump noted that the two countries were allies during World War II. At Thursday’s meeting, Xi said that the three nations fought side by side in the war against fascism, and Putin said that their shared victory in the war united them.
“We cannot overlook the fact that China and Russia are bringing up such ties,” said a high-ranking Japanese government official.
The peace declaration also warned that the International Criminal Court and other similar bodies “must not abuse their jurisdiction.” The ICC, based in The Hague in the Netherlands, has issued an arrest warrant for Putin over his country’s invasion of Ukraine, and has launched an investigation into alleged war crimes by U.S. military personnel.
As Japan supports the ICC, headed by Tomoko Akane, a Japanese national, the disagreement between Tokyo and Washington over the court may become a source of friction.
Speaking to reporters after the phone talks with Trump on Thursday evening, Takaichi admitted that Japan and the United States have different views. However, she stressed, “Japan’s position is to strengthen the rule of law while cooperating with relevant countries.”
In her message to an international conference on Ukraine held the same day, Takaichi said that any attempts to unilaterally change the status quo by force must not be tolerated.
