Console shipments fell 36.5% year-on-year to 6.4 million units during the third quarter, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence Kagan.
The data firm attributed most of the decline to Nintendo at 59%, as Switch 2 shipments dropped from 5.5 million to 3.3 million year-over-year, having "harvested early demand" during its launch period last June.
Sony accounted for 38.7% of the decline with PlayStation, and Microsoft represented 2.3% with Xbox.
S&P Global Market Intelligence analyst Neil Barbour exclusively tells GamesIndustry.biz he expects "the year-over-year trend to be flat-ish," after previously expecting console shipments to fall 19.5% this year. "We're too late in the cycle, and the prices are too high."
The standard US price of the base PS5 increased from $549.99 to $649.99 in April, the second price hike in under a year.
Microsoft raised the Xbox Series X price from $649.99 to $799.99 and the Series S from $399.99 to $499.99 in August, marking the third increase in 15 months.
As a result, PS shipments fell 35.3% to 2.6 million in Q3, while Xbox fell 14.2% to 500,000 units.
The data firm recognised that while marquee titles like Grand Theft Auto 6 and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake may temporarily boost hardware sales, they can "only do so much once the hardware costs more than many consumers consider a reasonable discretionary purchase."
"I can't say that I've seen any sales trends at the macro level that directly correlate with anything in the hype cycle for GTA 6," Barbour says. "What I would say is that the PS5 is fairly close to the PS4's lifetime sales. So, it's possible that most of Sony's GTA audience is already represented in the installed base.
"There's a more significant delta between Xbox Series and Xbox One sales, but Microsoft is pushing higher prices to consumers and lacks the co-marketing muscle that Sony has."
Barbour notes that PS5 Pro has "gained traction over the past few quarters," attributed to "consumers hoping for better performance for GTA 6."
"On the other hand, we've seen evidence of the Xbox sales mix trending back in favour of the Series S, perhaps because its pricing advantage looks even sharper as the other console price tags move past psychological thresholds."
As for whether GTA 6 will ultimately move the needle, Barbour notes that he would typically "expect there to be more aggressive promotions and bundling offers around GTA 6, which would lift sales. But it looks like there's just not enough wiggle room in the bill of materials right now."
"It's possible the game comes in way above expectations and pushes more people into these consoles, but right now, I'm thinking of the launch window and the holiday shopping season as one event, and it's bound by pricing."
