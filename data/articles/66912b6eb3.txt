The President of the European Commission has emphasised the importance of Black Sea security for Europe’s eastern flank, announcing additional funding for Romania and the maritime security hub set to be developed in Constanța and Varna.
Speaking about Europe’s support for Romania, Ursula von der Leyen said the European Union stands in "full solidarity" with the country and that it can "count on a firm and united European response." She highlighted the immediate support that Europe can provide through satellite imagery from Copernicus.
Von der Leyen also said, "We will now accelerate work on the Black Sea Maritime Security Hub, with a clear timeline for completion, and dedicated funding of 50 million euros."
According to Euronews, the security hub could become operational in the first half of next year.
The hub will have three main objectives: protecting critical infrastructure, sharing information between the two countries, and supporting trade, explained Florentina Lazar, Euronews Romania correspondent.
Following his meeting with the President of the European Commission, Romanian President Nicușor Dan stressed that the Black Sea is a key hub for global trade and Europe's energy security. He also underlined the importance of regional security for Ukraine.
Dan said that a stable peace in Ukraine and an effective reconstruction process would not be possible without a secure Black Sea, "We have had numerous incidents in the Black Sea in recent times, a whole spectrum of hybrid warfare, in which, obviously, Russia is attempting to escalate the conflict it started five years ago in Ukraine and involve European countries in this conflict." the president said.
The European funding announcement comes after Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev accused Romania of being indirectly responsible for attacks on commercial ships in the Black Sea. Radev said Bucharest had blamed Russia without providing evidence.
Meanwhile, the European Parliament has also condemned attacks on civilian ships in the Black Sea through a resolution holding Russia responsible.
According to a report cited by the Financial Times, more than 570 security incidents have occurred in the Black Sea this year alone, while at least 40 sailors have lost their lives as a result of the attacks.
