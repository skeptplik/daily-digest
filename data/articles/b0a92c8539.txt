ANA Holdings has announced the establishment of ANA Next Aviation, a new company dedicated to training pilots, to prepare for an expected increase in pilot retirements beginning around 2030.
The new company will offer basic training, which has been outsourced to overseas training providers, at a base in Kagoshima Airport in Kyushu.
This will enable the airline group to establish an in-house system for training pilots, including practical training using actual aircraft. It is the first pilot-training provider created by an airline for its own use in Japan.
Japan is expected to see a large number of pilots hired during the country’s economic bubble of the late 1980s and early 1990s retire starting around 2030. Global demand for aviation personnel is also expected to increase, prompting the parent of All Nippon Airways to ensure a stable supply of new pilots.
ANA Next Aviation is scheduled to begin operations in the second half of fiscal 2027. The new company will initially aim to train 20 to 30 pilots annually before increasing to around 90 from fiscal 2030.
ANA Holdings has two training programs for pilots before they become first officers — a basic training course and an aircraft-based training course.
“We will consider contributing to the aviation industry as a whole in the future, including by accepting trainees from outside the group,” ANA Holdings Executive Vice President Naohiro Terakawa said Wednesday.
