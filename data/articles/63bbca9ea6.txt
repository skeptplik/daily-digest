The brilliance of Saint-Louis crystal is glimmering once more in the windows of its historic Paris boutique.
The 13 Rue Royale boutique was first opened in 1992 and is situated at the heart of the French capital, between landmarks Opéra Garnier and Place de la Concorde. The space was redesigned in a contemporary context by Paris-based architecture firm RDAI, the design-forward practice founded by Rena Dumas in 1972, known for its design of luxury spaces like Hermès flagships worldwide.
A salute to the firm’s centuries-old origins in the town of Saint-Louis-lès-Bitche within the Northern Vosges Regional Nature Park, the redesign reflects the verdant forest landscape with a palette of browns, blues and grays. RDAI’s furniture and displays drew on the precision of Saint-Louis’s workshops and the curves of glassmakers’ molds, creating a refined, renewed setting for the collections.
Founded in 1586, Saint-Louis is one of France’s most historic crystal manufacturers and was embraced by the rising aristocracy of the time. Its intricate chandeliers and tableware famously illuminated the halls of Europe’s grandest palaces, including the Vatican.
In recent years, however, the firm’s chief executive officer Jérôme de Lavergnolle has been focused on growing the company’s modern lighting business and relationships with key architects and designers.
Recent high-tech introductions include the Folia portable lamp, an indoor/outdoor cordless lamp designed by French designer Noé Duchaufour-Lawrance; Torsade, a collection that draws inspiration from the armature of a chandelier designed by Stefania Di Petrillo, and a new outdoor Royal Sconce in clear crystal outfitted with IP44 technology, to protect the lamp from humidity and water.
De Lavergnolle joined the Hermès Group-controlled Saint-Louis in 2010, after the financial crisis of 2008 shattered the business generated by wedding registries.
In 2025, Saint-Louis embraced innovation further, debuting its chandelier configurator, which allows customers to compose bespoke-like lighting with ease on an iPad. “You can design the chandelier you want to have. Customers want to imagine something which has been designed just for themselves,” de Lavergnolle told WWD at the time, adding that lighting represented 50 percent of sales.
