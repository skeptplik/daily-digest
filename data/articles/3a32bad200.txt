OK, I’ll admit it: I wanted to hate You Can See Everything, the A24 documentary about Theranos fraudster Elizabeth Holmes, which finally arrived in UK cinemas last week. The marketing was slick, the hype was unparalleled. I didn’t believe a three-hour documentary about a disgraced tech CEO could be that good. But, like our colleague Adrian and basically every film critic who’s seen it, I left the cinema thinking: that was amazing and what on earth did I just watch?
In my 20s, I worked in corporate PR. Big companies employed our agency to promote their chief executives, tell the company’s story to the media and politicians, and protect everyone’s reputations. Our job, my job, was to create and control the narrative – to tell stories that made our clients the good guys. If you find the right angle, anyone can become the hero of a story. A betting company becomes one of the biggest employers on the UK high street, with bricks-and-mortar shops that provide much-needed jobs. An arms company is “one of the world’s leading manufacturers” – a national asset to be proud of. My colleagues were bright, young and talented. Most of them now work for big tech companies.
You Can See Everything is a chaotic and confusing battle for the narrative about Elizabeth Holmes. Her husband Billy Evans’s version of the story is that a smart but overly trusting young woman who was manipulated by others (and is madly in love with her brilliant spouse). Directors Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim encourage you to question Billy and Elizabeth’s relationship, presenting him as potentially controlling, narcissistic and money-hungry. Holmes, meanwhile, paints herself as misunderstood, innocent and unassuming, afraid of the limelight and at times Madonna-like. The appearance of Amanda Seyfried (who played Holmes in The Dropout, a 2022 TV drama about the CEO) is a much-needed pressure valve, in that she voices what most of the audience has been thinking for the first half of the film: what the hell is going on?. But even she attempts to shape the narrative around her portrayal of Holmes and the duty actors have to tell the truth about real-life subjects. At times, different versions of all these stories win out.
If I were advising Elizabeth Holmes on her image, I would have recommended she make a documentary. From Beyoncé to the Beckhams, biographical documentaries that let high-profile people construct a plausible-but-rose-tinted version of their lives have become part of the PR playbook. And a famous director is increasingly part of that package. Oscar-winning film-maker Fisher Stevens directed the David Beckham documentary, largely sidestepping the more uncomfortable aspects of Beckham’s public life, including allegations surrounding Rebecca Loos, along with any other controversies that might have harmed the family brand. Rick Rubin made an eight-part (!) docu-series about Jay-Z. Jon M Chu, who was behind Crazy Rich Asians and Wicked, directed two Justin Bieber documentaries.
I’m not sure I would have suggested Holmes invite Nathan Fielder to move into her home. That level of round the clock access makes it harder to control the image you present. But while You Can See Everything may not have been the film Holmes and Evans wanted, it has already changed the conversation around the former Theranos CEO.

Top five: Julia Roberts movies
On this Sunday’s culture swap, Lanre and I are watching classic films we’ve never seen. You’ll get to hear why I avoided the Terminator movies for so long, and Lanre’s very strong opinions about Richard Gere’s wardrobe and performance in Pretty Woman. My love for Julia Roberts runs deep – she is one of the ultimate film stars and such a joy to watch, even when the film itself falls short (see After the Hunt, Ticket to Paradise). So here are my top five movies starring the woman with the megawatt smile.
- Pretty Woman | A plot that’s also a critique of financial capitalism, and chemistry so good that she and Gere reprised it for the unofficial sequel Runaway Bride. I stand by this film as Roberts’s best work.
- Erin Brockovich | Based on a heartbreaking and inspiring true story of a woman’s fight to get justice for a community affected by groundwater contamination, this film rightly secured Roberts the Oscar for best actress.
- Notting Hill | United with Richard Curtis and Hugh Grant for one of the ultimate 90s romcoms. And a supporting cast to die for (Rhys Ifans! Emma Chambers! Gina McKee!)
- Mystic Pizza | An underrated cult classic about three young waitresses coming of age in coastal Connecticut. Inspired by a real restaurant that still exists.
- Mona Lisa Smile | Art, education and womanhood – a lovely comfort watch.
