When it comes to New York style, each neighborhood is its own microcosm. The It-girl DJs of Dimes Square are sartorially miles away from the midtown men zipped into their puffer vests (even if the finance-bro boyfriend, fashionista girlfriend is a common romantic pairing). But once the weather starts to chill, there’s one item that unites New Yorkers above all, and that’s a fabulous jacket.
A dip below 40 degrees can set off alarm bells for the start of seasonal depression elsewhere, but not here in the Big Apple. Last Friday’s chilly rain did little to stop the procession of on-trend faux furs I saw out and about in Chinatown, Williamsburg, and Park Slope. When you finally get to wear your statement coat the elements are irrelevant.
Recent news cycles affirm the mind games that a great jacket can play. Much has been written about former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s penchant for luxury leather, but the person with the real kink is the one who photoshopped him into Hedi Slimane-era Saint Laurent. Conner Ives’s faux-fur trimmed silk robe coat is stirring up almost as much fervor, with fashionistas everywhere trying to recreate the look, vintage or otherwise.
Try it on for size: Maybe you’re having a great day and throw on a pastel mohair overcoat for some extra pep in your step, or perhaps you’re in a bit of a slump and slip into your favorite buttery leather bomber to collect a few mood-boosting compliments. Vogue’s senior editor Joan Kennedy walked into the office on Monday in a seriously sensational velvet Victorian jacket; after two compliments in quick succession, she confided that’s exactly why she wore it: to brighten up the start of the week.
Dare to forgo your outerwear flair and you’ll see envy-inducing toppers everywhere. My camera roll is filled with pics of perfectly cropped tweeds (not the granny kind), elegantly draped trenches, and an antique-looking jacquard duster that almost made me chase its wearer down the Columbus Circle escalator.
As one user on X so aptly noted: “Everything in the world is about jackets, except jackets. Jackets are about power.” And in New York, there’s no greater one-up than a damn good one.
