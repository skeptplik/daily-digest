LONDON — British fashion brand Nicole Farhi has relaunched with a 37-piece collection of women’s and men’s wardrobe essentials.
Titled “Chapter One: A New Dawn,” the collection draws on the brand’s heritage and relaxed tailoring while emphasizing versatility, craftsmanship and longevity. Available on the brand’s official website, the prices range from 150 pounds for cashmere accessories to 1,200 pounds for a double-breasted wool coat.
The brand’s new chapter is led by Maxine Hargreaves Adams, who acquired Nicole Farhi out of administration in 2013. She now shares creative direction with Richard Biedul, while her daughter, April Adams, is chief operating officer.
“Biedul and I very much share the same vision for the brand. As co-creative directors, we work together across all aspects of the creative direction, from the development of the collections to the overall aesthetic, brand identity and how we communicate the brand,” said Hargreaves Adams.
Biedul brings experience as a model and creative consultant, she added.
“His extensive experience within the international fashion industry brings a distinct perspective to the brand. His natural creative sensibility, innate understanding of style, silhouette and proportion, and appreciation of understated luxury are very much aligned with the vision for Nicole Farhi,” continued Hargreaves Adams.
Founded in 1982 by French-born designer Nicole Farhi with the backing of French Connection founder Stephen Marks, the brand grew into an international lifestyle label known for soft tailoring and tactile fabrics. It entered administration in 2013 amid difficulties in the U.K. retail market. After Hargreaves Adams’ rescue, Farhi later left the fashion business to focus on sculpture.
“The return of Nicole Farhi feels incredibly timely. Having spent much of my life in the clothing and retail industry, I’ve always believed in creating clothes that people genuinely want to wear and return to,” said Hargreaves Adams, whose father John Hargreaves founded the British fashion retailer Matalan, which was sold to a group of institutional investors and lenders in 2023.
“What makes this new chapter particularly exciting is bringing together different perspectives and generations, while staying true to the values that have always defined Nicole Farhi. I don’t believe great style should be dictated by age or passing trends. For me, it’s about creating beautiful, effortless clothes that allow people to express their individuality, with quality at the heart of everything we do,” she added.
Material integrity is a big part of the value proposition. The collection uses Shetland herringbone woven by British mill Abraham Moon, cashmere sourced from a family-run mill in Inner Mongolia and sandwashed silk crêpe de chine from China.
Biedul said the aim was to reinterpret the house’s design codes without being constrained by them. “For me, this was always about honoring a legacy without being bound by it. Chapter One reimagines familiar house codes through a contemporary lens, creating a modular wardrobe that evolves over time.”
“At its heart is a simple philosophy: less but better. Fewer, more considered pieces, cut from exceptional materials in thoughtful proportions. Clothing designed not simply for a season, but to be lived in, loved and worn for years to come,” he added.
While the brand is relaunched as a direct-to-consumer business, Hargreaves Adams teased that she would like to build relationships with a small number of independent retailers, both in the U.K. and internationally.
“For me, it’s about growing the brand organically and working with the right partners, rather than growth for growth’s sake. We want to build a brand synonymous with effortless style, exceptional quality and understated luxury, creating clothes that have longevity and are not dictated by trends,” she said.
