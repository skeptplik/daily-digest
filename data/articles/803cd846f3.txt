Skyler Gisondo’s “Family Feud” moment didn’t exactly go as planned.
The actor is just back to his hotel from a taping of the game show, a “lifelong dream” of his.
“I loved game shows as a kid. I have two sisters, so we had five people in my immediate family and my dream was for us to be on ‘Family Feud.’ So that was a long time coming. And to be honest, I completely blew it,” Gisondo says. “I don’t think I got a single win on the board. I think I was a clean zero for three. But I’m proud of our team’s effort.”
The team in question was his “Focker In-Law” costars, including Ben Stiller, Robert De Niro, Ariana Grande and Beanie Feldstein, who Gisondo joins in the fourth installment of the “Meet the Fockers” series that Stiller and De Niro remade in 2000.
The movie, which opens Wednesday, comes on the heels of Gisondo’s star turn in “Primetime,” the Robert Pattinson-led thriller about the production of the show “To Catch a Predator.”
It’s been a fall season that the 30-year-old humbly describes as “definitely too much talking about myself,” but he’s more than happy to get to talk about these movies.
In “Primetime,” he plays an aspiring actor nicknamed “Decoy Dan” by Pattinson’s Chris Hansen, after he is hired by “Predator” to pose as a teenage boy for the show’s sting operations.
“‘Primetime’ was the most gratifying and rewarding experience of my life,” Gisondo says. “It’s been amazing because truthfully, when we were making the movie, whenever someone asked me how it was going, I would say usually, ‘It’s definitely going to be the coolest thing I’ve ever been a part of, and I don’t know how many people are going to see it,’ because it just felt like what we were doing was unlike anything I’d done. Lance [Oppenheim, the director] coming from the doc world, it felt very experimental and alive and just left of center in a way that nothing I had done had ever been a part of had been quite like that. And so to now see the way audiences have responded to this movie has been completely surreal and so exciting to talk to people about it because it certainly was the most formative experience of my career in so many ways, and life really.”
When Gisondo watched the movie for the first time, he almost didn’t recognize himself.
“I had never seen myself that way or in a movie like that, in a world like that,” he says. It’s also shown the industry a side of him never before seen, and it’s now paying off.
“I am reading different kinds of things. I’m getting new kinds of opportunities, which is so exciting and such a gift,” he says. “Now the goal is just don’t mess it up. Keep going.”
“Focker In-Law” has Gisondo showing off his comedic range, starring as Greg and Pam Focker’s adult son, who brings home his girlfriend, played by Grande, and tells his family he plans to propose.
“I was so stoked because it’s a movie that I, like everybody else grew up with, grew up just cherishing and loving and watching year after year. And so to step into this world, it was just completely surreal,” Gisondo says of joining the franchise. “And you’re opposite Robert De Niro, literally the greatest actor of all time. To breathe the same air as somebody like that, let alone have to play their grandson and get to mess around with him — unbelievable.”
He’d worked with Stiller when he was just 17 on “Night at the Museum: Secret of the Tomb,” an experience that was “massively formative for me,” and so reconnecting now as an adult in “Focker” was a nice full-circle moment.
“It’s a really great mile marker. Ben really took me under his wing [during ‘Night at the Museum’] and was so good to me, so generous with his time. And that experience, being 17 years old on a big movie like that, it really kind of woke my brain up to what this job was and what it could be. And here we were 12 years later and watching Ben in little moments before a scene or in the way that he prepared, I realized it was something that I’ve always done and I had forgotten even where I got it from. And so it all came together.”
As exciting and big as “Primetime” and “Focker In-Law” are, Gisondo’s phone had never blown up like it did when he was cast in “Shrek 5,” which will be out next year.
“I was getting texts from people I hadn’t spoken to in 15 years just really sincerely being like, ‘Dude, you made it. Oh my God, congratulations. This is amazing.’ And it does feel that way,” Gisondo says. “I mean, ‘Shrek’ was one of those movies for me that I just burned through the VHS. I watched over and over like everybody else in the world. And to be in that recording booth and doing my scenes; Eddie Murphy had already recorded his Donkey lines, and so they’re playing that playback and I’m responding to Mike Myers and Eddie Murphy, it’s so ridiculous.”
Gisondo is hoping to keep searching for the feeling of discovery that he found during “Primetime” as he ponders his next career steps.
“The thing I’m chasing is the challenge, just moving toward fear, moving toward uncertainty. There’s a movie we’re doing in the new year, another A24 movie called ‘Goblin’ that I don’t know how much I can say about but it’s definitely one of, if not the strangest, things I’ve ever read,” he says. “So I’m very excited to take a real swing.”
