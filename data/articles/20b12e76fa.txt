It's still a debate whether Grand Theft Auto 6 has the best gaming soundtrack of 2026, but it certainly has the most soundtrack of 2026. Rockstar Games' latest open-world crime epic features more than 800 songs, most of which you'll hear on its 26 genre-themed radio stations. Of all this breadth of wild music, you'd figure the most popular song on the soundtrack would be something that had overlap with a Fast and Furious OST. But it turns out the single best song in the GTA 6 soundtrack is something by... Nickelback?
Thanks to GTA 6, Nickelback's 2001 angst ballad "How You Remind Me" has been getting a lot of play — kind of. Technically, this version is a cover. GTA 6's Kaleidoscope FM radio station plays alternate-language versions of popular songs, the indisputable best of which is "Ola Afta Poui Fovamai," by the Greek singer and alternate former tourism minister Alkistis Protopsalti. As far as covers go, this is one for the ages. Seriously, listen:
You might be somewhat surprised to hear "How You Remind Me" making the rounds again, years removed from its days reigning over the Billboard charts. To this day, the most-played radio song of the 2000s has been relegated to background music in the daddest bars from Mississauga to Massapequa. It is arguably the epitome not just of Dad Rock but specifically of Divorced Dad Rock. If "How You Remind Me" was a person, its kitchen would include Slim Jims (assorted flavors), cold pizza (definitely pepperoni), and nothing else. It would drive a 2014 Corvette Stingray (bright red, obviously). It would live on the golf course.
But with "Ola Afta Poui Fovamai," Protopsalti injects a youthful, upbeat verve to Chad Kroeger's genius composition that legendarily emerged, nearly fully formed, from his brain in just 45 minutes following an argument with a partner. It's the sort of track you'd play in a convertible on a sunny day, or in the bathroom while getting ready for a party themed after The Devil Wears Prada (the good one). There is practically zero Divorced Dad energy in "Ola Afta Poui Fovamai." It is transformational.
I am far from the only person to completely lose my shit over the resurfacing of a song that hit number-one on charts in Austria, Canada, Denmark, Ireland, and the United Kingdom, in addition to peaking in the top ten in various other international markets. As of this writing, searches for "Nickelback" on Bluesky are all about "Ola Afta Poui Fovamai." On Facebook, Greek culture accounts are sharing the cover as a point of national pride. When Canadian streamer Dan "Greenskull" Hammill heard it for the first time in-game, he burst into a karaoke rendition that has since gone semi-viral.
Truth told, the Canadian rock quartet legitimately is one of my guilty pleasures. As a teenager, I once suffered a pretty serious concussion in a skiing accident; according to the paramedics, I spent the entire ride to the hospital singing "Photograph" in my unconscious state. Mortifying, but also hard to deny. Such is the allure of GTA 6. In being a mirror of our world, it's also a mirror of our lives, thus revealing truths we may not have otherwise been ready to admit. The boyfriends of the world start doing more chores for their partners. Those of us who've let the gym routine fall by the wayside pick it back up again. Nickelback? A guilty pleasure? I no longer see anything to be guilty about here.
