From the Scottish scenery of Skyfall to the Monte Carlo casino in GoldenEye, here’s all the places in Europe to add to your must-visit list ahead of the next Bond movie in 2028.
Five years after Daniel Craig had his last outing as the iconic British spy in No Time to Die (2021), we finally know who is stepping up as the seventh James Bond: Robert Pattinson.
Whether you agree with the casting or not – Euronews Culture’s David Mouriquand says it’s “simultaneously an inspired choice, a frustrating one, and a very safe bet” – the news has us feeling nostalgic.
Over the course of 25 official movies, the shaken-not-stirred martini drinker served Her Majesty’s Secret Service in countries across the globe, inspiring travellers to visit far-flung locations long before “set-jetting” had entered the lexicon.
Filming location pilgrimages will no doubt include Khao Phing Kan in Thailand, aka Scaramanga's Island in The Man with the Golden Gun (1974), and Udaipur’s Taj Lake Palace, which served as Octopussy’s floating palace lair. Mexico City’s Day of the Dead parade – which was actually reverse engineered from scenes in Spectre (2015) – is on the bucket list, too.
But what about Bond locations closer to home? Ahead of Pattinson’s turn serving His Majesty’s Secret Service (the first time ever for 007!) in 2028, we’ve rounded up some of the most iconic James Bond filming locations in Europe.
Scene-stealing Bond landscapes
Glencoe and Glen Etive, Scotland: Skyfall (2012)
Scotland has long been a popular choice for moviemakers for its dramatic scenery, with several James Bond films featuring scenes shot here, including The World Is Not Enough (1999), The Spy Who Loved Me (1977), and From Russia With Love (1963).
The most memorable, however, might just be Skyfall, when the double-oh agent and M make their way to the Bond family home, Skyfall Lodge, along Glen Etive road.
With the brooding hero and his Aston Martin DB5 set against the Buachaille Etive Mòr mountain, this iconic shot has been recreated plenty of times over the years.
Furka Pass, Switzerland: Goldfinger (1964)
Another recognisable road, this time in Switzerland, the Furka Pass had its starring role in Goldfinger.
The movie series is known for its car chases, and Sean Connery’s Bond sets after the eponymous villain along the winding roads here.
Today, drivers passing through can stop at “James Bond Strasse” which has a view over all the twists and turns.
Verzasca Dam, Switzerland: GoldenEye (1995)
Bond sure knows how to make an entrance, but the opening scenes of GoldenEye were particularly of note.
Marking Pierce Brosnan’s debut as Ian Fleming’s world-famous character in spectacular style, Bond bungee jumps off the Verzasca Dam to make his way into the secret base below.
The 220-metre-high dam is open to the public, and, if you’re feeling Bond levels of brave, you can even bungee jump off it.
Siena, Italy: Quantum of Solace (2008)
Like Scotland, Italy features in numerous Bond movies, including Craig’s second outing as the sartorial secret agent in Quantum of Solace.
While the Day of the Dead parade from Spectre is (or at least, was) fictional, the horse race that serves as the backdrop for a high intensity chase in the 2008 flick is very much real, and has been going on for centuries.
With the Palio di Siena going on below, Craig follows Quantum mole Mitchell across the rooftops of Siena, and while we don’t recommend attempting your own recreation of the various stunts here, you can enjoy similar views over the terracotta tiled roofs from the Siena Cathedral.
Bond-approved hotels
Hotel Cala di Volpe, Sardinia, Italy: The Spy Who Loved Me (1977)
Sardinia’s Hotel Cala di Volpe has long attracted celebrity guests, including the world’s most famous spy in 1977.
Roger Moore checked in here with Bond girl Naomi in The Spy Who Loved Me, although the suite they took was actually the property’s bar, which has since been transformed into Japanese restaurant Matsuhisa.
We’re not sure if Bond would approve, but during the summer months, you can wash down chef Nobu’s famed dishes here with a Matsutini, a twist on the classic with passionfruit and yuzu.
Palácio Estoril Hotel, Portugal: On Her Majesty’s Secret Service (1969)
Cascais’ Palácio Estoril Hotel has not one, but two connections to the franchise.
In George Lazenby’s one-time Bond portrayal, On Her Majesty’s Secret Service, the charismatic assassin with a licence to kill stays at the property.
You can even meet one of the film’s (ahem) stars as José Diogo, then an 18-year-old bellboy, who hands over the room key to Bond, still works here as a concierge.
As for the second connection, Bond author Ian Fleming stayed here in 1941, and the register in which he checked in as a government official is framed in the hotel’s royal gallery.
Plus, a card game between enemy spooks at Casino Estoril is said to have inspired the high-stakes showdown with Le Chiffre in Casino Royale.
Grandhotel Pupp, Czechia: Casino Royale (2006)
Speaking of, the 2006 reboot of the franchise, Casino Royale, may be centred around the Hotel Splendide in Montenegro, but its stand-in – the Grandhotel Pupp – is in Czechia.
Nearby, you’ll also find the Kaiserbad Spa, which was used for the exterior shots of the Casino Royale.
Bond’s bites, beverages and bets
Piz Gloria, Switzerland: On Her Majesty’s Secret Service (1969)
While you probably wouldn’t want to eat here in the Bond universe, given it’s where Ernst Stavro Blofeld plotted to create a biological weapon in On Her Majesty’s Secret Service, in reality Piz Gloria is home to a revolving restaurant with one hell of a view.
Located at 2,970 metres above sea level, after reaching the summit via cable car you’ll be surrounded by alpine peaks which stun year-round.
Sky high views obviously come with sky high prices, but for mega fans, it’s more than worth it.
Le Jules Verne restaurant, Paris: A View to a Kill (1985)
The 14th Bond film, A View to a Kill, certainly delivered on the view and the kill part, with a French agent assassinated over lunch with Moore’s Bond at Le Jules Verne restaurant in the Eiffel Tower.
The stunt element, which sees the assassin parachute off the tower after being pursued by the world-class operative on the stairs, was filmed on-site, although the restaurant interiors were shot on a set.
Hopefully, you won’t suffer the same fate if you choose to dine at the Michelin two-starred eatery yourself…
Casino de Monte-Carlo, Monaco: GoldenEye (1995)
Brosnan’s Bond plays the brilliantly-named Xenia Onatopp at baccarat at the Casino de Monte-Carlo in GoldenEye, with the exterior shots filmed on location in Monaco.
For a taste of Bond’s glamorous lifestyle, make a beeline for the casino’s Salle Europe Bar, which serves up both a shaken, not stirred martini as well as a Vesper, a cocktail invented by Bond in the first novel of the series featuring vodka, gin and white lillet.
Dukes Bar, London
Okay, so this spot isn’t featured in any of the James Bond movies, but as a regular hangout spot for Fleming, London’s famed Dukes Bar is well worth a visit.
Known for its martinis, which are served in frozen glasses with no ice, it’s said that the intelligence officer-turned-journalist-turned-author came up with the “shaken, not stirred” line while drinking here.
Open since 1908, the hotel the bar is in is currently under renovation but will reopen in mid-2027, so you’ll be able to wash down a few martinis steps away from your bed.
