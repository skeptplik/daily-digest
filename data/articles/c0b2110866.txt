CISA nominee LoSapio vows to bolster cyber defenses while denying Jan. 6 Capitol attack
President Donald Trump’s pick to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency on Thursday vowed to uncover any “gaps” within the organization that have been created by massive personnel and funding cuts.
“I'm going to get involved and understand where the gaps are and I will come back to this committee with the gap analysis,” Mike LoSapio, Palantir’s chief information security officer, told the Senate Homeland Security Committee.
LoSapio was nominated earlier this month to helm the nation’s cyber defense agency, which has been without a Senate-confirmed chief for almost two years. A previous nominee, Sean Plankey, withdrew from consideration earlier this year after spending months in limbo on Capitol Hill. Nick Andersen has led CISA in an acting capacity since February.
The agency has been a target of Trump’s ire over the role it played in debunking the conspiracy theories he spread that the 2020 presidential election was rigged. Since he returned to the Oval Office, CISA has lost one-third of its staff and had its budget slashed by millions.
In a brief nomination hearing, LoSapio described CISA as “effectively the cybersecurity version of the 911 system. You pick up the phone, you say you have an emergency, you need help.”
“If confirmed, I intend to bring together the community and the national labs to help collaborate and coordinate and to give defenders the tools they need to defend their networks,” including AI tools, he told the panel.
In his written statement to the committee, LoSapio also pledged to “recruit and keep talented people, give them clear priorities, and make sure every dollar Congress provides is spent with discipline and tied to measurable results.”
However, he demurred when asked to weigh in on the recent technical outages that have upended key sectors of the U.S. as he’s “not inside” the agency today. And he stopped short of saying CISA’s budget needed to be increased to meet digital threats.
“I don't necessarily believe more money means more security. I believe spending money on the right solutions in the right ways actually decreases risk,” LoSapio told Sen. Elissa Slotkin (D-Mich.).
He later testified that if analysis showed the need for more funding he would “come back to this committee and request for more resources.”
When asked by Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.) who won the 2020 presidential election, LoSapio repeatedly declined to directly answer.
“I'm not going to answer the question about an election six years ago,” he said. “My only involvement in the 2020 election was voting for the president of the United States.”
LoSapio also disagreed with Blumenthal when the policymaker asked if the Capitol was attacked on January 6, 2021.
“No, I don't believe there was an attack. No,” he said under persistent questioning.
In response, Blumenthal told LoSapio he was “disqualified from the position that you've been nominated to fill.”
The committee likely won’t vote on LoSapio’s nomination until after next week’s Thanksgiving recess. If advanced, he could face a full floor vote during the final weeks of the lame-duck session of Congress.
