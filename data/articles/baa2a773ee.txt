The Tamagotchi digital pet toy, created by Japan’s Bandai, on Monday marked the 30th anniversary of its release, having won fans across generations in and outside its home country.
Tamagotchi, launched in November 1996, caught on with high school girls before it quickly became a social phenomenon. However, as the initial excitement ended, Bandai was left with huge unsold stock. It booked a special loss of ¥6 billion for the year that ended in March 1999 and halted Tamagotchi sales.
Bandai resumed selling Tamagotchi in 2004, this time targeting elementary school girls. Since then, the company has expanded the lineup, introducing models that reflect changing times.
Tamagotchi Smart, released in 2021, incorporates food delivery features inspired by such services that became widespread during the COVID-19 pandemic, allowing users to have meals delivered to their pet characters.
The 2025 model includes a function to allow players to connect their devices and play together, reflecting a trend to seek real-world interactions. By summer that year, cumulative sales of the Tamagotchi series surpassed 100 million units.
Following the 2004 revival, Tamagotchi merchandise and animations gained popularity both in Japan and abroad. In 2007, Bandai opened a Tamagotchi official shop in Tokyo. Movies and television anime programs based on Tamagotchi were produced.
“There are people who buy stuffed toys of (Tamagotchi) characters even if they don’t have a device,” a Bandai official said.
At New York’s Times Square on Sunday, Bandai held an event to mark the 30th anniversary, showcasing Tamagotchi models over the years and featuring costumed performers acting as popular characters such as Mametchi. Local fans attended in large numbers, forming a long line.
“I’m deeply moved to be celebrating the 30th birthday (of Tamagotchi) in Times Square,” said Taro Tsuji, chief Tamagotchi officer and managing director of Bandai.
Bandai began to sell the Tamagotchi series in the United States and other foreign countries in 1997. Among those who showed up at the New York event was Japanese comedian Yuriyan Retriever.
