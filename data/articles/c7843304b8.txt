The government and ruling parties plan to extend by four years a special measure to reduce tax burdens for the sale of homes inherited from parents or grandparents, officials said Wednesday.
They also plan to expand the scope of homes eligible for the tax break.
The special measure is currently scheduled to expire at the end of 2027.
The envisaged extension, expected to be included in the government’s fiscal 2027 tax system reform package, is aimed at boosting the supply of homes amid soaring housing prices while reducing the number of vacant homes, which have been on the increase.
Under the tax break program, up to ¥30 million is deducted from the profits generated through the sale of houses that were renovated to meet the nation’s earthquake-resistance standards and of land after houses were demolished.
Currently eligible for the tax break are houses that were built on or before May 31, 1981, under older earthquake-resistance standards.
The government and ruling parties are considering expanding the program to also cover homes built between June 1981 and 2001 that meet the new standards.
According to a government survey, the number of vacant homes, excluding those offered for rent or sale, rose to about 3.86 million in 2023 from some 3.49 million in 2018.
With the number likely to continue increasing due to the nation’s declining population and vacant properties being at risk of collapsing if they are unattended, the government and the ruling camp hope that the tax break will encourage people to renovate inherited homes and put them up on the existing home market.
Meanwhile, the government and ruling parties are also considering expanding a tax exemption for children and grandchildren who receive financial assistance from their parents or grandparents to purchase homes, according to informed sources.
Under the proposal, the tax-free limit would be raised from the current ¥10 million to ¥15 million for households with children under 19 or couples in which one spouse is under 40.
The proposal was drawn up by the land ministry. The government and ruling coalition will continue discussions on the details as they work toward compiling the fiscal 2027 tax reform package, which is expected to be finalized in mid-December.
In response to requests from the housing industry for a higher exemption threshold, the ministry determined that a system was needed to provide targeted tax benefits to families with children.
