CrowdStrike (NASDAQ: CRWD) on Monday said it has agreed to acquire Zscaler (NASDAQ: ZS) in an all-stock transaction valued at approximately $43.9 billion.
The deal would combine one of the largest endpoint security providers with a pioneer in cloud-delivered zero-trust access and give CrowdStrike a much larger presence in network security.
The two companies already share more than 2,000 customers and have 16 product integrations. In September, they announced plans to expand their partnership so Falcon could continuously assess user risk and share that information with Zscaler’s access controls. CrowdStrike said it plans to bring the products together on a single platform with a common data layer and a single customer contract after closing.
Zscaler’s own acquisition spree
Zscaler has used acquisitions to expand beyond its core network security business. In February 2026, it acquired SquareX, whose browser extension helps enterprises enforce security policies on unmanaged devices. In November 2025, it bought SPLX, adding tools to discover, test and protect AI models, applications and agents.
Zscaler also completed its acquisition of managed detection and response provider Red Canary in August 2025, expanding its presence in security operations. The deal followed Zscaler’s 2024 purchases of Avalor, a $350 million acquisition that added technology for combining security data and prioritizing risk, and Airgap Networks, which brought network segmentation capabilities.
CrowdStrike expects the combined company to have more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue at closing and projects about $1 billion in additional annualized revenue within 30 months, in part from selling more products to existing customers.
The deal would put CrowdStrike in more direct competition with broad cybersecurity platform providers such as Palo Alto Networks.
Both boards unanimously approved the deal, which is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike’s fiscal 2028.
Unter the terms of the agreement, Zscaler shareholders would receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share. Based on Friday’s closing prices, the exchange ratio values Zscaler at $260.41 per share, a premium of roughly 34%. CrowdStrike shareholders would own approximately 84.1% of the combined company, with Zscaler shareholders holding the remaining 15.9%.
Zscaler founder and CEO Jay Chaudhry will continue to lead the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike’s board after the deal closes.
