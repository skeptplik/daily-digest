These are the stories making headlines in fashion on Wednesday.
Shein will pay $100 million to settle a Federal Trade Commission claim that it participated in unfair customer-service practices, with the funds going back to consumers. Regulators said the online retailer had frequent shipping problems, buried unfavorable feedback and made unsupported claims about where its goods and cotton come from. “The agreement does not constitute an admission of liability, and we continue to disagree with aspects of the FTC’s characterization of our business and compliance practices,” a Shein spokesperson told the Wall Street Journal. Meanwhile, the company cautioned that its European business may need roughly a year to regain footing after a new EU import fee caused a 27.6% drop in sales to $2.98 billion and a 6% slide in overall group revenue to $10.27 billion in Q3 results. {Wall Street Journal/paywalled; Reuters}
Abercrombie & Fitch lifted its full-year forecast after posting its 16th straight quarter of sales growth, sending its stock up nearly 20%. The company also reported record third quarter net sales of $1.4 billion, up 11%. The retailer now expects roughly 6% sales growth for the year and earnings of $13.70 to $13.90 per share, up from earlier projections. CEO Fran Horowitz wrote in a statement that strong profitability “allowed us to further invest in new stores, marketing and technology”. {Abercrombie & Fitch; WWD/paywalled}
Rod Little, president and CEO of Edgewell Personal Care Company, has been elected as Victoria’s Secret & Co.’s board chair. Little succeeds Donna James, who has led the board since the company became independent in 2021 and recently announced that she will not seek another term. “Donna has been an essential thought partner for me in designing our strategy and guiding the Company through a period of significant transformation. … I look forward to partnering with [Little] as Chair as we pursue the significant opportunities ahead,” Victoria’s Secret & Co. CEO Hillary Super said in a statement. {Victoria’s Secret & Co.}
The Paris-based luxury house has appointed Therese Lundström as chief marketing officer. She will be in charge of the brand’s worldwide marketing strategy. Lundström succeeds Sophie Condroyer and will report to CEO Catherine Jacquet. Lundström arrives from Courrèges, where she held the same title, and previously worked at Dries Van Noten and Givenchy. {WWD/paywalled}
