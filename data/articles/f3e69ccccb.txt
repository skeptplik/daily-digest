Whether you want to swim with pigs in The Bahamas or visit the rum distilleries of Jamaica, here’s how you can get to the Caribbean connection-free from Europe.
After a long, hot summer, you’d think we’d be embracing the cooler weather. And yet, as soon as the clocks go back and the seasonal depression hits, we start dreaming of our next escape in the sun.
With the exception of hurricane season, the Caribbean is a destination you can visit year-round for good weather, but it’s particularly popular among Europeans in the northern hemisphere’s winter months.
Put off by the thought of multiple connections? We’ve rounded up all the direct flights between Europe and the Caribbean, in order of most-to-least visited countries.
Dominican Republic
With 400 kilometres of beaches across the country, it would be very easy to treat the Dominican Republic as a fly-and-flop destination.
But beyond the coastline, you can go hiking through cave systems filled with Taino petroglyphs, try bird watching in the country’s tranquil lagoons, and party the night away during the elaborate annual carnival celebrations.
No wonder 8.9 million tourists chose to visit in 2025, according to UN Tourism.
You can fly direct to resort town Punta Cana from Madrid with Air Europa; from Paris Charles de Galle with Air France and Paris Orly with Air Caraïbes; from Zürich with Edelweiss Air; from Milan and Prague with Neos; from Munich and Frankfurt with Discover Airlines (as well as Condor for the latter); from Amsterdam with TUI Fly; and from London Gatwick with British Airways.
If you’d prefer to fly into the vibrant capital, you can do so from Madrid with Air Europa, Iberia and World2Fly; from Frankfurt with Condor; from Rome with ITA Airways; and from Paris Orly with Air Caraïbes.
For whale watching, you can fly to Samaná with Air Caraïbes from Paris Orly; for a taste of Cibao cuisine and to discover more about cigar making, you can fly to Santiago de los Caballeros from Madrid with Air Europa; and for a historical trip, fly to Puerto Plata direct from Frankfurt with Condor or from Zürich with Edelweiss Air.
Puerto Rico
The archipelago of Puerto Rico welcomed some 5.8 million people in 2025, up 4.7% on the year before, no doubt helped by Bad Bunny’s concert residency from July to September.
And with Puerto Rico hitting the headlines once again in 2026 thanks to this viral song, we imagine even more people are set to be “enchanted” in the coming months and years.
You can explore colonial history at the UNESCO-listed La Fortaleza, visit Taíno archaeological sites like the Caguana Indigenous Ceremonial Site, and discover the music scene in the cities (preferably with a piña colada, the national drink).
Iberia flies direct to San Juan from Madrid.
Jamaica
While tourism numbers took a dip in 2025 due to the impact of Hurricane Melissa, some 2.6 million people chose to visit Jamaica last year, with more expected in 2026.
Best known for its musical output as the birthplace of genres like reggae, ska and dancehall, Jamaica has plenty of live music venues and festivals, including carnival, held the week after Easter. Plus, if you’re a fan of rum, there are a number of estates you can visit for distillery tours.
Like the rest of the Caribbean, the coastline is fantastic, while the Blue and John Crow Mountains National Park offers hiking opportunities.
You can reach major cruise ship port and tourist destination Montego Bay direct from Düsseldorf and Frankfurt with Condor; from Amsterdam with TUI Fly; from London Heathrow with Virgin Atlantic; from Zürich with Edelweiss Air and from Milan with Neos. British Airways also serves the capital Kingston from London Heathrow.
The Bahamas
Spread out across 700 islands, each with picture-perfect blue waters and sandy coves, you could spend a lifetime exploring The Bahamas and still have more to see. Some 1.8 million people began, or continued, their exploration in 2025.
Naturally, diving is a big draw, but for those who want to stay closer to shore, you can also go swimming with the famous pigs of Big Major Cay.
From Europe, you can fly direct to Nassau, the nation’s capital, from London Heathrow with British Airways, or to San Salvador Island from Paris Orly with Air Caraïbes.
Barbados
If you’re picturing a stereotypical Caribbean escape, Barbados has you covered: Azure waters, white sands, coconut palms to frame your beach photos with, and plenty of rum.
But beyond the beaches, you’ll be able to wander around the UNESCO-listed Bridgetown, dig into culinary classics like Bajan fish cakes or rice and peas, and hike to Hackleton's Cliff in search of panoramic views over the island.
Last year, 1.5 million tourists visited Barbados, and you can become one of them by flying direct to Bridgetown from Manchester with Virgin Atlantic, from London Heathrow with Virgin Atlantic and British Airways, and from London Gatwick with British Airways; from Amsterdam with KLM; and from Düsseldorf and Frankfurt with Condor.
Aruba
A constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands, 1.5 million people visited Aruba in 2025 for its beautiful beaches and windsurfing.
You can fly direct from Amsterdam with KLM and TUI Fly.
Bonaire
Another part of the Kingdom of the Netherlands, Bonaire – which is a special municipality – covers 288 square kilometers and has a population of just 26,500.
Tourists flock here to dive among the coral reefs at Bonaire National Marine Park, and to see the flamingos at the Pekelmeer Flamingo Sanctuary.
You can fly direct from Amsterdam with KLM, TUI Fly and Corendon.
Curaçao
Completing the ABCs of the Caribbean, Curaçao is also a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands.
While it is, of course, known for its beaches, Willemstad, the capital, is UNESCO-listed for its colonial architecture and you can also see the wildlife while hiking in Christoffel National Park.
Given Curaçao captured the hearts of many during this year’s World Cup as the smallest nation to ever qualify, we imagine the tourist numbers will exceed the 790,000 visitors of 2025.
You can fly direct from Amsterdam with KLM, TUI Fly and Corendon; and from Düsseldorf with Corendon and TUI Fly.
Guadeloupe
For a taste of the French Caribbean instead, head to Guadeloupe.
An archipelago of six inhabited islands, the main two – Basse-Terre and Grande-Terre – look like a butterfly from above. The former is mountainous, with La Grande Soufrière, an active stratovolcano as its highest point at 1,467 metres above sea level, while the latter is known for its beaches, cliffs and mangroves.
The latest figures show 740,000 people visited in 2018. Become one of them by flying to Pointe-à-Pitre direct from Paris Charles de Gaulle with Air France, from Paris Orly with Air Caraïbes and Corsair, or from Nantes, Toulouse and Marseille with Corsair.
Martinique
Another overseas department of France, the entire island of Martinique was designated a UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve in 2021 for its biodiversity.
Naturally, hiking is incredibly popular here, particularly up to Mount Pelée, its highest point (and an active volcano…), but you can also visit its rum distilleries or simply relax by the beach.
In 2022, 560,000 people visited. You can fly direct from Paris Charles de Gaulle with Air France, from Paris Orly with Air Caraïbes and Corsair, from Bordeaux with Corsair; and from Frankfurt with Condor.
Saint Lucia
Instantly recognisable thanks to its UNESCO-listed Pitons, Saint Lucia is another great option for those seeking out a hiking holiday.
The 430,000 people who visited in 2025 no doubt also headed here for scuba diving and snorkelling, dolphin watching, and the sulphur springs, too.
British Airways flies here direct from London Gatwick.
Trinidad and Tobago
With just 370,000 tourists in 2025, Trinidad and Tobago is one of the lesser-visited Caribbean nations.
But if you do visit, you’ll be rewarded with incredible birdwatching, colourful carnival parties and superb hiking.
You can fly to Tobago (which has a more developed tourism industry) direct from Frankfurt with Condor; you can fly to the capital Port of Spain on Trinidad from Amsterdam with KLM or from London Gatwick with British Airways.
Antigua and Barbuda
Antigua and Barbuda is known for its beaches, with supposedly one for every day of the year on Antigua, while Barbuda boasts one of the best in the world (Princess Diana Beach) which has pale pink sands and no crowds.
If you’re looking for a more cultural holiday, you can explore the UNESCO-listed Nelson's Dockyard, or for a more active vacation, you can walk the trails of Wallings Nature Reserve.
Around 330,000 people visited Antigua and Barbuda in 2025. You can fly direct from London Heathrow with Virgin Atlantic, from London Gatwick with British Airways; and from Frankfurt with Condor.
Saint Martin/Sint Maarten
Spanning just 34 square kilometres, the island of Saint Martin is split in two, with the northern part being French while the southern part is a country in the Kingdom of the Netherlands.
Small but mighty, there’s plenty of activities at sea or on land including snorkelling, sailing and hiking. The island is perhaps best known for its planespotting opportunities as the runway is right next to Maho Beach.
You can fly direct to Sint Maarten from Amsterdam with KLM; from Paris Charles de Gaulle with Air France and from Paris Orly with Air Caraïbes.
