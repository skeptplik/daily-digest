WASHINGTON — The Trump administration on Monday said that the federal government will save $9.2 billion on drugs purchased through Medicare next year as a result of price negotiations, a net savings of 45% compared to what it would’ve paid last year had the negotiated prices been in effect.
This is the third time the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services has negotiated drug prices following the passage of the Inflation Reduction Act, but it’s the first time those drugs have included treatments administered in doctors’ offices, which are covered by Part B. Only retail drugs covered by Part D were eligible during the first two years.
The estimated $9.2 billion in savings does not account for discounts the drugmakers pay to the Part D Manufacturer Discount Program. That program was created by the same law that created the price negotiation program. If those discounts were included, the estimated savings would be about $5.5 billion, or 33% in lower net aggregate spending.
