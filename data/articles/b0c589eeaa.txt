GTA 6 players on PlayStation 5 have reported frequent crashes tied to error code CE-108255-1, and it looks like it's blocking them from progressing through the story while they wait for Sony and Rockstar to send out a fix.
Although Rockstar Games spent a decade making its new Grand Theft Auto game, it's no surprise to see the new highest-rated game of all time launching with at least a few hiccups. Some have it worse than others, though, with more than a few reports of crashes affecting PS5 consoles popping up online in the days following GTA 6's launch.
The most common cause seems to be tied to error code CE-108255-1, which, as defined by Sony, simply indicates that "an error occurred with the game/application." It seems that, although the majority of players aren't encountering the issue, those who have haven't been able to find a proper fix. Adding to the trouble is that those who have met these dreaded GTA 6 crashes are getting them pretty frequently, and what's triggering them isn't always clear. There are reports from players on both standard PS5 and PS5 Pro consoles running into these issues, with some even saying that crashes appear to be tied to the story, barring them from making any progress.
"I've noticed that the issue manifests differently for different players," one Reddit user said. "In my case, the game crashes on Jason's loading screen: it freezes and then displays the CE-108255-1 error code, meaning I can't even get into the game. Others manage to play for a few minutes before experiencing a crash. I really hope Sony and Rockstar work together to investigate and resolve this issue as soon as possible."
Game crashes are annoying for anyone, but when a massive game like GTA 6, which many feel has a less-than-generous autosave system, suddenly boots players back to the PS5 home screen without warning, it can be especially troublesome. Many who have reported the crashes to PlayStation Support say they've been directed to Rockstar, while those who report to Rockstar say they're being directed to PlayStation. One player said they feel like they're "stuck in an endless support loop, with a game I paid for and still can't play."
Meanwhile, those who have managed to get help say most assistance hasn't been able to completely resolve their issues. However, some have said they've had luck with a few tried-and-true methods, such as uninstalling and reinstalling the game, making sure both the PS5 and game have their most recent updates, and restoring licenses.
"I’ve done everything possible to solve this," another player said. "Deleted cache. Rebuilt database, deleted everything else from my console, took it apart, cleaned it, turned HDR off, switched to 1080p, went and updated game license, deleted and reinstalled and still crashing. Ugh. I’ve got a day 1 PS5 with a disc drive. Other friends of mine with the same console are playing with no issue."
Again, it's unclear if the crashing issue is tied specifically to the PS5 or GTA 6 itself. Until either entity comes up with an update, though, all affected players are stuck on launch weekend.
GTA 6 released for PS5 and Xbox Series X/S November 19 and, in its first 24 hours, made Rockstar $3.79 billion. It's the most successful launch in the history of entertainment, and although its exact unit sales have yet to be disclosed, parent company Take-Two Interactive was at least willing to share that it sold 32.5 million copies in pre-orders alone.
