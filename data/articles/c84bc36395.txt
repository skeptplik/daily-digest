U.S. House Republican moves to force vote on impeaching Pete Hegseth
Secretary of War says he is happy to litigate military actions of the administration 'all day long'
Republican Rep. Thomas Massie moved Monday to force a vote to impeach U.S. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth, putting pressure on his fellow Republicans to decide whether to remove one of the Trump administration's most prominent figures following sweeping election losses.
The impeachment resolution largely focuses on the war in Iran. It also includes references to Hegseth's role in carrying out military actions in Yemen and Venezuela.
Massie, who was defeated by a Trump-backed challenger in a primary earlier this year, said the secretary's actions amount to "high crimes and misdemeanours" and his leadership at the Pentagon "thus warrants impeachment."
A vote in the House could come by mid-week, putting pressure on Republicans — many of whom blamed their election wipeout on the Iran war — but next steps are highly uncertain.
House Speaker Mike Johnson had tried to prevent this outcome, and warned lawmakers against impeaching Hegseth while the U.S. is engaged in military actions in the Middle East.
"Impeaching a cabinet secretary right now, especially the Department of War in the middle of hostilities abroad would be a terrible, terrible look, a terrible thing for our allies and our enemies to see that," Johnson, a Republican, said as he opened the chamber.
"We don't impeach cabinet secretaries for political gain or for cheap tricks, and I think that's what this is and I think everybody knows it."
Hegseth defends actions
Speaking to reporters Monday afternoon, Massie said Hegseth has violated international norms and the U.S.'s own conventions of war in Iran.
"It's to the disadvantage and the dishonour of our country, and to the danger of our soldiers in future conflict," he said.
Hegseth said earlier Monday he's eager to litigate the military actions that he and U.S. President Donald Trump have taken against Iran, Yemen's Iranian-backed Houthi rebels and Venezuela's previous leaders.
"If you want to take issue with what we did in Epic Fury or Rough Rider or nabbing Nicolás Maduro, you're taking issue with the troops and what they accomplished, which is historic," Hegseth told reporters Monday, using the military code names for the Iran war and campaign of strikes to halt the Houthis' attacks on shipping in the Middle East.
Hegseth also announced that he would hold a rare news conference on Tuesday — his first in more than 180 days — on the impeachment threat. His last briefing was in May, and he has not held one on the Iran war, strikes on alleged drug-trafficking boats or other U.S. military actions since then.
The Pentagon chief is bearing the brunt of anger and calls for his ouster after the Iran war and its economic impact contributed to severe Republican losses in the midterm elections that cost the party control of Congress. Massie pushed forward an impeachment resolution over the objections of House Republican leadership as momentum grows to hold Hegseth accountable for the conflict.
Hegseth gave a State of the Force address at Marine Corps Base Quantico, Sept. 30, but has not held a media availablity since May. (Alex Wong/Pool/The Associated Press)
Hegseth waves off impeachment push as 'noise'
Hegseth, after giving a speech at the Naval War College in Rhode Island, brushed aside the possible impeachment push.
"There's a lot of politically motivated people who may have substantial policy disagreements, but their disagreements are with success," he told reporters. He added that "if we want to litigate" the military actions that the Trump administration has taken, "I'm happy to do it all day long."
"My team, we're not worried about the noise — it's what I told the troops, too — don't worry about the noise," he said.
Asked about U.S. Vice-President JD Vance blaming the Iran war for the massive Republican losses in the election, Hegseth noted that Trump said he would do it again, and "I agree."
He largely repeated a line that Secretary of State Marco Rubio said to reporters a day earlier: "It doesn't have to be necessarily popular, but it can be necessary."
Hegseth argued that the war was necessary because "someone had to confront the Iranian regime's pursuit of a nuclear weapon" and said "the choice was don't act and wish it away or take a bold move."
Iran has long maintained that its nuclear program is peaceful. According to the International Atomic Energy Agency, Iran has 440.9 kilograms of uranium that is enriched up to 60 per cent purity, a short, technical step from weapons-grade levels of 90 per cent.
Trump defended Hegseth to reporters on Sunday night.
"He's guilty of two things, basically winning two wars," Trump said. "I believe we saved the Middle East and Israel combined. And it's awfully hard to say let's impeach somebody who did that."
On Monday, he called Massie a "stone-cold loser" and said he would stand by Hegseth even if he is impeached.
Hegseth insists 'Iran is devastated'
Hegseth reiterated that "Iran is devastated," even though the Islamic Republic has been able to keep striking military bases in the Middle East that are used by the United States, causing billions of dollars in damage, as well as commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz.
Disruptions to shipping in the waterway, where one-fifth of the world's oil passed before the war, have led to higher energy prices that hit voters' pocketbooks.
He said the next steps on the war are up to Trump, including telling Iran: "Hey, we're done, and we're going to watch your nuclear material. You better not move it."
With anger in Congress rising, Hegseth has been on a social media blitz, posting a video last week acknowledging the threat of impeachment. He said it would be "all about the politics of personal destruction" and insisted that "won't work with me. You can rest assured of that."
Since then, scores of political appointees within the Pentagon have posted images of Hegseth emblazoned with the words, "Stand. Fight. Defend the Republic." The White House also posted a video to social media that featured praise from Trump.
