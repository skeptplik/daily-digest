In short:
Supercars teams have shared a mix of opinions over the new Adelaide track design they will race on next year.
After a long history, the track will undergo major changes to accommodate the controversial MotoGP circuit which will debut in Adelaide next year.
What's next?
Both events will occur in November 2027.
Every year Supercars teams count down the days to racing on the Adelaide Parklands Circuit, and some say this year's event is tinged with a touch of sadness.
Racing gets underway today with a 100-kilometre race, followed by 250-kilometre races on both Saturday and Sunday to crown this year's title winner.
Currently Matt Payne leads, with Brodie Kostecki, Broc Feeney and Chaz Mostert still in contention.
It is the last time the drivers will race around the street circuit, a shortened version of the track unveiled in 1985 to host the Formula 1 Australian Grand Prix, before the race was moved to Victoria in 1995.
The track made a comeback in 1999 for the newly created Adelaide 500 event up until a brief COVID hiatus, and a 2022 revival brought on by the Malinauskas election win.
Spectators and drivers alike have said they love the current circuit, with its tight corners and compact walls which make for exciting racing.
But in 2027, the Supercars race, now known as the Adelaide Grand Final, will move to the soon-to-be-built and controversial MotoGP track in the eastern parklands — with both the events scheduled for November next year.
The new track has some teams worried, including Ryan Walkinshaw, director of Walkinshaw TWG Racing.
"Is it going to dilute the value that we currently have in this current event? That's entirely a possibility,"
he said.
"It is perfect for Supercars, so the fact that's going to change I do have some concerns about."
Tom Moore, team manager for Dick Johnson Racing said he also shared some of those concerns.
"This circuit in particular will lose some of its raw edge that makes Adelaide different to everyone, but there will be some advantages," he said.
Triple Eight Race Engineering Team Manager Mark Dutton had a more positive view.
"As much as we all love this track, change is awesome, we'll all come here next year and we'll all adapt,"
he said.
Drivers were consulted about the design of the Supercars track and Ryan Wood said he was ready to embrace it.
He said he could see the benefits of a track that would have greater flow with the race walls being further back.
"Probably a bit more flowing than what the track's like at the moment, but I tell you what, it's always exciting to have a new track on the calendar, hopefully the locals can get around it," he said.
While racing over the next three days, the drivers will also spend some time reminiscing.
Current series leader Matt Payne said turn eight on the current track was not your average stretch of bitumen.
"I've lost the championship at turn eight and I've won one the next year in Supercar twos," he said.
Brodie Kostecki was more blunt when asked if he would miss the notorious spot.
"Not really," he said, with a chuckle.
