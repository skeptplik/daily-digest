The Russian government announced Friday that it will extend its ban on jet fuel exports until the end of March 2027 as the domestic energy market continues to feel the strain from Ukrainian strikes on oil refineries.
Russia first banned jet fuel exports in June following Ukrainian drone attacks on refineries that led to slumps in domestic production and localized shortages. That embargo was set to expire on Monday, Nov. 30.
The extension of the ban runs from Tuesday, Dec. 1 until March 31, 2027.
Exceptions are made for international intergovernmental agreements, as well as humanitarian aid shipments and supplies to the Russian military, Baikonur spaceport in Kazakhstan and Russian operations in the Svalbard archipelago off Norway.
Three additional exemptions are marked “for official use” in the published decree signed by Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin.
Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak acknowledged earlier this week that the situation in Russia’s domestic fuel market remains “quite tense” due to ongoing repairs of several major oil refineries.
“There’s definitely a deficit, which is being covered by external supplies,” Novak told reporters on Wednesday, adding that fuel is being imported from both neighboring and distant countries.
He said authorities will decide next month whether to continue importing gasoline in 2027 and extend current gasoline export restrictions, which expire in January.
“It will depend on the ability to process [fuel] and supply the domestic market,” Novak said.
