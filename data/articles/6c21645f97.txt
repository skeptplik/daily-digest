Victoria's state election could deal a blow to the Prime Minister's Labor party that has been ruling the region for 12 years, in favour of the country's conservatives.
Australia's conservatives hope for victory as polls closed on Saturday for Victoria's state election in the region of Melbourne, the country's second-largest city.
The election was set to deal a potential blow to Prime Minister Anthony Albanese's centre-left Labor party, which has been ruling the "Garden State" region for 12 years.
Recent opinion polls projected the Labour to be widely ousted by the state Liberal Party leader Jess Wilson, a 36-year-old first-term lawmaker who has vowed to restore fiscal discipline in the deeply indebted state.
If Wilson manages to unseat Labor incumbent Ben Carroll, she would become the first Liberal woman to take office as premier of the state — considered "Australia's toughest job" by the country's business newspaper, the Australian Financial Review.
Her main pledge is to reduce Victoria's debt — the highest in the country — through a total of Aus$20 billion (€12.2 billion) measures, including a controversial plan to privatise the sewerage system.
Labor defeat could be trendsetting
"I know there are a lot of polls, there are a lot of headlines, but this election is going to be closer than what people think," Wilson told Channel 7, one of Australia's major TV networks.
If Labor loses in Victoria, which is seen as a pacesetting state, it would pile even more pressure on Australia's PM, who has seen his approval ratings tumble to record lows due to discontent with the rising cost of living.
The country's central bank was again forced to raise interest rates to an 18-year high, citing the prolonged war in the Middle East and frequent inflationary pressures.
Far-right set to take seats in state parliament
The far-right anti-immigration One Nation party, led by Pauline Hanson, could pick up some seats in the state's parliament for the first time, according to polls.
Marginalised for years, One Nation has lately become one of Australia's most popular political parties, which could play a kingmaker role in the next Victoria government.
"We know that Labor's out of the state. Finished," Hanson told media, pre-emptively taking credit for Labor's defeat.
"They won't be in government, and that's where One Nation has played a big part in this," she said. "The Liberals could not have won it by themselves. No way in the wide world."
