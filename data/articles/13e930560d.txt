Amazon’s founder and billionaire chairman Jeff Bezos has said he was “heavily involved” in the decision to cast Robert Pattinson as the next James Bond.
Amazon paid more than $1bn for “creative control” of the Bond franchise last year, striking a deal with the longtime heirs and stewards of the films, shocking fans who worried that Bezos’ takeover would signal an end to the franchise as they knew it.
Bezos said he felt there was “something special” about Pattinson, revealing during an appearance at Semafor’s Silicon Valley & The World event that he reviewed several rounds of auditions.
“There was a big group initially, and we down-selected to five finalists. We did real, old-fashioned screen tests of those five finalists,” he said. “We did three different scenes with each of the five finalists, and then we got together on Friday to spend a couple of hours looking at all the tests and reviewing all that.”
He added that he and the film’s director, Dune’s Denis Villeneuve, were initially biased against Pattinson, seeking an unknown talent. “But in the screen tests, it was so obvious he is James Bond,” he said.
Pattinson, 40, made his big-screen debut in Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire in 2005 and has gone on to star in films including The Twilight Saga, The Lost City of Z and Tenet.
Bezos’ Amazon acquired the MGM studio in an $8.5bn purchase in 2022. Bond fans and film critics have speculated over where Amazon will take the Bond franchise, with industry experts speculating the company may seek to expand the films into a Marvel-style universe of spinoffs.
Amazon has grown into a massive conglomerate in recent years, which includes not only an entertainment arm, but also computing and the company’s original business of online sales. Bezos also separately founded the space exploration company Blue Origin.
Amazon’s surveillance technology has come under criticism as a Guardian investigation this week unveiled that Israel was using its facial recognition tool to surveil Palestinians as part of a larger project to move its data on to Amazon servers.
