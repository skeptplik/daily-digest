LONDON — The Fashion Awards is set to bring the global fashion industry together next Monday at London’s Royal Albert Hall, but for Laura Weir, chief executive officer at the fundraising night’s organizer the British Fashion Council, the event’s mission goes beyond celebration. It is a chance to invest in the talent shaping fashion’s future.
“Ultimately, that’s what makes the evening so meaningful: bringing our industry together to celebrate its achievements, recognize its cultural contribution and actively invest in the talent that will shape its future,” Weir told WWD.
She highlighted that last year’s event raised a record amount for the BFC Foundation, which supports British talent and creates opportunities across the industry.
Weir also revealed that the charitable organization has made progress toward its ambition to triple the number of scholarships it provides, and plans to reveal a new scholarship during this year’s ceremony, which will be hosted by former “Doctor Who” star Ncuti Gatwa.
The awards will also reflect fashion’s connections with other creative fields. “This year, we’re looking forward to celebrating fashion’s relationship with music, film, sport and the performing arts,” Weir added.
The Costume Designer of the Year Award, now in its second year, recognizes the role costume plays in bringing characters and stories to life on screen.
The honor goes to Ellen Mirojnick this year for her work on Christopher Nolan’s “The Odyssey.” The New York-born designer has worked across film and television, including “Oppenheimer,” “Fatal Attraction” and the first season of “Bridgerton.” For “The Odyssey,” her team created more than 5,300 bespoke costumes.
Among the evening’s honorees is Yohji Yamamoto, who will receive the Outstanding Achievement Award. The Japanese designer launched his label in Tokyo in 1972 and is being recognized for his distinctive design language, masterful tailoring, and radical reimagining of womenswear. His work extends from his namesake label to Y-3, his collaboration with Adidas, and costume design for film and opera.
“Few designers have changed the way we think about clothes as profoundly as Yamamoto,” Weir said when the award was announced. “His work has always challenged convention while remaining deeply rooted in craft, precision and an uncompromising creative vision.”
Awar Odhiang will receive Model of the Year after a year of runway, editorial and campaign work. Born in a refugee camp in Ethiopia and raised in Canada, she closed Matthieu Blazy’s debut show for Chanel, has appeared on magazine covers worldwide and fronted campaigns for Chanel, Chloé, Louis Vuitton, Saint Laurent, and Loro Piana.
Odhiang has also used her platform to speak about barriers facing Black models and the importance of representation. “You never have to dim your light to belong, nor lose your softness to prove your strength. I hope my journey continues to remind others of that,” she said.
The Designer of the Year race will be another focus of the ceremony.
The nominees are Ashley and Mary-Kate Olsen for The Row; Anthony Vaccarello for Saint Laurent; Haider Ackermann for Tom Ford; Matthieu Blazy for Chanel; Michael Rider for Celine, and Sarah Burton for Givenchy.
Sandra Choi will receive a Special Recognition Award.
The recognition aims to spotlight Choi’s three-decade run at Jimmy Choo, where she helped transform the label from a red carpet favorite into a global luxury player. Capri Holdings chairman and chief executive officer John Idol has previously projected Jimmy Choo as an $800 million business “over the next few years.”
Gabriel Moses, the British-Nigerian photographer, filmmaker and creative director from South London, will receive the Trailblazer Award. Stylist, director and brand consultant Joe McKenna is set to be honored with the Isabella Blow Award for Fashion Creator.
The British womenswear nominees include Clare Waight Keller for Uniqlo, Erdem Moralıoğlu for Erdem, Louise Trotter for Bottega Veneta, Burton for Givenchy, and Simone Rocha.
The menswear shortlist features Foday Dumbuya for Labrum, Kiko Kostadinov, Nick Wakeman for Studio Nicholson, Simon Holloway for Dunhill and Rocha. The Vanguard Award, which spotlights emerging talent, is being contested by Ellie Misner, Steve O Smith, Tolu Coker and Conner Ives.
As reported, the Fashion Awards is the primary fundraiser for the BFC Foundation, which was created in 2019 for charitable purposes. Its aim is to give grants to students and attract, develop, and retain talent through education and business mentoring.
The BFC Foundation offers support to designers through four talent prizes, such as the BFC/Vogue Designer Fashion Fund; the BFC/GQ Designer Fashion Fund; the BFC Newgen, and the BFC Fashion Trust. It also offers BA and MA scholarships to students, as well as links with industry through design competitions and Graduate Preview Day.
