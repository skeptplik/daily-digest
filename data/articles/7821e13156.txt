I am one of the messiest people I know, and I come by that reputation honestly. Legend has it that when my parents first met at a party at my mom’s apartment, my dad walked up to her to say that she should probably call the police; he’d passed her bedroom while in search of the bathroom and seen the window wrenched open, with belongings strewn all over the floor. He suggested that she report a robbery—but no, she was forced to admit; that was just how she lived.
I’ve inherited her struggles with neatness and attention to detail. While other mother-daughter duos brought in perfectly stenciled posters and Balthazar-quality brownies to class presentations and bake sales, our attempts were somewhat more haphazard, typically following screaming fights in the aisles of the Staples on 81st and Broadway about what qualified as “nice” graph paper.
When it comes to Christmas, my mom and I have always been aligned on caring more about gathering the people we love to drink mulled wine and watch A Diva’s Christmas Carol for the hundredth time than creating some holiday tableau fit for Instagram. Yet this holiday season finds me in a new place in my life. Since going through a major breakup last year, I’ve tackled all kinds of things that I didn’t previously know I could do, from fixing my hyperactive car battery without a man’s paid-for help to retiling my very own bathroom floor. Was it possible that I could also become the kind of person who looked forward to—or at least didn’t totally dread—my very least favorite Christmas chore, wrapping presents?
I love shopping, I love seeing someone open what I’ve bought for them, and when I was a kid, I loved carrying already-wrapped gifts down the stairs and arranging them under the tree. But there’s something about the finicky, frilly act of wrapping and taping and bow-tying that makes me revert to that chaotic middle-schooler with the slapped-together bake sale contribution; I hate it, but I also hate hating it.
So, a few weeks ago, I decided to break the cycle. I contacted It’s A Wrap, a gift-wrapping studio and floral boutique in Studio City, Los Angeles, to ask one simple question: Could a Christmas flop like me really be taught to make a gift look great?
Y Lane, who co-owns It’s A Wrap with partner Carolyn Haywood, has seen it all when it comes to outlandishly LA-coded gifting requests. (Ask her about the time she gift-wrapped a flat-screen TV for a guy’s Playboy Bunny girlfriend on Christmas Day.) But her patience as she walked me through the basics of wrapping—assembling a box; figuring out how much tape to use; creating clean, crease-free corners—was still amazing, given that I’ve had not one but two driving instructors quit on me and, for all I know, made any number of math and science tutors seek workers’ compensation.
I’m far from Y Lane’s first student; she offers gift-wrapping lessons semi-regularly at It’s A Wrap’s cozy, Santa’s-workshop-reminiscent storefront off Tujunga Avenue (that is, if Santa’s workshop were regularly passed by shirtless bros and Alo-clad beach babes sipping iced lattes from Aroma down the street). Demand predictably increases during the holiday season, which, for her, starts well before Halloween and stretches until the beginning of January.
I got the parts with the box and the paper pretty quickly, but once we got into the specifics of bow-tying, my mind went blank. I couldn’t tie a bow in my ponytail for school spirit day or French-braid the other girls’ hair at camp to save my life, so why would I be able to now?
Slowly but surely, though, my efforts came together into a sort-of bow, which Y Lane complimented and then promptly untied, instructing me to try a few more. “The nicer the box is, the easier it is to wrap,” Y Lane told me. “The most important word in wrapping is measure.”
A few weeks later, I have a stack of neatly wrapped gifts piled up under my little pink tree impressively early. And who’s to say? Maybe I’ll also become the person who saves the wrapping paper for next year.
