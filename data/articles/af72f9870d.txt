Logging off is en vogue. From touting analog bags to playing vinyl records, many of us are tired of living life behind a screen.
So 2026 may be the ideal year to gift some low-tech entertainment instead of the newest iPhone. These days, we’re all trying to connect with the “real world” more, and the latest string of tech outages has been a good nudge to buy stuff that won’t glitch.
We’ve experimented with screen alternatives such as MP3 players and “dating cards” throughout the year, sharing the ups – and some downs – in our series Do not disturb.
Here are 11 picks that would make perfect gifts, from the beautiful (displayable board games) to the practical (daily planners).
- Visit our ultimate holiday gift guide for obsessively vetted gift ideas, or get inspired by our full array of guides, including a gift guide for the person who has everything and a guide to cozy gifts.
For the introspective introvert:
No Particular Order Volume One Journal
Stationery brand No Particular Order’s guided journal is the ideal companion for self-discovery. It has 200 journal prompts for your recipient to fill out, including “Who’s in your circle?” and “Who or what do you hope to be in a future life?”. I used this the summer after finishing my undergraduate degree to stave off feelings of existential dread.
For the hardcover truther: 
Book of the Month subscription
My Book of the Month subscription sends a newly released novel to my apartment for under $20 per month. While I’m a Kindle girl at heart, it comes in handy when I’m craving the weight of a physical novel. There’s something fun about waiting for the announcement of each month’s selections. Most recently, I grabbed John Green’s new book, Hollywood, Ending.
For the flirt: 
No Particular Order Acquaintance Cards
Last winter, I tried No Particular Order’s dating app alternative, acquaintance cards. Inspired by Victorian-era calling cards, you fill out your information and hand it to someone who catches your eye. These cards helped me get someone’s phone number at a bar – yes, in 2026! Instead of asking your giftee when you can expect a grandchild, hand them these.
For the performative chess player: 
Papier Chess Set
This solid wood chess set is too pretty to hide in a dusty cabinet. Its colorful mosaic box and squiggly board are built to be shown off; the Filter’s commissioning editor, Karen Yuan, has it displayed on her living room coffee table. “Do I play chess? Not in years. Will I leave the box out just for compliments? Yes, shamelessly.” Gift this to the former captain of their school’s chess team or someone who’s always wanted to learn.
For the amateur artist:
Emily Lex Studios Watercolor book
I love watercoloring, but that doesn’t mean I’m producing paintings worthy of an art gallery. But I enjoyed the guided watercolor book from Emily Lex Studio, which has lightly outlined pencil drawings and simple instructions for painting over them. This is the present for someone who needs a winter evening activity that doesn’t involve scrolling on social media. While I tried the holiday theme, the Woodland and Animals workbook looks delightful too.
For a puzzler with patience:
Piecework Jigsaw Puzzle
It turns out I do have the patience for a 1,000-piece puzzle, as long as it looks as pretty as the eclectic ones from Piecework. This past summer, I waxed poetic about the brand’s Happy Camper puzzle: “Whenever I worked on it, I lost track of time.” The brand has plenty of festive options, including Tinseltown and Peppermint Parade. It has also branched out to other tabletop games, including Scrabble and Yahtzee.
For the oversleeper: 
Arne Jacobsen Alarm Clock
Last winter, I asked experts who study digital detoxing about how to reduce screen time. One of their tips? Leave your phone outside your bedroom. Catherine Price, the author of How to Break Up with Your Phone, says her husband uses this clock from Arne Jacobsen. And, like your iPhone, you can snooze the alarm as many times as you’d like.
For the precocious planner: 
Papier Hardcover Daily Planner
Whether gifting for a stressed student or workaholic loved one, you can’t go wrong with a planner. Like most things at Papier, this version marries function with an aesthetically pleasing design that they’ll want to show off on their desk. I wrote about how I use mine to stay organized – including reminding me to send a mega-important email I had forgotten about that day.
For the wannabe hipster:
Olympus Muji Film Camera
I’ve owned my secondhand Olympus film camera for four years and use it to snap candids of friends and family. It’s always fun to get photos developed, even if the older adults in my life can’t fathom why I would opt for it over the convenience of my iPhone’s camera. Gift this for someone who loves snapping photos and insists that everything looks better on film.
You can grab one secondhand at Mercari, eBay or Etsy.
For the foodie: 
Mi Cocina Cookbook
There’s nothing more annoying than trying to follow a recipe from your phone, getting food on your screen and squinting to see the measurements. A cookbook is an alternative for everyone from experienced cooks to amateur bakers. Recently, Filter contributor and Guardian senior editor Danielle Renwick interviewed Mexican chef Ricky Martínez, who wrote Mi Cocina, which is chock full of recipes such as sweet corn tamales and carne asada. Pair it with this sculptural book stand for extra brownie points.
