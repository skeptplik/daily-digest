Spoilers follow for The Rings of Power Season 3, Episodes 7-8.
The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3 finale, titled “The Doom of Men,” closes out a season that rejuvenated the flailing series with the promise that Sauron’s worst is yet to come in the just announced Season 4. For a season that was all about the One Ring and the anticipation of an epic battle between good and evil, “The Doom of Men” ends with Sauron proving that guile remains his most potent weapon against his adversaries.
The season’s penultimate episode (“Orodruin”) saw Sauron finally succeed in forging the One Ring with the aid of the Balrog (voiced by Simon Pegg). While it was intriguing to see the devil make a deal with another devil, Pegg's voice was so manipulated that any ol’ actor could’ve played him. Episode 7 also saw Míriel turn down Elendil and go through with marrying Pharazôn, thus uniting the Númenóreans. Meanwhile, Durin and his family are banished from Khazad-dûm but make off with one of the rings given to the Dwarf-lords. That’s the last we’ll see of their storyline until next season as the Dwarves sat out the season finale.
“The Doom of Men” finally resolves the Gandalf/Dark Wizard storyline with Pallando sacrificing himself to save Gandalf in a classic Heel-Face Turn ending in redemptive death. Gandalf freed his old friend from the corrupting influence of the ring, which Pallando had apparently worn for all of an evening before Gandalf tricked him out of it. (Pallando wore the ring just long enough for the show to make an allusion to Gollum’s “my precious” line.)
While the wizards vs. Nazgnagôl battle felt awkwardly executed with all its obvious wire-work, at least it provided Gandalf and Pallando a few moments to finally fight side-by-side as allies. I’m not sure, though, if the Pallando storyline was ultimately worth taking up so much dramatic real estate over the last two seasons for such a brisk payoff. At the very least, Pallando’s death and the resulting liberation of his followers robbed Sauron of a good chunk of his army.
I enjoyed how Sauron tested Gandalf’s incorruptibility in a very Last Temptation of Christ manner, offering him the rule of Middle-earth if he’d just submit. I also liked Sauron’s short-lived ruse of conjuring a figment of Nori Brandyfoot (Markella Kavenagh in a cameo) in an attempt to dupe Gandalf, although I never believed that the show was bringing back Nori in any real way.
Elrond evoking his brother to help instill some hope in the Númenórean soldiers on the eve of their likely deaths was a nice touch. Despite a moment where he allows himself to ask the advice of Galadriel and Celeborn – whose relationship woes I haven’t found particularly interesting thus far – Pharazôn’s hubris ultimately gets the better of him. The Elves foresee the Doom of Men when Sauron surrenders so I fully expect Season 4 to end with The Fall of Númenor.
