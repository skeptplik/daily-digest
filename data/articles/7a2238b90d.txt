In Les Kiss's words, the Wallabies "earned their corn" with their gritty 29-26 Nations Championship win over Wales in Cardiff.
After ending a four-match losing streak, the Wallabies coach struck a relieved but proud figure when he and captain Harry Wilson fronted the post-match media conference.
It couldn't be described as a polished display on the field, nor was there a moment during the match where it seemed the Wallabies were dominating their opposition.
Each time they opened up an encouraging lead on the scoreboard, it never felt as though Wales was too far away from clawing its way back into the contest.
But winning breeds confidence, which had been in short supply for the Wallabies following losses to New Zealand (twice), England and Scotland.
"They had to work hard for it and I think tonight was a true Test match [and] they earned their corn and got what they deserved," Kiss said.
An improved defensive effort was among the positives in the Wallabies' performance.
While three of the four tries they conceded came from driving mauls off line-outs, they denied Wales more than once with their scramble defence deep inside the 22.
"When we do it [defence] well, it's a real point of difference for us," Wilson said.
"I thought our D-zone [inside 22] defence tonight was unbelievable."
The Wallabies' aerial game in attack also showed glimpses of its potential, with Max Jorgensen's second-half try an example of the pressure they can apply to opposition teams.
The line-out was strong (they enjoyed a clean sheet on their throw), highlighted by Josh Canham's clutch steal when Wales was camped inside the Wallabies' 22 during the first half.
But the Wallabies let themselves down with poor discipline, especially inside their territory.
They conceded nine penalties in the opening 40 minutes, seven of which were on their side of the halfway line.
By full-time, the Wallabies had given 11 penalties away inside their half, with Wilson shown a yellow card in the 77th minute for one of those indiscretions.
If the Wallabies do the same against Ireland at Twickenham next weekend, they won't be shown any leniency.
Kiss admits the Wallabies' penalty count gave him "the shits".
"Discipline was a little bit disappointing, I must say … it kept them [Wales] in the game with field position and a few things like that," he said.
"So we're going to have to look at that as well."
Ikitau to the fore
Among individual performances, Carter Gordon looked assured at fly half.
The Wallabies were still pedestrian at times with ball in hand, however Gordon did enough to indicate he's the most suitable candidate to wear the number 10 jersey.
Len Ikitau was deservedly named player of the match, again proving he's a world-class operator.
But the question remains whether he's better suited to inside or outside centre.
When the Wallabies pulled off their upset 42-38 win over the world champion Springboks in Perth in September, his performance at outside was superb until he left the field injured.
On that occasion, he was partnered in the centres by Hunter Paisami, who is unavailable for the Wallabies' Nations Championship campaign in Great Britain.
Ikitau played inside centre against Wales, with Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii admirably slotting in at 13.
A permanent Ikitau-Paisami midfield combination may be the Wallabies' best option at next year's Rugby World Cup, although Suaalii's stocks at outside centre rose in Cardiff, even though he appears more comfortable on the wing.
For now, the Wallabies will turn their focus to the Ireland Test, which pits the two third-ranked teams from the respective Nations Championship groups against each other.
Their spirits will be buoyed by halting their losing streak, but Kiss knows the pressure of Test rugby never lets up.
"Test match footy doesn't hand you things easy," Kiss said.
