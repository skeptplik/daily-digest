I often find myself thinking where the best place to survive a zombie apocalypse would be. A walled-off stone house would certainly make for some good defenses, a boat would be great—but only if the zombies didn't pull a Fear the Walking Dead—or you could spend the end of the world in a camper van. That would certainly be whimsical.
You do just this in The Road of Dust and Sorrow's demo, although it's certainly not as whimsical as I first imagined it to be. You see, in this 2D horror game you have bigger responsibilities than just keeping yourself alive, you also have a young daughter to look out for as well.
This isn't an easy task. Supplies are dwindling, zombie-like humans infected by addictive chemicals amble across the land in search of more dust, but they'll settle for your blood instead. Plus there's this creepy Pastor who's definitely up to something very bad. I only got one glimpse of him in the demo and he was surrounded by a lot of gore and eldritch horror, so I've just decided that staying away from him is probably for the best.
But you can't hunker down and forget about all these terrors, because you have a daughter to feed and protect. So I spent most of my time in the demo gathering food, medical supplies, and trying to locate an item that may just grant us safe passage to the only sanctuary humanity has left.
The dev, Painted Black Games, says it took inspiration from horror series like Silent Hill and Resident Evil for The Road of Dust and Sorrow, and while I haven't come across anything particularly traumatic in a Silent Hill sense yet (although I wouldn't be surprised to see that here) I can see the through line to Resident Evil.
The action is smooth, accurate, and rewarding—which surprised me as I usually don't vibe with 2D gunplay, sometimes the controls are too finicky, but it certainly works here. Head-shotting the zombies is incredibly satisfying, as is kicking them to the floor and beating them with a baseball bat.
Then there are the puzzles. I didn't come across too many, but the ones I did find were very rewarding. It's not rocket science, but the solutions aren't always obvious either. They are, however, very intuitive; you can do a lot with a little, something I very much appreciate.

But my favourite aspect of The Road of Dust and Sorrow has to be just how cool this post-apocalyptic world is. Maybe it's the pixel art, but it manages to look dilapidated and creepy while also being oddly pretty to look at. I was searching an abandoned warehouse full of zombies and rotting cargo while thinking to myself that with a bit of elbow grease this place could really scrub up.
If you want pretty and cosy, though, the campervan is where it's at. I didn't spend as much time as I would have liked to there, but it managed to feel like a real home—the only safe place left in this world.
