Late-night hosts reacted to the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as CEO of Nvidia under political pressure from Donald Trump.
Jimmy Kimmel
“Who doesn’t love a good comeback story?” said Jimmy Kimmel on Monday evening, just a day after the board of Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang under pressure from Donald Trump to reinstall his friend as head of the trillion-dollar computer chip company. Huang was ousted just two weeks ago after it was revealed that he had multiple affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy and two with Nvidia subordinates.
“After 10 grueling days of forced unemployment, the board at Nvidia today voted to reinstate their former CEO, who, according to our president, did nothing wrong,” Kimmel explained. “All this guy did was cheat on his wife with two employees and a Chinese spy. Who hasn’t done that?”
The reinstatement included an assurance from the Nvidia board that they have “every confidence” in Huang to lead the company. “Which, of course, had nothing to do with the pressure they got from our Silicon man-in-chief, who has at least 62m reasons why he wanted his pal in charge,” said Kimmel, referring to Trump’s $62m reported Nvidia stock worth. “After the board made it clear that they were unequivocally moving on, Trump called them personally to harangue them. And if you think that sounds fishy, it’s not. It’s called being a good friend.”
“There’s nothing unusual about a president who owns a huge amount of stock in a company putting his little carrot thumb on the scale in a way that immediately boosts that company’s stock price,” Kimmel mocked. “I’m sure it also didn’t hurt that Huang donated $25m to the big, stupid ballroom he’s building.”
“And you think he would be ashamed of this! Not at all.” Trump wrote on Truth Social that the Nvidia board “made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO” and that the US government has “no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to National Security”.
“Says the man who stashed classified documents in the toilet at his country club,” Kimmel quipped.
“And if all this insanity wasn’t enough,” Trump went on to post an AI-doctored photo of his own 2024 assassination attempt, with Huang holding up a red bra. “What a profoundly stupid time to be alive,” said Kimmel.
Seth Meyers
And on Late Night, Seth Meyers also responded to the latest development in the Nvidia saga. “So Huang had an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, and his company possibly broke the law and allowed China to get its hand on their highly sought-after computer chips,” he said. “And yet, throughout all of that, the president’s main concern was empathy – not for American workers impacted by AI, but for his good friend Jensen.”
Speaking to reporters this weekend, Trump claimed that he asked Huang, “Did you have any idea she was a spy? And he said: ‘I had no idea that she was a spy.’”
“Yeah, that’s how spying works!” Meyers laughed. “They don’t wear shirts that say ‘ask me about being a spy.’”
“Trump is supposed to be the America first guy, the guy who says only he can take on China,” he continued. “Why would he excuse potentially illegal behavior of a tech CEO who might have harmed America's national security? Is there some other possible explanation here? And does it perhaps involve lots of money? Not for us, but for him.” Meyers then played several news clips reporting on Trump’s $62m in Nvidia stock, as well as the company’s ties to businesses run by his son Donald Trump Jr.
“Tech oligarchs consider us expendable,” Meyers concluded. “We can be replaced by AI. But the second they lose a job or face consequences for their actions, Trump will swoop in and save them.”
