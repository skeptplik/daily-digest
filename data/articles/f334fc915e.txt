Iran's foreign ministry condemned the US naval blockade as "military aggression" on Monday and said Tehran would use "every means" to counter it, a day after US President Donald Trump threatened to sink Iranian tankers attempting to breach it.
Spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei, speaking at his weekly press briefing, described the blockade as a violation of the UN Charter and an "act of crime," adding that Tehran considered itself entitled to use any means to defend itself.
"We will certainly not tolerate this situation and will use every means to counter US aggression," Baghaei said.
On Sunday, Trump warned that Iranian ships attempting to break the blockade would be sent to the "bottom of the sea."
Asked about the possibility by a Fox News reporter, he said: "I hope they do it; they'll all go down to the bottom of the sea, just like the rest of their navy."
Convoy uncertainty
Media outlets and figures close to the IRGC have called in recent days for a convoy of Iranian oil tankers to be dispatched to break the blockade, with some threatening that Gulf oil facilities would be targeted if the vessels came under attack. Iranian officials have said they have no concrete plan to do so.
IRGC-affiliated Tasnim news agency published an article on Sunday acknowledging the wave of reports, and said no competent or official authority in the armed forces, the Supreme National Security Council or the government had announced such a plan.
Tasnim said the reports stemmed entirely from proposals put forward by individuals in the media and on social media.
The news agency added that "Iran's plans to break the blockade are certainly being drawn up," but that no one outside the circle of security and military decision-makers had knowledge of them.
The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) confirmed on Sunday at least one incident involving an oil tanker in the Gulf of Oman, saying military forces had been involved in the encounter.
No further details were released about the identity of the forces involved or whether the tanker had been attempting to circumvent the blockade. The US Central Command (CENTCOM) had not commented on the incident as of Monday.
Also at Monday's briefing, Baghaei was asked how Tehran might respond to reported US planning for a possible attack on Iran.
"The best scenario is for them to get the hell out of the region," he said, adding that IRGC Air Force commander Majid Mousavi would deliver Iran's response to any potential attack "in a neat and elegant package."
Mousavi said on Sunday that IRGC aerospace capabilities had grown since the previous round of conflict and that "the field is ours and we determine the battlefield."
IRGC deputy commander-in-chief Major General Mostafa Izadi said Iran's missile range had been significantly upgraded and that any threat would draw an immediate response "without geographical limitations."
Azizi, chairman of parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Committee, reiterated the same position, saying Iran had determined it would face no geographical limitations in any future confrontation.
Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf cited remarks by Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei about the need to end the current situation, saying "sustainable deterrence" was the way out of the crisis.
"We must remove the shadow of war from ourselves," Ghalibaf said. Iran needed to fulfil "the directive of the Supreme Leader" Khamenei, he added.
What will it take to reopen Hormuz?
Baghaei elaborated on remarks by Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, who said the Strait of Hormuz could be reopened within two hours of the other side fulfilling its commitments.
Baghaei said the comment was meant to signal Iran's readiness to act quickly, not to minimise the complexity of a potential agreement.
Tehran's conditions, he said, include lifting US sanctions, ending the naval blockade and implementing commitments Washington accepted under the Islamabad memorandum of understanding.
Trump has described the Islamabad memorandum as a waste of time and declared it null and void, and has characterised ongoing negotiations with Iran as "a load of nonsense".
Diplomatic efforts are continuing despite the standoff. Qatar's prime minister is expected to meet US Secretary of State Marco Rubio on Monday, with Doha serving as a key mediator between Tehran and Washington.
