Nvidia says it has found no indication that its systems or networks were affected after the Gentlemen ransomware group named the company on its data leak website.
The group’s listing includes Nvidia’s logo and a general description of the chipmaker. It offers no explanation of the alleged attack, with the section about stolen information indicating that details will be provided later.
The company has acknowledged that it is investigating a possible security incident, but its statement points to a third party rather than Nvidia’s own infrastructure.
“Nvidia is investigating an incident that may have affected a third-party partner. We have found no indication that Nvidia systems or networks were affected. We continue to monitor the situation,” an Nvidia spokesperson told SecurityWeek.
The statement does not identify the partner or describe the nature of the incident. It also does not specify whether any Nvidia-related information may have been exposed.
The ransomware group’s listing does not establish that Nvidia was breached or that any of its data was stolen.
The Gentlemen ransomware operation emerged around mid-2025, initially operating as a closed group before launching an affiliate program later that year. Microsoft tracks its operators as Storm-2697.
The group uses a double-extortion model in which attackers steal data and encrypt systems, threatening to publish the stolen information to pressure victims into paying. Its affiliates have targeted organizations in numerous sectors, including manufacturing, healthcare, transportation, and financial services.
The Gentlemen has become increasingly active, with its leak website now listing more than 1,100 alleged victims.
According to Sophos, Gentlemen affiliates have used compromised credentials and legitimate administrative tools during intrusions. In one investigated incident, ransomware was deployed less than 24 hours after the earliest identified post-compromise activity.
