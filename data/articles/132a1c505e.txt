First it was Val Kilmer, then Greta Garbo, but finally AI has resurrected a star the terraces can cheer for: John Motson, the BBC football pundit who died in February 2023, is coming back to commentate on the next generation of footballers – at least on a video game.
The legendary commentator’s family has approved the use of AI technology to create a digital version of “Motty” – as he was ubiquitously known by generations of football fans – by EA Sports, which makes the popular football simulator FC27.
Eight years after the Lancashire-born broadcaster put down his microphone and hung up his sheepskin coat for the last time after a 50-year career, his AI recreation will be found burbling from speakers across the country about a new generation of superstars from Lamine Yamal to Chloe Kelly when his voice is added to the game in a software update.
Fred Motson, Motty’s son, said his family was grateful to the games company for “this touching tribute and preserving his memory in-game”.
He said: “His voice inspired millions of players across generations every time they picked up a controller, and now a whole new set of fans will get to experience his legacy and commentary.”
EA Sports is understood to have paid the Motson family, but how much has not been disclosed.
Motson’s tech-enabled revival was not universally welcomed. Users on a Reddit forum variously called it “disrespectful”, “gross” and complained that the world was a bleak place when “you can’t even escape death”. The practice of resurrecting famous people using AI has sometimes been dubbed “ghostploitation”.
The company said it created the voice using almost 10 years’ of recordings made while Motson was providing commentary for the game – then known as Fifa – between 1996 and 2006. It said it had been using new AI tools and was “bringing a mix of both his original audio and an AI-recreation trained by recordings of the man himself”.
In September, it emerged that the family of Greta Garbo, one of the stars of the golden age of cinema, had licensed her image and voice rights to create an AI version of her to appear in an advert for a Swedish industrial company.
An AI version of the Batman star Val Kilmer, who died in 2025, was unveiled earlier this year as the star of As Deep As the Grave, a film he had been cast in but had been unable to film before he died.
