How old are you, and what would you like for Christmas? That’s normally a question reserved for children visiting Santa’s grotto, but it’s one we put to hundreds of you of all ages to see what different generations have on their gift lists.
The results may just help you hone your approach to present buying across generations this Christmas, or at least offer an insight into the tastes of each group. But be warned: from the boomer who wants a Nintendo Switch to the 30-somethings collecting vinyl and brewing craft beer, some answers might surprise you.
We’ve also included input from celebrities, including Jamie Oliver, 51, who’d like a bottle of scotch and some socks, as well as some of our favourite Guardian names.
Special mention must also go to our oldest respondent, Joyce, 103, who asked for “someone to escort me on a clothes shopping trip to Peter Jones [a London department store]”. It’s never too late for a wardrobe reset.

The best gifts for millennials (30-45)
“What millennial isn’t currently obsessed with small interiors luxuries while they cannot afford to get on the housing ladder?” said 34-year-old Lissy, who asked for Piglet in Bed linen. Home comforts (or improvements) were big with this age group, as were more personal or nostalgic requests, such as Tom’s wish to recreate his childhood garden shed with the help of his dad and brother.
Vinyl
Since I got my record player six years ago, I’ve been asking people to buy me records for Christmas and birthdays. They sometimes look a bit scared and ask: “But which one?” And I reply: “Whatever you like!” I love to see what people choose as it’s often a reflection of what they like, and I think of them whenever I put it on. Amy, 35

The best gifts for gen X (46-61)
They may have partied their way through the 90s, but the sandwich generation is now craving comfortable clothes and an early night. As 52-year-old Jane put it: “I don’t care at all what I get, so long as someone has gone shopping with me in mind.”
Cashmere hoodie
The White Company brushed cashmere hoodie – I have one already and wear it all the time. Cashmere is a treat, so I like to ask for it as a gift. Jo, 47
Belt
I’d like a ratchet belt – really practical for men of a certain age and most appreciated post-Christmas lunch. Stephen, 57
Cast-iron teppanyaki griddle
Will I get a lot of use from this? Probably not. Is my kitchen big enough to accommodate it? Not in the slightest. But am I being chased around the internet by adverts for them because I must have Googled them at 2am once, and I’m hoping that getting one will somehow stop this? Oh god yes. Stuart Heritage, 46

Washing-up bowl

Initially I was surprised. We don’t often have domestic conversations. Over the months, though, I’ve moved past surprise, through ennui, into envy. I want one of these (metaphorically) gold-plated washing-up bowls. Zoe Williams, 53

Otherwise known as Generation Don’t-Buy-Me-Anything-I’ve-Got-Enough-Bloody-Stuff, this is the group who overwhelmingly asked for experiences instead of physical gifts. Small sentimental presents, gifts for hobbies and upgrades of useful things were also popular, though.
