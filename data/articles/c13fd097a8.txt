A neurological disorder threatened to cut Teagan Micah's career short, but the Australian goalkeeper has overcome it with a little help from her Matildas friends.
The 29-year-old was diagnosed with autonomic dysfunction after suffering a concussion in March while training with her current French club, Lyon.
She previously sustained a serious head injury in 2022 with Swedish side FC Rosengård and the latest concussion forced the shot stopper to miss March's Asian Cup.
It also led to her developing the debilitating condition, which stops the nervous system properly regulating automatic body functions.
Micah has recovered and returns to Joe Montemurro's Matildas squad for their two-game series against Colombia, beginning in Canberra on Saturday night.
Speaking to reporters on Wednesday, the Queensland product revealed the "big health scare" threatened to force her into early retirement.
"I had a lot of chats with my family, with doctors, my best friends … every day about retirement, and it was looking like that for a while," Micah said.
She experienced problems regulating her heart rate, which would rise to 190 beats per minute after walking for 2 minutes.
Micah consulted with specialists in Sydney, Geneva and France in a bid to get healthy and back on the field.
"It was probably about June, July time when I came home to [Australia] for about two weeks and I also saw my doctors here … it was kind of the turning point for me," she said.
"I sat down with my family and spoke a lot with them [about retiring] and it was just a matter of … get back healthy first and then making the decision.
"I didn't want to make a decision before that because I knew it wasn't going to be something I'd be comfortable with … I just knew I wanted to keep going."
Micah, who says she feels "alive again", credited ex-Lyon star Ellie Carpenter and other Australian teammates for getting her back on her feet.
"She got me through so many days," Micah said of close friend Carpenter.
"She sent me food to my apartment when I couldn't get out of bed for days.
"Steph [Catley], Ellie, Ras [Hayley Raso] were the main ones checking in all the time.
"Also the coaching staff here, the Matildas, were incredible as well … and at Lyon, those girls made me smile on days where I didn't think I had it in me anymore."
Competing with Mackenzie Arnold for the number 1 shirt, Micah said she would do everything possible to nab a spot in Australia's squad for the 2027 World Cup in Brazil.
"I need to probably go back to the drawing board and write down what I want to achieve now with the rest of my career," Micah said.
"Now that I feel good and I'm ready to go it's like where do I want my career to take me … [I want] to use this second chance I have."
The friendlies in Canberra and Geelong are world number 15 Australia's last before the World Cup draw on December 11.
World number 19 Colombia have established themselves as the second-best South American team behind hosts Brazil.
Along with Micah, Australia have regained Mary Fowler, Kyra Cooney-Cross, Alanna Kennedy, Tameka Yallop and Kahli Johnson.
Everton attacker Holly McNamara [hamstring] is out until late December, while Clare Hunt and Charlie Rule remain sidelined.
