The two countries want TikTok’s European business and key technology, including its recommendation algorithm, to come under European control, arguing that regulation alone cannot address security concerns.
Germany and Austria on Thursday called for TikTok's European business to be placed under European ownership and control, arguing that regulation alone cannot address concerns over data access and algorithms.
The Chinese-owned social media platform poses "fundamental security policy risks for Europe", German culture and media minister Wolfram Weimer said after meeting Austrian digital state secretary Alexander Proell in Vienna.
In a joint statement, the two politicians called for "a European ownership structure for TikTok's European business and European responsibility for the key technological infrastructure — from the recommendation algorithm to data access".
Germany and Austria will seek to build EU-wide support for their proposal at a meeting of the Telecommunications and Energy (TTE) Council in Brussels on December 2, the statement said.
On November 6, TikTok was preliminarily found to be in breach of Europe's Digital Services Act (DSA) over how its recommender system exposes users to potentially harmful content.
But Weimer and Proell said regulation did not answer "the basic question of who actually controls the platform".
"TikTok is no longer a toy, but part of the infrastructure of our democracy. This infrastructure must not remain in the hands of a geopolitical systemic rival," Proell said.
ByteDance, the Chinese parent company of TikTok, in January ceded control of the platform's US operations to a majority American-owned joint venture, in response to a threatened ban in the United States.
Weimer in May said he was "firmly convinced that Europe should follow the American example and that the company's ownership structure must be put up for discussion".
TikTok rejected the proposal as having "zero merit", saying it ignored existing European laws including the Digital Services Act and GDPR as well as the company's 12-billion-euro Project Clover data security programme.
TikTok also said its European operations are run by companies incorporated in Ireland and Britain and are subject to European law, while parent company ByteDance is majority-owned by global institutional investors and has no Chinese government ownership stake.
