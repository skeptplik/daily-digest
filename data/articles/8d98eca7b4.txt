One Nation’s general manager, Kelvin Morton, has taken leave amid speculation that he will be sidelined from his role after a series of leaks about internal party processes and vetting scandals.
Morton’s leave, confirmed by several One Nation sources unauthorised to comment publicly, comes as the federal party finalises its pre-selection process for the federal election.
Guardian Australia understands that the party’s head office has been rocked by complaints about staff behaviour and culture, which one insider described as “disorganised, chaotic, dysfunctional [and] politically incorrect”.
It is not known when or if Morton will return to his role. He remains a member of the party’s national executive, having replaced the former treasurer Alex Jones earlier this year.
Morton was appointed to the high-ranking position in February and tasked with improving the party’s internal processes, including candidate selection and vetting.
According to the party executive at the time, he was hired to build a “professional, scalable, and legally robust organisation capable of outright victory at the next general election”.
His appointment was heralded as a turning point for the professionalisation of the party but he has clashed with staff at the party’s Brisbane headquarters and officials at one of the party’s most active branches.
Leaked text messages seen by Guardian Australia confirmed that Morton referred to David Farley as an “asshole” just days before Farley’s historic Farrer byelection win for the party in May.
In September Guardian Australia revealed that Morton had written to the Lilley branch in Brisbane advising it of federal intervention, which included removing its entire committee of volunteers. This came after a dispute about access to promotional materials and a fundraiser that raised $12,000 for the party.
In May Guardian Australia reported that Morton had issued a directive ordering committee members to reconstitute their branches in response to “significant risks”.
The directive announced strict gag orders: all committee members and nominees had to sign non-disclosure agreements, while branches had to agree to a “media silence” policy and a social media ban.
“It is a mandatory process,” Morton’s directive said of the NDAs. “Please do not request an exemption as we value our members and refusal often offends.”
Last month Morton told the Nine Newspapers that he had overstated his own professional experience before taking the One Nation job.
The newspaper highlighted his claims to have served as an army officer despite not completing training and revealed that he had described a party employee as a “fat, ginger man-baby”.
Morton had also claimed to have conducted “local strategy work” on the Obama/Biden 2008 presidential campaign, when he instead he had volunteered for a few hours.
One Nation and Morton were contacted for comment.
