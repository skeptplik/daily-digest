Oscar-winning actress Dame Judi Dench, who starred alongside Pierce Brosnan and Daniel Craig in the Bond franchise, shares her thoughts on Robert Pattinson as the new 007 – but says her eye condition may prevent her from watching...
Dame Judi Dench, who played 007's boss M in the film franchise opposite Pierce Brosnan and Daniel Craig, has shared her verdict on Robert Pattinson's casting as the new James Bond.
The 40-year-old British actor was officially confirmed as the seventh Bond last week, beating frontrunners Jack Lowden, Callum Turner and Josh O'Connor to the coveted role.
Several stars have shared their verdict on the new Bond, including previous 007s George Lazenby and Pierce Brosnan.
Now, Dench, 91, has revealed what she thinks of Pattinson. Speaking on the British ITV morning show Lorraine, the Oscar-winning actress said she “can’t wait” to see him in action.
However, she added that she’ll struggle to see him clearly due to her worsening eye condition: “I can’t see now properly. I can vaguely see – but he looks terrific to me."
Earlier this year, Dench shared with BBC Radio 4 that her eye condition has greatly affected her ability to work, leaving her unable to perform on stage or read scripts.
Dench was diagnosed with age-related macular degeneration (AMD) in 2012. AMD is an eye disease that blurs central vision, a common condition that is the leading cause of vision loss for adults, which happens when aging causes damage to the macula (the part of the eye that controls sharp, straight-ahead vision). The condition has made it incredibly difficult for Dench to even recognise faces.
“I can’t be on stage. I can’t do a play anymore. I can’t do a film anymore. I can’t read anymore,” she shared, adding: “You’ve got to adjust to all that, and make allowances for it, and just get on and don’t think about it too much.”
When asked about ageing, Dench replied: "There's nothing to say for it at all!"
Dench featured in both Brosnan and Craig’s respective Bond tenures. She made her first appearance as M in GoldenEye (1995), becoming the first woman to play the role.
She went on to play the MI6 boss in Tomorrow Never Dies (1997), The World Is Not Enough (1999), Die Another Day (2002), and continued into Craig's tenure with Casino Royale (2006), Quantum of Solace (2008) and Skyfall (2012).
In response to his casting, Pattinson, who has become one of the world’s most famous stars since his breakout roles in the Harry Potter and Twilight franchises, followed by Tenet, The Odyssey and Primetime, called the role the “honour of a lifetime” and “a dream come true”.
Production on the as-yet-untitled Bond 26 is due to start next year, with Denis Villeneuve directing and Steven Knight on script duties.
Before filming can start, Pattinson will have to complete filming The Batman Part II – production on which was temporarily paused after director Matt Reeves was faced with a personal issue. Steven Soderbergh has stepped in to help shoot the final few weeks.
This means that the earliest audiences will get to see the next Bond adventure will be in the second half of 2028 - making it seven years since Daniel Craig's swansong No Time To Die, and the longest gap between Bond films since 1989's Licence To Kill and 1995's GoldenEye.
