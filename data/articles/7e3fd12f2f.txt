Tia Hinds may only be 24, but she's already experienced much in her young rugby career.
Representing Australia, claiming world titles, travelling to almost every continent and even playing alongside her mum, Janine, in a Randwick club match.
"She played on the wing for one game," Hinds told ABC Sport with the hint of a giggle.
"We were short of numbers."
There's one experience, though, Hinds hopes she can reflect upon when the time comes to hang up the boots.
The goal of winning an Olympic gold medal burns brightly for the unassuming Sydneysider, as it does for her Australian women's rugby sevens teammates.
Following the historic 2016 Rio Olympics triumph, Australia didn't reach the podium at the Tokyo and Paris Games, finishing fifth and fourth respectively.
The Paris result was a particularly bitter pill to swallow, given Australia was reigning world series champions.
"We're such a high-performing team on the world series circuit, it's been a bit hard to deal with that," said Hinds, who was a member of Australia's Tokyo and Paris squads.
"We've fallen short two times now."
The Australians, who won last season's World Championship, are chasing automatic qualification for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics.
To book their berth, they must finish inside the top four in the 2027 World Championship, which will be decided across three tournaments (Hong Kong, Valladolid and Bordeaux) in April and May.
The six-leg world series takes place before those events, beginning in Dubai on Saturday.
Under the guidance of new coach Reg Tayler, Australia is expected to meet New Zealand in the final of the season-opening tournament on Monday morning, AEDT.
But ahead of any potential match-up with its trans-Tasman rival, Australia must advance from its round-robin pool, which includes the well-credentialed USA and France.
"It's definitely one match at a time, as the USA and France are two powerhouse teams," Hinds said.
"We would love to play in the final and of course it's usually against the Kiwis, so it would be naive not to have a look at how they're doing in their pool matches.
"But we definitely will focus on the pool stage first."
Hinds's pivotal role
A calf injury robbed Hinds of the chance to play in last season's World Championship-deciding tournament in Bordeaux.
But Australia wouldn't have been in a position to lift the silverware in France if it hadn't been for the talented playmaker.
At the preceding World Championship leg in Valladolid, she triggered Australia's comeback in its thrilling 28-26 semifinal win over New Zealand.
With Australia trailing 14-0 in the first half, Hinds's outstanding ability to read the game came to the fore when she took advantage of lazy defence to score a runaway try.
Hinds's goal kicking also proved crucial, perhaps the by-product of playing Australian rules as a junior with the Maroubra Saints, where Sydney Swans AFL star Errol Gulden was a teammate.
A loss to New Zealand in Valladolid would have effectively dashed Australia's hopes of clinching the World Championship.
Expressing modesty when speaking about her influence on the result, Hinds instead heaped praise on captain Bella Nasser for steering Australia home.
"We've got a really captain in Bella, who is really composed and got her head screwed on," said Hinds, who sustained her season-ending injury in the Valladolid semifinal victory.
Rugby sevens isn't the only international stage graced by Hinds, as she also represents Australia's national 15-a-side team, the Wallaroos.
Making the Wallaroos squad for the 2029 Women's Rugby World Cup is among her ambitions, especially as the tournament will be held on home soil.
But before the dream of playing in her second World Cup can be realised, climbing the podium at the LA Olympics remains a major priority for Hinds.
"There's some unfinished business, to tick off that goal of hopefully medalling at an Olympic Games," Hinds said.
