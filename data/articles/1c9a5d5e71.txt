The White House has dropped its nomination of Heidi Overton to lead the Food and Drug Administration, a White House official told the Guardian.
Donald Trump nominated Overton – a physician and prominent abortion opponent – in August. Overton has spent most of her professional career working directly for Trump or conservative groups aligned with his agenda. Her nomination in August was seen as a move to bring a stabilizing Trump loyalist to the agency after a tumultous year under Trump’s first FDA commissioner, Marty Makary.
But Overton faced bipartisan questions in the Senate about her willingness to break from Trump, particularly on his unsupported claims about the dangers of childhood vaccines.
Republican senator Bill Cassidy, a Louisiana doctor who chairs the Senate health committee, said earlier on Tuesday that he remained undecided about her nomination ahead of a Senate health, labor and pensions committee vote scheduled for Wednesday.
Cassidy had previously called Overton’s support for Trump’s plan to break up combination vaccines – including the measles, mumps and rubella shot – “almost disqualifying”. And he pressed her during a September hearing about her scientific independence and willingness to push back against Trump and health secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr, a longtime vaccine critic.
A prominent reproductive rights group celebrated the White House’s decision to withdraw Overton’s nomination.
Mini Timmaraju, the president and CEO of Reproductive Freedom for All, formerly Naral Pro-Choice America, said: “Heidi Overton posed a serious threat to the future of medication abortion access. Voters rejected that anti-abortion agenda nationwide, and our members kept the pressure on to tank her confirmation. If Republicans try to rush through another nominee who threatens our healthcare, we’re ready to keep fighting.”
As the head of the FDA, Overton would have overseen the agency that regulates medication abortions. In a 2023 policy brief for the conservative America First Policy Institute, Overton said Congress should strictly regulate the abortion pill mifeprestone “to protect women and children”, criticized federal policies that allow for the mailing of abortion pills and wrote that abortion “is corrosive to children, women, and society broadly”.
To win confirmation, Overton would have needed the votes of all 12 Republicans on the Senate health, education, labor and pensions committee. Democrats hold 11 seats. Without support of the committee, Overton faced little chance of winning a full Senate vote on chamber floor.
Republicans are racing to try to confirm all of Trump’s current nominees before they hand over power to Senate Democrats in January.
