A cargo ship that became stranded off the coast of southwestern France was towed back out to sea on Tuesday after running aground overnight into Friday.
The Maltese-flagged Miro G had been heading to the port of Rouen in Normandy when it came into difficulty amid heavy swells off Lit-et-Mixe in the Landes department.
Nineteen crew members were winched to safety and taken to the town of Saint-Julien-en-Born after the ship ran aground.
The vessel was reportedly not carrying any cargo when the incident occurred.
France's Atlantic Maritime Prefecture confirmed on Tuesday that the operation to refloat the vessel had been successfully carried out.
"Following the operation, the French Navy deployed a Falcon 50 aircraft to conduct a maritime surveillance flight and assess the environmental situation," the prefecture said in a statement. "No marine pollution was detected in the area where the ship ran aground or along its transit route."
"We're getting off very, very lightly, and it's truly remarkable... We're heading toward the most favourable scenario," Landes Prefect Gilles Clavreul told reporters, per the AFP news agency.
The Miro G is now set to be helped on to Le Verdon-sur-Mer, where it is due to undergo a technical inspection.
