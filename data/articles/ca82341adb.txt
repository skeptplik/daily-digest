Japan will declare its aim to become a leading artificial intelligence nation after the United States and China, it was learned Wednesday.
The government will include this goal when it revises the country’s three key national security documents later this year.
On Wednesday, the government presented an outline of the economic security sections of the revised documents at a meeting of the ruling Liberal Democratic Party and obtained broad approval.
According to the outline, the revised documents will call for reducing Japan’s AI reliance on other countries, noting that diplomacy and national defense will depend on economic and technological capabilities.
The revised documents will also include plans to invest intensively in automated driving and physical AI technologies, as well as the goal of acquiring a technological advantage that renders Japan indispensable to other countries.
Under the revised documents, Japan will conduct risk assessments for energy, food, medical and other strategic goods and strengthen supply chains through collaboration with reliable countries and regions. The government will increase state support where economic rationality prevents stable supplies.
The revised documents will emphasize the need for a fair and predictable international economic order that is not affected by economic coercion, apparently referring to China’s export restrictions.
They will also call for establishing collective resilience by advancing the Free and Open Indo-Pacific vision.
