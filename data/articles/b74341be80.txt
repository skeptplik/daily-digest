“Are you going to Beaverton,” John Mersho, the newly minted president and chief executive officer of the JD banner in North America, asked as soon as he sat down for an interview with FN at the brand’s Times Square flagship store on Monday.
Mersho, of course, was referring to Nike’s upcoming investor day on Tuesday, the Swoosh’s first in nine years. “I’m excited about the product,” Mersho, who ascended to his new role in September, said. “And for us, the Nike team is our family. We’re constantly dealing with them, constantly seeing them. So, for us, we’re excited to see what they’re going to come out with for the future.”
JD Sports Fashion chief executive officer Régis Schultz, who was also at the Times Square store Monday, added that his team wants to “show its love” to Nike at investor day. “We love the brand and we love the team,” Schultz told FN. “We want to have a constructive dialogue with the company, and I think that Elliott [Hill, CEO of Nike Inc.] is the champion of that. He is listening to what we are saying and is listening to the feedback. And this industry needs a strong Nike, it’s good for everyone.”

This all comes one day before Hill is expected to lay out the troubled athletic brand’s future plans to the world nine years after the Swoosh hosted its last investor day, and five years after issuing its most recent multi-year financial targets.
The main item on many attendees’ agendas on Tuesday will be learning more about Nike’s path forward, namely the expected announcement of a new five-year financial model that will go through fiscal 2031.
Within this plan, investors are hoping to hear more about how the company plans to restore demand beyond performance product, how it will fix the China business, what is being done to stabilize the Jordan brand and more specific details of the recently announced Pace cost savings program.
Speaking of Jordan, Schultz noted that the slowdown in the brand’s business was bound to happen due to being too widely available.
“The more you see the product, the less the product is attractive and the less it’s fashionable because you say, oh, I can see it everywhere,” Schultz noted. “So that means that it’s not the right product in the market. That’s exactly what happened. So, I think what they are doing is exactly the right thing to do, which is to reduce the amount of product in the marketplace. I think they need to reduce the number of store where you can see the product.”
Schultz added that JD Sports stands to benefit from a stronger Jordan brand. “Our market share of Jordan is much higher than anyone else in this country, so we will always benefit from Jordan doing a better job than it has been,” the Group CEO said. “So the fact that they reduce allocation, it will also reduce the number of bad retailers having the product.”
Mersho noted that he doesn’t see the Jordan brand downturn “lasting that long,” citing new product launches coming next fall.
“We’re clearly investing in Jordan,” Mersho said. “They’re giving us some SMUs [special made units, or exclusive styles], including our own Air Jordan 1 Mids and Lows in colors unique to us. I think that can really help us in the short term.”
He also cited the recent release of the Air Jordan 11 Retro “Green Screen,” which “blew out” at a 97 percent sell-through. “So, the brand can still bring the energy, and they still can bring the lines of customers,” Mersho said. “It just got to be at a smaller scale right now. But overall, we are excited about the Jordan brand moving forward.”
Since entering the U.S. through the acquisition of Finish Line in 2018, North America has become JD’s largest region by sales, accounting for 38 percent of the company’s organic sales. Across the region, the group now operates more than 2,500 stores through five complementary banners: JD, Finish Line at Macy’s, DTLR, Shoe Palace and Hibbett. North American revenues reached $6.3 billion in fiscal 2026.
Mersho, who has held the role of chief commercial officer for JD North America since 2025, stepped into his new position in September following the retirement of John Hall after a four-decade career in retail.
As a member of the Mersho family who founded Shoe Palace in 1993 — eventually becoming CEO in 2024 — the executive is focusing on strengthening JD’s product proposition, enhancing the customer’s omni-channel experience, and expanding its brand awareness and presence across North America.
“I’m laser focused on what the customer is looking for,” Mersho told FN on Monday. “We had a really good team at JD Finish Line, and for me, it’s more like, how do we amplify it? How do we push forward? Basically, I want to keep being disruptive. I don’t want to just sit on my hands and figure things out. We’re all locked in.”
In its most recent first half results issued in September, overall company revenue took a hit with organic sales in the period declining 0.7 percent to 5.90 billion pounds, down from 5.94 billion pounds the same time last year.
Schultz said at the time that the company’s performance on the increase in youth unemployment and cost of living pressure, while the footwear product cycle continued to evolve into a highly promotional market.
As for how JD Sports is curtailing the athletic market’s promotional state, Schultz said that the retailer is relying on more newness and more exclusive product.
“I keep having this discussion with brands,” Schultz noted. “If you give the same product to everyone, you just divide the pie. But if you give different product to different channels based on the customer, you extend the pie. We have to give the customer a reason to come to our stores. They want to come here to find something they can’t find anywhere else.”
And this strategy seems to be working, according to Mike Grimes, who was appointed chief marketing officer for JD Group in September.
“We have a pretty good methodology to see how our consumers are reacting to JD through our loyalty program, especially when we flip a Finish Line store to a JD Sports,” Grimes told FN on Monday. “What we found is we’re really good at bringing existing customers over, as you’d expect. We’re also reactivating customers we haven’t seen in a couple of years. Perhaps we had maybe lost them a bit with the Finish Line brand over time. And all of a sudden, people we haven’t seen start to come back. And, maybe most important, we’re starting to bring in a newer, more diverse, younger demographic than what we’d seen in the past with the Finish Line brand. You put those three things together: the productivity of a JD store versus a Finish Line store have been pretty dramatic.”
