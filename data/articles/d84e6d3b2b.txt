2026 AFL draft live updates: Every selection from the second round and beyond on night two
The AFL draft continues on night two as the second round gets underway with Brisbane holding three consecutive selections after failing to land Caylen Murray.
Brisbane had acquired the selections in the hopes of matching a bid for Murray, an academy prospect, only to fall short of matching Sydney's bid on night one of the draft.
Port Adelaide will kick off night two before Essendon, Richmond and West Coast use their bidding compensation picks.
Follow the live blog below for all the selections and major draft storylines from night two.

Key Event
Pick 24: Jake Soutar - West Coast
A mature-age intercepting defender, Soutar is coming off an exceptionally strong season in the WAFL. Only 190cm so not true KPD height, but plays taller due to his aerial strength.
It'll be interesting to see how quickly Soutar makes his AFL debut as a mature-ager.
The Eagles have been crying out for some key defensive help around Reuben Ginbey, and Soutar will be a welcome addition.
They once again continue their WA-themed selections after taking Mellody last night.
Key Event
Pick 23: George Gale - North Melbourne
A tall, big-bodied midfielder with a run-all-day engine that has seen him become an effective winger as well as inside midfielder. Made the step up to senior WAFL footy impressively.
Gale beaming as he chats to Fox Footy from his living room.
"Wow. I'm feeling amazing," he says.
"It's definitely a dream come true."
Gale says he models his game on Nat Fyfe, which makes sense given he's a Perth kid.
West Coast has traded pick 23 to North Melbourne
The Kangaroos are up next with the Eagles trading pick 23 and a 2028 third-rounder in exchange for a 2027 first-rounder.
This upcoming pick will be North's first after they traded their first to Sydney for Nick Blakey.
Key Event
Pick 22: Noah Williams - Richmond
A tough and competitive left-footer, Williams is a reliable option in the midfield or off half-back. He is an good kick of the footy and is strong in the tackle.
There's a ceiling fan in Williams's living room and a few of his mates come close to being decapitated as they jump for joy in celebration after his name is read out.
"I'm over the moon, to be honest," he tells Fox Footy.
"So grateful for this support around me. It's an unreal feeling."
Key Event
Pick 21: Harvie Cooke - Essendon
A busy smaller midfielder with a burst of pace and elite endurance, Cooke was named the Talent League’s best player in 2026 after a dominant season. Finds plenty of the ball and usually uses it well.
Cooke is in tears as he chats with Fox Footy. Another Bomber draftee overcome with emotion. He's got Bombers defender Zak Johnson in his living room as well!
"I don't know what to say, to be honest," he says.
"An unbelievable club and I'm so grateful for the opportunity.
"I spoke to them a couple of times, one at the combine and one at my house, and I didn't really get too much, so it's a bit of a surprise."
Cooke earns comparisons to Hawthorn's Dylan Moore.
An Indigenous domination in round one
There has been a bit of a concern about the representation of the Indigenous community in the AFL, but there were signs last night that the future is bright.
Five of the top 14 players selected last night — Dougie Cochrane, Cody Walker, Caylen Murray, Jake Eime and Toby Krasna —were Indigenous prospects.
Brilliant stuff!
Key Event
Pick 20: Wil Malady - Port Adelaide
An exciting athlete with game-breaking potential, Malady is your third-tall sized forward but with electric pace and agility. There are rough edges, but Malady is capable of special things on the air and at ground level.
Malady is already decked out with a Port cap and scarf as he checks in from his living room.
"Over the moon," he tells Fox Footy.
"Mum and dad must've done some shopping.
"I had a bit of an idea, but obviously, this draft has been really weird, so anything could happen, but I'm happy to be a Port Adelaide player now."
He says he models his game on Jack Gunston and Ollie Dempsey.

What does Brisbane do with all its picks?
One thing to keep an eye on tonight will be what exactly Brisbane does with its second round picks.
The Lions got some extra picks in the hope that they'd be able to match a bid on Caylen Murray, only to fall short of Sydney's bid at No.9 last night.
Brisbane now holds picks 27, 28, 29 and 35 in the second round.
Can they cobble those up to potentially move higher up the order or somehow nab a valuable future pick?
I can't imagine they're interested in adding four kids.
