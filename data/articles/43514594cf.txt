Russia and Ukraine exchanged 43 mostly elderly civilians in a family reunification mission in Belarus, Belarusian state media reported Friday.
The Belta news agency said 23 elderly Russian citizens and 20 people from Ukraine were involved in the exchange, which took place at the Novaya Guta border checkpoint in Belarus.
Ruth Hetherington, who heads the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) in Belarus, said the exchange was part of ongoing humanitarian efforts to help reunite people from the two warring countries with their relatives.
“Today’s operation has become one of the largest since 2022,” Hetherington told Belta, referring to the year Russia launched the full-scale invasion of Ukraine.
“During this time, we’ve assisted more than 2,000 people. Many of them are the elderly and vulnerable,” Hetherington added.
Belta released photos of the exchange, showing the elderly people and ICRC volunteers.
Neither Russia nor Ukraine has officially confirmed the humanitarian mission, which comes one day after the two countries held their first exchange of fallen soldiers’ remains since U.S.-mediated peace talks resumed in Abu Dhabi earlier this month.
U.S. and Ukrainian officials also announced plans for a major exchange of prisoners of war before the winter holidays following talks between Russian President Vladimir Putin and U.S. special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner.
Belta reported that Belarus has helped facilitate more than 70 humanitarian, POW and fallen soldiers’ exchange missions since 2022.
