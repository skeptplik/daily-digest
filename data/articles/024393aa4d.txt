Jersey primary school children have enjoyed learning about space in a lesson delivered by an astronomer.
Dr Robin Catchpole of the Cambridge Institute of Astronomy was invited to the island by the Jersey Astronomy Club and shared his extensive knowledge with pupils aged 10 and 11 at First Tower School.
Year 6 pupil Lola, 11, said her favourite part was learning about how the planets were created while Vihaan, 10, said he was fascinated by black holes and how they were formed.
Catchpole, who has written and co-written more than 100 research papers and articles on astronomy, said his trick for making space accessible to children was to show them planets they would be familiar with.
Catchpole said he first became interested in astronomy when his grandfather gave him a book about the subject and his mother showed him the Moon through a telescope on the beachfront in Torquay, Devon.
"I saw craters and shadows of mountains and I thought that was very exciting," he said.
Year 6 teacher Lee Bradshaw said it was a great opportunity to have an expert like Catchpole answering questions and children proved inquisitive about the subject.
