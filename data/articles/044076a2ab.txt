From 1980 until 1995, no one was safe from the dark wit of Gary Larson, not even Mickey Mouse. For 15 years, while he was publishing his beloved single-panel comic strip The Far Side, Larson satirized just about everything, including popular culture. And while Disney didn’t own Marvel, Star Wars, the Muppets, and so many other brands back then, the company was still a huge target that Larson took aim at several times.
Here are the 10 best Disney parodies featured in Gary Larson’s The Far Side.
10 Feb. 12, 1983
Jiminy Cricket, Pinocchio's conscience in the 1940 Disney classic and the ghost of Christmas Past in 1983’s Mickey's Christmas Carol, finds himself in a glass jar in this comic strip. I assume this guy is an entomologist, or at least a bug collector.
As funny as this idea is on its own, there are a few dark details here that add to the macabre humor. For one, the guy is singing the Pinocchio song “When You Wish Upon a Star,” which makes me wonder if he intentionally lured Jiminy to him. Also, if you look closely in the jar with Jiminy, he’s being kept in some sort of water or solution, with his hat floating on top of the liquid. Is Jiminy already dead?
9 Apr. 1, 1992
Pinocchio's conscience isn’t the only one to feel the wrath of Gary Larson. So does the wooden boy himself in this hilarious 1992 comic panel which is all about the worst possible time for Pinocchio to become a real boy.
8 Nov. 30, 1994
A year after the lion comic, Larson returned to Pinocchio and made him a victim of yet another feline. Honestly, if I were a cat, I would definitely turn Pinocchio into a scratching post. He’s perfect for that.
7 Aug. 3, 1994
This strip is a play on the word “Mousketeer” — at least, I think it is. This feels like one of those Far Side comics that is more of a rough idea than a perfectly defined joke. Still, the visual alone of this kid on a pirate ship is enough to get a laugh.
6 Mar. 9, 1982
I realize that Peter Pan isn’t just a Disney property, but the 1953 animated film is the most famous version of the character, and I’m guessing that’s the version Larson had in mind in 1982 when he depicted Peter Pan having just flown into a window. To be fair, Peter Pan never struck me as the brightest Disney character, so I buy that he might fly himself into a window.
5 July 15, 1992
Larson poked fun at another Peter Pan character a decade later, when he showed Captain Hook with a career counselor who is helping him decide between a job in piracy or being a massage therapist. While it’s obvious that he becomes a pirate, I do wonder if Hook might have been happier had he gone the other way (although his patients might have suffered).
4 May 29, 1991
Yet another Peter Pan character is featured in this one. If I were the frog that managed to catch Tinkerbell, I’d forever have bragging rights at the pond. Good for this frog. He earned it.
3 Feb. 19, 1986
The Incredible Journey is a 1963 Disney film based on a 1961 novel by Sheila Burnford. It’s about three lost pets — two dogs and a cat — who travel a long distance to reunite with their human family. Kids of the 1990s (like me) may be more familiar with the 1993 remake Homeward Bound: The Incredible Journey starring Don Ameche and Michael J. Fox as the dogs and Sally Field as the cat.
To make fun of this movie, all Larson had to do was team up three ridiculously mismatched animals: a cow, a squid, and a snake.
I hope they make it back home.
2 July 2, 1981
If Goofy were my pilot, I would also be very worried. Have you seen how bad skiing goes for that guy? It’s not good. I also kind of wonder how he even got a pilot’s license.
1 June 3, 1988
Larson was a master at finding the worst possible scenario to put a character into. Pinocchio on safari is a great example of this, as is Dumbo in a mid-air collision.
