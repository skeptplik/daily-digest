Chase outage disrupts online banking and Zelle payments in United States
American users of several major banking apps and online portals reported outages on Monday, according to the monitoring site Downdetector.com, with one institution ruling out a cyberattack as the cause. Among those affected by the outages were Bank of America, JPMorgan Chase and the popular money transfer app Zelle.
Thousands of customers reported difficulty accessing online accounts at several major US banks and the money transfer service Zelle on Monday after a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase caused ripple effects throughout the financial system.
However, Chase said Monday that the technical problems were not the result of a cyberattack, and that it was working to bring banking services back online.
“JPMorgan Chase has experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in an on-premise hardware system — it is not cyber-related,” said Joe Evangelisti, spokesman for the bank.
The disruption comes days after two major outages affected other parts of the US economy: A major outage by Microsoft happened Thursday and lasted for hours, severely impacting services including Outlook, Teams and AI assistant Copilot.
This was followed by an outage affecting the nation’s air traffic control system Friday that grounded all US flights for several hours and continued to disrupt air travel across the country over the weekend.
In the same statement, Evangelisti said that this was a Chase-only issue.
“This is not a systemic issue — other banks may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us,” he said.
Monday's technical issues seem to be centered at Chase customers and customers at other banks trying to transact with Chase. Those banks, including Bank of America and Citigroup, were not having issues with their banking services.
A spokesman for US Bank said its systems are not directly impacted but that “clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays.”
Zelle is the peer-to-peer payment system that major US banks use to allow customers to send money to friends and family at near-instant speed. Zelle is owned by Arizona-based Early Warning Services, which in turn is owned by financial institutions including Bank of America, Capital One, JPMorgan Chase, PNC Bank, Truist, US Bank and Wells Fargo.
A spokesman for Early Warning Services said the Zelle network was fully operational.
“An issue at the financial institutions may be delaying Zelle payments for some of their customers,” the spokesman said.
Some Chase customers received a message that said, “Some services aren’t available. We’re working on them. Please try back later.”
