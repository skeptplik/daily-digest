The long-awaited Blade Runner 2099 arrives on Prime Video tonight at 9 p.m. Eastern time, which, frankly, feels a bit late at night to drop eight episodes the night before a major holiday. Executive produced by original Blade Runner director Ridley Scott, the sequel series takes place in a future Los Angeles where the replicants have taken over and demoted humans to second-class citizens. It’s the kind of show that looks like it deserves a dedicated, fully alert, all-day binge session, not to be drowsily cut short so you can wake up early enough to begin preparing the stuffing tomorrow.
But I won’t question the wisdom of Prime Video. In fact, if you’ve got some time to kill before Blade Runner 2099 arrives tonight, I suggest using the streamer to rent another Ridley Scott sci-fi epic (assuming you’ve already prepped with the original Blade Runner and the sequel Blade Runner 2049).
Written by Drew Goddard (Project Hail Mary, Cloverfield), The Martian is Ridley Scott’s 2015 adaptation of the Andy Weir novel of the same name. It begins on Mars in the year 2035, where NASA’s Ares III expedition team is exploring the Acidalia Planitia region of the Red Planet. However, a severe dust storm cuts their mission short and they must hurry back to their ship. In the confusion and limited visibility caused by the storm, Dr. Mark Watney (Matt Damon) is left behind. A malfunction with his suit also makes him appear to be dead, which prompts the solemn crew of Ares III to leave Mars without his body.
Watney, however, is very much alive, and he awakens after the storm to find himself abandoned and alone. Watney knows that the next mission to Mars is in four years, so he must sustain himself on the planet until then, making use of a limited food supply that was left behind due to the storm. Fortunately, Watney is a botanist, and he uses a supply of potatoes to plant a garden full of the vegetable, which is fertilized with his own waste and the waste left behind by his crewmates. He also tries to find a way to make contact with Earth.
Why The Martian is so good
Unlike Scott’s other future-set sci-fi films like Blade Runner and Alien, which depict a future far removed from our own reality, The Martian is meant to be just a few years from our own time. Just like his follow-up book, Project Hail Mary, Weir's story relies heavily on actual science about space travel and agriculture. That level of realism is key to the story, and the limitations of the technology cause real-feeling complications for Watney and any conceivable rescue mission to retrieve him.
The Martian is one of those “left all alone” movies like Cast Away where, in lesser hands, such a story might get dull, but with the right combination of actor and director, you get a really inspiring story about the resilience of the human spirit. With Scott as director and Damon as the lead, The Martian is a poignant, often funny story about how to survive under harsh conditions.
The humor of the movie actually became a subject of some controversy in 2016, when it won the Golden Globe for Best Comedy, beating out a few actual comedies, including The Big Short, Joy, Spy, and Trainwreck. The win sparked many people to point out (accurately) that The Martian is not a comedy and that it only won the category because a humorous drama appears to have more prestige than “just” a comedy. Strangely, its Golden Globe win seems to hurt this movie’s legacy due to the category dispute.
Anyway, there’s really no need to rehash the old argument because The Martian is an excellent film regardless of its genre. While it is, actually, definitely a drama, it’s a thoughtful, humorous one filled with humanity and inspirational moments. It’s one of Ridley Scott’s very best films and an uplifting way to spend your pre-Turkey Day — until Blade Runner 2099 arrives.
The Martian is available for rent on Prime Video and Apple TV Plus.
