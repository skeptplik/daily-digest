Abstract
Disruptions to leading-strand synthesis impair and stall replication forks because DNA synthesis by the leading-strand polymerase, Pol ε, is essential for optimal template unwinding by the replicative CMG (CDC45–MCM–GINS) helicase1,2. Failure to efficiently restart and/or protect stalled replication forks is a major source of genomic instability3,4,5,6. Stalled forks can be restarted by a variety of mechanisms, including repriming downstream of the impediment4. In human cells, the primase–polymerase PRIMPOL promotes fork progression during replication stress7,8,9,10, but how its activity is coordinated by the replisome is unresolved. Here, by reconstituting replication fork stalling and restart with purified human proteins, we show that the CMG helicase directs PRIMPOL for efficient replication fork restart. Unexpectedly, the interaction between PRIMPOL and replication protein A9,11 is not critical for fork restart in this system. We show that PRIMPOL binds directly to CMG, and a cryo-electron microscopy structure illustrates how this targets PRIMPOL specifically for leading-strand priming to minimize the size of post-replicative gaps. Ablating the CMG–PRIMPOL interaction abolishes fork restart in vitro and PRIMPOL-dependent DNA damage tolerance in human cells. Our work establishes PRIMPOL as an integrated component of the human replisome dedicated to leading-strand priming.

Main
DNA lesion bypass by leading-strand repriming was demonstrated for the core Escherichia coli replisome, in which primer synthesis is performed by the primase also responsible for lagging-strand replication12,13. By contrast, eukaryotic replisomes reconstituted from purified Saccharomyces cerevisiae proteins cannot efficiently resume synthesis downstream of leading-strand lesions1 because the Pol α–primase complex that is responsible for priming each Okazaki fragment is targeted to the lagging-strand template14. This targeting mechanism is conserved in the human replisome14, indicating that inefficient priming on the leading strand by Pol α–primase is a universal feature of eukaryotic replisomes.
Unlike S. cerevisiae, humans have an additional primase called PRIMPOL7,8,9. PRIMPOL is a 560-amino-acid (aa) monomeric enzyme comprising a 21-aa amino-terminal helix of unknown function, an archaeo-eukaryotic primase (AEP) domain, a single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) binding zinc-finger (ZnF) and two C-terminal replication protein A (RPA) binding motifs (RBMs)6,15. Extensive evidence from human, mouse and chicken cell lines shows that PRIMPOL promotes replication fork progression under conditions that stall replicative DNA polymerases8,9,10 and that this requires PRIMPOL primase activity10,16,17,18, leading to the formation of daughter-strand gaps5,19. Current models indicate that PRIMPOL is recruited to replication forks through a direct interaction with RPA9,11,16. However, because RPA is constitutively associated with the lagging-strand template, it is unclear whether this mode of recruitment is sufficient for efficient PRIMPOL priming on the leading strand, or whether an additional mechanism directs PRIMPOL specifically to the leading-strand template to rescue stalled replication forks.
Reconstituting human replication fork restart
To investigate leading-strand repriming by PRIMPOL in the context of the replisome, we leveraged a system that we recently developed to assemble fast and efficient human replisomes from purified proteins20. In this approach, CMG is first loaded onto the forked end of a 9.7-kb DNA template before additional proteins are added to assemble replisomes that perform robust leading- and lagging-strand DNA replication (Fig. 1a). Pol ε performs the bulk of leading-strand synthesis, whereas Pol α–primase and Pol δ replicate the lagging strand. RFC and CTF18-RFC load the polymerase processivity factor PCNA onto the lagging and leading strands, respectively, and TIMELESS (TIM)–TIPIN, CLASPIN and AND1 associate with CMG to promote rapid replisome progression20. To monitor fork stalling and restart, a single cyclobutane pyrimidine dimer (CPD)—an important ultraviolet (UV)-induced DNA lesion and a potent block to replicative polymerases—was introduced to the leading-strand template 3.5 kb from the forked end1,20.
Figure 1b shows that the CPD is a potent block to leading-strand DNA synthesis (3.5-kb stall lead product in denaturing gel), which causes replication forks to stall, as visualized by the slowly migrating species in the native gel (stalled fork). Similar replisome behaviour was observed when the CPD was substituted for a tetrahydrofuran abasic site (Extended Data Fig. 1a). Addition of PRIMPOL caused a reduction in the stalled fork population and the accumulation of full-length products in the native gel, concomitant with the appearance of an approximately 6.2-kb product in the denaturing gel (Fig. 1b, lanes 5–8, restart lead product). This result strongly indicated that PRIMPOL was promoting fork restart by leading-strand repriming. To confirm that the 6.2-kb products resulted from leading-strand repriming, products were analysed by two-dimensional gel electrophoresis (Fig. 1c). This showed that the 6.2-kb products were exclusively associated with the full-length product in the native gel and were roughly twice as intense as the 3.5-kb stall leading-strand product, as expected for bona fide restart products. Moreover, replication of the CPD template in the presence of a primase-defective PRIMPOL mutant (ZnFD2A)10 generated only stalled leading strands and Okazaki fragments (Extended Data Fig. 1b). These data illustrate the successful reconstitution of replication fork restart by leading-strand repriming using purified human proteins.
Replication fork restart also occurred efficiently after polymerase stalling at an abasic site (Extended Data Fig. 1c), suggesting that the mechanism of repriming is agnostic to the chemical nature of the initial blocking lesion. Restart also occurred independently of Pol δ (Extended Data Fig. 1d) and Pol α–primase (Extended Data Fig. 1c), illustrating that only Pol ε is required to extend the primer made by PRIMPOL in this system. Therefore, unless stated, Pol δ is omitted from subsequent experiments to avoid complications from strand-displacement synthesis.
Unlike Pol α–primase, which has a strong preference for rNTPs for primer synthesis21, following the 5′ rNTP, PRIMPOL incorporates dNTPs with a much higher efficiency than rNTPs owing to a sugar discrimination mechanism7,8,22. Consistent with these properties, omitting GTP, CTP and UTP did not compromise leading-strand restart (Extended Data Fig. 1e, compare lanes 7 and 8), indicating that ATP is sufficient for primer initiation in these assays. However, despite previous reports that manganese stimulates PRIMPOL priming activity in vitro8, its addition did not enhance the efficiency of restart (Extended Data Fig. 1f).
PRIMPOL generates small ssDNA gaps
The observation that restart products were close to their maximum possible length (6.2 kb) indicated that they were initiated a very short distance from the lesion. To obtain a high-resolution view of leading-strand restart, we devised a strategy to map priming sites at near single-nucleotide resolution (Fig. 2a). To do so, products were post-replicatively digested with restriction enzymes that cut upstream and downstream of the lesion to liberate fragments that could be resolved in polyacrylamide sequencing gels. Figure 2b shows that, apart from the stalled leading strand and a minor population of read-through products, a ladder of products was generated upon digestion of the PRIMPOL-containing reaction (lane 4). We conclude that each band in the ladder corresponds to a unique leading-strand repriming site. Notably, most of the PRIMPOL-dependent primers used to restart replication were synthesized within 50 nucleotides of the CPD, even when the PRIMPOL concentration was reduced to 5 nM (Extended Data Fig. 2a). An indistinguishable ladder pattern was observed following replication of the abasic site template (Extended Data Fig. 2b), showing that the chemical nature of the DNA lesion plays no part in priming site specification. Because CMG is likely to continue slowly unwinding DNA after polymerase stalling1,2, these data indicate that PRIMPOL activity is targeted to the leading-strand template immediately after polymerase stalling, which limits the size of the daughter-strand gap to generally <50 nucleotides on this template.
DNA sequence influences priming sites
Previous work established that PRIMPOL exhibits sequence preferences for the initiation of primer synthesis23,24,25. We therefore reasoned that the distinct pattern of restart products in our experiments was a consequence of the DNA sequence downstream of the CPD. To test this, we modified a 162-nt region downstream of the CPD (Fig. 2c). Replication of this modified template in the presence of PRIMPOL generated a distinct pattern of restart products to those from the original CPD template (Fig. 2d, compare lanes 3 and 4). This shows that leading-strand repriming by PRIMPOL in the context of the human replisome is modulated by DNA sequence. Importantly, despite the altered sequence, most restart products were again initiated within <50 nucleotides of the CPD, with one prominent product initiated about 100 nt from the CPD (Fig. 2d, lane 4). This is consistent with both templates containing multiple close matches to the preferred tri-nucleotide primer initiation sequence for PRIMPOL25.
PRIMPOL RBMs are not essential in vitro
The discovery that PRIMPOL-mediated repriming occurred a short distance from the CPD on two distinct templates suggested that an efficient mechanism directs PRIMPOL to the exposed leading-strand template. We hypothesized that this was mediated by the RBMs in the PRIMPOL C terminus11, recruiting PRIMPOL to RPA bound to the leading-strand template following polymerase stalling at the CPD and continued template unwinding by CMG. To examine this, we generated two PRIMPOL mutants defective in RPA binding: one lacking both RBMs and the other carrying a mutant in RBM-A (RBM-ARA) that abolished PRIMPOL localization to chromatin in human cells11 (Fig. 3a). Figure 3b shows that both PRIMPOL mutants promoted leading-strand restart with comparable efficiency to wild-type PRIMPOL. Moreover, analysis of restart products showed that both mutants exhibited the same repriming site pattern as wild-type PRIMPOL (Fig. 3c). We conclude that RBM-mediated interactions between PRIMPOL and RPA are non-essential for replication fork restart with reconstituted human replisomes.
PRIMPOL binds directly to CMG
The non-essentiality of the PRIMPOL RBMs for fork restart, together with the very close proximity of repriming to the lesion, prompted us to explore the idea that PRIMPOL might be directed to stalled forks through an interaction with the core replisome. Omission of all non-essential core replisome components individually did not abolish leading-strand restart (Fig. 3d). We therefore hypothesized that PRIMPOL might be recruited directly by the CMG helicase. Consistent with this idea, the pull-down experiment in Fig. 3e shows that PRIMPOL interacts specifically with CMG.
PRIMPOL–CMG cryo-EM structure
To investigate how PRIMPOL is recruited to CMG, and how this might facilitate efficient leading-strand repriming, we determined a structure of PRIMPOL bound to CMG by single-particle cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) (Extended Data Fig. 3a–d and Extended Data Table 1). Well-resolved cryo-EM density was recovered for the PRIMPOL AEP domain alongside the ATPase domains of MCM4 and MCM7 that form part of the C-terminal tier (C-tier) of the two-tiered hexameric MCM ring (Fig. 4a,b). This arrangement is mediated by a conserved 21-aa α-helix at the N terminus of PRIMPOL binding to MCM7 and an approximately 20-aa loop immediately downstream of the helix that is sandwiched between a shallow groove on MCM4 and the PRIMPOL AEP domain (Fig. 4b,c and Extended Data Fig. 4a,b). The PRIMPOL AEP domain is positioned laterally adjacent to the exit of the MCM central pore, through which the unwound leading-strand template emerges. Moreover, superimposing the crystal structure of PRIMPOL bound to primer-template DNA26 onto the AEP domain in our structure shows that its catalytic core is oriented to support primer synthesis directly as template DNA is fed from CMG (Fig. 4d and Extended Data Fig. 4c).
CMG–PRIMPOL interaction enables fork restart
Guided by our cryo-EM structure, to evaluate the role of the CMG–PRIMPOL interaction in replication fork restart, we removed the first 39 aa of PRIMPOL (PRIMPOL∆1–39) (Fig. 5a and Extended Data Fig. 5a). Figure 5b shows that wild-type PRIMPOL but not PRIMPOL∆1–39 was robustly pulled down by CMG, confirming that the PRIMPOL N terminus harbours the primary CMG interaction motifs. PRIMPOL∆1–39 retained primase activity on a ssDNA template (Extended Data Fig. 5b) but was unable to promote fork restart by leading-strand repriming in the human replisome (Fig. 5c,d, lane 4). This defect was not rescued by Pol δ addition (Extended Data Fig. 5c). We conclude that the interaction between PRIMPOL and CMG is essential for leading-strand repriming by reconstituted human replisomes.
We next investigated whether the interaction between PRIMPOL and CMG functions exclusively in PRIMPOL recruitment or whether accurate positioning of the AEP domain is necessary for efficient replication fork restart. Abolishing PRIMPOL interaction with MCM7 by removing the α-helix at the extreme N terminus of PRIMPOL (Fig. 5a, PRIMPOL∆1–21) resulted in a modest reduction in leading-strand restart (Fig. 5c, denaturing, lane 5), with a disproportionate effect on initiation events that occurred closest to the CPD (Fig. 5d, Urea PAGE, lane 5). Retaining α-helix 1–21 but substituting the ensuing loop (aa 22–39) with a glycine–serine linker (Fig. 5a, PRIMPOLGS22–39), or substituting six residues in the loop to alanine (Fig. 5a, PRIMPOL6A loop), resulted in complete loss of restart products (Fig. 5c,d, lanes 6 and 7). These results highlight the importance of this loop, which sits between MCM4 and the PRIMPOL AEP domain, for leading-strand restart.
Furthermore, targeting three loop residues that sit at the interface with MCM4 (Arg31, Leu32 and Ser33; Fig. 5a, PRIMPOL3A loop) resulted in a less severe defect than a mutant targeting the side chains of Tyr28 and Arg29 to disrupt the bridging interaction between MCM4 and the PRIMPOL AEP domain (Fig. 5a, PRIMPOL2A loop) (Fig. 5c–e). This observation strongly suggests that rigid PRIMPOL positioning alongside the MCM C-tier is necessary for efficient leading-strand restart in the human replisome.
RBMs aid restart at low PRIMPOL levels
In our reconstituted system, the interaction between PRIMPOL and CMG (Fig. 5c), but not between PRIMPOL RBMs and RPA (Fig. 3b), is essential for fork restart. Therefore, because PRIMPOL RBM-A is required for PRIMPOL activity in cells9,11,16,17, we hypothesized that the RBM-mediated interaction might serve to increase the local concentration of PRIMPOL at the replication fork. This function may not be required under our standard in vitro reaction conditions, which use a relatively high concentration (20 nM) of PRIMPOL. To test this idea, we performed a titration of wild-type PRIMPOL and PRIMPOLΔRBM. With wild-type PRIMPOL, leading-strand restart became less efficient only when the protein concentration was reduced to 0.1 nM (Fig. 5f and Extended Data Fig. 5d). By contrast, leading-strand restart was compromised at 1 nM PRIMPOLΔRBM and was barely detectable at 0.1 nM. These findings are consistent with a model in which the RBM-mediated interaction between PRIMPOL and RPA facilitates PRIMPOL localization at the replication fork.
PRIMPOL–CMG interaction promotes cisplatin tolerance
Loss of PRIMPOL does not sensitize many human cell lines to DNA damage because of redundant DNA damage tolerance pathways27,28,29. Therefore, to evaluate whether the interaction between PRIMPOL and CMG is required for PRIMPOL function in human cells, we took advantage of our recent finding that loss of PRIMPOL sensitizes translesion synthesis (TLS)-deficient TK6 lymphoblastoid cells to cisplatin, most prominently observed in cells unable to ubiquitinate PCNA at lysine 164 (ref. 27). PRIMPOL was removed by CRISPR–Cas9 targeting in pcnaK164R cells. Figure 5g shows that loss of PRIMPOL further sensitized pcnaK164R cells to cisplatin, consistent with our previous work27. Whereas expression of wild-type PRIMPOL fully rescued this phenotype, expression of PRIMPOL∆1–39 did not complement the additional cisplatin sensitivity of pcnaK164R cells (Fig. 5g, Extended Data Fig. 6 and Extended Data Table 2). These data indicate that the interaction between CMG and PRIMPOL, which is essential for fork restart in vitro, is important for PRIMPOL-mediated DNA damage tolerance in human cells.
A model for PRIMPOL-mediated repriming
On the basis of our results and previous work, we propose the following model for replication fork restart by PRIMPOL-dependent repriming (Fig. 6a). Our biochemical reconstitution has demonstrated that the PRIMPOL RBMs are not essential for leading-strand repriming in vitro. However, they do become important for fork restart at low PRIMPOL concentrations, and evidence from cellular studies shows that RBM-A is required for PRIMPOL recruitment to chromatin and fork progression during replication stress9,11,16,17. To accommodate these observations, we propose that the interaction of PRIMPOL with RPA, which is low affinity11 and therefore likely to be transient, serves primarily to localize PRIMPOL to actively replicating genomic loci, which may be necessary because PRIMPOL is a relatively low abundance protein in human cells15,30 (Fig. 6a).
On encountering a DNA lesion, leading-strand synthesis by Pol ε is blocked, but CMG continues to translocate along the template at a greatly reduced rate1,2, exposing ssDNA on the leading-strand template between the helicase and polymerase (Fig. 6a). Through its interactions with MCM4 and MCM7, PRIMPOL is positioned to capture the exposed ssDNA to initiate leading-strand repriming (Fig. 6a, pathway (i)). After primer synthesis, Pol ε must reengage the leading strand for optimal fork progression to resume. The anchoring of PRIMPOL on CMG suggests a pathway in which the primer might be handed off directly to the leading-strand PCNA loader CTF18-RFC and then to Pol ε. Alternatively, Pol δ might facilitate the recoupling of leading-strand synthesis by extending the primer made by PRIMPOL until it reaches CMG, at which point the 3′ end is transferred to Pol ε. This pathway has been observed following leading-strand lesion bypass by TLS in the yeast replisome31.
The direct interaction of PRIMPOL with CMG raises the possibility that PRIMPOL could travel with CMG to facilitate its immediate deployment on helicase–polymerase uncoupling (Fig. 6a, pathway (ii)). In this regard, it is noteworthy that PRIMPOL does not clash with any known core replisome protein, including Pol ε (Fig. 6b). However, because the MCM4 and MCM7 ATPase domains undergo conformational changes during CMG translocation that could affect PRIMPOL binding, single-molecule studies will be required to determine if PRIMPOL can stably associate with moving replisomes.
