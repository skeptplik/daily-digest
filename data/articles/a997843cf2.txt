A Queensland court has been played a prison recording of the former Bachelor star Sam Wood sobbing as he tells his father that he needs to talk to his wife, Snezana, and that his domestic violence charges would be dropped if she recanted her statements to police.
The court of appeal is hearing a third application by Wood, 46, for bail over allegations he strangled and assaulted his wife while the family was holidaying on the Sunshine Coast.
The court was played a recording of a phone conversation between Wood and his father, Andrew, immediately after he was denied bail by the supreme court this month.
The court of appeal justice David Boddice said the call appeared to amount to a discussion about “classic interfering with witnesses”.
In the call, Wood sobs as he is told that he will remain in Brisbane’s Wolston’s prison.
“Oh no, Dad, oh my God,” he says. “Let me talk to Snez.
“I need to be able to talk to my wife. There’s no way she would want this.”
He goes on to say that if Snezana were to recant her allegations “then surely that opens to get this all changed”.
“But if she changes her statement, makes a statutory declaration to say this actually did not happen, then the police don’t have a leg to stand on and [this] goes away.
“Yes, we’ve been fighting, but I’m a good husband and I’m a great father and I’ve learned my fucking lesson to just control my temper.
“I’m in jail with a bunch of murderers and rapists and paedophiles I’ve never done anything in my whole life. This is disgusting. How is this just?”
Wood’s barrister, Paula Morreau KC, made submissions about the call and denied it amounted to a suggestion of interfering with a witness.
“What the appellant said on that call was ill-judged,” Morreau said. “At its height it contains an expression of desire for contact with his wife, for the charges to be withdrawn. The context matters.”
Wood’s legal team is proposing that conditions – including that Wood live in Victoria and be prohibited from contacting his wife – can mitigate any risk of a breach.
Morreau said the comments on the recording had been made in a state of “emotion”.
Boddice responded: “Bail conditions are breached in states of emotion. That’s the whole point of conditions. It’s the danger that in a state of emotion sanity will not prevail.”
Wood’s legal team is asking the appeal court to overturn a supreme court decision to refuse bail, which could keep the fitness influencer in the Brisbane prison for more than a year until a trial can be held.
He has been in custody for more than six weeks, since his arrest on 4 October.
Wood is charged with two domestic violence offences: assault occasioning bodily harm and strangulation in a domestic setting. The strangulation charge places Wood in a “show cause” position where there is a presumption against bail unless he can demonstrate he does not pose a risk of reoffending.
His legal team have indicated he will fight the charges and he has not yet entered a plea.
This month the supreme court justice Patrick McCafferty said Wood posed “an unacceptable risk” of further offending and should remain in custody. The court heard that a trial might not take place until 2028.
The supreme court heard claims that Wood used drugs and alcohol, engaged in a “party lifestyle” and “struggles with anger”.
Morreau told the supreme court he had had been suffering “significant stressors” but “he usually leaves and takes time”.
“He shows he has the capacity for self-control,” she said.
The supreme and magistrates courts have heard that Snezana Wood has alleged in a video-recorded statement that Wood had banged her head on the floor of a Noosa holiday rental unit and choked her until she was unable to breathe and “thought she was going to die”.
