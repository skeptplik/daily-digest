Norway says hackers exploited Microsoft security flaw to attack energy firms Equinor, Skagerak Energi
Editor's note: This article was updated with comment from Microsoft at 1:00 p.m. EST on Monday.
Norwegian energy companies Equinor and Skagerak Energi are among the organizations affected by a Microsoft cloud security vulnerability that hackers have exploited to target sensitive credentials, Norway's cybersecurity authorities said.
The vulnerability affected Azure API Management (APIM), a Microsoft service that allows organizations to manage and secure connections between applications and other systems. According to Norway's National Security Authority (NSM), an unidentified threat actor has been actively targeting access tokens and other sensitive information stored in the platform.
Equinor, one of Europe's largest natural gas suppliers, and electricity producer Skagerak Energi both reported cyberattacks last week.
The vulnerability was first discovered by Norway's energy sector, with Equinor playing a key role in identifying the issue and alerting authorities, the agency said on Friday.
Equinor said it detected the attack last Tuesday but did not fully contain it until Friday. The incident disrupted some internal services, although the company's oil and gas production continued normally.
"There is no indication that data has been stolen or altered," Equinor said in its latest statement, adding that the attack did not appear to have specifically targeted the company.
Skagerak Energi, which produces and distributes electricity and is majority-owned by Norway's state-owned renewable energy company Statkraft, disclosed a separate cyberattack on Wednesday.
The company said the incident had not affected its operations or electricity production and that it had found no indication that information had been compromised.
In a non-public security advisory reportedly sent to customers and shared by a purported recipient on Reddit, Microsoft said it discovered on November 18 a security issue affecting the older “Classic” tiers of Azure API Management and closed the access path the following day.
Microsoft warned that hackers may have accessed passwords and other sensitive information stored in vulnerable APIM systems before the company discovered the issue.
The problem mainly affected customers who had not followed Microsoft's security recommendations or had stored sensitive information directly in APIM instead of using Azure Key Vault, a separate service designed to keep such data secure. Newer versions of APIM were not affected, the company said.
Microsoft did not disclose how many customers may have been exposed or whether the same threat actor was responsible for the incidents involving the Norwegian energy companies.
On Saturday, Microsoft published guidance explaining how affected customers could identify and replace potentially exposed credentials. The guidance did not explicitly acknowledge the vulnerability or link it to the Norwegian attacks.
Microsoft told Recorded Future News it had identified and fixed a security flaw affecting several tiers of its Azure API Management service, including Developer, Basic, Standard and Premium. The company said it had notified potentially affected customers and provided guidance on how to protect their systems. A company spokesperson said they could not provide further details.
Norwegian authorities said the vulnerability affected organizations across multiple sectors in Norway and abroad.
"It remains unclear how extensively the vulnerability is being exploited or has been exploited in the past," said Martin Albert-Hoff, NSM's director of operational cybersecurity.
He added that authorities had found no evidence that successful or attempted compromises had disrupted services at Norwegian organizations.
NSM warned that more hackers could try to exploit the vulnerability now that it is publicly known and urged organizations using Azure API Management to take steps to protect their systems.
"The national cyber risk level is high," Albert-Hoff said. "Incidents in recent weeks confirm that the risk of cyber operations remains elevated."
