PRESS REVIEW – Wednesday, November 25: Some 21 percent of women say they have suffered "economic violence", while others are exploring new types of justice for their abusers. In other news, recent leaks on Russian troop losses show 349 deaths in Mali, Russia's biggest military deployment in Africa. As Spain prepares to head to the polls, Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez announces a raft of social measures. Finally, a plucky young cat from Essex has taken a train to London.
Some 21 percent of women have suffered "economic violence", says French newspaper Libération, citing research by Crédit Mutuel bank. Examples include preventing a partner from taking up employment, keeping them in the dark about debts or loans, imposing an unfair split of expenditure, or denying them the freedom to choose how they spend their money. Some 69 percent of women who faced economic violence also faced other forms of abuse.
Meanwhile, as convictions remain rare for acts of sexist and sexual violence, journalists at L'Humanité have spent time with a reparative justice organisation in Toulouse. La Valise offers victims of these crimes a mediated confrontation with their abusers, so that they might see the abuse acknowledged and even receive an apology. With only 10 percent of these crimes reported to the police, it's one possible avenue for some of the other 90 percent to seek closure.
Next, as part of a major leak to the Guardian on Russian war losses, the newspaper reveals that 349 Russian soldiers have died in Mali fighting rebel groups in support of the ruling junta, replacing a long-running French counter-terrorism operation in the country. It's possible that Kyiv has pushed the number up, as recent reporting by CNN showed Ukraine had sent people to support Mali's rebel groups in order to exact higher battlefield losses on Moscow.
As Spain prepares to go to the polls on Sunday, ABC calls Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez an "out of control populist", as he decrees a raft of new social measures ahead of Sunday's election. It's an election Chatham House suggests will be a test case for Europe's "far-right firewall".
Finally, Zeus the cat is a serial escape artist but he made his longest journey yet when he boarded a train to London, The Times explains. He lives opposite a railway station, so the temptation is understandable, and he has also been known to attempt to board taxis and buses. After his recent escapade, though, employees of the local train line have his photograph, and have been warned of his travel-savvy tendencies.
