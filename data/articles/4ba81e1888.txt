These are the stories making headlines in fashion on Monday.
School of the Art Institute of Chicago graduating senior Talia Bourne has won the 2026 Supima Design Competition during the second annual Supima Design Lab, held on Nov. 5 in New York City. Bourne and six other finalists presented a capsule collection using premium Supima cotton fabrics. Bourne’s winning collection drew inspiration from the lifecycle of knots as they unravel. She took home a $10,000 cash prize. {Fashionista Inbox}
Depop and “Chicken Shop Date”‘s Amelia Dimoldenberg have launched a collaborative collection of curated secondhand fashion. The collection spotlights Depop listings inspired by Dimoldenberg’s signature looks and those of her celebrity guests. On Nov. 12, Depop will also unveil “Amelia’s Shop Drop,” an edit featuring items from Dimoldenberg’s personal wardrobe. {Fashionista Inbox}
Max Mara Fashion Group quietly launched M-M For Mondays and More, a contemporary label centered around the capsule wardrobe. The brand is rooted in minimalism, modularity and movement, and offers pieces women can wear in their day-to-day life. M-M For Mondays and More is available at select retailers across Europe, as well as through its own online store. {WWD/paywalled}
At the U.N.’s annual COP31 climate summit — which kicked off on Monday in Antalya, Turkey — world leaders and policymakers plan to discuss electrification, climate finance, green industrialization and the circular economy. The fashion world is paying close attention to COP31, as how government leaders respond to climate issues largely affects the industry. Climate extremes have especially impacted the manufacturing industry and threatened garment workers’ health and productivity. However, even if policymakers addressed fashion’s climate needs, it will be difficult to scale due to significant financial investments, mounting economic uncertainty and regulatory rollbacks in the U.S. and EU. {Business of Fashion/paywalled}
Podcasts have emerged as major streaming platforms, with the global podcast industry reportedly generating $9.2 billion in sales in 2025. As a result, podcasters have gained significant cultural relevance, and fashion brands are tapping in: Luxury houses like Dior and Louis Vuitton invited podcasters to sit front row at their fashion shows, while Alo Yoga tapped “Therapuss”‘s Jake Shane as a brand ambassador. That said, as brands tap into the podcasting space, these partnerships must feel authentic, or podcasters risk losing their audience. {Vogue Business/paywalled}
