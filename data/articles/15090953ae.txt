You’ve set the date and selected the ceremony gown; now, it’s time to consider your after-party look. This is your chance to loosen the rules and experiment with something less traditional. In fact, the best thing about a second (or third!) dress is the flexibility it offers: Keep things sleek and understated in a silky mini, or make a statement with sequins and embellishments. The hardest part may be settling on just one outfit for the dance floor. (A word to the wise: Be sure to choose the right shoes.)
Vogue’s Favorite Wedding After-Party Dresses:
Now for the fun part: We took a deep dive into all things bridal white—and metallic! and fringed! and feathered!—to bring you a range of styles for a standout after-party outfit change. You’ll find a mix of favorites from beloved bridal designers, including Danielle Frankel, Lein, Clio Peppiatt, and Vivienne Westwood. Browse minimalist picks from St. Agni and Róhe, vintage-inspired options from Dôen and Simone Rocha, and an iconic sequined mini by Rabanne—it’s practically built for the dance floor. Affordable pieces make the list, too: L’Agence’s ’90s-inspired maxi is a timeless choice, and Mango’s embellished slip is great for the bride who loves a bit of soft glam.
Below, shop our edit of the 52 best wedding after-party dresses—and get ready to celebrate in style.
The Playful Embellishments
Feathers, bows, and beading make for the most joyful kind of reception dress. Rachel Gilbert’s feather-hemmed mini is irresistibly flirty; Clio Peppiatt and Markarian scatter pearl beads and crystal appliqués across otherwise simple silhouettes. Consider it permission to be a little more daring once the vows are said.
The Modern Romantic
Romance doesn’t have to end at the ceremony. Between Danielle Frankel’s silk mini, complete with a voluminous skirt, Givenchy's floral lace number, and Shushu/Tong’s bow-adorned dress, there’s plenty of reason to keep the aesthetic going after dark.
The Metallic Moment
Bring the glitz to the after party in a metallic dress that’s guaranteed to keep all eyes on you. Simkhai, Ilkyaz Ozel, and Rabanne go all-out disco ball, covering their designs in head-to-toe sequins. Another route is Emilia Wickstead’s floral cloqué mini, which trades sparkle for a more subtle silver sheen.
The Buttoned Beauty
It doesn’t take much to amp up a tailored silhouette. A row of three buttons adds a touch of whimsy to Self-Portrait’s soft yellow mini, while Aje opts for buttoned cuffs on its long-sleeve, bubble-skirt dress. Or, consider Destree’s fringed-thread mini if you’re after something unique.
The Vintage-Inspired
For a second look with a nod to old-school romance, turn to silky fabrics, lace trim, and lingerie-inspired silhouettes. Dôen’s puff-sleeve midi is soft and feminine; Ganni’s luxe satin, boudoir-inspired style channels the glamour of time-honored nightwear; Posse’s floral-embroidered design brings a fresh, present-day spin to the look.
The After-After-Party
When the night stretches into the wee hours, something slinky, fitted, and, dare we say, risqué feels entirely appropriate. These pieces from Wiederhoeft, Fendi, Sir, and Alaïa pull out all the stops—you’ll just have to decide whether you want all-over sequins, something semi-sheer, or a sexy strapless option.
The Minimalist Musts
On the other hand, there’s no reason to wear something metallic and feathered if it’s just not in your style repertoire. Stay true to your minimalist aesthetic with clean-cut minis from Balenciaga and Solace London, or embrace slip styles with St. Agni and Róhe. All are pared-back, but just as party-ready as the rest of our list.
The Sateen Dream
Few fabrics feel as undeniably bridal as glossy satin. L’Agence, Rixo, and Fforme offer understated polish for anyone on the hunt for a no-fuss dress. Elsewhere, Reformation channels Hollywood glam with a slinky halterneck maxi, tailor-made for a night of dancing.
The Something Blue
Why save your “something blue” for your shoes or jewelry? These bridal dresses are an easy way to incorporate the adage. From Acne Studios’s slip silhouette to Galvan’s bias-cut satin mini and McQueen’s off-the-shoulder mini, consider keeping to tradition by way of a non-traditional after-party look.
The Floral Accents
Pull inspiration from the bridal bouquet; once the real one’s been tossed, you’re ready to celebrate hands-free. Khaite brings texture via rose-print jacquard fabric; Rat & Boa delivers an ethereal feel with its silk-tulle maxi that features an oversized, hand-stitched hibiscus; Magda Butrym adds a softer touch with a floral-inspired neckline.
The Soft Sculpture
Gathered detailing adds an artistic flair to otherwise simple shapes. Vivienne Westwood and Victoria Beckham carefully use draped corsets to accentuate the body. Stella McCartney and Liberowe take a different approach, relying on soft gathering to create a more effortless silhouette.
The Statement Sleeves
Ready to ditch the veil but don’t want to lose the drama for the after-party? Look no further than little white dresses with statement sleeves. Reiss’s mini fits the bill perfectly with its one-sleeved, draped design. For more excitement, go with Lapointe; there’s a bit of puffed-up volume in its feathered cuffs.
The Fringe Effect
These dresses were made for the spotlight (and the photographer). Go long with Zara’s ’20s-inspired frock, turn heads with Staud’s pearlescent sequin mini dress, or keep it short, sweet, and trendy with Taller Marmo’s iteration. Each makes a strong case for choosing your second outfit based not just on how it looks standing still, but on how it moves once the music starts.
