Was Google feeling a little left out of the recent wave of tech outages? The company took to X on Tuesday to announce that its Gmail service is actually running just fine. In a post, the company said it was not currently seeing evidence of a broad Gmail service disruption and reminded users they could always check Gmail’s real-time status on its online dashboard.
As it turns out, the company was trying to head off reports of a Gmail outage that began circulating on X on Tuesday around the same time that Apple was seeing a number of verified outages of its own. While some posts on the social network suggested Gmail was experiencing a disruption, others said they were not having any problems.
The concerns seemed to stem from an X post by Downdetector, a crowdsourced website where users can report service disruptions themselves and see if others are having problems. Downdetector is not an official source for verifying or understanding the true scope of internet outages, and as people become more concerned about possible cyberattacks, the site’s data may become less valuable over time if every minor glitch and hiccup is reported as an “outage” by non-technical users.
The fact that Google had to announce it was not having an outage speaks to the growing anxiety around the reliability of major tech services. Over the past week, Apple and Microsoft experienced outages, an FAA network failure led to a nationwide ground stop, and some banks and airlines also reported disruptions.
However, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said on Fox Business Network’s “The Claman Countdown” on Tuesday that the U.S. is not currently facing coordinated cyberattacks. He told the outlet, “We do not see the indicia of a coordinated cyberattack — let me just be clear about that — and we do not see the indicia of state-sponsored activity here.”
Instead, Clayton attributed the rise in issues to businesses patching their systems. The patching may be a downstream effect of AI, as newer models have become increasingly adept at uncovering previously unknown vulnerabilities. “They’re upgrading them, they’re putting patches in place,” he said, adding that this is happening at “a much larger scale” than before.
As a result, some of the systems are “not coming back as expected in all cases,” he added.
