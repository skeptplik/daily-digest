In the United States, a sudden deluge of Christmas commercials on Nov. 1 is a sure sign that winter is fast approaching. But in Japan, the true harbinger of the approaching oshōgatsu (new year) holidays is when shops from ordinary konbini (convenience stores) to the most posh depachika (department store basement malls) start advertising they’re now accepting reservations for osechi ryōri.
This multicourse smorgasbord of traditional dishes is usually eaten on New Year’s Day and for several days after, and its preparation in the final weeks of the year was usually part of the classic year-end rush of cooking, cleaning and meeting friends and family.
Though buying a premade osechi ryōri has become more popular in recent decades, making just one of the traditional dishes can be an effective way to impress any guests — especially any family members you’ll be seeing for the first time in a while during new year get-togethers.
