The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth takes place eight years after Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood and follows the life of stuntman Cliff Booth (Brad Pitt) after Rick Dalton (Leonardo DiCaprio) moves out of the country. Booth now performs dirty odd jobs for celebrities to keep himself afloat, and his life is just as exciting as it was before.
Tarantino decided not to direct this movie, as he wants his 10th and (allegedly) final film to be something bigger. He wrote the script and handed it to David Fincher, known for Se7en (1995), Fight Club (1999), and Gone Girl (2014). Polygon's Jake Kleinman noted that this director swap gave the movie a "moody, noir-ish score"; it was still "beautiful to behold, and Fincher's camera luxuriates in that aesthetic even more than Tarantino." What fans noticed was the use of generative AI in the film, but the director has an explanation of sorts.
David Fincher used AI to reduce production time
Speaking on The Ringer's "Big Picture" podcast, David Fincher admitted to relying on AI tools in the post-production of The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth. In previous movies, Fincher, a known perfectionist, would make actors shoot scenes over 50 times. Fincher says AI helps him achieve creative focusand easily address imperfectionswithout having to reshoot a scene. In The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, Fincher only kept actors an additional four to five days to re-record lines over scenes. Afterward, he was able to sync up "the best thing on the eye" with "the best thing on the ear" and create the perfect take.
“It’ll still be based on real human beings,” Fincher said. “It’ll still be based on real humans, the way humans move and how they traverse a set and how they, how they pull up their trousers before they sit down or take their coat off. All that stuff will be there, people will only have to do it 15 times.”
While Fincher notes that AI is a great way to handle small flaws in a take, such as making sure "that hair doesn’t cross right in front of your cornea," it seems to have been used to make more noticeable changes. This interview took place before allegations emerged about The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth utilizing blatant generative AI, such as a scene with a carriage with a horse on the wrong side.
Previously, speaking to the Los Angeles Times, Finch said that he didn’t fear the emergence of AI as a filmmaking tool: “It exists. Should we not use it?”
The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is now playing in IMAX theaters and is coming to Netflix on Dec. 23.
