Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea, but not all the dramatic footage circulating online is real. Multiple AI-generated videos falsely show the bridge collapsed with extensive damage. Some even used branding resembling a Ukrainian news agency to appear credible. While the bridge was indeed struck and damaged, the viral videos were fabricated using AI, as Vedika Bahl explains.
The 19-kilometre bridge, which connects Russia to the occupied Crimean peninsula, was struck in Ukrainian attacks over two consecutive nights this week. The strikes caused genuine damage to the crossing, but some of the most dramatic images shared online do not show what actually happened.
One account, called Sueta News, published two videos claiming to show the aftermath of the attacks. The first purported to show vehicles unable to cross the bridge, with damage to the road surface in two places and a visibly deformed supporting arch. A second video, filmed from a closer angle, appeared to show large sections of the bridge collapsing into the water, with debris allegedly blocking the shipping channel.
However, analysis using Google's SynthID detector identified AI-generated content in both videos. The tool detects invisible digital watermarks embedded in content created or edited using certain AI technologies. One video was identified as having been generated using Google AI, while the other carried a watermark associated with a SynthID partner.
There are also visual inconsistencies that expose the footage as fake.
In the first video, the railway bridge running alongside the road crossing has a straight, linear structure. But authentic photographs published by news agencies show a much more curved and intricate design. The video also contains inconsistencies between its own frames, with the appearance of the damaged arch changing between shots.
The investigation also uncovered another video attempting to pass itself off as footage from an established Ukrainian news organisation.
The clip carried a prominent UNN logo and claimed that both road and rail traffic had been halted, with debris blocking the shipping channel.
The video was also AI-generated. Although the original Instagram post carried an AI-generated content label, SynthID independently detected a watermark indicating that the footage had been created using Google AI.
The account behind the video raised further questions.
The logo appeared to refer to the Ukrainian National News agency, an established Ukrainian media organisation. However, the video was not published by the genuine news outlet, despite the Instagram handle being @unn.media.
Instead, the account linked to a different website calling itself the United News Network, which claimed to be based in Ukraine, though had a Singapore-based address, alongside a US phone number. Sueta News, the account behind the other fabricated videos, is also among its associated outlets.
The official Ukrainian National News agency does not list @unn.media among its official social media channels, and there is no evidence linking it to the account that published the video. The branding therefore risked giving viewers the impression that the footage had been shared by a recognised Ukrainian news organisation, when it had not.
The fake videos emerged as reports of the Ukrainian strikes were still developing, alongside genuine images of damage released by Ukraine's Security Service.
The distinction is important: the attacks on the Kerch Bridge were real, and the bridge did sustain damage. But the viral videos purporting to show its extensive destruction were AI-generated.
