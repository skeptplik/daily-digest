Ten leading designers have unveiled posters that celebrate 10 years of London’s Design Museum in Kensington, which was completed by OMA, Allies and Morrison and John Pawson in 2016.
Originally located in an old 1940s banana warehouse on the south bank of the River Thames, the Design Museum opened at its current site in Kensington, west London, on 24 November 2016.
To mark this 10-year anniversary, the museum asked 10 international designers to create posters for the occasion.
The lineup includes British graphic designer Margaret Calvert, who is known for creating the seminal 1960s Motorway and Transport typefaces with fellow British designer Jock Kinneir.
The hugely popular typefaces were invented to clarify the UK’s road signs and are still used today.
Calvert designed a subtle road sign-style poster for the Design Museum’s anniversary, depicting a fork in the road between Kensington and Shad Thames, where the original museum stood between 1989 and 2016.
Former Apple designer Jony Ive also designed a poster in collaboration with his studio LoveFrom, which he founded with Australian practitioner Marc Newson in 2019 after his departure from the technology brand.
Ive and LoveFrom‘s poster features a minimalist grey and white design composed of two understated shapes that resemble the number 10.
American filmmaker Tim Burton is also among the lineup and applied his trademark playful drawings to a poster illustrated with two characters sitting atop the number 10.
Burton’s extensive portfolio was on display at the Design Museum in 2024, charting 50 years of his cinematic career.
Dutch industrial designer Hella Jongerius, meanwhile, created a poster depicting a colourful woven tapestry in a nod to her work, which typically blends contemporary technology with traditional crafts.
The other six designers who make up the lineup are London studio A Practice for Everyday Life, Brazilian graphic designer Marina Willer, British fashion designer Paul Smith and British designers Sebastian Conran, Simone Brewster and Tom Dixon.
The Kensington Design Museum is located within the former Commonwealth Institute building.
Dutch architecture practice OMA worked alongside London office Allies and Morrison to restore the shell of the Grade II*-listed building, while British designer Pawson conceived the museum’s interiors.
