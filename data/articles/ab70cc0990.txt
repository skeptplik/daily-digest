Thousands rally in Istanbul against Turkey’s crackdown on opposition and media
Thousands marched in Istanbul on Sunday against a widening crackdown on Turkey’s opposition and independent media, days after police raided Sozcu TV and arrested senior staff. The main opposition Yeni Parti organised the protest after authorities installed a government-appointed trustee at the broadcaster, following similar moves against other independent outlets.
Several thousand people marched through Istanbul on Sunday to protest a growing crackdown on opposition and independent media outlets.
The protest was called by the main opposition Yeni Parti days after police raided Sozcu TV, installing a government-appointed trustee and arresting senior staff members.
It was the third such takeover in a year after similar moves against Tele1, Halk TV, Turkey's most-watched news channel and leading digital news website T24.
Press freedom has come under sustained pressure over the past decade through prosecutions, digital censorship and government accusations that journalists are spreading "fake news", prompting criticism from rights groups at home and abroad.
Carrying a huge banner reading: "We want freedom of thought, freedom of the press and freedom for journalists," protesters marched through the Besiktas neighbourhood, chanting, cheering and whistling, an AFP journalist said.
As they reached the shores of the Bosphorus, several voiced concern at the narrowing space for voices perceived as critical of President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and his government.
"Our press has been silenced, all opposition channels have been silenced!" a 64-year-old protester called Tunay told AFPTV, without giving her surname.
"All our freedoms are on the verge of being restricted," she sighed. "As a country, we are very unhappy. We are anxious about the future."
The direction Turkey was heading in was "not good", agreed another demonstrator called Koksal, also 64.
"We are living through a period in which all democratic rights have disappeared," he told AFP.
"All democratic journalists and opposition television channels are being shut down, blacked out!" he explained, saying that he, like many others, were being forced to get their news from social media.
"But they are shutting down social media too, all forms of it that are effective."
Over the past two months, Turkey has arrested several journalists over their coverage of a multi-billion-dollar investment fund scandal, at least two of whom remain in pre-trial detention.
Twenty-five journalists are currently behind bars in Turkey, according to figures from the MLSA media rights group.
