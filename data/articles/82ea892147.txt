In recent years, Nathan Fielder has rightfully earned a place as one of the most provocative figures in modern comedy. Most recently, the actor, filmmaker, and pilot (it’s a long story) co-directed You Can See Everything, a three-hour, brain-bending documentary about Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes and her partner Billy Evans in the days before Holmes began her 11-year prison sentence for defrauding investors.
Despite this seemingly obscure premise, the A24 movie has exceeded all expectations, grossing an impressive $93 million on a $7 million budget to become the 10th highest-grossing documentary of all time. The film has received widespread critical acclaim, with a 97% Tomatometer score and an even more impressive 97 average review score on Metacritic. Audiences are equally enthusiastic; the movie has a 95% user score on Rotten Tomatoes.
When is You Can See Everything coming to streaming?
According to WhenToStream’s sources, You Can See Everything is coming to Video On Demand (VOD) and digital platforms on Nov. 24, 2026. You Can See Everything is still in theaters in the U.S. and hasn’t opened worldwide yet. It’s coming to the U.K. on Nov. 20, and to France on Dec. 30.
Why has You Can See Everything generated so much buzz?
It’s difficult to explain what makes You Can See Everything so mesmerizing without spoiling its bizarre twists and turns, but the gist is that the movie pitches itself as one thing, before slowly spiraling into something else. Fielder once again shows self-referential circumstances built around deception and performance.
The movie intersects with many contemporary issues, including the increasing right-wing radicalization of tech bros, and grows increasingly meta until it eventually calls into question whether documentaries can really uncover the “truth” at all. There’s a lot going on, and it’s best to see the madness unfold yourself.
