The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) warned federal agencies that attackers are now exploiting a recently patched critical Cisco Expressway vulnerability in the wild.
Cisco Expressway Series devices are enterprise collaboration gateways (deployed as virtual machines or physical hardware appliances) that connect internal voice and video systems with outside networks and remote users via a secure connection.
Tracked as CVE-2026-76466, this security flaw stems from the use of hard-coded credentials and a failure to fully validate client certificates, enabling unauthenticated attackers to remotely execute arbitrary operating system commands as root on affected Cisco Expressway-C and Cisco Expressway-E devices configured in a cluster.
"An attacker with possession of the cookies could exploit this vulnerability by connecting to the database service and invoking specific remote procedure call (RPC) commands," Cisco said in late October, when it released security patches to address the issue.
While Cisco says that its Product Security Incident Response Team (PSIRT) is not aware of active exploitation in the wild or publicly available proof-of-concept exploits targeting this vulnerability, CISA tagged it as actively exploited on Friday.
The cybersecurity agency also added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) Catalog on Tuesday and ordered US Federal Civilian Executive Branch (FCEB) agencies to secure their systems within three days, as mandated by Binding Operational Directive (BOD) 26-04.
Although BOD 26-04 targets only federal agencies, CISA urged all network defenders, including private-sector security teams, to prioritize patching this flaw to block ongoing attacks that may target their networks.
"These types of vulnerabilities are a frequent attack vector for malicious cyber actors and pose significant risks to the federal enterprise," it warned.
Internet intelligence company Censys now observes nearly 14,900 Cisco Expressway hosts exposed online, but there is no information on how many have already secured against attacks targeting the CVE-2026-76466 vulnerability.
Exploited to gain root on Internet-exposed devices
While CISA has yet to share details on these ongoing attacks, Russian cybersecurity company F6 said on Thursday that it spotted attackers using an unnamed AI assistant to successfully exploit CVE-2026-7646 and gain remote command execution with full root privileges on Internet-exposed Cisco Expressway devices.
"The attackers initially compromised two Cisco Expressway internet-facing devices. Through one of them, they gained access to two additional cluster nodes located on the organization’s internal network," F6 security researchers noted.
"After obtaining root access, the attackers began preparing tooling for further lateral movement across the infrastructure. They used custom Python scripts designed to scan the network, discover accessible SMB resources, interact with SQL Server and WinRM, and explore GitLab repositories in order to exfiltrate credentials and other sensitive information."
Two weeks ago, CISA also flagged a high-severity Cisco BroadWorks vulnerability (CVE-2026-20320) as actively exploited in attacks and ordered federal agencies to patch their systems by November 24.
According to threat intelligence firm Malbear Labs, a Chinese-speaking threat actor abused CVE-2026-20320 in autonomous AI attacks targeting a European telecommunications provider and 13 organizations that used its data centers and cloud infrastructure.
CISA has added 103 Cisco vulnerabilities to its list of actively exploited flaws since November 2021, seven of which ransomware gangs have also used in the wild.
