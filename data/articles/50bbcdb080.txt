European politicians react to the 'powerful political signal' out of US
Left-leaning Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez, who has repeatedly clashed with Donald Trump in the past year and who faces snap elections himself at the end of the month, said that American society "voted against the policies that only impoverish," such as hate, machismo, climate denialism and the privileges of the elites.
He drew parallels to Brazil's presidential election and the early vote he called, facing a legislative deadlock with his minority government, in Spain.
"It happened in Brazil. It just happened in the US. And it will happen soon in Spain," Sanchez wrote. "The ultra wave that [Alberto Nunez] Feijoo and [Santiago] Abascal are trying to ride is no longer growing: it is crumbling."
French Finance Minister Roland Lescure reminded BFM TV that even after losing control of Congress, Trump would remain in charge on the vast majority of US foreign policy issues, and seemed to warn that the president might not prove the type to favor compromise and collaboration.
Lescure said, "Either he decides to rise above the fray, acknowledging that changes are necessary," particularly on issues like gasoline prices and his foreign policy, "or he does not." Lescure said this would be something "we will need to keep an eye on in the coming days."
Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store told Reuters news agency it was a "powerful political signal about a change of course among American voters." He said voices were emerging in US politics that "are closer to us than the president and the administration have stood for, for example on climate and environment, and in foreign policy and development aid policy."
The co-leader of Germany's Green Party, Franziska Brantner, said the result was a "stop sign towards Trump and his billionaires." She said that while this pointed to "hope" for the US democratic system "hopefully getting back on its feet," it was too soon to breathe a sigh of relief in the European Union.
"On the contrary, we must really bet on Europe and on our own strengths," Brantner said. She said Trump had made it clear that he did not respect the EU and wanted to divide it, and that he wanted less and less to do with supporting Ukraine. "That means, we have our own responsibility for ourselves, no matter what the final result."
Christel Schaldemose, a European Parliament vice president from Denmark, said she was "cautiously optimistic" on Europe's behalf.
"Not because everything will now change for the better, but because it could benefit support for Ukraine, the green transition, and relations with the rest of the world," she said. But she also added that the EU must continue its course of standing on its own two feet, saying "that's not up for debate."
Ramaphosa sees 'new era' after Democratic wins
South African President Cyril Ramaphosa said the Democratic sweep of Congress could usher in a "new era in world relations" after voters dealt President Donald Trump a major electoral setback.
"The Democrats have taken control of the Senate and of the House, and the people of America have spoken," Ramaphosa told reporters. "I do think that this is going to introduce a new era in world relations."
Relations between the US and South Africa have deteriorated under Trump. During a televised Oval Office meeting in May 2025, he confronted Ramaphosa with misleading footage and news clippings promoting false claims of a genocide against white South Africans. Ramaphosa repeatedly sought to rebut the allegations.
The Trump administration has also cut aid, expelled South Africa's ambassador and, this week, moved to end its duty-free access to the US market under the African Growth and Opportunity Act from January.
Ramaphosa said South Africa would continue trading with the US while seeking new export markets.
"Diversification has now become the order of the day," he said.

RECAP — Democrats claim control of Congress, flipping several Senate seats
Welcome to our coverage of the US midterm elections, particularly if you're outside US time zones and were tucked up in bed as the poll-closing drama unfolded overnight. Here's the lay of the land stateside in what's now the early hours of the morning-after the vote which many saw as a referendum on President Donald Trump.
The Democrats have fared better than polls predicted, at least based on what should now be reliable projections. They have comfortably taken control of the House of Representatives and claimed a majority in the upper chamber of Congress, the Senate.
In a major blow for the Donald Trump administration, they have swung at least seven of the 35 Senate seats up for grabs — in North Carolina, South Carolina, Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, Texas and Maine. Some of those states were Republican-held for decades; Kansas' two Senate seats had been reliably Republican since before World War II.
Counting in the House races is slower, with all 435 seats up for grabs, but the Democrats have already passed the post to win the majority of the chamber with the better part of 100 seats yet to be called.
These results should markedly strengthen the Democrats' hand in Congress, giving them the power to block legislation (if perhaps not executive orders directly from President Donald Trump) and launch investigations into Trump's administration. In the Senate, they will be able to block his appointees as judges or for senior roles in government.
Trump himself praised the Democrats in an interview on Fox News on election night, saying he thought they did a "good job" and "fought hard."
"I don't view it as a hit on me," he said, despite having urged voters to act as if his name was on the midterm ballots during the campaign.
Democrats claim majority of US governorships
Democrats have won a majority of US governorships for the first time in two decades, capturing offices long held by Republicans in Texas, Ohio, Iowa and Georgia.
The gains give the party more power at the state level to challenge President Donald Trump's policies on immigration, voting rules and the deployment of National Guard troops.
Several victories have also made history:
- Georgia: Former Atlanta Mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms has become the first Black woman elected governor in the US, defeating Republican billionaire Rick Jackson
- Texas: Gina Hinojosa has defeated Republican Governor Greg Abbott to become the state's first Latina governor, despite being outspent five-to-one on advertising
- New Mexico: Deb Haaland, a former US interior secretary, has become the first Native American woman elected governor
In Ohio, Democrat Amy Acton defeated biotech billionaire Vivek Ramaswamy, describing it as "the honor of my lifetime." The victory came after a bitter campaign in which Ramaswamy's use of Acton's estranged mother in an attack ad drew widespread criticism.
In Iowa, state auditor Rob Sand thanked voters for his victory over Republican Zach Lahn. Preliminary results put Sand around 12 percentage points ahead in a state Trump won by roughly 13 points in 2024.
Republicans have held Florida, where Congressman Byron Donalds defeated David Jolly, a former Republican who ran as a Democrat.
The results could also shape the next presidential election. Governors influence election administration, while three Democrats considered potential presidential contenders in 2028 — Pennsylvania's Josh Shapiro, Illinois' JB Pritzker and Maryland's Wes Moore — have won reelection.

German leaders congratulate Democrats, stress foreign policy power remains with the Trump administration
Metin Hakverdi, who serves as German Chancellor Friedrich Merz's coordinator of transatlantic cooperation, said he expects the US midterm results to significantly impact the political situation between Berlin and Washington.
"America has chosen change," he told news agency DPA, while stressing that Trump remains largely in charge of foreign policy and that Germany will continue to reach out to both parties.
"We must especially not limit our contacts to individual political camps," he added, while also highlighting the historic significance of the first Indigenous woman being elected governor of New Mexico and the first black woman as governor of Georgia.
Hakverdi, a Social Democrat, has extended family in the United States and attended high school there.
Defense Minister Boris Pistorius congratulated Democrats on what he called their "historic victory." He also emphasized that the White House will remain the same and Berlin will continue to cooperate with it.

Mood on the ground at an election-night watch party in Detroit
The mood was cheery at the Michigan Democrats' headquarters in Detroit as it became increasingly clear that progressive Abdul El-Sayed was going to win his Senate race.
Crowds gathered at a watch party in Detroit's Eastern Market as results from across the country were projected on a big screen.
Cheers erupted from between the music for every Democratic win.
Attendees voiced surprise that the Democratic Party had already achieved a majority in the Senate without having called the state of Michigan yet.
Others shared concerns about President Trump's repeated announcements to make use of his veto power in response to the Democrats’ majority.
Stocks recover ground after Democratic wins unsettle markets
Global stocks have recovered some early losses after Democrats won control of Congress, while the dollar has fallen to a one-month low.
Technology shares have been among the hardest hit as investors assess what the result could mean for businesses that have benefited from Trump's policies.
Nasdaq futures, which indicate how US technology stocks could trade when markets open, fell more than 2% before recovering to around 0.5% lower.
In Asia, chipmakers Samsung, SK Hynix and TSMC have regained some ground. South Korea's main stock index turned slightly positive after initially dropping 3%.
Investors are concerned that Democrats could push for tighter oversight of artificial intelligence companies and the construction of data centers.
But the initial selling has eased.
"Markets may be recognising that political gridlock doesn't necessarily mean weaker corporate earnings," said Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo.
Investors have also bought government bonds, seen as safer investments, while cryptocurrencies have fallen amid concerns about stronger regulation.
Democrat Rob Sand elected governor of Iowa after 16 years of Republican control
Voters chose Rob Sand as Iowa's next governor, defeating first-time Republican candidate Zach Lahn.
The victory is key for Democrats ahead of the 2028 presidential election, because Iowa's early caucuses play a significant role in shaping national campaigns.
Sand, however, had kept the party at arm's length during his race. Instead, he focused on his centrist policies and stressed his humble upbringing.
"We have to have bigger hearts and minds than just our party," Sand told the crowd at a victory event. "Come January, we will be forced to work together."
He promised to work across the aisle to shape Iowa's future.

Obama urges Democrats to act after election victories
Former US President Barack Obama said the election results showed Americans wanted leaders who brought people together and offered practical solutions.
With President Donald Trump in office for another two years, Obama urged Democrats to use their gains to pursue change and "prevent further damage."
"Tonight, the American people sent a clear message — they want a politics that brings people together rather than divides them," he wrote on X.
Obama credited strong candidates across the country, including in places once considered unlikely prospects for victory. He also thanked election officials and volunteers from both parties for ensuring a smooth vote.
"Our job now is to roll up our sleeves, make changes wherever we have the power to do so, prevent further damage, and lay the groundwork for the future," he said.
