Japanese government bonds rallied across the curve after a three-day holiday, with gains holding even as domestic equities surged on easing concerns over the outlook for artificial intelligence investment.
The benchmark 10-year yield led the moves, falling 7 basis points to 2.715%. Five-year debt fell 5.5 basis points to 2.125%.
The 225-issue Nikkei stock average rose as much as 5% after Jensen Huang’s return to lead Nvidia eased concerns about a slowdown in AI investment. The gains in bonds and stocks showed JGBs holding firm even as risk appetite recovered.
“The calming outlook on energy market disruptions and the U.S. Fed’s policy path in 2027 are the likely explanation for the positive session in the morning for Japanese government bonds,” said Homin Lee, senior macro strategist at Lombard Odier in Singapore.
While short-covering in JGBs appears to be driving long-end yields lower, a strong rebound in AI-related stocks could ease the risk-off mood in financial markets, bringing the possibility of a December rate hike by the Bank of Japan back into play, said Ryutaro Kimura, senior bond strategist at BNP Paribas Asset Management.
Still, major domestic institutional investors appear to be staying on the sidelines as they assess what the Nvidia news means for the global economic outlook in relation to AI, Kimura added.
Japanese yields have swung sharply this month as investors reassess the outlook for inflation, BOJ policy and demand for government debt, after yields climbed to multi-decade highs earlier this year. Investors will be looking to an auction of 40-year government debt on Wednesday for more clarity on bond demand.
