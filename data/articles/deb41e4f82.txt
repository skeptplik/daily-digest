Chinese media have reacted with glee to the dramatic reinstatement of Jensen Huang as the chief executive of Nvidia, with one commentary warning against future “witch-hunts”.
An article published on Tuesday in the state-run outlet the Global Times said US media reports about Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset were “malicious” and “negatively impacted American public perception of China”.
Huang was reinstated to the US artificial intelligence company on Tuesday, weeks after he was ousted by the board after allegations that he had three affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy and two with employees. But the Nvidia board reinstated him after an intervention from Donald Trump.
The Global Times warned that future “political witch-hunts” against companies and individuals accused of links to Chinese intelligence could take the form of investigations and congressional hearings.
“Certain American media outlets and politicians turned the episode into a Hollywood-style spectacle, complete with gossip, conspiracy theories, and political maneuvering [sic],” it said.
In another article, the influential blogger Niu Tanqin wrote that Huang’s return indicated that the “so-called female spy allegations are complete nonsense”.
Niu also said that China would be flattered by Donald Trump’s suggestion, made in an interview with CNBC, that Huang could be poached by China.
The US president said on Monday that Xi Jinping, China’s leader, described Huang as a “brilliant man” in their recent meeting in Shenzhen, a city in southern China.
Trump said that, despite the national security concerns surrounding Huang’s affair with the alleged China intelligence asset Alice Huang – no relation – “the bigger threat would be losing the brainpower of somebody like Jensen”.
Nvidia produces the world’s most advanced semiconductors, which underpin the artificial intelligence sector. US export controls have blocked Nvidia from shipping its most sophisticated models to China in a bid to slow down the country’s AI development. Huang has pushed for looser regulations, arguing that it is better to keep China dependent on US technology rather than spur Chinese innovation.
Trump said he had personally intervened with the Nvidia board, which had previously ruled out reinstating Huang. “I spoke to them, let me put it that way,” Trump told CNBC on Monday. “And I convinced them … I made the deal.”
Huang’s comeback caused Nvidia’s shares to soar on Monday, adding almost $1tn (£763bn) in market value to the company, which props up much of the US stock markets.
Senator Tom Cotton, the Republican chair of the intelligence committee, said: “I have warned the administration from the start about communist China obtaining our most advanced chips through both smuggling and remote access. “The controversy with Nvidia and Megaspeed is just the tip of the iceberg, and it’s not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back,” he said.
China’s foreign ministry declined to comment on Huang’s reinstatement. The government has consistently denied the espionage allegations, calling them “slander and defamation”.
Several accounts on Chinese social media shared memes and AI-generated videos showing Huang in various revenge scenarios. In one video, shared by an influential blogger, with more than 1.1 million followers, Huang is reimagined in the Arnold Schwarzenegger movie The Terminator, in the famous “I’ll be back” scene.
In another video, Huang is reimagined as Jack Nicholson’s murderous Jack Torrance in the 1980 horror film The Shining. Huang is shown using an axe to destroy a locked bathroom door, terrorising an AI-generated Colette Kress, who was Nvidia’s interim boss, who is cowering behind it. The video ends with an AI-generated “Here’s Jensen!” channelling Nicholson’s famous line, “Here’s Johnny!”, from the movie.
Analysts believe that the saga also demonstrated to Beijing how power works in the US.
“The lesson Chinese policymakers are likely drawing is neither subtle nor complicated: Donald Trump remains the most influential political figure in America and, in many respects, the most consequential variable in the trajectory of US-China relations,” wrote George Chen, a partner at The Asia Group, a consultancy, in a post on Substack.
