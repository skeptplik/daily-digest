Costa Rica's government said on Thursday (November 26, 2026) it had denied entry to UN rapporteur Francesca Albanese, who has accused Israel of genocide, because she was the subject of an "international alert over links to terrorist acts."
Ms. Albanese, the UN special rapporteur on the Palestinian territories, arrived on Thursday (November 26) at the airport serving the capital, San Jose, to receive an honorary degree from the University of Costa Rica, a public university.

Ms. Albanese "is the subject of an international alert over links to terrorist acts," Omer Badilla, director general of Costa Rica's immigration authority, which falls under the interior ministry, said in a video posted on social media.
"I hope this clears up any doubts and makes clear the threat this woman poses to national security," he added.
The rector's office at the institution, the country's most important and prestigious university, said in a statement that immigration authorities were "blocking" Ms. Albanese's entry and asked the government to explain the decision.
The government of Costa Rican President Laura Fernández is a close ally of U.S. President Donald Trump and has pursued closer trade ties with Israel since she took office in May.
Last December, Ms. Albanese criticised a free trade agreement between Costa Rica and Israel and called for its ratification to be put on hold, prompting a rebuke from the government, which accused her of interfering in domestic affairs.
'Face consequences'
The United States, Israel's main ally, imposed sanctions on Ms. Albanese after she criticised Washington's policy on Gaza.
"You see the face of empire the day you touch a nerve or cross its boundaries," Ms. Albanese wrote from the airport.
The university said it was awarding the honorary doctorate, which it grants annually to a prominent figure, in recognition of her "solid academic background, experience, publications and work exposing wrongdoing."
The Italian independent expert has faced harsh criticism from Israel and some of its allies over her longstanding accusations that Israel has committed genocide and other war crimes against Palestinians in Gaza and the occupied West Bank.
Israel's ambassador to the United Nations, Danny Danon, welcomed Costa Rica's decision.
"Those who exploit their UN mandate to spread vile propaganda, legitimise terrorist organisations, desecrate the memory of the six million Jews murdered in the Holocaust, and relentlessly vilify the US and Israel should face consequences," Mr. Danon wrote.
The war in Gaza -- which began following a Hamas attack inside Israel on October 7, 2023 -- has killed more than 73,000 Palestinians, according to Gaza's health ministry, which is controlled by Hamas. Many of the Palestinians killed in Gaza are women and children.
The October 7 Hamas attack killed 1,221 people, according to an AFP tally based on official Israeli figures.
Ms. Albanese was appointed by the UN Human Rights Council in 2022 and, like other independent experts, does not speak on behalf of the United Nations.
