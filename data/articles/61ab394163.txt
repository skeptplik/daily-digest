Reform UK is promising to scrap parking fees at NHS hospitals in England for all patients, visitors, and staff if it wins power at the next election.

The health department said it expected charges to be reasonable, with fees used to maintain facilities or put towards funding health services.
Reform's announcement is part of a broader effort by the party to win credibility on the NHS, as it prepares to appoint someone who has worked in the NHS as its health spokesperson in the coming weeks.
Leader Nigel Farage's previous support for an insurance-based healthcare model has harmed the party's standing on the health service, insiders admit.
The latest policy is also part of a series designed to improve the party's recent struggles in the polls by addressing what Reform see as their perceived weaknesses, on Russia, on defence and now the NHS.
Reform's Treasury spokesman Robert Jenrick told the BBC the party's spending plans would be set closer to an election, but the party would ensure there would always be "real-terms increases" in funding for the NHS.
Echoing a line traditionally used by British politicians to claim stewardship of the health service, he said: "What we are saying is that the NHS is safe in our hands".
Parking rules
Parking at hospitals in Wales and Scotland is largely free, but NHS trusts in England are allowed to charge and set their own fees and rules.
Government guidance means parking is free for disabled people holding Blue Badges, frequent outpatients, parents of sick children staying overnight and NHS staff working night shifts.
But most visitors are generally expected to pay, along with staff working day shifts.
Reform says its policy to compensate NHS trusts would cost Â£285m a year to deliver, assuming a Â£25m annual saving from payment collection costs that would not longer be required.
It plans to recoup lost revenue by charging overseas visitors 200% of the national tariff for NHS services, instead of 150% currently, and extending charges to A&E visits, ambulance services and GP care.
It also says it would make all foreign visitors from countries that do not have reciprocal healthcare arrangements with the UK take out travel insurance covering NHS care, allowing it to "more aggressively collect" money due.
Private contractors
Siva Anandaciva, policy director at the King's Fund health think tank, said parking fees could be a "burden" for those visiting hospitals, but questioned whether Reform's proposals would raise enough to fully compensate trusts.
He said the additional charges on visitors for care would bring extra administration costs, whilst hospitals would need to spend money on systems to ensure free spaces were available to those who need them most.
The experience in Scotland had shown it can also take an "awfully long time" for hospitals to exit contracts with private firms running car pars, he said.
He added: "You might not end up saving as much money as you think".
The health department said it expected fees charged by trusts to be "reasonable and in line with the local area".
"Where charges do apply, the money made is used to maintain facilities, with surplus income put back into the NHS to benefit both staff and patients," a spokesperson added.
In response to parliamentary questions earlier this year, the department also argued, external fees were a "significant control mechanism" to manage capacity given the limited number of car parking spaces available.
