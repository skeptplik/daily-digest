Liberal members 'tossing up whether to leave or remain', PM says
The Coalition’s got its focus set on the economy again today, with Angus Taylor asking Anthony Albanese if he can “name a single economic indicator that has gone in the right direction this year”, with inflation and wages going backwards.
The prime minister says employment numbers and the gender pay gap are going in the right direction.
The employment rate – now, there is 14.7 million Australians employed. There were 13.5 million employed when we came to office. So that’s up … The gender pay gap is at a record low, 11.3%. It was 14.2% when we came to office.
He then also tries to take a crack at Leah Blyth’s defection. You can tell his office has spent some time trying to come up with different ways of sticking in the knife:
People are looking at ways in which they could have growth. My friend Andy Burnham in the UK is looking at revisiting Brexit there.
It looks like Liberal members are also tossing up whether to leave or remain.
‘Stop talking the Australian economy down’: Chalmers under pressure over inflation numbers
The shadow treasurer, Tim Wilson, is next and asks if the prime minister, who yesterday said Australia’s inflation rate is at least better than the Philippines, can “identify a single major advanced economy with a higher core inflation rate than Australia?”
Jim Chalmers takes the question (cue groans from the Coalition benches) and says broadly, “inflation is going up around the world”.
Around the world, I think every major advanced economy, from memory, has seen inflation go up in October.
But Chalmers won’t name any countries – other than the Philippines.
Wilson tries to bring Chalmers back on track, making a point of order on relevance, which Milton Dick isn’t having.
Chalmers goes for the jugular.
I hear the leader of the opposition interjecting. He’s very mouthy for someone who lost a shadow minister, Mr Speaker. Stop talking the Australian economy down.
Chalmers defends budget: ‘I won’t be taking lectures on budget responsibility from those opposite’
The Nationals MP Jamie Chaffey doesn’t bother asking the government this time how much more mortgage holders are paying than when the Albanese government was first elected.
Chaffey says, after 17 interest rate rises, the average new mortgage holder is more than $30,000 a year worse off, and asks the treasurer to admit “he has broken the economy” and apologise to households.
Jim Chalmers says he acknowledges the extra pressure that interest rate rises put on households.
He then pivots, taking a shot at the Coalition, accusing opposition MPs of wanting to “talk about budget repair – they like to talk about responsible economic management. They just don’t like to vote for it.”
You know what they did when inflation was north of 6% and roaring? They had a net spend in their last budget of $40bn. And you know how many saves they had in that last budget, Mr Speaker? Absolutely none. So I won’t be taking lectures on budget responsibility from those opposite.
PM urges parliament to support gas reservation scheme
Over to the crossbench, the independent MP Dai Le asks the prime minister if he will support her bill to ensure 30% of goods in commonwealth projects are made in Australia.
The government rarely, if ever, supports crossbench bills.
Anthony Albanese says by that measure, Le should be a fan of the government’s Future Made in Australia program.
“No!” shout a bunch of Coalition MPs.
Albanese says Le and the Coalition should support the gas reservation scheme:
One of the reasons why we have had a domestic gas reservation for the east coast that hopefully will pass the Senate today is because of what that will do – make Australian manufacturing more competitive by making sure that there is a limit on the price of gas, which is absolutely critical for Australian manufacturing.
The government’s dixers have so far been used to talk up the gas reservation scheme and pressure the Coalition to support it.
So far, the Coalition have said they “will not oppose it”.
At the end of a dixer, Pat Conroy finishes his answer with this burn to the Liberals: “Time to get off the fence, otherwise you’re just going to get some splinters.”
Milton Dick then reckons it’s time to thin out the chamber a little. He kicks out deputy Nationals leader, Darren Chester, and Liberal frontbencher Simon Kennedy.
Taylor ‘can’t even run his own frontbench’, Albanese says
Angus Taylor is back to the dispatch box with another tight question: “How much has the cost of the average annual mortgage payment gone up over the last four years?”
Spoiler alert: he won’t get a number.
Anthony Albanese says very broadly that house prices have risen more than 400% since the capital gains tax discount was introduced by the Howard government.
Taylor, obviously unhappy with the answer (or lack thereof) so far, gets up with a point of order and says:
It’s a very specific question – how much has the cost of the average annual mortgage payment gone up over the past year? I’ll give you a hint – it’s over $30,000 a year.

He wants to run the country, but he can’t even run his own frontbench, Mr Speaker … We understand why he’s angry.

Albanese ends his question continuing his attacks.

Victorian Coalition promises to privatise Melbourne’s sewage treatment and sell state-owned land and renewables to fund election commitments
A Victorian Coalition government would privatise two sewage plants and sell off state-owned land, renewable energy assets and the “air rights” between Richmond train station and the Melbourne Cricket Ground if elected.
The opposition leader, Jess Wilson, announced a $14.47bn “asset recycling” plan as part of the Liberal and Nationals parties’ pre-election policy costings on Thursday.
She said the plan included a sale of state land to developers, and a $9.2bn, 90-year lease of the eastern and western treatment plans that process the vast majority of Melbourne’s sewage.
Wilson said there would be “no change” to water bills as a result of the 90-year lease.

Nicolette Boele says Bowen not learning lesson on fossil fuels
Luca Ittimani
Nicolette Boele, the Bradfield MP, has said Chris Bowen has not learned from the Cop31 focus on fossil fuels.
Boele, an independent, said Bowen’s statement on climate was all focused on the upside of electrification. She said in a statement:
As long as we keep expanding fossil fuel exports, we’ve got a firehose in one hand and a flamethrower in the other.
I was in Antalya for Cop31, the only non-government Australian MP on the ground. Australia has committed at previous Cops to transition away from fossil fuels, but despite leading this year’s negotiations, we still don’t have our own plan to do so.
Bowen said coal stations needed to be replaced but said gas would be “an important part of our transition” and said Australia would remain a “reliable exporter” of gas.
Boele said the government needed to plan to phase out all fossil fuels, starting with caps on the fuel tax credit.
Australia still set to fall short of 2035 target to cut emissions
Australia will fall massively short of a 2035 target unless the federal government substantially ramps up its climate policies, according to new official projections.
Under existing policies, the country is on track by 2035 to cut climate pollution by only 48% compared with 2005 levels – far below its target reduction of between 62% and 70% by that date, climate department projections suggest.
Australia’s progress towards the 2035 target has not improved since the last projection released a year ago.
Chris Bowen did not mention progress for 2035 in his address.

Energy prices to cause ‘real pain’ at Christmas, says Coalition
Luca Ittimani
The Coalition’s energy spokesperson, Dan Tehan, has focused on “real pain” from energy bills in response to Labor’s climate statement.
Chris Bowen has just noted that wholesale energy prices in Australia’s national energy market fell 27% in the year to September and called on retailers to pass that on to consumer bills.
However, retail energy prices rose 25% in the year to October, mainly reflecting the expiration of the Albanese government’s rebates.
Tehan said it was the fifth anniversary (though he’s a week early) of Labor’s 2021 promise while in opposition to cut household electricity bills by $275 a year by 2025. He said:
I shouldn’t laugh, because this is actually causing real pain to Australians and real pain in their lead-up to Christmas.
Tehan referred to Rystad Energy’s forecasts for Australia’s wholesale energy prices to double by 2030. The research company has predicted coal power will shut down too fast and renewables will enter too slow to avoid a price squeeze.
Cop31 shows world ‘no longer entertaining’ climate deniers, says Bowen
Chris Bowen has defended Australia’s leadership at Cop31, saying the world has lost patience with climate deniers.
The energy minister, who served as president of negotiations in Turkey, acknowledged the 190 countries at Cop31 didn’t find consensus on the “overarching cover decision” but said there was agreement on 44 of 60 substantive items.
He said “so many countries” signed on the “35 by 35” pledge to raise electricity’s share of global energy consumption to 35% by 2035.
This is a demonstration of the international cooperation required to address climate change and commitments made at Cop31 this year will help frame the fight against its impacts.
The international community is no longer entertaining those who claim that climate change isn’t real.
The science is unanimous. The economics are irrefutable. The evidence is before us.
It’s the hottest year on record. Year after year, after year, sets a new record. It’s increasingly devastating, to see bushfires that threaten our landscapes and our communities, not just in Australia but right across the world now.
He concluded by saying Australia was becoming a world leader in renewables helped by families’ choices to affect “their everyday life, on their bills and on their environment”.
But a household alone cannot change our emissions. A single country cannot solve the climate change crisis. It’s through collective action – nations banding together for the global good – that we can achieve real change.
Australia on track to reduce emissions by 42% by 2030, says Bowen
Luca Ittimani
The federal government has forecast Australia will reduce its carbon emissions by 42% on 2005 levels by 2030 – just under its target for a 43% cut.
Chris Bowen has told parliament the nation’s emissions fell by 6.2m tonnes in the year to June, accelerating from the previous decade. Annual emissions are already 25.3% below 2005 levels, he said.
The projects for Australia to fall “just 1% short” of the 2030 target showed the importance of electrifying national systems, Bowen said. He described the 2035 target, of a 62% to 70% reduction, as “ambitious but achievable”:
Our emissions projections for 2027 and beyond show that we’re making good progress and are on the right track. Households and businesses around the country have done their part …
The energy minister has declared coal-fired power stations must be replaced, with the stations facing at least one breakdown every day for three years.
Chris Bowen has told the house Australia’s coal power stations are about 40 years old, on average. He said:
While coal has played an important, a vital role empowering this nation, our coal fire power stations are ageing. … And as they become older, they become increasingly unreliable.
They must be replaced.
Bowen said coal power outages had occurred every day for the last three years and seen energy prices “skyrocket”.
When coal-fired stations go down, your bills go up.
And there hasn’t been one day in the past three years – not one day, in three years – without a coal-fired power station breaking down.
Australian homes have more solar panels than swimming pools, says Bowen
Chris Bowen is now addressing the House of Representatives with the annual climate change statement, talking up the “story of the clean energy transition in real life”.
Solar panel installation has sped up, he said.
There are now more solar panels on rooftops around the country than there are back yard swimming pools.
And newly installed rooftop solar capacity was 45% higher in the first three-quarters of this year than it was in the same period in 2025. So our transformation is gaining even more speed.
Bowen says the government’s cheaper home batteries program – at risk of being cut as Labor looks for budget savings – has helped households install more than 622,000 batteries, more than 75% of which were in outer suburban and regional areas.
That represents 17.35GWh of storage, increasing Australia’s home battery capacity by more than five times, Bowen said.
He is making the statement days after the climate negotiations broke down at Cop31 in Turkey, sharing leadership with Australia. The minister has said he was frustrated after many countries including the European Union refused to back a proposal that excluded mention of fossil fuels.
