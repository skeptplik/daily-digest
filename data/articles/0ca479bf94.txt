LONDON/BRUSSELS – A decade after the U.K. voted to leave the European Union, the bloc’s proposed “Made in Europe” rules are testing the country’s vision of a “global Britain” that can pursue its own trade deals with partners around the world.
That was a key argument for Brexit: The U.K. would not be bound by EU policies set in Brussels. But tougher geopolitical conditions and rising European protectionism are forcing Britain’s government to choose between closer ties with its neighbors and much-needed investment from China, current and former U.K. policy advisers say.
The EU’s scheme would require companies to include a certain percentage of European-made components as a condition of accessing EU subsidies or selling to EU governments and agencies.
