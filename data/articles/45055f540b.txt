Japan’s largest pension fund may be moving closer to deciding whether to revise its asset allocation, according to JPMorgan Chase, potentially reviving speculation that it could steer more of its ¥314 trillion ($2 trillion) portfolio toward domestic bonds.
The Government Pension Investment Fund discussed its basic portfolio at a Nov. 13 meeting of its Board of Governors, according to an agenda released by the fund. The topic last appeared on the agenda at an unusual meeting in August, strategist Junya Tanase wrote in a note to clients.
The latest development may suggest that a review of figures related to the basic portfolio has been completed and discussions have moved to whether changes to the allocation are warranted, according to JPMorgan. Details haven’t been disclosed and the bank said its interpretation “remains speculative.”
