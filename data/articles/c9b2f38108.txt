Zelenskyy criticizes Trump for cozying up to Putin as EU kicks Ukraine funding down the road
EU budget summit exposed divisions within the bloc on how much to keep spending on war in Ukraine
A summit of European leaders has collapsed without a budget deal — and caught up in the EU’s in-fighting is Ukraine, which was to get a $45 billion euro ($70 billion) loan in the package.
The president of the European Council, Antonio Costa, convened the gathering Thursday with the aim of reaching consensus on the European Union’s 2028-2034 budget.
The meeting was slated to last two days, but it ended in acrimony in the early hours of Friday and without an agreement in place.
For months, the European Union’s 27 member states have been deeply divided on the issue and split between two groups, the so-called frugal nations and the "friends of cohesion."
Ahead of the meeting, Costa urged European leaders to come to Brussels with an open mind and find a compromise. To help achieve that, he had proposed a reduction of 204 billion euros ($318 billion) to the budget at current prices, from the nearly €2 trillion euros ($3.1 trillion) originally tabled by the European Commission.
European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen, left, speaks with European Council president Antonio Costa during a round table meeting at the EU summit Thursday. (Geert Vanden Wijngaert/The Associated Press)
Ultimately, the efforts failed with the frugal group arguing that the cuts don’t go far enough and failed to address concerns over spending discipline and the burden of financing the budget.
'Security comes at a price'
For nations such as Poland that are pushing for a bigger budget, greater spending is needed to bolster the bloc’s collective security and help the EU better compete economically with the likes of the United States and China.
“A cheap Europe could turn out to be a very expensive mistake,” Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk wrote in a social media post on Thursday.
"Security comes at a price. As simple as that."
Addressing the media at a press conference at the end of the summit, European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen said progress had been made on issues such as a digital services tax but urged greater action to reach a deal by the end of the year.
“Today's conversations were useful. But there is still a lot of work ahead of us," von der Leyen said. "Now, all the pieces of the puzzle must be put together. This will be a challenge. But we all know we need an agreement."
Ukraine, meanwhile, is left wanting. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz is pushing for the $70 billion loan to be included in the bloc’s next long-term budget but faces opposition from several member states, including Slovakia and Hungary.
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Hungary's Prime Minister Peter Magyar shake hands before a meeting in Uzhhorod, Ukraine, earlier this week. Hungary is one of the EU countries advocating for a reduction in funding for the war. (Ukrainian Presidential Press Service/Reuters)
The debate comes as Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, told the BBC that a recent interaction between U.S. President Donald Trump and Russian counterpart Vladimir Putin was, "strange."
The two leaders met at a summit in China last week where Trump was heard calling Putin a "friend."
Zelenskyy said in the BBC interview , which took place in Kyiv and was broadcast Friday, that Putin is "an aggressor and a killer." Calling him a friend would be like calling Adolf Hitler a friend in the Second World War, he said.
"He can't be a friend. Just cannot be."
Meanwhile, the U.S. president claimed on social media that, during that conversation with Putin, he told the Russian leader to, "pull back all military aggression against territories throughout Europe, in the hopes that a deal can be made between Russia and Ukraine."
Zelenskyy accused the U.S. of not paying enough attention to the conflict and delaying delivery of the Patriot missiles it promised.
"The deficit of these missiles, that is people's lives," he said.
Ukraine's needs 'cannot be postponed,' analyst says
The Nordic nations, as well as Germany and Netherlands, are on the frugal side of the EU’s budget debate and among Ukraine’s biggest supporters in Europe.
Jacob Funk Kirkegaard, senior fellow at the Brussels-based think tank Bruegel, says this, ironically, could help get a budget deal over the line.
"In general, as the frugals know, it will be far more expensive for them to support Ukraine on their own. In the end, they will agree to a budget compromise," he said.
"As such, the needs of Ukraine, which due to the war and upcoming European elections, cannot be postponed, will help seal a deal in mid-December."
European leaders will next convene in the Belgian capital on Dec. 17. It will be a last chance to get a budget deal done by the end of the year.
French far-right leader Marine Le Pen and France's President Emmanuel Macron, seen at the presidential Elysee Palace, in Paris in June 2022, inserted their political rivalry into the EU budget debate. (Ludovic Marin/Reuters)
But even that deadline has been the subject of debate. Marine Le Pen, the far-right National Rally candidate and current frontrunner in the upcoming French elections, has accused the European Commission of seeking to get the multiannual financial framework (MFF) agreed before the French people head to the polls in April.
That’s a view French President Emmanuel Macron has firmly rejected.
"It is a strange kind of arrogance to think that she is already in charge of France’s destiny because, until proven otherwise, she has not been elected, and yours truly is elected until May 13, 2027,” he said speaking to reporters on the sidelines of this week’s budget negotiations.
