A Bahraini court sentenced 64 Shia Muslims, most of them clerics, to life in prison on terror charges on Monday (November 16, 2026), a rights group said, as authorities crack down on groups it accuses of ties to Iran.
Since the Iran war began with U.S.-Israeli attacks, Gulf states that have borne the brunt of the Islamic republic's retaliatory strikes have cracked down on what they call cells with links to the Revolutionary Guards.
Bahrain, a Sunni Muslim kingdom, has a large Shia population that has long complained of marginalisation.
Authorities in the country have denied cracking down on citizens based on their religious identity.
On Monday (November 16), the criminal court sentenced 64 Shias, 57 of them clerics, to life in prison.
It also sentenced five others to 15 years in prison, according to the Britain-based Bahrain Institute for Rights and Democracy (BIRD), citing a lawyer who attended the court session.
“The mass trial involves 69 Bahraini Shias, including 60 clerics -- 13 of whom were tried in absentia, including prominent cleric Ayatollah Isa Qassim,” according to BIRD.
Since the United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran in February, Bahrain has cracked down on anyone expressing support or sympathy for Tehran.
It has arrested hundreds of mostly Shia Muslims it accused of espionage, terrorism or links to the Revolutionary Guards, according to activists.
Dozens have also been stripped of their nationality.
Torture allegations
The Shias sentenced on Monday (November 16) had initially been arrested and tried in separate groups earlier this year.
In May, Bahrain said its security services had dismantled an organisation accused of links to the Revolutionary Guards and arrested 41 of its suspected members. It named 11 others it said were in Iran.
In June, 15 more people were arrested in connection with the case, with authorities later saying two more people were on the run.
They had been accused of various crimes including forming a terrorist group, collaborating with the Revolutionary Guards and plotting to overthrow Bahrain's system and spread Iran's ideology.
In an open letter, rights groups including BIRD and Human Rights Watch called on authorities to investigate "serious allegations of torture" in the case.
"Since Bahrain's judiciary was established in the 1920s, the country has never witnessed persecution of Shia clerics on this scale," said Sayed Ahmed Alwadae of BIRD.
The rights groups said defendants had reported "being subjected to forced standing, prolonged blindfolding and handcuffing, sleep deprivation, and threats of physical and sexual violence against themselves and their families, as well as citizenship revocation and deportation".
They had also allegedly been pressured to sign long statements they were not allowed to read, they said.
