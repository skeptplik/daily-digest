Would AI have come up with a better word of the year than the Cambridge Dictionary?
The publication has chosen 'human' in response to concerns around artificial intelligence
The Cambridge Dictionary has chosen "human" as its word of the year, reflecting concerns about artificial intelligence.
The choice announced Wednesday in the U.K. highlights the debate over how AI blurs the lines between machines and people.
Explaining the choice, the dictionary said, "Throughout history, the adjective human has signified people, as opposed to animals and gods (and, more recently, machines). Increasingly seen in compounds such as human-written, human-generated, and human-made, it now also denotes that something is created by or typical of people as opposed to AI."
Colin McIntosh, the dictionary's lexicographer, notes that though AI has been a growing presence in society for years, in 2026, it seemed to be everywhere — and changed how people talk about being human.
"2026 has been all about a refound appreciation of the human factor in what we consume (with a consequent human premium)," he said in a statement, "and a new value is being placed on the human element in our lives in general: all confirmed by our analysis of the language data, internet trends, and search habits."
Given that, he said, human was the perfect choice.
Last year's word of the year was also AI-related. Cambridge Dictionary chose "parasocial," explaining that "as social media intensifies the intimacy that fans feel with their adored celebrities, and with the rise in popularity of AI companions that can take on personalities, the word for these one-way relationships — parasocial — is having its own moment."
Four other words made the shortlist for the 2026 word of the year: analogue, agent, wholesome and chic.
