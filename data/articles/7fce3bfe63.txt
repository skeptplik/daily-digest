Russell T Davies’s award-winning series It’s a Sin, about young gay friends living through the Aids crisis in 1980s London, is getting a new life as a dance piece, with a trailer released on Monday ahead of an opening at Aviva Studios in Manchester in February.
“We had a few approaches, but a lot of people wanted to make it a musical, which made my heart sink slightly,” said Davies. “I always imagined an aria between those two women on the seafront at the end and I thought: ‘Oh, that could be so terrible!’” Dance was different, he said. “It felt so original and so pure. The story distils down to these emotional moments that are going to be done so beautifully on stage.”
The idea came from Benoit Swan Pouffer, artistic director of Rambert, which celebrated its centenary this year with a commitment to bringing in new audiences, in part by reinventing familiar titles, as in its dance version of Peaky Blinders. “Dance needs to be democratised and this is the way to do it,” said Pouffer, who is choreographing It’s a Sin.
Davies has a cameo in the trailer, although his own dance career peaked with a performance for Princess Diana with West Glamorgan youth theatre (she told him he should continue to “Dance, dance, dance!”). The dancefloor, however, has always been an inspiration. “They’re such potent places, so loaded with potential.” Davies spent many nights in the clubs of Manchester’s Canal Street, watching lives play out to music. “Those dancefloors were places of such extraordinary freedom,” he said. “Especially in the 80s, with that layer of secrecy. That’s where you’d be out – you might not be out at home – becoming the person you aspired to be.”
The dance world was notably affected by the Aids epidemic. “I had friends and colleagues who left us too early,” said Pouffer. He was dancing in the US in the mid-90s, and saw as much stigma about HIV and Aids in the dance world as anywhere else. “There was shame, it was hidden. Even Alvin Ailey, that’s not what we said [that he died of an Aids-related illness] when he passed. Even now there is still a stigma,” said Pouffer. “The trauma, you put it in a little capsule and you don’t talk about it. Now we have this young PrEP generation who don’t know how traumatic it was. The closest they can think of is Covid,” he suggested. “Remember in the beginning when we didn’t know anything about the virus?”
Pouffer also wants to convey the excitement and creative spark of the era. “The fashion, the music, the dance of the 80s, it was one of the peaks of history,” he said. There will be video and narration to help tell the story, with scripts by Kaite O’Reilly. Davies is an executive producer, as are Pet Shop Boys, whose title song will feature alongside an original soundtrack by Roman GianArthur.
When Davies originally pitched the TV series he was turned down by all the major UK networks, even Channel 4, which eventually commissioned it. “It was simply an assumption that it would be miserable, even when they had the first script to read,” he said.
The series went on to be a huge hit. “Not a week goes past without someone stopping me in the street,” said Davies. Yet it was hard to celebrate, “because no matter how proud we were, it’s a story that’s founded on a lot of deaths”.
There is “a great heaviness at the heart of it, a great sadness and loss,” he continued. “But we told it with joy and vigour and life. The most important thing is to remember the life that those people had, not the death … I think the show worked because it turned around and said: ‘Hey, we loved those boys. Remember the love.’”
- It’s a Sin is on tour from 16 February to 13 November 2027
