UPDATE: Hamburg shooting suspect still at large — report
According to media reports, heavily armed Hamburg police were deployed following reports of shots being fired in an apartment just 100 meters (around 330 feet) away from the Elbphilharmonie.
Ambulances were also deployed to the scene.
Police have not yet commented on the background of the incident, but two injured people are believed to be a man and a woman. The man had reportedly been shot in the hip while the woman was suffering from a neck wound.
The suspected shooter had not yet been apprehended, according to the Hamburger Abendblatt. However, a police spokesperson said they posed no danger to the general public.
37 years after fall of the Berlin Wall: How divided are Germans today?
When the Berlin Wall fell on November 9, 1989, ending East Germany's isolation from the West, the event was celebrated on both sides of the border. Yet, 37 years later, many people on both sides remain deeply skeptical of each other.
Surveys show that only a minority of respondents report feeling a strong sense of community between those from former East and West Germany, and many continue to perceive significant differences.

Large police presence in Hamburg's HafenCity
A large number of police have been deployed to the HafenCity / Am Kaiserkai area in Hamburg, close to the famous Elbphilarmonie, according to a post by Hamburg police on X.
It wasn't immediately clear what the cause of the deployment was, but the police said two people had been injured.

Wadephul skeptical of Russia ahead of Abu Dhabi talks
German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul has expressed doubt over Russia's willingness to genuinely enter into negotiation talks to end the war in Ukraine.
"Is Russia truly willing to negotiate?" he asked. Or, "is this just a negotiating charade intended to distract from the fact that Russia intends to continue a brutal war of aggression, particularly against the civilian population?"
His comments come as representatives from Ukraine and Russia began the first round of talks in nine months in Abu Dhabi. US and European representatives will also be present.
Russia "must finally give up its maximum demands," Wadephul added on the sidelines of a G7 foreign ministers meeting.
Aigner to meet with CDU/CSU, Greens and SPD in Bundestag
The top candidate to replace Frank-Walter Steinmeier as Germany's president, Ilse Aigner, is set to visit the Bundestag, where she will meet with the center-right CDU/CSU bloc, the Social Democrats and the Greens.
She has said she will not meet with the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) after one of their top members called her nomination a "disgrace." She also has not received an official invitation from the Left party.
Aigner was officially nominated by the CDU/CSU bloc, also known as the Union, on Monday, practically securing her future as German president. She already has support from the SPD and Greens.
The official vote will take place on January 30. The president is chosen by the Bundestag. Despite being the highest political office in the country, it is a largely ceremonial role.
