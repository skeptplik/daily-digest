Family relations during the holidays can be tricky territory for millions — and the royal family is no exception.
After riding through a bumpy few months media-wise, Meghan Markle and Prince Harry soon will be heading into a high-stakes festive season following their relocation to the U.K. three months ago.
The Sussexes have dominated the negative headlines of late, in large part because Markle’s father, Thomas, was charged with two counts of possession of child pornography. Never mind their nearly overlapping public appearances with the working members of the royal family and Harry’s recent politically charged comments about the war in Ukraine.
Meanwhile, there has been a lot underfoot in the House of Windsor, with King Charles III still being treated for cancer, Kate Middleton sharing her own recovery journey and the family still grappling with revelations about Andrew Mountbatten Windsor’s friendship with the late convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.
Further intensifying the season was the arrest of two men Tuesday for the suspected burglary of Prince William’s and the Princess of Wales’s country estate, Anger Hall. Mirgen Doku, 34, and Arilen Ndoci, 32, who reportedly have no fixed addresses, have been charged with suspicion of burglary and trespass.
Now, all eyes are on how the entire family will approach the holidays.
Harry and Meghan have reportedly been snubbed from the annual carol service that the Princess of Wales will host Dec. 11 at Westminster Abbey, which this year is not so ironically themed “Together at Christmas.” The Sussexes and their children, Prince Archie and Princess Lilibet, also reportedly won’t be joining the royal family at Sandringham for Christmas. Friends of the couple have told the media they always planned to have their own holiday with Markle’s mother, Doria Ragland.
Harry, Meghan and their children got a jump on the holiday season by turning up at the VIP preview for “Winter Wonderland” in London’s Hyde Park last Wednesday night.
As they navigate the weeks ahead, WWD asked some crisis PR and celebrity specialists to offer some unsolicited guidance for the Sussexes.
People’s Revolution’s founder Kelly Cutrone said, “This is an inelegant PR statement to make. If they were my clients, I’d advise them to sit down and be quiet.”
Flagging how Harry had the birthright to be a central player in the royal family as a working royal, and the couple were given an estimated $42.5 million wedding, Cutrone said, “And they split. They chose to quit. It’s a family, but it’s a family office.”
Cutrone said Markle should avoid solo appearances while the investigation into her father plays out. “They’re facing two giant lions — with the royal family on one side and her father, an alleged pedophile, on the other,” she said. “As a publicist, I would totally acknowledge that she had nothing to do with her father. But she is associated with this alleged crime from now on, whether she likes it or not.”
While “it’s too early for Markle to turn up at a Toys for Tots party,” Cutrone suggested, “Focus on your own family. This is not the time to show up at Princess Beatrice’s house with a turkey. They should stay away from any royal stuff. Why do you need to be making comments on things? Harry should refrain from talking about politics.”
Calling for a quiet Christmas for the Sussexes, crisis public relations specialist Lauren Beeching speculated some will now reconsider the presumptions they made about Markle’s estrangement from her father.
Ursinus College professor Alice Leppert, whose focus is celebrity culture, added that the accusations against Thomas Markle “help give credence to Meghan’s longstanding desire to distance herself from and disavow him.” But the accusations may also make the royals “more uneasy” about Harry and Meghan’s place in the family, given King Charles’ stripping of Andrew’s titles.
In the weeks ahead, Markle will face a “difficult balance” with her As Ever business, which is rooted around home, family and entertaining, Beeching said.
Newsweek recently reported that As Ever’s site traffic in October was approximately 179,000 visitors versus about 197,000 in October of last year, based on Similarwear data. However, last month’s tally was up compared to September’s total of 141,000, but far from January’s figure of 268,000.
With the holidays ordinarily being primetime for sales, Markle will need to be thoughtful about the timing of any promotional activity, Beeching said. Still, she added, “Above all, I think Meghan deserves some compassion. Whatever people’s opinions of her, this must be an incredibly upsetting situation.”
Paula Froelich, host of NewsNation’s “The Scoop” and “Paula Froelich Uncensored” on YouTube and Substack, said the Sussexes “need to not do what they did in years past like plug As Ever merchandise on the day of Catherine’s annual Christmas carols concert. Or having friends tell newspapers that they don’t care about not being invited to Sandringham for Christmas,” she said. “But silence is Harry and Meghan’s enemy. They’ve become this complete ADD couple. By that, I do not mean attention deficit disorder, but Attention Dependency Disorder.”
The observations come as William and Kate are seeing their popularity increase, thanks to recent high-wattage press moments, like their trip to India.
With 74 percent of Britons having a favorable opinion of Prince William and 72 percent feeling the same way about the Princess of Wales, they are the most popular royal family members, according to YouGov’s October rankings. Harry and Meghan had 29 percent and 20 percent, respectively, in comparison.
Longtime publicist R. Couri Hay sized up the situation differently: “With Charles having cancer, Kate having cancer, William dealing with the pressures of being the heir apparent, the Epstein situation with Andrew, and now you’ve got a pedophile linked to Harry’s wife’s father, it’s like wow. Talk about being knocked around,” he said.
Suggesting the Sussexes “read the room, pack their bags, go back to California and try to salvage some kind of a dignified life,” Hay said. “All of this changing lands and changing schools can’t be easy on those kids.”
While Queen Elizabeth “took great pains” to stand by Prince Andrew publicly at mass, amid the Epstein backlash, Hay said, “There will be no attempt by the royal family to try to shore up Harry and Meghan. They are staying as far away as humanly possible.”
On board with a lay-low strategy for Harry and Meghan, he said, “[Her dad’s indictment] is a real scandal for her and for the royal family. Nobody wants to be associated with a suspected pedophile. If they go to things that are too red carpet, too glamorous or too commercial, that will not reflect well on their judgment.”
No matter where they go — or don’t — there’s no question that all eyes will be on the couple and the entire royal family.
“Christmas is such an important part of the monarchy’s public image, and everything from the Sandringham gathering to the King’s [Christmas] message will inevitably be scrutinized.”
