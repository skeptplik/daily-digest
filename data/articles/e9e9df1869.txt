A senior US defence official has criticised Ukraine's "deep strikes" against Russia, saying they had been ineffective in bringing the war to an end and arguing that, in some cases, they had likely "hardened the Russian public's resolve."
"Our assessment is that such strikes have not been effective in driving an end to the war, nor do they contribute to blunting Russia's advances on the front line," Under Secretary of Defence Elbridge Colby said while speaking from NATO's headquarters in Brussels on Tuesday.
"Such strikes, especially those that target civilian infrastructure such as Wildberries, have actually probably hardened the Russian public's resolve and the Kremlin's own willingness to escalate," he continued.
He added that support for Kyiv moving forward should focus on "enabling and advancing Ukraine's ability to hold the line of battle and where possible make gains."
Ukraine has increasingly struck targets deep inside Russian territory as the war has gone on, using its burgeoning drone technology to carry out long-range attacks.
Ukrainian forces have particularly looked to target energy infrastructure such as oil refineries and terminals, as well as military sites like air bases, as they have sought to hamper the Russian economy and hit Vladimir Putin's war machine.
US President Donald Trump has previously demanded that Ukraine halt strikes on Russian oil refineries, saying the attacks were to blame for rising diesel fuel prices.
'Quite a stupid thing'
Colby's comments have drawn pushback from some European officials.
German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius called the warning "quite a stupid thing," while his British counterpart Wes Streeting said there was "no doubt" in his mind that Ukraine's long-range strikes had "helped to degrade Putin's war machine, buying time and saving lives."
The comments came as Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy once again urged allies to speed up deliveries of interceptor missiles.
"Ukraine needs Patriot and IRIS-T interceptors from the stocks you already have right now," he told the meeting of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group in Brussels via video link.
"And I ask the United States – please, do not delay these deliveries," Zelenskyy added. He also said Ukraine needed a further 40 F-16 fighter jets to help boost its air defences.
The pleas come as Ukraine gears up for what looks set to be another brutal winter under heavy bombardment from Russian forces.
Russia has repeatedly sought to ramp up attacks on Ukraine in wartime winter periods, targeting the country's energy sector as Moscow has attempted to break its resolve.
