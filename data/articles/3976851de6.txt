A fatal tank explosion that occurred during a live-fire drill at the Hijudai training area in Oita Prefecture in April was caused by a fragment of a silicone rubber band left inside a tank gun chamber, the Ground Self-Defense Force said Thursday.
According to the investigation results released on the day, the fragment was left in the chamber at a high temperature after the previous round was fired and came into contact with the next round, triggering an explosion.
The incident involved a state-of-the-art Type 10 tank. A 120-millimeter anti-tank shell exploded, killing the 45-year-old tank commander, the 31-year-old gunner and a 30-year-old safety officer who were in the turret at the time. The driver sustained serious injuries.
The band fragment also prevented the breech mechanism, which is designed to shield the turret from the blast when a round is fired, from functioning properly.
An analysis of data showed the explosion occurred just 15 seconds after the abnormality was detected, suggesting that those inside were caught in the blast while attempting to respond.
According to the GSDF, a similar incident in which a rubber band remained in a tank gun chamber occurred during training at the GSDF’s Fuji School in Shizuoka Prefecture in 2018.
Since that incident did not result in an explosion or casualties, the information was not shared within the organization.
The GSDF said it will develop more durable projectiles as a preventive measure.
It has stopped using the anti-tank shells in the Type 10 and Type 90 tanks since the April incident. Other shells do not pose a similar risk due to their mechanism, the GSDF said.
At a news conference on Thursday, Masayoshi Arai, the GSDF’s chief of staff, expressed regret over the incident.
“It was extremely difficult to foresee the incident because multiple factors coincided,” he said, indicating that no disciplinary action would be taken within the GSDF.
