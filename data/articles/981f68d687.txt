Germany's federal prosecutor's office on Thursday said it had ordered the arrest of two Iraqi citizens in Germany who are believed to be members of a pro-Iranian terrorist group.
It identified the men as Mostafa Al-R. and Ameer J., and said that they were arrested in Heidelberg in Baden-Württemberg in the southwest, and in Cologne, several hours' drive to the north.
Officers from Germany's federal criminal police force carried out the arrests.
Prosecutors also announced searches and raids of 17 sites in total across three separate states, saying these were ongoing.
What accusations do the men face?
Prosecutors said the two men were "strongly suspected" of membership in a domestic terrorist organization.
They said that Mostafa Al-R. wa an associate of a man in the US known as Mohammad Al-Saadi, who is accused of setting up a group with the acronym HAYI (Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiyya) that is thought to be "responsible for a series of attacks against US, Israeli and Jewish institutions in Europe from March 2026," in the aftermath of the outbreak of Donald Trump's war with Iran.
Mostafa Al-R. and Al-Saadi allegedly "founded a separate organization to prepare and carry out further attacks in Germany and Europe at the end of 2025."
Prosecutors say Ameer J. joined this group. It gathered funds and "recruited several external people to carry out explosive attacks."
"On the group's orders, unknown assailants headed on two consecutive nights in late March 2026 — presumably with explosives — to synagogues in Cologne and in one other European city," prosecutors wrote. "The plan was, for reasons as yet unknown, ultimately not carried out."
Prosecutors said that intelligence behind the arrests could be traced back to the federal domestic intelligence agency, the BfV.
More than 250 police officers from various forces were part of Thursday's operation.
Both suspects were expected to appear before judges later on Thursday for a decision on them being placed in investigative detention.
One other person is being sought as part of the investigation, with several others not currently suspected of criminal offenses affected by it.
What happened during the series of antisemitic attacks in Europe in spring?
The stated date for the plot to attack Cologne's synagogue, late March this year, would have roughly coincided with a series of crimes in Europe targeting Jewish people or institutions in the weeks after the outbreak of war with Iran.
These included an explosion in front of Liege synagogue in Belgium on March 9, which caused property damage but no injuries.
Police in the Netherlands investigated a fire at Rotterdam's synagogue a few days later. An explosive device was set off outside a Jewish school in Amsterdam soon after, prompting heightened security across the Netherlands.
In London, a Jewish ambulance service was targeted in arson attacks in March, not long before two Jewish men were stabbed in broad daylight in what investigators treated as a likely act of terrorism.
Several months later, in September, police in Wuppertal in Germany arrested an Afghan national on suspicion of trying to set a fire at the city's synagogue.
