Almost 500 people from 71 countries joined the Women in Games Individual Ambassador Programme over the past year, with 14 countries represented for the first time since its founding in 2016.
Women in Games published its Individual Ambassador Annual Report 2025–2026, supported by Keywords Studios, which coincides with the programme's 10th anniversary.
The Ambassador Programme includes over 2,700 women and allies from 100 countries. Between 2025 and 2026, 498 new members joined, with 60% coming from outside the UK and US.
The new countries include: Algeria, Armenia, Bahrain, Bolivia, Cameroon, Côte d'Ivoire, Egypt, Iran, Lebanon, Montenegro, Slovakia, Vietnam, Zambia and Zimbabwe.
Overall, 33.1% of ambassadors have at least four years of industry experience, 31.2% have one to three years, and 28% have over eight years. Most ambassadors are employed full-time, while 22.6% are self-employed or freelance.
More than one in three ambassadors described themselves as being neurodivergent, while one in five said they have a disability or long-term condition.
The report highlights the community's activities and impact over the past year, such as meetups, mentoring, career support, and advocacy between 2025 and 2026. For example, the Careers Expo 2026 attracted over 1,300 registrations and participants from more than 65 countries.
This included the formation of Women in Games Europe in August, established to create "new opportunities for individual Ambassadors to connect, participate and raise their visibility across the European games ecosystem," as noted by Women in Games CEO and co-director Marie-Claire Isaaman.
"As we strengthen our regional presence, we aim to grow and connect our European Ambassador cohort, create more opportunities to contribute to events, panels, research and projects, and build partnerships with industry, education, cultural organisations, policymakers and other networks."
Women in Games also launched the Quarterly Ambassadors Connects series in May to facilitate networking among new and existing Ambassadors.
"When we launched the programme in 2016, we wanted to make it easier for women in games to find one another, share what they knew and create opportunities for those coming after them," said Isaaman. "Ten years on, 2,800 Ambassadors are doing that across 100 countries."
"The figures matter, but the real impact is in the connections: the person who finds a mentor, the student who discovers a career they didn't know existed, or the local group that gains the confidence and support to make change. This report shows how those actions add up to a stronger, more connected global community."
