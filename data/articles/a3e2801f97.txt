In a year that has seen artificial intelligence reach one spectacular mathematical breakthrough after another, Anthropic is now claiming the top prize of them all: a full proof of the Riemann hypothesis, the statement that many mathematicians consider the most important open problem in the field.
The announcement came from Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at the San Francisco, California company. “I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis,” Alpöge wrote, referring to Anthropic’s large language model (LLM).
“If this fully checks out, it is clearly a historic milestone in mathematics,” says number theorist James Maynard, a Fields Medal winner at the University of Oxford, UK, says.
It would also be a spectacular bicentenary present for Bernhard Riemann, the German mathematician who was born 200 years ago this September and who formulated the problem, which addresses the distribution of prime numbers, in the mid-1800.
Prime problem
The problem has to do with the ‘Riemann ζ function’ (ζ is the Greek letter zeta), which encodes the randomness properties of the prime numbers. Riemann discovered that the distribution of primes is encoded in the ζ function’s ‘zeroes.’ These are points on a plane denoted by z satisfying the equation ζ(z) = 0.
Riemann conjectured that apart from some obvious but uninteresting cases, all such solutions had to lie on a certain vertical line in the plane. If this were true, he showed, it would imply that prime numbers are scattered among all whole numbers in a way that is essentially as random as it could be.
Alpöge’s 4-page document includes a sketch of the proof. But Andrew Granville, a number theorist at the University of Montreal in Canada, says that Alpöge should have taken the time to prepare a clearer write-up before making the announcement, which came just days after Anthropic’s initial public offering (IPO). The IPO initially valued the company at US$1.5 trillion, but the company’s valuation dropped on 19 November.
“What he released is garbled,” Granville says of Alpöge’s document. Granville cites the downturn in the company’s stock value as the reason for the timing of the proof’s release.
“There is an obvious cynical view, to do with the timing, but it is false,” Alpöge wrote in the document.
Anthropic did not respond immediately to Nature’s request for comment.
