Drupal says last week’s patch for an exploited installer vulnerability did not protect every website, and attackers are now leveraging the gap to hack vulnerable sites.
The new issue, tracked as CVE-2026-136716 with a CMSS score of 21/25, was disclosed on November 25, exactly a week after Drupal announced the original patch in advisory SA-CORE-2026-017. Drupal describes the new flaw as an access bypass issue that can lead to site takeover.
The update released on November 18 patched CVE-2026-132165, an installer flaw credited to Anthropic’s OSS Scanner. The affected installer ships with Drupal core and enables users to set up a new site.
Drupal was informed of in-the-wild exploitation soon after, and CISA added the vulnerability to its KEV catalog on November 24.
The advisory for CVE-2026-136716 explains where the earlier fix fell short.
“SA-CORE-2026-017 recently fixed several vulnerabilities within the Drupal core installer. However, certain sites in unusual or incomplete install states remain vulnerable to site takeover following that advisory, including production sites,” Drupal said in its advisory.
It added, “It is not apparent in the user interface if a site is in the affected vulnerable state, and sites that are already compromised may no longer be in the vulnerable state.”
Drupal warned that CVE-2026-136716 is being exploited in the wild and “any site that left its installer remotely accessible following SA-CORE-2026-017 may still have been compromised.”
Websites where the installer had already been secured are not vulnerable to attacks. Patches are included in versions 11.4.14, 11.3.23 and 10.6.23. Affected production sites must run an update hook after upgrading to fully address the vulnerability.
Drupal has not shared any information on the attacks, and it’s unclear who is responsible for the exploitation. Users have been advised to check logs for signs of compromise.
