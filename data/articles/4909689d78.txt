What Is a Vibration Plate?
Vibration plates are all over my TikTok algorithm, but athletes widely used them in the ’70s and ’80s to improve performance and recovery. “They were first used in rehab and even in astronaut training to help prevent muscle and bone loss, but now they’ve become popular in gyms and at home for general fitness and wellness,” says Jeff Paul, the director of personal training at Chelsea Piers Fitness.
Today, it seems like everyone on my For You page has incorporated the vibrating plate into their routine, so I decided to see what all the chatter was about for myself. “Vibration plates gently vibrate while you stand, sit, or exercise on them. Those small, rapid vibrations make your muscles contract many times per second—way more than they would during a normal workout,” adds Paul. “When used correctly, vibration plates can help improve circulation and lymphatic flow, which supports recovery and reduces fluid buildup.”
It seems simple: a vibrating plate, but when you stand on the jiggling, high-motion platform (though speed settings are adjustable), your muscles are gently activated to promote lymphatic flow, aid workout recovery, and increase blood circulation. These plates claim to activate hundreds of muscles (even those your regular Pilates or cycling class can’t target), which may help slightly increase muscle mass. They also claim to increase bone density, helping to prevent osteoporosis, a condition that often occurs naturally as we age.
“Research supports vibration plates [are beneficial] for muscle strengthening, improved bone density, and reduced risk of falls," says Sarah Koehl, doctor of physical therapy at WAVE Physical Therapy & Pilates. "They can be particularly helpful for older adults or for anyone who cannot perform traditional strength training at higher loads.”
When shopping for vibration plates, I came across several categories—the two most popular being linear and oscillating, which move from side to side and tend to be gentler on joints. I opted for the latter option from LifePro. 
However, if you want to bulk up, Koehl adds that vibration plates provide slightly more—if not the same—muscle-mass increase as regular strength training, and a vertical plate is better for building muscle.
How I Use It
Vibration plates have a slew of versatile benefits. They can also be used for lymphatic drainage, which is what motivated me to adopt the device. Alternatively, you can work out on the plate, adding it to a push-up or squat routine to intensify the exercise. Using the device is pretty simple: I stand on the plate for 10 minutes a day and let the vibrations do the work. When I first started using the LifePro Vibra device, I used speed setting 2, but I’ve worked my way up to 6. What I love about the LifePro Vibra is that it is voice-controlled and comes with a remote, so I can change the speed and treatment time with my voice or a touch—without getting off the plate mid-session.
Paul urges you not to overuse a vibrating plate. “Too high a setting or too long a session can cause joint discomfort or dizziness,” he explains. “If you have a pacemaker, recent surgery, or any medical condition, definitely check with your doctor first. Otherwise, start slow and see how your body responds.”
Verdict
I consistently used the LifePro Vibra for 10 minutes every morning, usually after a workout, to aid in recovery and reduce morning bloat. I’ve heard stories of people who experienced immediate bowel movements or reduced bloating after using the device. While my results haven’t been that dramatic, I’ve definitely noticed a difference since I started using it. First, my muscles feel less sore, even after trying new workouts that target different muscle groups than usual. I also love using the plate as an extra layer of lymphatic drainage because the movement kick-starts my lymphatic system and helps avoid stagnant fluid, even on days when I don’t work out.
"Vibration plates are not a magic solution," Paul says. "You’ll get the most out of them when you pair the vibration with movement. So instead of just standing on it, try squats, lunges, or balance work. That’s when you’ll see real improvements in muscle engagement, circulation, and stability. On their own, they’re more of a supplement to your training than a replacement for it."
One thing to note: the device is bulky, especially in a small New York City apartment like mine. However, it can easily slide under a bed or behind a dresser. Whether you want to channel an ’80s fitness routine or improve lymphatic drainage, vibration plates are definitely worth trying if you have the space.
