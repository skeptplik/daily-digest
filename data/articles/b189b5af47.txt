Beijing – A malfunction in the passenger boarding stairs was likely responsible for a large gap between the stairs and a Japanese government plane carrying Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi when she arrived at an airport in Shenzhen last week, sources said Tuesday.
When Takaichi arrived at the airport to attend the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, the prime minister appeared surprised as she stepped off the aircraft over the gap, which was about 40 centimeters high.
Given strained Japan-China relations following Takaichi’s remarks in parliament in November last year regarding a potential Taiwan contingency, some speculated that China may have deliberately made it difficult for her to disembark.
Unlike the arrivals of leaders from other countries, there was also no group of Chinese young people gathered to welcome her, fueling perceptions that she had received a cold reception.
The gap between the aircraft and the boarding stairs is currently believed to have been caused by a malfunction in the stairs’ lifting mechanism, the sources familiar with the situation said.
Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara told a news conference Tuesday that the Japanese side noticed a gap after the boarding stairs had been positioned and informed the group aboard the plane of the problem just before they disembarked.
Takaichi and the others descended the stairs while taking care to ensure their safety, according to Kihara.
Takaichi visited China for the first time since taking office in October last year, traveling there on Wednesday and Thursday. Although she had an opportunity to shake hands with Chinese leader Xi Jinping, a Japan-China summit meeting did not take place.
