UN human rights experts have called on the Iranian authorities to end the escalating persecution of independent lawyers. They warn that limiting access to legal representation increases the risk of arbitrary detention, coerced confessions and unfair trials.
"Independent lawyers are increasingly being prosecuted and intimidated for their professional work and for defending human rights," the experts said in a statement in mid-November.
They said that between September 2025 and September 2026, at least 135 lawyers were issued with a summons, arrested, detained, suspended, dismissed or banned from traveling. In January and February 2026, 23 lawyers, including five women, were reportedly arrested after nationwide protests.
Defending the politically persecuted is becoming dangerous
A current example is the case of Amirsalar Davoudi, a human rights lawyer who was arrested in October in the central Iranian city of Isfahan, where he was defending a client in the Revolutionary Court. According to Iran Human Rights (IHRNGO), he was taken to hospital on November 9 after suffering a heart attack in custody.
"There is a long history to the persecution of and coercive measures taken against lawyers," Sina Yousefi, a 41-year-old lawyer and human rights activist, told DW.
In October 2022, Yousefi was arrested by security forces in Tabriz during the nationwide "Woman Life Freedom" protests. Later, he was sentenced to six months' imprisonment for "propaganda against the system." He has since left Iran, and now lives in the German capital Berlin.
"The pressure was already increasing on lawyers back then, because they were undertaking the defense of people who were arrested and publicly expressing their support for the demonstrators," said Yousefi. "The security services, judiciary, and criminal prosecution apparatus increasingly used targeted mechanisms to move against independent lawyers."
Even renowned and internationally known lawyers such as Nasrin Sotoudeh, who was co-awarded the Sakharov Prize in 2012, have been affected by this repression. In 2026, Sotoudeh was awarded the Vaclav Havel Human Rights Prizeafter being repeatedly arrested and imprisoned.
Yousefi said arrests and threats against lawyers working on political and human rights cases not only curtailed the rights of those accused to a defense, but also discouraged other lawyers from accepting such briefs. This, he added, compromised the fairness of trials.
Criminal proceedings have also been instigated, or other measures taken, against lawyers who undertook to represent demonstrators. Comments on social media about the violation of civil rights can also be cited as grounds for investigation and judicial proceedings.
Restrictions on the right to choose a lawyer
Yousefi also warned against tightening restrictions on a person's right to choose their own lawyer. According to the note to Article 48 of Iran's Code of Criminal Procedure, for people arrested on certain charges, access to lawyers of their own choosing is already restricted during the investigation phase. Defendants must resort to lawyers approved by the head of the judiciary. In some cases, even these lawyers are not granted access to all the files and evidence.
According to Yousefi, in many cases in which charges have been brought against demonstrators, lawyers have only been granted access to the files "when the preliminary investigations have been concluded and the evidence for the prosecution has been gathered — including confessions made during their imprisonment." Even then, he said, "their access to the records is limited and they have little time to prepare the defense."
"In many cases," Yousefi added, "the judges ascribe greater importance to reports by security agents than to the defense. Verdicts are handed down predominantly on the basis of this."
Shabnam Moinipour, the program director at the Center for Supporters of Human Rights (CSHR) in the UK, sees the independence of the bar as a fundamental precondition for the effective defense of citizens. The organization monitors the situation of lawyers in Iran, documenting violations of their rights, and following up at an international level.
"A lawyer must be able to call into question the charges brought by the public prosecutor and the credibility of the security agencies' reports without fear of criminal prosecution," Moinipour told DW. "Because when a lawyer's freedom and professional activity are threatened, their client's right to defense is impaired."
Fear and self-censorship among lawyers
In Moinipour's view, when the decision as to which lawyers are approved is made by the most senior representative of the same court authority, it raises serious questions about the independence of the bar.
"This restriction has psychological as well as legal consequences," she stressed. "For the accused, it may reinforce a sense of being unprotected, as well as feelings of fear and mistrust. It can also prevent them from disclosing details of threats, torture, and illegal behavior."
Moinipour considers this practice incompatible with Iran's obligations as a member of the UN Human Rights Committee. International law stipulates that people must have immediate access to a lawyer of their own choosing, be able to communicate with them in confidence and be supplied with sufficient means to mount a defense.
Referring to a joint report by the Tehran-based Defenders of Human Rights Center and the International Bar Association's Human Rights Institute (IBAHRI), as well as the recent statement by UN human rights experts, Moinipour said: "Even without mass arrests, the actions taken against a limited number of lawyers can send a threatening message to others and restrict their professional activity. This phenomenon is described as a chilling or deterrent effect."
However, Moinipour stressed that, despite this pressure, "many lawyers in Iran continue to defend the rights of their clients and accept the risk of arrest, criminal prosecution, and professional restrictions. In doing so, they are prepared, if necessary, to pay a high price for this resistance."
