An international team of marine geologists using a remotely operated submarine in an abyss in the western Pacific Ocean has found evidence of the deepest-known explosive volcanic eruption on Earth as much as 6,500 meters below the surface.
The researchers sailed to the Mariana Trench, among the deepest oceanic valleys on the planet, to deploy a remotely operated vehicle, or ROV, called Jason/Medea. They used the ROV to study the trench walls around the deepest-known point of seabed on Earth, called the Challenger Deep.
The ROV observed and collected pyroclastic rocks, telltale evidence of an explosive volcanic eruption composed of ejected rock fragments and volcanic glass.
