IIHF confirms cyberattack after website goes offline
Attack comes days after federation confirmed Russia won't compete at next month's world juniors in Alberta
The International Ice Hockey Federation has confirmed it has been targeted by a cyberattack, with the organization's website offline since Tuesday morning.
"We strongly condemn these actions and are taking all necessary steps to investigate the source of the attack and restore our systems," the IIHF said in a statement posted to social media Tuesday afternoon.
"Our platforms will be back online as soon as we are confident they are secure."
The cyberattack comes just days after the federation confirmed Russia would not compete at next month's world junior hockey championship in Alberta.
Multiple media reported that, early Tuesday morning, a pro-Russia image and caption appeared on the IIHF website. CBC News has not been able to confirm whether an image or message linked to Russia was posted.
The site has been offline since, displaying a notice saying it was "temporarily unavailable."
CBC News has contacted the IIHF for comment but has yet to hear back.

Russia and Belarus have been banned from competing in international hockey tournaments since 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine that year. But last month, the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) ruled Russia must be allowed back into the ice hockey world championships.
Despite this, Canada announced it was barring Russian players from competing in the world juniors.
"Canada’s position is unequivocal: as long as Russia continues its war of aggression against Ukraine, there can be no return to business as usual. Canada will continue to stand with Ukraine," Secretary of Sport Adam van Koeverden said in a statement provided to CBC News.
On Sunday, the IIHF confirmed Russia will not have a team at the championship, citing Canada's restrictions. They added that Canada will remain as host despite going against CAS's ruling.
The Russian Ice Hockey Federation called the IIHF's decision "unacceptable" and "unlawful," and demanded the tournament be removed from Canada. Russian Sports Minister Mikhail Degtyarev called for "consequences" that would affect Canada hosting future sports events.
CBC News has contacted Hockey Canada but has yet to hear back.
This year's world junior championship in Edmonton and Red Deer begins Dec. 26. Sweden claimed the gold medal at last year's tournament in Minnesota, while Canada took home bronze.
