The inaugural list from TIME crowned the UAE flag carrier Emirates as the best overall, despite travel disruption in the Middle East over the past nine months.
TIME has just unveiled its inaugural list of the world’s best airlines for 2027 – and Emirates took the top spot.
The ranking was based on passenger surveys, with those polled asked to rate their experience on up to five airlines they had flown with over the past three years. They were quizzed on elements like cabin comfort, service quality, entertainment, value for money and the food and beverage offering.
This data, which was collected by intelligence provider Statista, was then combined with an airline’s operational performance from travel data company OAG for an overall rating.
Despite travel disruption in the Middle East in 2026, Emirates also led in the rankings for best value and business travel airlines.
“Undeterred by the challenges of this year, we continue to invest in product and service across almost every touchpoint,” Emirates Airline president Sir Tim Clark said. “We will maintain that focus because, as this top ranking in TIME shows, customers notice when an airline gets the fundamentals right.”
The best airlines in Europe
No European airline broke into the top 10 for TIME’s inaugural list, although several made it into the top 20.
Turkish Airlines was the highest ranked in Europe, coming in 14th. Finnair was hot on its tail (fins), coming in 15th, followed by LOT Polish Airlines in 16th, and British Airways in 17th.
The final European carrier to make the top 25 was Iberia in 24th.
It’s not all doom and gloom for European carriers though. Ryanair, Europe’s largest airline, was named most reliable, while Virgin Atlantic took the title for best long-haul travel experience.
EasyJet also came eighth among the most reliable carriers, with Turkish Airlines in 11th, Wizz Air in 12th, British Airways in 15th, Vueling in 21st and Iberia again in 24th.
Turkish Airlines came fifth for long-haul travel, with KLM in ninth, Iberia in 10th, British Airways in 14th, Air France in 17th, Lufthansa in 18th and SWISS in 19th.
In the passenger’s choice category, which was purely based on travellers’ assessments and their willingness to recommend it to others, Switzerland’s Edelweiss placed seventh.
Finnair came out in 13th place, with LOT Polish Airlines in 14th, Turkish Airlines in 16th and British Airways in 21st.

The best airports in Europe
TIME and Statista also worked together to produce a list of the world’s best airports by size, and uhh… Europe only fared slightly better in comparison to the airlines ranking.
The rankings were split into four categories in the interest of fairness: Major airports with more than 50 million passengers annually, large airports with 25-50 million passengers, mid-sized with under 25 million, and small airports with under 10 million.
No European airport cracked the top 10 in the major category, though Oslo Airport placed sixth among the large airports.
For mid-sized airports, Helsinki placed 10th, while Newcastle came fifth and Bilbao ninth for small airports.
Again, there are some wins to be had: Among the best major airports for connections, Amsterdam Schiphol came fifth, with Munich in eighth and London Heathrow in ninth.
For large airports, Milano Malpensa ranked second, Stockholm Arlanda third and London Gatwick fourth.
