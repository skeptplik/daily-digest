Skies cleared along the US eastern seaboard on Friday, and power was being restored to hundreds of thousands of customers whose Thanksgiving plans were disrupted by heavy rains and high winds that swept over the region a day earlier.
The storms snarled travel, downed trees and power lines and led to water rescues from New York to Virginia. At its peak, nearly a million customers were without power.
The storms prompted the first-ever postponement of the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day parade in New York City, as well as the delay or cancellation of thousands of flights. In New Jersey widespread flooding reached levels not seen since 2012, after Hurricane Sandy.
By Friday morning, conditions had significantly improved, though hundreds of thousands remained without power and air travel was still affected by significant delays.
On Friday afternoonPoweroutage.us reported 400,577 customers were still without power, including 98,597 in Pennsylvania, 63,658 in Missouri and 60,297 in New Jersey.
Flightaware said delays within, into, or out of the US on Friday stood at 2,489, and cancellations at 144. On Thursday, more than 2,200 flights to or from US destinations had been delayed and more than 400 had been cancelled.
“The bulk of the storm impacts are behind us, but tidal flooding will continue today,” the National Weather Service said early Friday.
Parts of the midwest and the south were dealing with unusual snowfalls for the time of year, with St Louis, Missouri, getting 7.3in, and snow reported in Alabama and Tennessee. An ice arena in Wentzville, Missouri, was empty when the roof caved in at about 3am, according to the city.
“The south and south-east US will start with very cold morning temperatures today and Saturday as lows drop into the upper 20s and the middle 30s,” the National Weather Service said. “Freeze headlines are in effect for portions of the deep south. High temperatures Saturday will begin to moderate into the 60s.”
In New York City, authorities were rushing to clean up the damage the storm left in its wake. Several people had to be rescued from their cars amid flooding, said Zohran Mamdani, and similar rescues were reported in other coastal communities.
Strong winds and flooding disrupted train lines, toppled trees and caused power outages on Thanksgiving night, New York City emergency management officials said. Several scaffolding structures collapsed in the wind, but no injuries were reported.
New York City police said they had recovered a body of man along the Staten Island shoreline. It was not yet clear if the death was storm-related.
The storms also flooded areas from North Carolina through to New Jersey as high tide rolled in. Roads were closed, and emergency workers were rescuing people from cars stranded in water.
Downtown merchants in Avalon, New Jersey, scrambled to pump out saltwater on Friday after Thanksgiving brought some of the most significant tides in the seaside community’s history.
Cheryl McKenna of Ocean City, New Jersey, said water flooded her garage and surrounded her property, keeping her family inside for six hours on the holiday. She said flooding had become more frequent since Superstorm Sandy in 2012.
“Our family’s been here for 36 years,” McKenna said. “But honestly, we’re thinking is this the right place for us to be? We’re now retired, and we want to stay down here, but how often can we continue to do this?”
Donald Trump signed a disaster declaration on Thursday for Florida in response to earlier flooding that damaged hundreds of homes and required water rescues.
The US president had approved funding earlier this week, but gathered local officials on Thanksgiving to talk about it in a small signing ceremony at his home in West Palm Beach. “Since October it has been raining continuously,” the president said. “It’s the craziest thing. It stopped now.”
Meanwhile, authorities on the west coast were assessing the damage from high-tide-driven waves and strong winds.
Record tides along the California coast forced evacuations in areas from Humboldt county in the far north to Ventura county near Los Angeles.
Workers in Pacifica, California, rushed to evaluate the damage after part of the Pacifica pier collapsed into the ocean on Wednesday amid powerful waves.
The high tides continued to affect train service on Friday. Buses were pressed into service as Amtrak’s Pacific Surfliner canceled or shortened more than a dozen trains between San Diego and Los Angeles.
