Swiss and Italian cybersecurity agencies are warning about automated cyberattacks powered by AI agents targeting organizations in the critical infrastructure sector.
The two agencies listed four AI-powered penetration testing and red-teaming frameworks that have been involved in recent breaches and hacking incidents: ARTEX AI, CyberStrikeAI, DeepSeek Harness - Red Team, and PentAGI.
Both agencies have urged organizations to reduce the number of systems exposed on the internet, prioritize patching, and monitor for unusual activity.
Large-scale attacks in Italy
CSIRT Italia says it detected large-scale malicious campaigns that used autonomous AI agents to identify and exploit vulnerabilities and misconfigurations on exposed endpoints.
In some cases, the agency observed multiple AI agents working in parallel to compromise a large number of systems within a few hours.
In one instance involving a DeepSeek Harness setup, a coordinator assigned tasks to five AI agents that undertook a broad spectrum of operational stages, from information gathering to exploitation and persistence.
The agents chained vulnerabilities, misconfigurations, and compromised credentials, leveraging exposed systems as a foothold to reach internal networks.
“During the observed campaigns, autonomous agents demonstrated the ability to carry out complex, sustained activity to detect misconfigurations, link even low-impact vulnerabilities into complex attack chains, and dynamically adapt their operating modules,” CSIRT Italia says.
“They went as far as writing malicious code in real time to evade endpoint defenses and carry out actions deeper within the infrastructure.”
The Italian cybersecurity agency advises administrators to prioritize patching internet-facing systems, focus on addressing flaws known to be actively exploited in the wild, and disable remote management interfaces if not needed.
The agency also recommends monitoring control plane operations, authentication sources, traffic to internal networks, and outbound server traffic to detect suspicious activity.
First AI breaches in Switzerland
Switzerland’s National Cyber Security Center (NCSC) warned of similar campaigns targeting organizations in the country, confirming the first cases of successful AI-assisted cyberattacks.
“In recent days, the NCSC has received its first reports of cyberattacks against organisations in Switzerland in which the attack was carried out with the assistance of an AI agent,” the Swiss NCSC said.
“Various organisations and operators of critical infrastructure across at least two sectors have been affected by these cyberattacks.”
“In all cyberattacks known to the NCSC, the use of AI agents led to the successful compromise of the victim’s IT systems.”
The agency described rapid attacks in which agents exploited weak passwords and known vulnerabilities in exposed endpoints, using frameworks to verify weaknesses and independently decide what to do next.
NCSC says that the number of AI agents publicly (and often freely) available to cybercriminals has nearly doubled since October, resulting in these massive waves against Swiss organizations, including critical infrastructure operators.
The agency recommends that administrators of internet-facing systems patch critical vulnerabilities within 24 hours of release, add multi-factor authentication (MFA) or passkey protection, and enforce least-privilege principles.
