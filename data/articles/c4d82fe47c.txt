Rings of Power is back on Prime Video for a third helping of fantasy storytelling, and we won’t pretend we aren’t enamored with all the shenanigans happening in Middle-earth right now. Still, considering how much Amazon has spent on the series (and how incoherent parts of the story can be), we’re not convinced it's the streamer’s best fantasy show. In our minds, that honor belongs to another.
What makes a fantasy series brilliant depends on perspective. It’s true that when it comes to fantasy, most of us think of the traditional swords-and-sorcery we see in The Lord of the Rings, and while that sort of fantasy is indeed magnificent, there’s much more to the genre.
Thankfully, the folks at Critical Role, Titmouse, and Prime Video agree. If you’re looking for a fantasy animated series that pairs the epic quests of The Lord of the Rings with The Boys’ foul-mouthed humor and antics, we’ve got just the thing: The Legend of Vox Machina.
The D&D tabletop experience, in 48 episodes
Set in the fantasy world of Exandria, The Legend of Vox Machina follows the equally uncouth and loudmouthed adventuring party known as Vox Machina, voiced by the talented troupe of D&D-loving actors behind Critical Role. The party consists of elf twins, the rogue Vax (Liam O’Brien) and the ranger Vex (Laura Bailey), gnome paladin Pike (Ashley Johnson), elf druid Keyleth (Marisha Ray), gnome bard Scanlan (Sam Riegel), human gunslinger Percy (Taliesin Jaffe), and goliath barbarian Grog (Travis Willingham).
Broke and down on their luck, the group finds new purpose after Uriel, ruler of the Tal'Dorei continent, tasks them with finding and killing the blue dragon Brimscythe. However, as in any action-packed story, circumstances quickly change, forcing the group to decide what truly matters to them.
It’s hard to describe how perfectly The Legend of Vox Machina captures what it’s like to be at a table playing Dungeons & Dragons in just a few short episodes. The story opens in a tavern — the usual starting point for a humble D&D adventure — and quickly spirals into a brawl that gets the party kicked out. Moments later, Vox Machina are searching for their next job. It’s the same aimless wandering as a tabletop party looking for a new quest, complete with the fart and poop jokes you’d expect from friends casually hanging out around someone's dining room table.
This approach to adapting the campaign’s beginnings can work in some ways, such as getting audiences straight into the action. However, if you don’t know anything about the original tabletop campaign, these characters' backstories may initially feel underdeveloped, leaving you wondering why you should care.
That said, there’s a good reason for that, and if you stick with The Legend of Vox Machina through its later seasons, you’ll see how these characters’ relationships and beginnings pay off in a major way.
The Legend of Vox Machina finds its feet
The Legend of Vox Machina had fairly humble beginnings. It started as a Kickstarter project, originally aiming to adapt Critical Role's 115-episode Dungeons & Dragons tabletop campaign into a one-off animated special. That was until passionate Critters threw as much money as possible at the project, with Critical Role raising a record-breaking $11.3 million (enough for an entire season). That quickly caught Amazon’s attention. The company acquired streaming rights and commissioned two full seasons.
There was one major catch: nobody knew whether the show would make it past two seasons, forcing Critical Role to squeeze hundreds of hours of D&D shenanigans into just 24 episodes. The result was two enjoyable seasons that moved at a breakneck pace and left Critters who hadn’t watched the original campaign struggling to keep up.
Thankfully, the pacing has eased as Critical Role and Titmouse moved into seasons three and four. With more room to breathe, character arcs and development have improved sharply across the board, with Johnson’s portrayal of Pike’s struggle with her faith ranking among the rawest performances we’ve seen in an animated series.
The Legend of Vox Machina’s latest entry, season 4, is one of its most marvelous seasons of fantasy television. While less intriguing shows would send our heroes on yet another fantasy adventure after their massive win in season 3, season 4 focuses on the cost of being a hero. What makes a hero? Can a hero ever truly be happy?
If you’re curious, now is the perfect time to dive into The Legend of Vox Machina, with its final season set to debut sometime in 2028. It may be a while before we see our favorite band of misfits again, but it's better to start one of TV’s best fantasy series now than wait until the hype and discussion have faded. And if you want even more animated adventures from Critical Role, you can always check out The Mighty Nein, too, which has a second season coming early next year.
The Legend of Vox Machina seasons 1-4 are available to stream now, exclusively on Prime Video.
