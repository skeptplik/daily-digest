California-based SBC Medical Group Holdings Incorporated (NASDAQ: SBC) has disclosed a data breach impacting over 2 million individuals in Japan.
The medical services organization provides consulting, management, and operational support to medical corporations and clinics.
Hackers accessed an online platform SBC Medical Group manages and compromised databases containing customer information associated with certain clinics in Japan, the company said in a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).
SBC Medical Group continues to investigate the attack but has confirmed that personal and medical information was likely compromised.
The hackers stole the basic information of 1,624,000 individuals, including names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, gender, patient card numbers, member IDs, and survey responses.
They also compromised the reservation-request information of 255,416 people, as well as the content entered in web inquiry forms by 217,769 individuals.
According to the filing, 4,513 people also had their treatment descriptions and stored image file names stolen, while 15,007 had the basic information, treatment names, and review text exfiltrated. For 14 individuals, credit card information was also compromised.
“The Company has confirmed that all of these credit cards were in a state in which additional payments could not be processed, such as having expired,” SBC Medical Group said.
Additionally, the hackers stole customer passwords, along with 20,000 records containing employee names, IDs, and passwords.
“For 459 employees, information provided at the time of employment, including bank account and pension-related information, withholding tax certificates and dependent exemption declarations, was also affected,” the company said.
In a separate incident involving the third-party reservation service Tribeau, hackers accessed administrator accounts and accessed the information of approximately 19,000 individuals.
For 12,641 of them, the threat actors compromised Kana names, phone numbers, age, and requested treatments. For three people, the hackers also accessed message contents, dates of birth, and gender, and for 6,804 they compromised reservation information and treatment details.
“The operator confirmed that the obtained information was not linked to case or review information, including photographs, on Tribeau. Email addresses, residential addresses, credit card information, passwords, and electronic medical records were not included in that platform incident. The company and the provider continue to investigate the access and its cause,” the company said.
SBC Medical Group said both incidents have been remediated. It isolated the affected server and evicted the hackers, and rotated the passwords for all the clinics to which it provides management services on Tribeau.
“The company is continuing to assess the potential impact of the incidents on its business, financial condition and results of operations,” SBC Medical Group said.
