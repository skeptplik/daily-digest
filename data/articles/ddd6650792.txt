Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen say they are continuing their attacks on Saudi Arabia, claiming to have damaged a key oil refinery as well as the petromonarchy's financial district in the capital Riyadh.
Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree on Wednesday said that "accurate and direct" attacks targeting Ras Tanura — Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery and a major export terminal for crude — had "caused fires to break out at the facility."
Saree said an attack on Saudi Arabia's financial district, located in the capital Riyadh, was "carried out in three phases, with strikes hitting their targets accurately."
Saudi capital has been repeatedly attacked by Houthis
Saudi defense officials said that air defenses in the kingdom had taken down four Houthi drones at Ras Tanura and that 11 people had been injured when a tower in the financial district was hit.
The building in question was also targeted on November 19, but no one was injured in that attack.
The Houthis and Saudis have been fighting for over a decade as part of a civil war that has become a regional conflict with increased significance as a result of the ongoing conflict in Iran and the Strait of Hormuz.
