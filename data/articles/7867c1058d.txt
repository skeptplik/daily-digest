The UK should coordinate its response to any future pandemic with the Republic of Ireland because of the added complexity of disease control across the Irish border, the Covid-19 inquiry has said.
In its latest 'Test, trace and isolate' report into the programme of test and trace, it said cooperation was needed "to ensure that border control measures are effective and operable in practice".
During the pandemic, Northern Ireland chose to align its international border control measures with the UK rather than with the Republic.
Inquiry chair Baroness Heather Hallett said despite this decision, there was close liaison between the UK government, devolved administrations and the Irish government.
Data-sharing across the island
In addition, in May 2020 the Northern Ireland Executive announced it would develop its own contact tracing app rather than adopt the one in development in England.
It used the same technology and provider as the Republic of Ireland and the cross border apps were the "first in the world to achieve interoperability", the report said.
"The Northern Ireland Executive entered into a Memorandum of Understanding with the Irish government on 7 April 2020 to allow regular data-sharing and the sharing of information on international travel policies," said the inquiry report.
This included weekly meetings between the two governments' health departments and the sharing of data in relation to the trajectory of the pandemic and information about international travel policy.
It also involved working closely together in order to inform policies about border control.
"The complexity of relations between the four nations, together with the additional complexity arising from the land border between Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland, must be carefully examined and addressed in the development of any future border control approach."
Both sides of Irish border left open
The report also said both sides of the Irish border were left open to potential infection risk because of the lower level of measures put in place by the UK government.
"By April 2020, 93% of countries and territories had put measures in place â such as flight suspensions, border closures and travel restrictions â to control their international borders," said the report.
By contrast, it said, the UK government only requested (rather than directed) travellers from Wuhan, China, to self-isolate in January 2020 and travellers from some other high-risk areas to self-isolate in February 2020.
"This did not prevent a coachload of tourists from Wuhan entering the UK and travelling from England to Scotland and then to Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland in February 2020.
"The UK government implemented no further border control measures until June 2020."
Test and trace 'in part' successful
The inquiry found that the test and trace system was in part successful in Northern Ireland.
Despite having only 78 tracers trained by May 2020, the programme worked effectively because it operated from a centralised office.
"The team adopted a specific strategy that emphasised being personable and convivial in their interactions.
"By June 2020, 92% of people who had tested positive for Covid-19 and those who had been in close contact with them were reached within 24 hours," said the report.
It said the centralised system worked in Northern Ireland because its population is relatively small and it has a single, comparatively small healthcare system that enabled flexibility and a more rapid response.
"The scale of its system meant that it was able to replicate some of the advantages typically associated with local teams."
Inquiry 'exposed lies'
The inquiry report also found Northern Ireland was successful in the operation of payment entitlements to people self-isolating because it differed from other parts of the UK in using an existing Discretionary Support scheme.
On a UK-wide basis, Baroness Hallett said failings in testing and tracing left the UK "effectively blind" to how quickly Covid was spreading early in the pandemic.
"The UK was simply not prepared. Too many lives were cut short," she said.
Campaign group Covid-19 Bereaved Families for Justice UK said the inquiry has "exposed the lies and incompetence that left thousands of us grieving loved ones who should still be here".
The group said the government failures, such as the slow testing rate and sidelining of local public health teams "cost lives".
