With the wellness industry still booming, we’re inundated with products and devices that promise a shortcut to total health. Now, there’s a long list of gadgets, apps, topical creams, and ingestible supplements designed to clear your mind, calm your nervous system, boost energy, and improve sleep.
One supplement class that continues to reign in the mainstream is greens powders, more than just green juice in powder form. Below, the experts unpack everything you really need to know about the new generation of greens supplements.
What Are Greens Powders?
These supercharged green food powders aren’t just specialty items at vitamin shops; you can now find them everywhere—from drugstores to Amazon. But according to Philippa May, co-founder of women-first greens powder brand Naere, the big players have one glaring problem: they’re created by men, for men.
“We created Nectar 01 as an antidote to the male-dominated market,” she says. “The highly successful green powders already in the category were built around male-focused language: performance, optimization, stronger. And supplements aimed at women often leaned into insecurities: de-bloat, anti-aging, fixing your skin, helping you ‘bounce back.’ We felt there was nothing truly speaking to women as they are, beyond problems to be solved, and instead as modern individuals navigating the natural flux of daily life, in need of support rather than correction.”
This is where the next generation of greens powders comes in. Sitting alongside Naere is the much-hyped LymaID²: the biohacking superbrand’s latest launch (and its most accessibly priced offering, too). Founder Lucy Goff enlists the most revered specialists in a field to power her revolutionary launches, so she created ID² in collaboration with Professor Paul Clayton, a clinical pharmacologist and senior scientific adviser to the U.K. government.
Similarly, Catherine Hurley has focused on organic powdered fruits and vegetables, rather than a long list of trendy ingredients that may appeal to the world’s biohacking bros. Her Newroad 30 powder contains a megadose of polyphenol-rich fruits and berries, alongside vegetables and pulses to support the gut microbiome. In short, it’s not a hack for superhuman fitness, but a real-food supplement to boost your existing diet.
Most of these powders contain greens—from common produce like spinach and kale to specialty greens like alfalfa grass and chlorophyll. These powders often also include fiber-rich foods, probiotic sources, and digestive enzymes to help further reduce bloating.
Are Greens Powders Worth It?
But what do the experts really think? Nutritionists largely agree that greens powders can support wellness, but only when paired with a healthy, varied diet. (If your core food groups are Pret sandwiches and cortisol, mainlining a glass of greens each morning won’t save you.)
“The starting point for me is always food,” says Jessica Shand, a naturopathic nutritionist and author of The Hormone Balance Handbook. “No powder will ever replace the benefits of eating a wide variety of vegetables, fruit, legumes, nuts, and seeds because whole foods provide so much more than individual vitamins and minerals alone. They contain fiber, polyphenols, phytonutrients, and thousands of interactions that we simply cannot fully replicate in a supplement,” she says.
That said, we also have to be realistic about how people live. “Busy schedules, frequent travel, limited access to fresh produce, or simply not consuming enough plant variety are all situations where a greens or plant powder may be helpful,” Shand adds. “However, I would only ever recommend a high-quality product, and, in my opinion, those are relatively few and far between.”
