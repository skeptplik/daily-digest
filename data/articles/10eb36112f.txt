US District Judge Jessica Clarke has acquitted two brothers accused of stealing $25 million in cryptocurrency in about 12 seconds through a scheme that exploited a bug in the Ethereum blockchain.
Clarke's decision comes one year after a mistrial in the same case, after jurors failed to agree on whether to convict or acquit former Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) students Anton Peraire-Bueno and James Peraire-Bueno in this first-of-its-kind scheme, as Reuters first reported.
"The question before the Court is not whether the Peraire-Buenos' conduct was right or moral, or even whether it violated uncharged crimes. It is simply whether the evidence at trial, construed in a light most favorable to the Government, could support wire fraud and related convictions in this case," the federal judge said.
"Because the Government failed to prove that the Peraire-Buenos made any material misrepresentations in the execution of their scheme, the motion is granted, and a judgment of acquittal is entered for Anton Peraire-Bueno and James Peraire-Bueno."
The Peraire-Bueno brothers were arrested in Boston and New York in May 2024 on wire fraud and money laundering charges and were facing 20 years in prison for each count if convicted.
According to prosecutors, the brothers allegedly manipulated blockchain transaction validation processes by accessing pending private transactions, altering them, obtaining victims' cryptocurrency, and rejecting requests to return the stolen funds.
The indictment claimed they learned their victims' trading behaviors while preparing the attack (starting in December 2022) and took measures to hide the stolen proceeds and their identities.
They were also accused of using multiple cryptocurrency addresses and foreign exchanges and setting up shell companies. After the attack, they also allegedly moved the stolen crypto assets through a series of transactions to obscure their source and ownership.
The prosecutors also said that, while planning and executing the attack, the brothers deployed a series of "bait transactions" designed to identify "victim traders," exploited a vulnerability in the MEV-Boost relay code, causing the relay to release the full content of a proposed block prematurely, and reordered the proposed block to their advantage.
In the next stage of the scheme, they allegedly reordered the block on the Ethereum blockchain, which resulted in the theft of approximately $25 million in cryptocurrency from the victim traders.
Throughout the process, the brothers also allegedly searched online for information on carrying out the attack, concealing their involvement in the Ethereum exploit, and laundering the criminal proceeds through cryptocurrency exchanges with lax verification procedures.
Prosecutors also said they hired attorneys with expertise in cryptocurrency, extradition procedures, and the crimes outlined in the indictment.
