The US has lost its measles elimination status, global health authorities announced, an official recognition that the infectious disease is endemic in the US after being eliminated for 26 years.
The announcement comes as the US endures the worst measles outbreak since 1991, with more than 5,400 cases and eight deaths in 2026.
“Measles is resurging globally amid the persistent gaps in vaccination coverage, and this global trend is also affecting the Americas,” said Dr Jarbas Barbosa da Silva Jr, the director of the Pan American Health Organization, a regional branch of the World Health Organization. “The endemic measles transmission has now been re-established in Bolivia, Mexico and United States.” Silva made the final decision on US elimination status.
In a statement to the Guardian on Tuesday, Barbosa da Silva commended “the significant efforts undertaken by the United States to manage the measles outbreak through strengthened surveillance, timely case detection and response, targeted vaccination activities and coordination across the public health system”.
Measles is one of the most contagious viruses known to medicine and requires 95% of the community to be vaccinated to prevent outbreaks. The measles vaccine is safe and highly effective. However, pockets of low vaccination have grown in the US following pandemic-era disruptions and widespread misinformation.
The nation’s largest doctors group, the American Medical Association, described loss of measles elimination status as a “deeply troubling and entirely preventable setback for our nation”.
The loss of measles-free status comes after Donald Trump and his health secretary, Robert F Kennedy Jr, spent nearly two years sowing confusion about the safety of vaccines. In just one recent example, Trump called for the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) combination vaccine to be split into separate shots, arguing without evidence that “together, there could be a possibility they’re quite lethal”.
“As a pediatrician, I saw firsthand how decisions made in Washington hurt our children and families,” said Dr Annie Andrews, a Democratic senator-elect. “Nothing makes that clearer than the loss of our measles elimination status.” The loss of elimination status has already heightened criticism of Kennedy. High-profile epidemiologists called Kennedy’s work at the health department “malpractice” and equivalent to a “crime scene”.
In a statement posted on X late on Tuesday afternoon, Kennedy said the US “will stop measles outbreaks, protect American families, and work to regain U.S. measles elimination status”. He added that he would “continue to recommend MMR vaccination as the most effective way to prevent measles” and that officials with the Department of Health & Human Services “are working to close vaccination gaps and rebuild public trust. We will answer families’ questions honestly about vaccine benefits and risks and work with trusted health care providers and community leaders to prevent measles, limit its spread, and support timely treatment.”
Barbosa da Silva called the role of misinformation in measles transmission “dynamic” and urged health departments to develop bespoke campaigns tailored to undervaccinated communities.
Experts caution against piling blame for measles outbreaks solely on the Trump administration. The MMR vaccine has been the target of misinformation for more than two decades, including work by Kennedy before his White House role.
“There is a profound problem with measles in all three North American countries,” said Noel Brewer, a behavioral scientist at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill who studies vaccination behaviors, and specifically measles. He chairs a committee that considers evidence on US measles elimination status, and contributed to the scientific work that underpinned Pan American Health Organization’s decision.
“The US has had large outbreaks and exported those to Canada and Mexico, and those cases have come back again to the US,” said Brewer.
Canada lost its measles-free status in 2025, but Barbosa da Silva said the country now represented the “good news” in the region. The nation stopped measles transmission for 60 days. This is the first step on the way to regaining measles elimination status. The UK and several European nations have also recently lost elimination status.
“What we’re experiencing is not a United States-specific thing,” said David Margolius of the Big Cities Health Coalition. Instead, there is a, “global surge in cases, global decline in vaccination rate”.
Public health leaders with the Big Cities Health Coalition (BHC), which leads health departments that serve 47 million Americans, described the announcement as a “profoundly sad moment for our country”.
“Recent declines in measles vaccination rates have opened the door to outbreaks across the country, resulting in preventable illness, hospitalizations, and tragic loss of life,” BHC members said in a statement. “These outcomes are especially heartbreaking because measles is a disease that we know how to prevent. Yet too many voices spreading fear and false information are exacerbating the problem.”
Expert epidemiologists recommended US measles status change during a closed-door meeting in Washington DC. A range of evidence would have been presented, including interviews with positive patients collected by local US health authorities and whole genome sequencing that can provide a fingerprint of the virus’s origin.
Although measles elimination status is an official designation, it carries real consequences for Americans. Where measles cases were once typically imported by international travelers, endemic measles transmission means Americans face new risks of infection, and are being asked to dig up decades-old vaccination certificates and possibly quarantine for 21 days.
“Now is the time to go and find that evidence, because now this is a real day-to-day life thing that could happen to you,” Margolius said earlier in November.
Re-establishing elimination status will require “a national plan to eliminate measles again”, said Brewer, “an integrated plan that’s publicly communicated with specific tasks assigned to named parties”. He added that the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the president should speak out “forcefully” in favor of vaccination, and Congress should allocate more funding.
