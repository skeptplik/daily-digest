Nvidia’s decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO sends a message “that the national security of this country is not as important as your relationship with president Trump,” Democratic senator Ruben Gallego said.
Huang, who co-founded the company in 1993, was ousted by the board after allegations that he had three affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy and two with employees. But the Nvidia board reinstated him after an intervention from the US president.
“We also have to figure out how corporate America doesn’t take a bad lesson from this, and thinks that somehow it’s OK for them to evade national security for the purposes of just selling more,” Gallego, an Arizona Democrat considering a White House campaign, said.
Meanwhile, California representative Ro Khanna wrote on social media that “rules should apply to everyone,” noting that “the rich + powerful, like Jensen, do not get to evade export controls or accountability just because it props up the stock price.”
Huang returned to the company’s HQ in Santa Clara, California, on Monday and received a hero’s welcome, with crowds of employees chanting “Jensen, Jensen, Jensen!”
His return as CEO came despite Huang being subject to several government and internal investigations, shareholder lawsuits and accusations that he’s a national security threat.
Nvidia is one of the most valuable businesses in the world, worth about $4tn, and the entire AI ecosphere relies on it. The company engineers advanced chips used to train AI models and power tech firms such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google.

Juliana Stratton, the incoming Democratic senator from Illinois, said she would support significantly reforming or abolishing the filibuster in order to allow Congress to deliver on the promises they made American voters.
The filibuster means that most successful legislation in the Senate must have 60 supporters instead of a simple majority. Eliminating that requirement is one of several reforms Democrats can act on after they won an unexpected 56-44 majority in the next US Senate, their largest majority in nearly two decades.
Stratton, currently the Illinois lieutenant governor, was a professional mediator before she entered politics. For two decades, she ran a dispute resolution firm, bringing people who did not agree together to find ways to solve problems.
The experience will serve her well in a divided government, she told the Guardian. She welcomes Republicans who want to stand up to Trump and “have a real spine” to work with and Democrats in the US Senate.
“We cannot go in kind of waffling on, ‘Well, we’ll see what we can do, it’s going to be hard.’ That’s the same kind of thing that really turned so many voters off this election cycle – people going in, telling them how hard everything is and that there’s so many hurdles,” Stratton said. “Sure, there are hurdles, but we were elected to do hard things.”
A top House Democrat is broadening his investigation into Donald Trump Jr’s venture capital fund 1789 Capital by demanding records from four companies that secured an investment before benefiting from lucrative federal contracts or policies, AP reports.

US envoy 'not certain' military aid to Israel will end this year as Trump promised
US ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee has cast doubt on whether Washington would end its $3.8bn in annual military aid to Israel by the end of this year, as promised by president Donald Trump on the day of this month’s midterm elections.
In his surprise announcement as voters headed to the polls on 3 November, Trump said he had made a deal with prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu several months earlier to stop providing Israel with around $4bn in annual US funding.
Trump said Israel no longer needed US funds, and the issue had been used as a “bad talking point” by ‌his left-wing critics, Reuters reported.
The election that ‌day subsequently saw Democrats, including many who publicly oppose military funding for Israel, win control of both houses of Congress ‌from Trump’s Republicans.
Trump did not provide details or explain how the funding would be halted. The US has a longstanding agreement with Israel, known as a Memorandum of Understanding, to provide it with $3.8 billion a year until 2028 to fund purchases of American-made military equipment.
Congress to grill Jensen Huang over ties with China and Trump’s intervention
Shrai Popat
Jensen Huang’s reinstatement as Nvidia’s chief executive is “exhibit A” of the “political corruption and rot at the heart of the Trump administration’s big tech and AI policy”, Chris Van Hollen, the Maryland Democrat vying to become the next Senate majority leader, told reporters Monday.
Van Hollen said he intends to focus on Huang’s “efforts to increase and accelerate the export of high-end chips to China, how he’s been accomplishing that, how he’s been getting his way with the administration” when the reinstalled Nvidia CEO appears before the Senate banking committee on 3 December.
It will be one of several appearances on Capitol Hill next week where skeptical lawmakers from both parties will grill Huang over his ties to a woman that some in the intelligence community have said was a spy for China. Huang will also sit for transcribed interviews with the House foreign affairs committee, as well as the select committee on the Chinese Communist party and the House intelligence committee.
“It’s completely hypocritical for the Trump administration, on the one hand, to say the reason we have to take all the brakes off AI development here at home is because China will leap ahead if we don’t,” Van Hollen said. “When in fact, it’s the Trump administration that’s helping China leap ahead.”
Nvidia’s board reinstated Huang after Donald Trump intervened on the executive’s behalf, assuring company leadership that he did not pose a national security risk despite questions surrounding his relationship with Alice Huang, a tech executive of no relation who has been accused of acting as a Chinese asset and routing advanced chips to Beijing via her data center company, Megaspeed.
In an interview with CNBC on Monday, the US president defended his involvement in Huang’s reinstatement, arguing it would be a “bigger threat” for Nvidia to lose Huang’s “brain power” in the AI race. Trump also confirmed that he had provided the board with assurances regarding any national security concerns.
“I spoke to them, let me put it that way. And I convinced them,” Trump said.
