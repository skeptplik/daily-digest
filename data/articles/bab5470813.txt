The backlash to “pervert glasses” has been swift and severe.
Smartglasses such as Meta’s Ray-Bans, which have built-in cameras, microphones, and AI assistants, have been banned at some Australian swimming pools and playgrounds, and condemned as a possible invasion of privacy.
There are widespread fears about the glasses being used to film people, especially women, without their consent, and the resulting footage being posted on social media.
Now “pervert glasses” have gained a new layer of infamy: being crowned Macquarie dictionary’s word of the year.
The term “pervert glasses”, which also won the people’s choice word of the year for 2026, was described by the committee of word experts as a triumph of linguistics.
“The coinage pervert glasses is a succinct reflection of the state of the world in 2026 in so many ways,” they said.
“On the one hand, we’ve seen a massive acceleration in the capabilities of AI and products that integrate it. At the same time, the undeniably critical nickname mirrors the widespread calls to hit the brakes on this seemingly unchecked development.
“The name represents the year we’ve all endured, with AI, the manosphere, increasing surveillance in supermarkets, and more. Sure these new gadgets might be marketed as smart glasses or camera glasses, but it seems to us that pervert glasses paints a more apt reflection of the year.”
Honourable mentions from the committee went to “PMOS”, or polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome, which has received greater recognition alongside spreading awareness of medical misogyny; “agentic”, which refers to the ability, especially of artificial intelligence, to make decisions independently; and “Marrickville pause”, a colloquial term referring to a temporary halt in conversation by people under Sydney’s flight path.
“Digital dementia”, which refers to short-term memory loss from a reliance on technology and artificial intelligence, received an honourable people’s choice mention.
The committee said digital dementia was “not a real medical diagnosis but that hasn’t stopped the fear of an increasing reliance on digital devices from resonating deeply”.
“People are finding that they’re struggling to navigate without Google Maps and to make and remember plans without iCal,” they said.
“It will be interesting to see if all of this outsourcing does, in fact, lead to impaired cognition in the long term.”
In a sign of the times, other words on the shortlist included “nudify”, referring to AI-generated pornography, and “AI;DR”, short for “AI; didn’t read”.
Last year, AI slop was dubbed the term that was the epitome of 2025 linguistics, with the committee saying it embodied the word of the year’s general theme of reflecting “a major aspect of society or societal change throughout the year”.
The committee’s choice and people’s choice winning words are chosen from the list of new entries included in the annual update of the Macquarie dictionary online. The people’s choice winner was selected from public votes cast on its website.
