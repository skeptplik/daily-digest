Japan’s concerted push to bolster the yen is running into a major headwind, after the nation’s biggest life insurers slashed their currency hedging to a record low in the fiscal first half.
Nine of the largest life insurers collectively hedged 43.3% of their foreign-asset holdings with derivatives in the six months ended Sept. 30, according to analysis of their earnings reports. That’s the lowest-ever level based on data going back to 2011. The volume of currency derivatives fell 7.6%, outpacing a 1.5% decline in overseas securities.
The data serves as a reminder of the Herculean task confronting Japan’s authorities as they seek to prop up a currency that’s been buffeted by elevated crude prices and the nation’s wide interest-rate gap with the U.S.. The yen breached the closely watched ¥160-per-dollar level for the first time since September earlier this month, putting traders on alert for more intervention by policymakers.
