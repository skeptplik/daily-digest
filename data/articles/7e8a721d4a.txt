Demonstrations began Friday in over 25 European countries and are expected to continue through the weekend as part of a coordinated international protest movement.
Workers and students took to the streets across Europe on Friday, demanding higher wages, better living conditions and greater investment in public services.
The demonstrations follow weeks of protests over rising housing costs, worsening education conditions and increased military spending, with many calling for peace and more funding for healthcare and welfare.
Italy: Protesters demand higher wages and better public services
In Italy, a 24-hour strike called by the Italian General Confederation of Labour (CGIL) trade union sparked demonstrations in more than 100 locations.
Protesters criticised stagnant wages, rising living costs and what organisers described as the government's failure to improve public services and protect workers' rights.
The protests also drew opposition leaders, who joined demonstrations and criticised Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's government over wages and social spending.
The strike, which coincided with the International Day of Student Mobilisation, affected both public and private sectors, disrupting public transport in several cities, as well as schools and some health facilities.
Planned strikes by air transport workers were cancelled for Friday, although separate walkouts involving airport staff remain scheduled for Sunday at Milan and Venice airports.
Authorities deployed additional police in several Italian cities where multiple demonstrations took place.
Croatia, Austria and Germany: Students call for education over military spending
In Croatia, students boycotted classes and protested in around a dozen cities, demanding more investment in education, housing and public services rather than military spending.
In Austria, several hundred people gathered outside the University of Vienna for a youth-led protest calling for more education funding and less spending on weapons. Police dispersed the demonstration following reported disturbances, using pepper spray and making several arrests.
In Germany, demonstrators called for greater investment in education and opposed a possible return to compulsory military service. The rallies followed protests on 31 October that drew thousands of people into the streets, with violence reported in some cities.
