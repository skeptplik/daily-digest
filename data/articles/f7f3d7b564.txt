All season the North Melbourne AFLW side was not allowed to use the word "three-peat".
The team came into the year as back-to-back premiers and outright favourites for the flag. They started the finals series as minor premiers, having lost just one game (to Hawthorn), their first defeat in three years.
For the duration of the year, the AFLW world was talking about the Kangaroos' incoming third consecutive flag. But not the Roos themselves.
As Jasmine Garner said post-game, it's a cliche as old as football, but one that's tried and tested: treat the season as one game at a time.
Then the siren sounded. They had defeated Melbourne by 26 points under lights at Princes Park after beating Brisbane the two years prior.
And they let the emotions overrun them.
All Australian Erika O'Shea said it was one of "the best moments of my life, 100 per cent".
"Oh my god, it's the most surreal feeling,"O'Shea told ABC Sport.
"Nothing will ever feel like this. I started crying instantly when I heard that siren.
"I think a sigh of relief, but also just all the hard work that we've done during the year, and yeah … three-peat. I can't believe it.
"To finally be able to say it with a smile is the best thing ever."
Head coach Darren Crocker said it was up to the AFLW community and media to decide whether the side was a dynasty or not, but he was pretty sure the consecutive flags made for one.
He said the 2024 flag — the first AFLW cup for North Melbourne — meant a lot. The second broke new ground when the first side became the first to go back-to-back in AFLW history.
And the three-peat felt pretty good.
Even opposition captain Kate Hore said "we'll talking about this team forever".
Garner said this year was more difficult than last.
"I always had the belief that we were good enough to do it, but you know, this year, it hasn't been perfect," she said.
"We've lost one game in 42, but we've been challenged. We've had injuries. We've had different hurdles pop up along the way.
"But we felt this year we were kind of building slowly and we kept our best footy to the finals. I think that's what got us over, our experience and our calmness.
"I reckon this final series has been the best it's ever been, but it's just so good for women's footy. Just really proud of the group."
Kangaroos forward Kate Shierlaw said this win made her the most emotional of the three.
"I'm very overwhelmed with emotions. Relief, excited," Shierlaw said.
"It's just like a lot of hard work over four or five years … just Kearns [Emma Kearney, North Melbourne stalwart defender and Shierlaw's partner] and I having a great time out here, living our dreams. Three in a row."
Shierlaw and Kearney hinted at possible retirement. Shierlaw said she would "talk to the club" and Kearney told the broadcast she may have played her last game.
