This year Simon Skinner was named emerging designer of the year at the Dezeen Awards. Here, he selects five projects that showcase his work.
Swedish designer Skinner set up his studio in 2020 and has rapidly become known for his sculptural furniture and installations that explore rethinking and reusing found materials.
“My practice is centred around material culture, exploring the relationship between objects, meaning and everyday life,” Skinner told Dezeen.
“What connects my work is an interest in how forms carry associations and how these can be reshaped to offer new perspectives on things we know and take for granted.”
Skinner finds that the things that excite him about design are also “the same things that make me suffer”.
“You never quite know what’s coming next when running your own practice, but there’s also something exciting about that uncertainty,” he said.
“A material or an object you’ve overlooked for years can suddenly become the starting point for a whole body of work.”
Over the coming year, along with creating new collections and public installations, Skinner hopes to dedicate more time to research.
“I want to make more room for research and experimentation, and give myself time to follow an interest before knowing exactly where it might lead,” he said.
“When I started researching Afro combs at Konstfack, I became aware of how much history and symbolism an everyday tool can hold. That experience still informs how I work,” he continued.
“I want to keep exploring the objects and forms we take for granted, looking at the meanings they’ve inherited and what happens when you change their material, scale or context.”

Vanity Table
“Extending the exploration of beauty rituals within the Syntax collection, the Vanity Table offers an alternative reading of an object associated with personal grooming.”
Upholstered furniture
“These one-of-a-kind pieces, a daybed and an armchair, are upholstered in hand-dyed canvas in deep magenta hues.
“Combining traditional upholstery techniques with an experimental approach to colour and detail, the pieces embrace the irregularities of the dyeing process, making them an integral part of their character.”
Aluminium stool/side table
“This piece of furniture explores our relationship to the mechanical lock. The lock is integrated into the leg, with keys used to secure the connection between the leg and the seat.
“The keys can also serve as hooks for keychains and personal belongings, inviting individual expression.”
