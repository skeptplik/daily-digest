Speaking at a White House following his trip to Asia, Trump defended his ties with Xi and Putin, claiming the Russian leader wanted to end the war in Ukraine through diplomacy. He also called for a "real deal" with Iran.
US President Donald Trump defended his relationships with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin following his four-day trip to Asia, while addressing the wars in Ukraine and Iran and outlining new trade proposals.
Trump praised his meetings with both leaders, calling their relationship a "positive" development.
He also called critics of his relationships with Putin and Xi "stupid", following criticism of his decision to describe Putin as a "friend".
Asked whether Putin was a friend of the United States, Trump said he could not speak for the country, but described his relationship with the Russian leader as "fantastic" and a "very good thing".
"He's got lots of nuclear — he's second to us in nuclear weapons, it's very good. I think it, I think it gives us a lot of additional strength. And he's having a hard time right now because he's in a war that should have lasted one day and it's lasted for five years".
Russia-Ukraine war: Trump says Putin wants to end the conflict
Turning to the war in Ukraine, Trump said Putin had expressed a desire to end the conflict "by diplomatic means".
However, Trump acknowledged difficulties in reaching a peace agreement, blaming tensions between Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.
"The problem is President Zelenskyy and President Putin have tremendous hatred, and that's never easy," he told reporters, adding: "We're trying to help them [...]. They don't know how to end it. They want to end it. They're both stuck in it".
Asked about security guarantees for Ukraine: “We don’t want to talk about security guarantees yet, because first we need the deal.”
While the war is still ongoing, US, Russian and Chinese leaders issued a joint peace declaration in China, pledging to "maintain strategic stability and international security". They also outlined shared ambitions to reform the UN and keep the International Criminal Court in check.
Trump says he wants a ‘real deal’ with Iran
On the ongoing conflict with Iran, Trump said Tehran wanted an agreement with Washington but had yet to offer acceptable terms.
"We want to make a real deal, not a fake deal," he said, accusing the Iranian government of "bull-----ing people for 51 years."
Trump also predicted further falls in US petrol prices, although he acknowledged that much would depend on how the conflict with Iran is resolved. Despite his claim that petrol prices have fallen recently, they remain around €0.13 per litre higher nationwide than when he took office.
The President also warned that Washington would not allow Iran to develop a so-called dirty bomb, citing unconfirmed reports.
"There is a rumor that they're building a dirty bomb," he told reporters. "We will not allow that."
Trade deals with Mexico and Canada
Trump said the US was "getting close to a deal" with Mexico following talks with President Claudia Sheinbaum, but insisted that Washington would not accept a trade surplus in Mexico's favour.
"We can't have you have a surplus," Trump said he told Sheinbaum. "We're not going to let that happen."
On Canada, Trump said "more work remains to be done", accusing the country of taking advantage of the US in trade for years.
He also said Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney wanted to revive the Keystone pipeline project, but warned that Canada would need to offer further concessions before an agreement could be reached.
Heavy taxes on US data centres built abroad
Trump also announced plans to impose heavy taxes on American companies building data centres overseas, saying he wanted the facilities to remain in the United States.
"We're not allowing data centres to go into other countries," he told reporters, warning that companies could face "a tremendous tax".
The President offered no further details and acknowledged that he had not yet discussed the proposal with his own officials.
He also encouraged Americans to support data centre projects in their communities while negotiating benefits from developers.
