Josh Giddey has had 23 points, 22 assists and 14 rebounds for the Chicago Bulls.
It is the most assists for the Chicago Bulls since 1985.
It is the first time since 1966 that a Bulls player has had at least 20 points, 20 assists and 10 rebounds in a single game.
Josh Giddey has broken his own NBA record and rewritten 60 years of Chicago Bulls history with a remarkable triple-double in an overtime loss to Denver.
The point guard improved his own assist high for Australians in the NBA with 22, while adding 23 points and 14 rebounds in the Bulls' 151-145 loss to the Nuggets.
No Chicago player had notched more than 20 assists since 1985, and Guy Rodgers in 1966 was the last Bull to record at least 20 points, 20 assists and 10 rebounds in a game.
Giddey, who hit 10 of 17 field goals and scored seven late points to force overtime, recorded his 33rd NBA triple-double to move to 14th on the all-time list — equal with countryman Ben Simmons.
Remarkably, it came after he managed just two points and was benched by coach Tiago Splitter in the fourth quarter of a loss to Washington.
He had combined 18 points, 14 rebounds and nine assists in the game before that, rebounding from a four-point effort in the game prior to sum up a rollercoaster season for the battling 4-12 Bulls.
"He [Splitter] can relate to us. He was good about it and doesn't sugar-coat it. I wasn't surprised or confused as to why I wasn't out there," Giddey said post-game of his recent struggles.
"You appreciate it a lot more after the fact. During the moment, you don't want to hear those things.
"I have learnt you've got to be able to move on. I can't come into practice still pissed off and dwelling on things."
Nikola Jokić had 37 points, 18 rebounds and 11 assists for the 202nd triple-double of his career — closing the gap on NBA leader Russell Westbrook (209) — while Jamal Murray scored eight of his season-high 45 points in overtime.
Giddey's stat line came without their two leading scorers, the injured Norman Powell and Caleb Wilson (fractured metacarpal), who are averaging a combined 39.7 points per game this season.
"He played extremely well," Splitter said.
"We've got to give them flowers when they deserve and last game they didn't have it. Tonight they brought it."
Earlier on Sunday (Monday AEDT), Giddey's Boomers teammate Dyson Daniels put the brakes on Detroit star Cade Cunningham to hand Atlanta a fourth-straight win.
Daniels had 14 rebounds to go with eight assists, five steals and five points in his 39 minutes on court in the 107-97 win.
He kept Cunningham (five points) under wraps — the All-Star failed to hit double figures for the first time this season.
The 23-year-old leads the NBA in steals at three per game through 17 matches this season, and also tops the league in deflections and loose balls recovered.
The Hawks are now perched in third place in the Eastern Conference with an 11-6 record.
The Pistons led 85-74 at the start of the fourth quarter before the Hawks took a 92-85 lead with 18 unanswered points. Isiah Joe's three-pointer with 4 minutes and 52 seconds remaining ended Detroit's scoreless streak.
Nickeil Alexander-Walker posted 27 points, while Corey Kispert scored a season-high 18 points and Lu Dort 16 for the Hawks, who were without CJ McCollum (left calf strain) and Australian Jock Landale (right big toe strain).
