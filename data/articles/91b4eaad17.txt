The social media company has 60 days to disable the ‘Get tips from your gallery while browsing Facebook’ feature and introduce explicit consent.
Italy has fined Meta €805 million for using photos and videos from Android users’ galleries on Facebook without specific consent or adequate disclosure.
The Italian Data Protection Authority said the feature, called “Get tips from gallery when browsing Facebook”, violated the European privacy directive known as the ePrivacy directive.
The directive protects smartphones as part of a person’s private sphere and requires “free and informed consent” for such access, the authority said.
Investigators found that the feature was enabled by default and that Facebook requested gallery access through a single permission covering multiple app functions with different purposes.
The authority gave Meta 60 days to disable the feature and seek separate consent for its use. Italy has about 20 million Android users, the agency said.
The fine was imposed on Meta Platforms Ireland Limited, which challenged Italy’s jurisdiction, according to the statement.
The authority said Meta’s “lack of cooperation” with the investigation factored into the penalty, which amounts to approximately 0.47% of the group’s global turnover.
The proceedings did not look at the possible use of user content to train AI models, something Meta also denies doing.
Meta said it would appeal the decision. A Meta spokesperson said the company “strongly disagrees” with the regulator’s assessment, its interpretation of the law and the “disproportionate level of the fine”.
“This is a simple feature that helps people share photos and videos on Facebook,” Meta said in a statement.
“Users must grant permission on their device before the feature can operate, and they can change that permission at any time in their settings," Meta added.
The investigation was launched in July 2025 following a complaint by the Bolzano Consumer Protection Centre and was based on a technical analysis conducted by Italian authorities.
