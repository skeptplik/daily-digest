On a recent cold Sunday morning, I had no interest in doing anything except hiding from the world. Then I did a few small tasks that completely shifted my mood. First, I spritzed a home fragrance with notes of orange and cinnamon around my apartment; next, I raised the blinds to let in some sunlight and tidied up the living room—clearing away clutter, folding blankets, and fluffing the pillows on the couch. As I did, I recalled the words of Loewe’s perfumer, Nuria Cruelles, about one of her candles: “It has the power to transform your house into a home.” That’s exactly what I was doing, simply by adding a bit of scent and straightening up. Another expert, Dr. Rodrigo Arteaga, explains that anyone can turn their home into a “place that improves their health” by following a few simple habits.
There are indeed many small routines we can adopt to turn our home—the place where we relax and let our guard down—into an environment that fosters well-being. Psychologist María Martínez echoes this, encouraging people to practice the Japanese philosophy of kaizen, which emphasizes the transformative power of making gradual, continuous improvements to our lives. “It’s important to create an environment where you look around and like what you see,” she says. “This releases endorphins and makes you feel good. If instead, everywhere you look, there’s a reminder of a task you need to do, something unattractive, or clutter that causes stress, you’ll end up feeling worse. If it gets bad enough, you’ll soon stop wanting to be in your own home. You’ll find reasons to leave or stay late at the office, coming home only to sleep. These are all signs that you need to make changes—not just in your life, but in your home, which should serve as a sanctuary where you can recharge.”
Here are seven practical tips to help create a home environment that helps boost your mental and physical health.
1. Buy fresh flowers
When Florence Pugh was asked in a recent Vogue interview what a perfect day off looks like for her, one of the first things she mentioned was buying flowers. “I love going to my florist and putting together a beautiful bouquet; it makes me so happy,” she said. She’s not alone. According to a study by Dr. Nancy Etcoff of Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard Medical School, flowers ward off worry, anxiety, and sadness. Study participants felt noticeably better after having flowers in their homes for just a few days—and tended to place them in the kitchen, dining room, and living room, the spaces where they spent most of their time.
2. Let natural light in during the day, and create “light islands” at night
Whenever possible, open your curtains and blinds in the morning. As Dr. Arteaga points out, “This gives your brain a powerful signal to synchronize its biological clock. Natural light helps your body recognize when it’s day and when it’s night, giving you more energy during the daytime and helping you rest better at night.” After dusk, create “light islands” inspired by the Danish philosophy of hygge, which offers plenty of ideas for designing a wellness-focused home. As Meik Wiking explains in his book My Hygge Home, you can bring warmth to dark corners of a living room using lamps that cast light downward and bulbs that emit a soft, warm glow. Floor or table lamps placed near a sofa or reading chair work especially well. For more general lighting in spaces meant for relaxation, hygge experts recommend ceiling or wall fixtures with glass or fabric shades that diffuse light gently, rather than harsh overhead chandeliers or pendants.
3. Put on your favorite music
Studies show that listening to music you love provides an immediate boost to your well-being. “Music has an instant, almost magical effect on us,” explains psychologist Marta Calderero. “Within just a few seconds of hearing a favorite song, your brain is flooded with endorphins and oxytocin. That’s why you suddenly feel that rush of euphoria and such an intimate connection to a track. And it doesn’t stop there—after a couple of minutes, the brain begins releasing dopamine, making us enjoy each second a little more than the last. It’s the most beautiful way I know to regulate emotions and reconnect with the present moment.”
4. Create your own “wellness corner”
Hygge inspired another recommendation on our list. As Wiking explains, styling a small corner of your home with cozy lighting, blankets (a hygge staple), pillows, a plush rug, and a few favorite objects creates a dedicated spot to unplug from the world. It’s a simple way to build an inviting nook that signals to your brain that you’re in a safe space where it’s okay to relax and unwind.
5. Walk barefoot
Going barefoot indoors is another of Dr. Arteaga’s recommendations, and Beatriz Crespo, an expert in healthy micro-habits, seconds the idea. Beyond its physical perks—like improved circulation and balance—walking barefoot reduces stress and encourages relaxation through direct contact between the ground and the nerve-rich soles of your feet.
6. Prioritize fresh air and ventilation
Proper ventilation offers clear microbiological benefits, as biologist María Dolores Cima Cabal explains: “It helps reduce the density of microorganisms that accumulate inside the home.” At the same time, it delivers real psychological perks, since breathing fresh air reenergizes the body and supplies oxygen to the brain. A Harvard University study confirms that cognitive function can suffer—and reaction times slow down—in stuffy environments.
7. Light a scented candle or spritz your favorite home fragrance
Returning to that chilly Sunday morning at the start of this piece, candles have a unique ability to turn a house into a home through the evocative power of scent. When you choose an appealing fragrance—whether as a room spray or a candle—your brain registers it as a cue that you’re somewhere you can let your guard down. “Smell is the sense that most powerfully triggers memories, and the one our brain responds to with the greatest intensity,” concludes Calderero. “It forms a direct link between a scent and an emotion. If you keep a signature scent at home, that olfactory connection becomes so strong that the moment you smell it, your brain associates it with rest, comfort, and warmth. Choosing a specific fragrance helps your brain recognize that it’s in a space where it doesn’t need to stay on high alert—and your nervous system naturally slows down.”
