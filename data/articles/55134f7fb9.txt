Democrat Mary Peltola has won Alaska's hard-fought Senate race, completing a sweeping victory for the party in the US upper house in the midterm elections.
The win gives the Democrats a 56th Senate seat following a tumultuous and controversial election in Alaska, which featured alleged spoiler recruitment efforts and unprecedented court battles.
It took two full weeks of counting to call a winner in the 3 November poll due to the logistical challenge of collecting ballots from across the state's remote expanse.
The Democratic win is the ninth Senate seat the party took from Republicans in this year's midterms, giving the party control of the US Congress for the last two years of Donald Trump's presidency.
A historic set of victories nationally also saw the party win back the House of Representatives.
In her victory statement, Peltola said the "status quo in DC is not working for Alaska".
She recalled speaking to "elders in tears about empty fish racks" and to parents in rural Alaska choosing between heating their homes and putting food on the table.
Peltola's campaign had come under fire after the New York Times reported Democrats attempted to recruit a candidate named Daniel J Sullivan, a complete political unknown residing in a small coastal town who shared a name with the state's current US Senator and incumbent, Dan S Sullivan, in an alleged attempt to confuse voters.
After legal challenges, state courts ruled the second Daniel J Sullivan could remain on the ballot, and election officials distinguished them on election materials.
Peltola denied having knowledge of the alleged plot but her campaign swiftly came under fire, and both the justice department and Alaska's Republican governor said they would launch investigations.
The Trump administration then filed a lawsuit attempting to stop the certification of the election results if the incumbent Republican Senator Sullivan lost.
A federal judge, though, dismissed the case, following legal challenges from Alaska election officials as well as the Peltola campaign and voting groups.
Due to Alaska's ranked-choice voting system, voters confused about which Dan Sullivan was the incumbent could simply choose each man as their first and second choice.
If they did so, after the first round of voting was complete, tabulation would re-allocate any votes from the non-incumbent Dan Sullivan - who stood little to no chance of winning - to the US Senator.
Experts told the BBC that this process made it difficult for any potential spoiler candidate to have any impact on the results.
An analysis by BBC Verify found that even if every vote for the other Dan Sullivan had gone to the incumbent, Peltola would still have won.
The seat initially was one of several toss-up races that appeared as a must-win for Democrats if they wanted to gain control of the Senate - but by the time Peltola was called the winner, they had seats to spare.
With 99% of ballots counted, Peltola had slightly more than 50%, while Sullivan trailed on less than 46%.
Sullivan, a former Alaska attorney general and an ally of Trump, campaigned on his record of delivering energy and military investment for the state.
Peltola promised Alaskans a more independent voice in Washington.
"As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my life to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans' support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first," she said in her victory statement.
"I will stand up to anyone to take on the rigged system in DC and deliver results for Alaska's working families."
