Tokyo Electric Power Company Holdings said Thursday it would review its plan to complete by August 2029 an anti-terrorism facility for the No. 7 reactor at its Kashiwazaki-Kariwa nuclear power plant in Niigata Prefecture.
The contracting company has informed Tepco that construction needs to be extended by more than a year because of factors including labor shortages, the power supplier said.
The development is expected to further delay the restart of the reactor after Tepco previously pushed back the facility’s completion from October 2025. No revised completion target has been set.
An anti-terrorism facility has also yet to be completed for the plant’s No. 6 reactor, which was restarted in January this year. The reactor will be unable to continue operating unless the facility is finished by April 2031.
Takeyuki Inagaki, manager of the plant, told a regular news conference on Thursday that Tepco aims to complete the facility for the No. 7 reactor by April 2031.
Japan’s new nuclear plant safety standards — introduced after the March 2011 triple meltdown at Tepco’s tsunami-hit Fukushima No. 1 nuclear power station in Fukushima Prefecture — require the installation of antiterrorism facilities within five years of the approval of reactor construction plans.
The initial deadline for the Kashiwazaki-Kariwa No. 7 reactor had been October 2025.
But Tepco pushed back the completion of the antiterrorism facility to August 2029 as regulatory reviews for the reactor’s restart took longer than expected. As a result, the company opted to bring the No. 6 reactor back online ahead of the No. 7 reactor.
