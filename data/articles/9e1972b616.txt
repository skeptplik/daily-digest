India has escalated its targeting of religious minorities during a voter list revision that struck off tens of millions of names nationwide, a U.S. panel on religious freedom said on Thursday (November 19, 2026).
“The Indian gov’t is targeting Muslim citizens by removing them from voter rolls, calling them ‘foreigners,’ and now illegally deporting them,” Asif Mahmood, the chair of the U.S. Commission on International Religious Freedom, said in a statement.
India’s voter list revision removed 130 million names, roughly the population of Mexico, in the largest deletion of voters in the country’s history.
Data shows the omissions disproportionately affect Muslims, India’s largest minority, who have faced what rights groups describe as exclusionary policies and rising discrimination since Prime Minister Narendra Modi took power in 2014.
The Election Commission of India says it was removing duplicate, deceased, shifted and otherwise ineligible voters and that anyone excluded in error can pursue appeals.
The revision has become entangled with a separate drive to identify and expel suspected illegal immigrants, even though removal from the rolls does not itself establish that someone is a foreigner.
“The Indian government’s expulsion of its own citizens based on their Muslim faith is a shocking escalation of tactics that target religious minorities,” said Gene Mills, a commissioner on the U.S. panel.
Mr. Mills urged the State Department to designate India as a “country of particular concern.”
USCIRF is a bipartisan body of the U.S. government that monitors religious freedom around the world and makes policy recommendations, which are not binding. Washington has not acted on its recent recommendations on India.
The Ministry of External Affairs has cast USCIRF as biased. New Delhi has repeatedly dismissed such criticism and called it interference in the country’s internal affairs. The Indian Embassy in Washington did not respond to a request for comment.
The voter roll revisions sparked protests in recent weeks, with opposition groups and student demonstrators saying the revisions affected those unlikely to vote for the BJP. Rights experts condemned India’s crackdown on the protests.
U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said last month the issue was a “domestic political matter” and Washington would not comment on it.
UN experts and human rights groups say abuse of minorities has risen under Mr. Modi.
They point to rising hate speech, a religion-based citizenship law that the UN calls “fundamentally discriminatory,” the demolition of Muslim-owned properties, and anti-conversion legislation that challenges freedom of belief.
Civil rights advocates have also been critical of the treatment of Muslims in the U.S. while noting a rise in Islamophobia in recent years and documenting escalating anti-Muslim rhetoric from Republicans during election campaigns.
