A magnitude 6.9 earthquake shook the Andes in central Peru on Friday (November 20, 2026) with no immediate reports of damage or casualties.
The U.S. Geological Survey said the earthquake struck at 7:09 pm local time (0900 GMT Saturday). Its epicentre was in the Andes, 34 kilometres (21 miles) north of Junin, at a depth of 108 kilometres (67 miles).
Peru's Geophysical Institute said no damage or casualties had been reported. The earthquake was felt in the Peruvian capital, prompting residents to rush into the streets.
Peru lies along the "Ring of Fire", a 40,000-kilometre (25,000-mile) arc of volcanoes and fault lines around the Pacific Basin, where most of the world's earthquakes occur.
In August, a 6.7 magnitude earthquake in the Peruvian Andes left two people injured and damaged several homes, health centres and schools, while in July, a 5.5-magnitude quake in the Junín region resulted in six deaths and 30 injuries.
