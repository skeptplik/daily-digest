Suspected ransomware attack disrupts cancer treatments, surgeries at Guadeloupe hospital
France’s national cybersecurity agency is investigating a suspected ransomware attack that impaired the computer systems of the University Hospital of Guadeloupe, forcing doctors to postpone surgeries, suspend some cancer treatments and revert to pen and paper.
France’s national cybersecurity agency, known as ANSSI, said Tuesday the incident appeared to involve ransomware and that investigators were examining whether hackers gained access to the hospital’s systems through an unnamed IT supplier that was reportedly compromised several months ago.
French prosecutors have launched a criminal investigation into the incident. It remains unclear who was behind the attack. The hospital’s management told local media on Monday that it had not received a ransom demand and ruled out paying a ransom.
The hospital — the largest on the French Caribbean island of approximately 380,000 residents — said on its website it had canceled some surgical procedures, medical consultations and imaging tests through at least Friday.
Radiation therapy has been temporarily suspended over safety concerns, while chemotherapy sessions scheduled for Monday were canceled. The hospital is reviewing upcoming appointments based on each patient’s medical needs.
Emergency services, dialysis treatments and urgent cardiac procedures remain available. The hospital has asked residents to visit its emergency departments only if they need immediate medical care.
Hospital employees first experienced computer and telephone outages on Saturday evening, according to local media reports. What initially appeared to be a technical malfunction turned out to be a cyberattack that encrypted the hospital’s databases.
“We’re back to pen and paper, relying on couriers who travel from one department to another,” the hospital’s director told local media.
As of Tuesday, healthcare workers were still unable to access patients’ electronic medical records and were manually processing prescriptions and collecting laboratory results, slowing patient care.
The hospital has encouraged patients to bring paper copies of their medical records, prescriptions and previous test results to appointments.
It remains unclear whether the hackers stole any personal or medical information. The hospital said it would notify anyone whose data was compromised and warned patients to watch out for phishing attempts by criminals posing as hospital staff.
The hospital plans to gradually restore the affected systems after checking them for malware. Recovery is expected to take several days, but it remains unclear when normal operations will fully resume.
The attack came as the hospital was relocating its operations to a new facility in Belle-Plaine. The French government financed the construction of the new hospital after a major fire damaged its previous facilities in 2017.
According to hospital management, the new facility’s computer systems have stronger security protections, making the disruption easier to manage there. Despite the attack, the hospital has maintained its plans to transfer pediatric emergency services to the new site.
Healthcare under attack
Two other French healthcare organizations — Bordeaux University Hospital and France’s National Cancer Institute — disclosed cyberattacks earlier this week.
Bordeaux University Hospital said it discovered its computer systems had been compromised after detecting that two of its email accounts had been used to send mass emails to employees.
A threat actor known as Scarlettgroup claimed responsibility for the breach, threatening to publish allegedly stolen medical records, prescriptions, laboratory results and financial documents.
The hospital has not confirmed the hackers’ claims and is investigating whether any information was stolen. It temporarily restricted external access to one of its servers as a precaution but said the incident had not disrupted patient care.
France’s National Cancer Institute confirmed Monday that a cyberattack had compromised personal information and disrupted two of its websites. The institute said it had stopped the intrusion but has not disclosed how many people were affected or what information was exposed.
It remains unclear whether the three incidents are linked.
French Health Minister Stephanie Rist said Tuesday the government was working to improve cybersecurity across the country’s healthcare sector and planned to secure long-term funding for these efforts through France’s proposed 2027 social security budget.
“The cybersecurity of our hospitals is also the safety of our patients,” Rist added.
