Nepal PM Balendra Shah set to arrive in Delhi
Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah is set to arrive in New Delhi on Sunday for a two-day visit, his first bilateral trip since taking office in March.
The 36-year-old former rapper will meet Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday. He will also meet Foreign Minister S Jaishankar and briefly visit President Droupadi Murmu.
Reconstruction after August's devastating floods is expected to dominate the talks. The disaster left some 1,500 dead and more than 5,200 missing, with roads, bridges, power plants, farms and homes destroyed.
Nepal estimates rebuilding will cost $4.78 billion.
Nepal is also pressing for an extra route for commercial flights to Bhairahawa and Pokhara.
"India is a major and important partner for Nepal," Foreign Secretary Amrit Bahadur Rai told reporters, saying the leaders would discuss trade, transit, investment, energy, connectivity and infrastructure.
New Delhi said the visit "reflects the importance India attaches to its relations with Nepal," AFP reported. Local media reported that talks may also touch upon unresolved border disputes.
India is Nepal's largest trading partner, accounting for more than 60% of its imports, worth $8.6 billion, versus China's 13%, according to World Bank figures. India and China regularly compete for influence in Nepal.
Shah will be accompanied by Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle and Foreign Minister Shishir Khanal.

Youth Congress protests at Meta offices over restricted Instagram accounts
Workers of the Indian Youth Congress (IYC), the youth wing of the opposition Indian National Congress, protested at US tech giant Meta's offices in Hyderabad, Mumbai and Gurugram on Friday, demanding that the group's leaders' Instagram accounts be restored.
In Hyderabad, protesters entered the building, smashed plants and clashed with police, according to footage the IYC released. Meta declined to comment.
The IYC said Instagram withheld the accounts of its president and in-charge on November 19 "on BJP orders," referring to the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party, and restored both shortly after the protests.
Manu Jain, who runs the group's social media, told Reuters thousands of accounts of journalists, creators and influencers critical of the government remain restricted.
BJP spokesperson Pradeep Bhandari said: "Congress is a party of goons! Goons of Indian Youth Congress are not only vandalized and destroyed Meta's office in Hyderabad, & also are flaunting it on social media!"
Meanwhile, Samajwadi Party chief Akhilesh Yadav warned foreign companies against getting involved in Indian politics.
The Indian government says it orders takedowns only for content that breaches local laws or threatens public safety or national security. In February, it cut the deadline for removing unlawful content from 36 hours to three.
Meta restricted more than 5.4 million posts or accounts in India in the first half of 2026, up from about 70,000 in the previous six months, saying most were cases where local law required it to restrict content proactively.
Earlier this month, Brazil’s top electoral court fined Meta's Facebook $1.3 million for failing to comply with judicial orders during the country's presidential election runoff campaign. President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva's team accused the company of political bias.
Rahul, Gill puts India 118 ahead of New Zealand on day three in Wellington
India closed day three of the first Test against New Zealand on 486-4, a lead of 118 runs. KL Rahul made 136, Shubman Gill 98 and Rishabh Pant was unbeaten on 55 as every one of India's top five passed 50.
Rahul reached his 13th Test hundred off 207 balls, batting for more than eight hours and sharing stands of 165 with Devdutt Padikkal (80) and 91 with Gill. It completed a set of Test centuries in every major Test-playing nation outside the subcontinent for the Indian wicketkeeper-batsman.
"It feels really awesome, actually, to be able to achieve something like that," Rahul said.
He survived an LBW appeal on 5 that was given not out and New Zealand chose not to review. "Those 15 seconds were probably the slowest 15 seconds of my life," he said of the review process.
Gill and Pant added 100 in the final session before Gill was bowled by Jacob Duffy just before stumps. Duffy finished with 1-180 from 29 overs, the fourth most expensive figures in New Zealand's Test history, AFP said.
The pitch, green on day one, flattened out. "We expected a lot of grass on the wicket, we expected a lot of movement and it has probably not panned out that way," Padikkal said.
New Zealand were all out for 368 in their first innings.
The two-match series ends next week in Christchurch.
Death toll from suspected spurious liquor in Manipur's Chandel rises
At least 14 people have died and more than 200 have been hospitalized after drinking suspected adulterated liquor in Manipur's Chandel district.
At least 12 people remain in critical condition, local media reported.
The victims drank the liquor in the Chakpikarong area, about 75 kilometers (47 miles) from the state capital city of Imphal, and deaths were reported between November 18 and 20.
Chief Minister Yumnam Khemchand Singh visited the hospitals where most patients were being treated on Friday. He ordered an inquiry and said the culprits would be arrested soon.
Police said the liquor came from Sugnu in neighboring Kakching district, with no known local brewing in Chandel. A case has been filed, and two people have been arrested.
The local administration has advised residents not to drink liquor from questionable sources and asked anyone who consumed liquor from the same source to get a medical check-up, even without symptoms.
US judge jails Indian national in foiled plot to kill Sikh separatist, New Delhi denies involvement
A US federal judge sentenced an Indian national on Friday to 19 years and seven months in prison for plotting to hire a hitman to kill Sikh separatist leader Gurpatwant Singh Pannun in New York.
Nikhil Gupta, 55, pleaded guilty in February to murder-for-hire, conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire and conspiracy to commit money laundering.
"I'm sorry to Mr. Pannun," he said in court. Judge Victor Marrero said he faces deportation to India after serving his sentence.
Prosecutors say an Indian government employee recruited Gupta in 2023. The government employee remains overseas and is not in US custody.
Gupta paid $15,000 (€13,670) to a person he believed was a hitman, who was actually an undercover US agent.
Prosecutors also said Gupta had told that agent the day after Hardeep Singh Nijjar's June 2023 killing in Canada that Nijjar "was also the target," the Associated Press reported.
His lawyer, Isabelle Kirshner, denied he had any role in the Nijjar killing.
"There is no question he was not the brains behind this operation," she said. Canada has separately alleged India arranged Nijjar's killing, and India dismissed that, with both countries expelling diplomats.
India denies any involvement in the Pannun plot. New Delhi has designated Pannun a terrorist.
Pannun, who leads the group Sikhs for Justice, called Gupta a foot soldier and said outside court: "I'm ready to face India's bullet if that is the price I have to pay."
