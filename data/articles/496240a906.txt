Macy's has postponed its 100th parade in Manhattan as more than 600,000 homes and businesses lost power.
Storms brought flooding and strong winds to parts of the United States on Thanksgiving, complicating holiday travel.
Streets were inundated in New Jersey's Sea Isle City and Ocean City, while water spread across parts of Avalon. Borough of Avalon business administrator Scott J. Wahl said the town was seeing some of the worst flooding in its history. A few drivers had to be rescued after driving around barricades and into floodwaters, but no injuries were reported, Wahl said.
At Philadelphia International Airport, aircraft were seen stranded on the tarmac amid the bad weather. The National Weather Service warned of disruption at eastern US airports through Friday morning, citing poor visibility, turbulence, strong winds and ice forming on aircraft.
Wind gusts of 80 km/h or higher were expected along the Interstate 95 corridor. All of New Jersey was under a state of emergency declared by Governor Mikie Sherrill, and parts of New York were under one declared by Governor Kathy Hochul. More than 600,000 homes and businesses were without electricity on Thursday afternoon, mostly in East Coast states, according to the outage tracking website poweroutage.us.
Macy's postponed its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade in Manhattan until Saturday. It said it acted in an abundance of caution after consulting city officials, amid worries about how the parade's giant balloons would fare in the wind.
Rain and snow also affected the St. Louis area in Missouri, where up to 33 cm of snow was expected. In Wentzville, Missouri, heavy snow partly collapsed the roof of the Wentzville Ice Arena.
On the West Coast, coastal flood warnings were issued from Southern California to the Oregon and Washington coasts because of king tides. These occur when the gravitational pulls of the moon and sun align to raise water levels. A wave tore down part of a pier in Pacifica, California, on Wednesday.
Although both coasts of the US were affected, travel is usually busier in the days leading up to Thanksgiving than on the holiday itself.
