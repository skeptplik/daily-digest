Rockstar Games has opened up about GTA 6 protagonist Jason's viral personality and the two specific inspirations that helped bring him to life.
As 13 years of waiting for the next Grand Theft Auto ended last week, players have found themselves enamored with Jason Duval. Stoic, nonchalant, calm, composed, resigned; whatever you want to call one of GTA 6's new protagonists, Rockstar says it carefully crafted his already iconic demeanor by pulling from a Steve McQueen classic and real-life veterans.
The studio's senior vice president of narrative, Rupert Humphries, talked about how the studio behind the mega-hit video game built Jason from the ground up during a conversation with Destructoid. Centering the story on a "couple on the run" marked new territory for Rockstar, but it's far from a new concept in entertainment. So, the team turned to movies, particularly Sam Peckinpah's The Getaway.
"Steve McQueen was famous for pulling lines out of scripts and saying things with his presence alone," Humphries explained. "We wanted to see how close we could get to that with a game character, where so many of our protagonists have been the opposite."
He continued, saying that Rockstar's research into real-world veterans and Army Rangers helped refine its approach. Those who have played the game (and keen-eyed trailer watchers) already know about Jason's military background, so the influence here definitely tracks.
"These guys typically aren’t running their mouths or reacting to something unless it’s strictly necessary, while still being strong and still asserting themselves."
Jason is the talk of Vice City and has been for some time. As Rockstar shared more details about its seemingly shy lead ahead of GTA 6's launch, some fans began to wonder if he could be the franchise's first-ever neurodivergent protagonist. In addition to his personality, Humphries described Jason as "wired differently" during a recent interview with Love magazine.
While Jason steals more than just wads of cash in Vice City, his partner in crime has proven to be just as impactful. Game writer Michael Wiafe said looking into the kind of criminal activity known to make headlines in Florida was one of the first things the team did when creating both Jason and Lucia. For Rockstar, it wasn't just about creating true-to-life criminals; it was about creating fully realized human beings.
"From then on, it was about coming up with authentic characters that didn’t stray too far from reality but also were not totally bound by it either," Wiafe said. "The majority of our main characters are undeniably reprehensible on paper, full-blown criminals, but also decent human beings. Their moral makeup isn’t necessarily cut-and-dry, and we don’t attempt to make excuses for them. What’s important is how they resonate, no matter where you’re experiencing the game in the world."
Between the chaos of robbing gas stations and drug dealers and getting into high-speed car chases, Jason and Lucia can become quite the power couple. Game writer Elizabeth Gauvey-Kern said to Destructoid, "You really want them to make it." The quiet moments where the duo scrolls through social media in their bed or drives the other one to work can be just as important as the action set pieces. Gauvey-Kern added, "Those need to hit too, and you need to connect with how the characters are feeling in those moments."
