When kids look up at the night sky and marvel at the moon and the stars, the mysteries of space can spark a sense of wonder and curiosity. One of France's most beloved astronauts, Thomas Pesquet, joins Eve Irvine to take us one step beyond with his new book, "Space in 100 Questions", offering space enthusiasts of all ages an otherworldly glimpse into the Final Frontier!
He shares with us his stellar adventures and fire and ice experiences during his two missions aboard the International Space Station (ISS): What does weightlessness feel like and how does it help advance medical research? How do astronauts prepare physically, psychologically, socially and academically for a life exploring the heavens and the Earth, paving the way for new scientific discoveries?
"Faster than the fastest Formula One car on Earth: We gain 100 kilometers an hour more every 1.8 seconds for 8 minutes 48. Once we get to that speed of 28,000 kilometers, which is enough to free fall around the Earth, we stop the engines and we start floating."
Once in orbit, the sensation is altogether different: “Once you get to space, you remove that parameter, you can adjust that parameter, you discover things that are hidden on the ground.”
