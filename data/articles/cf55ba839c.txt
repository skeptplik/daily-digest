There are few breakfasts that feel as healthy and refreshing as the ever-mutable smoothie. You can make them virtuous, with lots of greens and nut milk, or luscious, with full-fat dairy and cocoa powder – but in order to make a good one, you need a really good blender.
And while smoothies are the classic use for a blender, these appliances can do so much more; a truly great blender is a kitchen workhorse that can make your morning smoothie, your afternoon dip, your evening soup and even a frozen drink if you’re feeling frisky.
While blender technology has definitely improved in the last few decades – we now have high-speed contraptions that can make peanut butter in less than a minute – they’re all basically attempting to turn large pieces of food into a smoother result. If you’re looking for a new one, we’ve tested the best in the business.

Best overall blender:
Breville Fresh & Furious
This Breville is one of only three blenders that could crush ice without the assistance of a tamper or a splash of water. If you’re looking to make frozen margaritas on the cheap, this one is for you.
The Breville website claims that the blades offer a mix of blending and food processing functionality; I tried chopping some carrots, and it was more of a shred. Still, it’s a useful option. It also whipped up a bright-green pesto quickly. While it wasn’t as silky smooth as the Vitamix pesto, it got pretty close. The smoothie, too, had a bit more texture than the Vitamix smoothie, but at nearly a third of the price, it feels like an acceptable tradeoff.
I also liked the lid design: it fits very tightly and securely into the jug and features a loop that helps you pull it off easily.
It’s a shame that … Although it comes in seven different colors, from “red velvet cake” to “olive tapenade”, this isn’t the sleekest-looking blender of the bunch. And if you’re obsessive about texture, you may find that your smoothies and soups aren’t perfectly smooth.
- Jar size: 50 ounces
- Power: 1,100W
- Speeds: five, plus features such as crushed ice and pulse
The very best blender (luxury):
Vitamix 5200
Across all my rounds of testing, the two Vitamix blenders I reviewed consistently produced the smoothest product, though this one had the edge. (Read our full Vitamix 5200 review.)
Whether I was sipping a smoothie, spooning a soup or (let’s be real) dipping a finger into a batch of pesto, this blender produced luscious, vibrant results, even keeping green and purple smoothies from turning muddy in color. It also worked quickly, turning a chunky soup into a puree in less than a minute, while other competitors took 90 seconds or more to create a mulchier result.
After a few rounds of testing, the touchscreen of the more expensive Ascent ($750 compared to the 5200’s $470) stopped working, and the blender was unusable. Even if the Ascent had kept working throughout my testing, the results were basically the same as those of the 5200.
I also appreciated that the 5200 offered an array of speeds, without an excess of settings. There’s an on/off switch and a switch that lets you choose high speed or variable speed; if you choose variable, a dial at the center of the control panel lets you go from zero to 10 as you like. The device will also “pulse” in case you want to make something chunkier, such as salsa. And it comes with a tamper, to help break down anything super thick like peanut butter. Finally, it has a seven-year warranty.
It’s a shame that … It’s definitely loud. At just over 20in tall, it’s also too large to fit under some people’s cupboards.
- Jar size: 64 ounces
- Power: 1,380W / 120V
- Speeds: 10
Best glass blender (also best for ice crushing):
KitchenAid Pure Power Blender with Glass Jar
There is a growing market for blenders with plastic-free jugs, and we’re here for it. Unless you want to shell out for the Vitamix Stainless Steel container ($250), this is the best blender you can buy that’s made with glass.
Of all the glass-jug blenders I tested, the KitchenAid offered the smoothest results. Its smoothies and soups didn’t have the silkiness you can achieve with a Vitamix or Blendtec, but they were much smoother than those of every other glass-jar blender that I tried. I even threw full handfuls of curly kale in there, stem and all, and my smoothie still came out rich, creamy and mulch-free.
Like the Vitamix 5200, this model has simple dial control. When I’m under-caffeinated in the morning and just trying to make myself some quick breakfast, I get impatient with blenders that have too many buttons. Unless I’m crushing ice, or trying to pulse a pesto without making it too smooth, I’m content just slowly turning it up to speed 10 and waiting 30 seconds. (The KitchenAid Pure Power made quick work of my smoothie and soup, too.)
Finally, its ice crush setting might just be the best I have tested, and it comes in a pleasing array of colors – from understated matte black to cherry red.
It’s a shame that … Since this jug has a wider, rectangular base, you won’t be able to make a single serving of pesto the way you can with a narrower base, but it can handle thicker mixtures easily.
- Jar size: 62oz
- Power: 1200W
- Speeds: 10
Best glass blender under $100:
Hamilton Beach Smoothie Start Glass Blender
A sub-$100 blender isn’t going to give you the smoothest, silkiest result compared with some of the most expensive picks on this list. But it can absolutely be a reliable part of your kitchen routine, even for a smoothie-head.
The Hamilton Beach Smoothie Start is designed for drinks and it can easily turn some fruit, yogurt and ice into a drinkable breakfast. If you’re trying to add a bunch of fibrous stuff, such as kale, seeds or oats, you’ll have to accept a slightly textured drink, but I know from experience that some people like drinks with a bit more texture. Its ability to crush ice also gives it a day-to-night vibe – you can use it for virtuous smoothies in the morning and frozen margaritas in the evening.
I liked the simplicity of the buttons: the main one is “smoothie”, with options for “icy drink” and “ice crush.” (Don’t let the hyper-specific marketing fool you – you can also blend a soup, though it’ll stay a bit chunky.) The pour-spout on the lid also made pouring my morning smoothie easier than some blenders, especially when I was bleary-eyed.
It’s a shame that … this blender isn’t a good choice for whipping up perfect peanut butter or pesto any time soon, but it will help you get your daily allotment of morning fruit without much fuss.
- Jar size: 40oz
- Power: 850W
- Speeds: three
Best compact personal blender:
The Beast Mega 1200 Stainless Steel
Last year, I called the Beast Mega 1200 Plus the best personal blender. This year, the company has upgraded its offerings with a stainless steel option, and I’m loving it. Like other Beast blenders, the Mega Stainless Steel comes with everything a blender owner (and smoothie drinker) might need: a straw, various jar sizes, drinking spouts and caps, a straw cleaner and even a brush for cleaning the blender blades.
Of the personal blenders I’ve tested, the Beast models consistently produce the best results, blending everything evenly from pesto to soup. While the 1200 couldn’t blend nut butter or crush ice, I’m not sure that there’s a personal blender on the market that can, and it was definitely the best for thick smoothies. I also liked the color options – I tested a dark blue model. It offers two speeds: “pulse” and “blend”.
Beast blenders come in three sizes, with three different motors between them. I tried the mid-sized “Mighty” this time, and found that I could tell the difference between its 850 watt motor and the Mega’s 1,200 watts. (I tend to put a lot of chia seeds in my smoothies, and this was the tell.) So I’m recommending the Mega Stainless Steel here. One bonus I enjoyed is that the stainless steel jug is lighter than the plastic one. I’ve actually been using it as a smoothie jar.
It’s a shame that ... The stainless steel models still come with two Tritan plastic jars – a small one that’s great for pesto, and a mid-size one that’s quite flexible. They are nice jars (though very heavy plastic), but why include plastic jars with a stainless steel model? Hopefully Beast will phase out the plastic and phase in more steel in the future.
- Jar size: 23.5oz
- Power: 1200W
- Speeds: 1 (with a pulse option)
Alternative pick: The Beast 850 Mighty ($159) is an excellent choice, too. It’s just a little less powerful. And the Beast Mega 1200 Plus ($219) is essentially the same, but has a plastic jar.
Best blender for quick smoothies:
Nutribullet Pro 900
If you want a personal blender that will make good smoothies and shakes you can take to the gym – or you balked at the Beast’s $219 price tag – the Nutribullet is your friend.
At about $100, the Nutribullet blended smoothies that were only slightly more textured than those of its more expensive counterpart, and it handily blended soup and pesto without any problem. It also comes with a second jar, a lid for drinking and a clunky-looking but totally effective detachable handle. This jar was basically designed to be thrown in a gym bag.
It’s a shame that … Its smoothies weren’t the smoothest, but they still did the trick. Its jars are also made of plastic.
- Jar size: 32 ounces
- Power: 900W
- Speeds: one
The most plastic-free blender:
Oster Classic Series Heritage Blender with Glass Jar
The Oster felt decidedly nostalgic. I’m pretty sure that my parents had the same one in the 1990s, with its flip-on switch and tapered glass jug. It’s also the only blender that arrived with zero plastic packaging: all cardboard, no useless baggies.
If you’re looking for a blender that will minimize potential exposure to microplastics, this is a great option, thanks to its glass jug. The lid is still plastic, but has less contact with your food. It also offers a 10-year warranty – even longer than the Vitamix’s.
Its a shame that … its blending capabilities left something to be desired: it produced a decidedly mulchy green smoothie, and it never got soup as smooth as its competitors.
Because the base is so narrow, it’s also difficult to clean by hand, as the area beneath the blades is a challenge to reach.
- Jar size: 48 ounces
- Power: 1,000W power/600W blending
- Speeds: two

Blender features you should look for
Choosing your blender will have a lot to do with how you want to use it. Do you just want to make shakes for the gym, or are you an obsessive home cook who has always wanted to make hummus?
When it comes to jug blenders, the motor’s power is the factor that most contributes to the blender’s ability to do its job, but jug shape also comes into play. A more narrow, round base will blend small quantities better than a wider, square base.
If you’re just looking for a smoothie-making machine, or you have very limited counter space or budget, a compact personal blender (single-serve) is likely your best bet, thanks to its small footprint and because the jar turns into a to-go vessel for your drink.
Jug blenders are better for larger jobs, or thicker mixtures, such as thick smoothies, nut butters, spreads and soups. A higher-power blender is going to offer a smoother consistency and faster results.
How to clean a blender
Most high-speed blenders can clean themselves with a little help. Rinse out your blender as best you can, add a squeeze of dish soap and fill halfway with water. Then blend on high for a minute. Unless you were blending something particularly sticky, this should clean out your blender, and all you have to do is rinse it. You may have to wash the lid by hand.
Personal blenders are a little different. Cleaning the jars is the same as cleaning a water glass. Be careful when cleaning the blades. I like to soak them in hot soapy water for about 20 minutes, which will loosen any bits of soup that have lodged in crevices.
