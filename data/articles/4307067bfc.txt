Elon Musk looks to break up with MAGA. To what end?
'I'm not MAGA', Musk said this week, 2 years after declaring himself 'dark MAGA'
From his first time donning a black MAGA hat and declaring himself "dark MAGA" at an October 2024 rally for Donald Trump to his multimillon-dollar splurge on Republican candidates ahead of this month's midterms, Elon Musk has certainly seemed to embrace the Make America Great Again movement.
Why does that matter? Because the Tesla and Space X CEO now appears to be distancing himself from his MAGA bonafides, saying that he never really considered himself a MAGA adherent.
"I'm not MAGA," Musk declared earlier this week, in an interview with The New York Times.
"I'm not sure what MAGA means," he said during the newspaper's DealBook Summit. "If that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don't have that. I think there are positives and negatives in both parties."
That may come as a surprise considering Musk made at least five appearances between 2024 and 2025 in which he associated himself with the movement, according to Mediaite, a news website.
But Musk's MAGA split appears to come at a time when association to the brand could prove to be a business and legal liability for the world's richest man.
"It's so transparently convenient," said Matthew Dallek, a professor of political management and historian at the D.C.-based George Washington University, who has researched the MAGA movement.
Musk's comments follow the Republicans' disastrous showing in the U.S. midterms and the so-called Democratic blue wave that saw the party take control of the House and the Senate.
Along with MAGA-boosting appearances, Musk also spent more than $400 million U.S. on Trump-aligned Republican candidates in the midterms — the most money of any individual campaign donor.
Can Musk disassociate from MAGA?
"Now that [MAGA]'s on the losing side, all of a sudden, he's not MAGA," said Todd Belt, a professor of political management at George Washington University.
"I mean, come on. This is just laughable. This is the guy who glossed himself 'dark MAGA' and had black MAGA stuff put together ... when Trump took over. He was completely in with it."
Prof. Matthew Dallek says Elon Musk, seen speaking before Donald Trump at a campaign rally on Oct. 27, 2024, made his 'MAGA bed' and now must lie in it. (Evan Vuccu/The Associated Press)
Dallek said Musk has spent years making his "MAGA bed."
"Now he's got to lie in it, even when it's politically uncomfortable."
Musk, who also urged his followers on X to vote Republican, now says he feels "pretty foolish" for spending so much money on the midterms. He also said in his NYT interview that ideologically he is "quite moderate" and doesn't have any "extreme political views."
But Dallek said if he's really such a moderate and not part of the MAGA ranks, there were non-MAGA Republicans he could have backed during the elections.
Instead, Musk spent around $200 million US backing Texas Senate nominee Ken Paxton, who Dallek called, "arguably the most MAGA of all the midterm candidates."

Musk has based himself and several of his companies in Texas, including Tesla, SpaceX and a planned chipmaking venture between the two. But the state is almost all blue following the U.S. midterms.
"The government is no longer in the hands of his allies," Dallek said. "He is in a very vulnerable position. Businesses are very vulnerable."
Dallek also questioned just how "moderate" Musk is, as he's has turned X into a platform for extremist views. As well, a February 2025 NBC News report said Musk had been encouraging right-wing political movements, policies and administrations in at least 18 countries as part of a global push to slash immigration and curtail regulation of business.
"It's just transparently false when he says, 'I'm a moderate,'" Dallek said.
What's driving Musk's MAGA retreat?
One reason: The MAGA brand is at its lowest net favourability ever, said CNN’s chief data analyst Harry Enten.
"It is a brand that is failing amongst the American public," he said.
But perhaps more importantly, Musk may be trying to engender better relations with Democrats as he faces government probes and red tape.
Democrats have indicated they may want to investigate the months Musk spent in 2025 spearheading the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE).
DOGE team activities led to widespread dismantling of foreign aid programs, as well as allegations that team members breached privacy laws by illegally accessing the sensitive data of Americans.
Musk may also face slower approvals for the billions of dollars Space X plans to invest in data centre campuses in Texas and Arizona.
"They're going to be turning their eyes toward him and his space facilities, and the environmental issues that they're causing and tax issues," Belt said. "Things that could really make life difficult for him. He doesn't want to poke the bear."
