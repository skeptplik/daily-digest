LONDON — Stephanie Phair, former British Fashion Council chair and now an angel investor, is backing Parfumerie, an AI-powered fragrance discovery and shopping platform launching Thursday that aims to make online retail more personal by organizing recommendations around individual taste.
A veteran of luxury e-commerce, Phair said she joined a group of investors to back the platform raising two million pounds in pre-seed round because she sees Parfumerie as a potential model for the future of online shopping.
“The existing sites still look the same as they did 20 years ago. They’re catalogues of products, but the world has changed. Customer behavior has changed. People are getting inspiration from social, from bricks-and-mortar, from creators, but the enablers to do it differently for taste didn’t exist,” she said in an interview.
“It’s not about another search. That is not what people are asking for. What they want is for inspiration and discovery to come to them based on their personal signals, not just what an editor or creators are telling them,” added Phair.
Founded by Shannon Edwards and Natalie Varma, Parfumerie is launching shopping in the U.K. through retailers including Selfridges, Sephora, Space NK, Liberty, Cult Beauty and Lookfantastic. Its initial shoppable assortment includes more than 300 fragrance brands.
U.S. shopping, additional retail partners and direct brand partnerships are planned for 2027.
Edwards has more than 17 years of experience across luxury fragrance and beauty. She has held various roles at Parfums Christian Dior, Tom Ford Beauty, Estée Lauder, L’Oréal and Charlotte Tilbury. She founded Comtent, Parfumerie’s London-based parent company. Varma brings technology know-how from DeepMind, Farfetch and Net-a-porter, including work on AI for fashion and product personalization.
The founders said Parfumerie’s role is to help people discover products they might love but would not know to search for.
“The challenge was a harder one: What is the right product that you’re going to love? That was really undersolved and underserved,” Edwards said.
Iris, Parfumerie’s AI-powered fragrance editor, lets users explore scents in their own words, by mood, occasion, note, or a fragrance they already enjoy. Iris can compare products, offer layering advice, and direct shoppers to retailers, with current pricing and stock information.
Powered by multiple “best-in-class” models, including Gemini and Claude, Varma said its technology is grounded in brand tone and factual product information, which the company claims helps represent fragrances accurately.
She described Parfumerie’s two-part approach as category knowledge paired with an understanding of each shopper.
“First, we have built a really structured understanding of the category. The second part is taste intelligence: understanding each user, their taste and preferences, and placing both our users and our fragrances within that same latent space. That allows us to personalize, orchestrate and narrate the experience for them,” she said.
A key distinction in Parfumerie’s retail positioning is that Iris’s recommendations will not be sponsored. The company said recommendations are driven by each person’s taste, and purchases are fulfilled through authorized retailers and brands.
Fragrance is the company’s first category in part because consumer behavior has changed. Edwards pointed to the influence of short-form video, fragrance communities, and younger shoppers’ interest in collecting and layering scents.
More consumers, she said, are building “fragrance wardrobes” rather than relying on a single signature scent. But fragrance is inherently sensory, making online discovery challenging even as the number of brands and products grows.
Phair said taste-based categories require a different approach from standard search.
“Taste-based categories are not a pull mechanism; they’re a push mechanism. You need to be able to nudge your customer along so they discover things they didn’t even know they wanted. In order to do that, you need deep industry experience,” she added.
Parfumerie is positioning itself as a discovery layer, not a retailer with its own stock.
It earns affiliate commissions when shoppers buy through retail partners. The company said its taste-led approach can help emerging houses be discovered alongside established names and encourage shoppers to explore beyond bestsellers.
“We don’t hold inventory. We’re really an intelligent discovery layer and a brand-building environment,” Edwards said.
