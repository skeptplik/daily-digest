US President Donald Trump has called on Nvidia's board to reinstate Jensen Huang as its CEO.
Trump's appeal adds to growing calls for Huang's return, including a petition with more than 200,000 signatures that Elon Musk has signed.
Huang, who co-founded the AI chip giant in 1993, is due to answer lawmakers' questions in Congress on 1 December.
"I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang. It would be the best thing for the company, and also, for the U.S.A.," Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform on Sunday.
Trump has stepped up his support over the past week for Huang's return.
Last Monday, he said he "would not mind" seeing Huang reinstated. On Friday, he told reporters at the White House that he "would love to see Jensen be given a second chance".
Huang resigned on 12 November after it emerged that he had two extramarital affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy.
Beijing has rejected the espionage allegations and US authorities said they have not reached a conclusion.
Musk and other tech leaders back a return
The "Stand with Jensen" petition, an open letter to Nvidia's board, had more than 206,000 signatures as of Monday morning, according to its website. More than 10,000 signatories claim to be current Nvidia employees.
The letter asked the board to "bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts". It also highlighted that Nvidia and Huang have not endorsed it.
"A petition is underway to reinstate Jensen and I'm signing it too," Musk wrote on X on 13 November.
Tobi Lütke, CEO of e-commerce platform Shopify, and Tim Sweeney, CEO of Fortnite developer Epic Games, have also signed it.
Huang said on 17 November that he would be "honoured to answer these calls and return as CEO" if the board judged that best for the company, its staff, its shareholders, and America.
US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Fox News on 15 November that Nvidia needed "a dramatic shake-up" and a CEO from outside the company.
However, in an interview with CBS on Sunday, he stated: "I said they should go to the outside, but he's on the outside now".
Thus far, Nvidia's board does not plan to bring him back. Chair Harvey Jones told analysts during the company's earnings call last Tuesday that the board had "unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO".
Colette Kress, Nvidia's finance chief, has taken over as interim CEO during the search for a permanent replacement. An outside law firm is currently undertaking an investigation.
Nvidia's shares closed around $125.5 on Friday, representing a 48% collapse since their intraday all-time high price of roughly $243.3 on 6 October.
They lost over 20% just last week, their seventh weekly decline in a row and the longest losing streak since 2008.
The slide continued despite Nvidia beating earnings forecasts last Tuesday.
Friday's 14% drop followed a Reuters report, citing two people briefed on the matter, that the Securities and Exchange Commission, the US markets regulator, was examining possible accounting issues at Nvidia.
Questions in Congress
Huang will answer questions from three House committees behind closed doors on 1 December: Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party.
The committees are looking into the possible diversion of advanced Nvidia chips to Chinese businesses.
The session will be a transcribed interview and will not be open to the public.
Huang's public testimony will come before the Senate Banking Committee, according to the office of its chair, Republican Senator Tim Scott. No date has been set, though Scott had originally asked for 3 December.
Two Nvidia shareholders, the investment firms Altimeter Capital and Atreides Management, have asked the board to keep Huang's return open and to hold off on a permanent CEO until all the facts are known.
