Major League Baseball is seeking federal court protection for an anticipated lockout next week, filing a lawsuit Wednesday aimed at preventing the MLB Players Association from using antitrust litigation to challenge a potential work stoppage.
“Today’s filing is a defensive measure simply intended to keep the parties at the bargaining table to work towards a new collective bargaining agreement and a full 2027 season,” MLB spokesperson Glen Caplin said.
The legal maneuver comes as negotiations for a new CBA remain deadlocked. Club owners are pushing for a salary cap, and the union is strongly opposed to the proposal.
The current five-year labor agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. Eastern Standard Time on Dec. 1. Owners are expected to impose a lockout if the sides fail to reach a deal by then.
Caplin said the league wants to “proactively disarm the Union’s nuclear option of disclaiming” and preserve the collective bargaining process.
In a 38-page complaint filed in U.S. District Court in Manhattan, MLB asked a judge to declare that a lockout would not violate federal antitrust laws and could not be halted through a court injunction.
The league believes the union could disclaim, or relinquish its status as the players’ collective bargaining representative, potentially allowing players to pursue antitrust claims against MLB and its 30 clubs.
Such a strategy, previously employed during labor disputes in other professional sports leagues, could expose MLB to damages worth three times the players’ proven financial losses.
The players association rejected that explanation, accusing MLB of attempting to gain leverage through the courts rather than negotiating.
“The lawsuit filed today by MLB is completely frivolous,” the MLBPA said in a statement. “We will respond appropriately, but MLB’s decision to run to court in the middle of negotiations rather than bargain in good faith is incredibly disappointing.”
The complaint names the union and all eight members of its executive subcommittee, including pitcher Tarik Skubal, who recently agreed to a nine-year, $450 million contract with the New York Mets.
MLB has proposed implementing a salary cap of $258.2 million and a payroll floor of $180.2 million beginning in 2028, while the union has maintained its opposition to a capped system.
MLB’s last lockout delayed the start of the 2022 season, but all 162 games remained on each team’s schedule. Baseball has not lost regular-season games to a labor dispute since the 1994-95 players’ strike.
