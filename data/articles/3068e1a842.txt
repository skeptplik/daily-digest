The EU has approved UniCredit’s takeover of Germany’s Commerzbank, a cross-border deal aimed at strengthening Europe’s banking sector.
The EU on Monday approved Italian bank UniCredit's takeover of Germany's Commerzbank as Europe seeks to reinforce the bloc's banking sector.
With the acquisition of Commerzbank, UniCredit plans to cement its status as a European heavyweight, a goal shared by the European Union.
The EU believes better integration of the bloc's banking sector will help it keep up with US lenders and boost its competitiveness.
"Europe needs stronger banks that can compete, grow and support our businesses and families," EU competition chief Teresa Ribera said in a statement.
"Cross-border mergers can help make this happen, allowing banks to operate more efficiently and better withstand economic shocks," she said.
The European Commission, the EU's powerful competition regulator, approved the acquisition after determining the deal would not raise competition concerns.
"Our approval of UniCredit's acquisition of Commerzbank shows that it is possible to support a stronger European banking sector while safeguarding competition and choice," Ribera said.
The EU this year announced plans to overhaul the rulebook on company mergers as Brussels seeks to forge what it calls "European champions".
The European Central Bank had already approved UniCredit's hostile takeover.
The bank has pursued Commerzbank for the past two years, raising alarm in Germany where the bank is a key financing source for small and midsize industrial companies.
Despite fierce resistance from Berlin and the German lender, UniCredit had built up a stake of nearly 50% in Commerzbank.
