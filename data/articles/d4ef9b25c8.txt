Thousands reported disruptions to Google’s Gmail and Apple’s iPhone apps, such as Apple Pay and iCloud, on Tuesday, according to Downdetector. Apple confirmed the outages in a statement but said it had resolved them, while Google said it had not seen internal signs of outages.
Apple’s own system status page as of 2.30pmET showed problems with services such as the App Store, iCloud Mail, iCloud Web Apps and AppleCare.
“Many of our systems are back up and running after an internal update caused some outages this morning. We’re working to get the remaining systems online as soon as possible,” an Apple spokesperson said.
A Google spokesperson said: “We’re not currently seeing evidence of a broad Gmail service disruption. Our teams monitor service health closely, and real-time status updates are always available on the Google Workspace Status Dashboard.”
The outage reports come on the heels of major disruptions in technological infrastructure, particularly in the US but also worldwide, that range from air traffic control systems to email accounts.
The outages began in earnest last week when Microsoft Outlook users across the globe could not access their email accounts or other critical Microsoft Office applications.
The day after that outage began, the US Department of Transportation grounded all flights in the US in response to a communication system malfunction.
On Monday, United Airlines experienced service outages, and thousands of Chase Bank customers also reported they had trouble using their accounts.
In a statement on Monday, JPMorganChase said it had “experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems” and said it wass “taking longer than usual to service some customers”.
The bank said there was “no evidence this is cyber-related”. As of Monday evening, a Chase spokesperson told NBC News that transactions were being processed normally again.
Before the news of the Apple and Gmail disruptions on Tuesday, reports of an outage involving the US National Security Agency (NSA) made headlines. CNN reported that a highly classified network the NSA uses for its internal communications, known as NSANet, was down.
A US official told CBS News that the NSA experienced a “temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance”. They said that “while some internal systems were affected, mission/critical capabilities remain fully operational and our ability to defend national security continues”.
White House spokesperson Lauren Bis told NewsMax on Tuesday of the outage of NSANet: “We see no evidence that this was a cyber-attack.” She added that “there is no crisis, or anything like that, for Americans to be worried about”.
Also on Tuesday, Charles Schwab said its website suffered a “brief webpage loading issue” but said that by noon ET it had been resolved.
Markwayne Mullin, the homeland security secretary, appeared on Fox News this week where he said that artificial intelligence, which Trump and members of his administration call “super-intelligence”, or “SI”, could be behind the recent outages.
“I talked to one guy in the tech industry, he asked not to mention his name, it’s not Elon, I asked, what could be causing these outages? He said I believe it’s the SI, he said because these programs aren’t designed to work as fast as SI is,” he said.
And when asked about the flurry of recent outages on Tuesday, Donald Trump said: “No, it’s life.”
“When you’re big and powerful and you have all the money, things like that happen,” the president added. “It’s fine.”
