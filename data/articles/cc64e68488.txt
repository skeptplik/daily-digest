Google announced on Monday that it’s rolling out new Maps features that are designed to help people plan trips and everyday tasks using AI. The company is launching the ability for Maps to create trip itineraries and find appointments for users.
The new features reflect a broader shift in how tech companies are positioning AI, which is less as a tool that just answers questions and more as an assistant that can complete tasks on users’ behalf. While many companies have released AI agents to complete these tasks, Google is working to bring that approach into a service that millions of people already use every day.
Google says users can use the navigation platform’s “Ask Maps” tool to build step-by-step trip itineraries and get real-time details about their route right when they pull up directions.
For example, you could use Ask Maps to plan a holiday shopping day trip or something more complex, like a three-day road trip featuring coastal hikes and oceanfront dining, Google says. You’ll get a day-by-day itinerary with insider tips and numbered map pins for every stop. Once you’re happy with your itinerary, you can share it with friends and family or export it to Google Docs on desktop.
When you’re ready to head out, you can use Ask Maps to reopen your itinerary or find the “itinerary” badge in your chat history. You’ll have access to real-time information, including traffic conditions, business hours, and a look at what the “vibe” is at your next stop, the company says. You could also adjust your schedule by asking Maps to do something like add a scenic viewpoint or lunch stop along your route. You can then tap any destination in your itinerary to get directions and navigate to your next stop.
Additionally, users can use Ask Maps to find beauty, pet care, auto, and home repair appointments online and call local businesses for you. For example, you could ask: “Find me a 4 p.m. nail appointment near me.” Ask Maps will then display open slots across nearby businesses.
The new agentic calling feature arrives as tech companies continue to introduce AI agents designed to handle time-consuming tasks, such as scheduling DMV appointments and making reservations. For example, Meta’s AI agent Muse recently gained the ability to call businesses on users’ behalf.
Google also announced that you can now use Ask Maps to quickly find places on your lists by asking something like: “Which spots saved in my lists have availability for five people in the next 30 minutes?”
The tech giant says the new updates are starting to roll out on Monday on Android and iOS. As part of the news, Google announced that Ask Maps is expanding to 30 countries across Europe, including Spain, Germany, and the U.K.
