The heartwarming story of the Arizona grandmother and the stranger to whom she inadvertently texted a Thanksgiving invitation in 2016 – leading to their sharing the holiday together annually ever since – is getting the Lifetime movie treatment, according to the principals of the tale.
Jamal Hinton announced recently that he, Wanda Dench and their Thanksgiving tradition were taking “the next step” with the cable television channel known for its sentimental films, especially those themed around the festive season.
“Very excited to be in business with Lifetime! They believe in our story, and it truly feels like we’re in the best hands as we take this next step,” Hinton wrote in a recent social media post.
Dench for her part told the Arizona Republic that she and Hinton signed the rights to their story over to Lifetime for a dramatization of their friendship – with filming expected in early 2027, after the holiday season ushered in by Thanksgiving on Thursday.
Ebony Magazine on Sunday reported in its own post that the Lifetime movie based on Dench and Hinton would be titled The Thanksgiving Grandma, premiering in November next year.
“Through holiday traditions, loss and life’s ups and downs, their bond has only grown stronger,” the magazine’s post said alongside a photo of Hinton and Dench in a kitchen. “Sometimes, family isn’t about blood … it’s about who makes room for you at the table.”
Hinton was 17 and in high school when Dench tried to text her grandson an invitation to dinner on Thanksgiving 2016. But she messaged Hinton instead, realizing the mistake when the pair from Chandler, Arizona, swapped selfies.
“You not my grandma,” Hinton said to Dench. “Can I still get a plate tho?”
“Of course you can,” Dench reportedly replied. “That’s what grandmas do … feed everyone.”
Hinton indeed went to Dench’s to celebrate that Thanksgiving – and has continued going back for each one since.
Their serendipitous friendship took a particularly poignant turn when Dench’s husband, Lonnie, died from Covid in April 2020 relatively early in the coronavirus pandemic.
He arranged for a care package containing recordings from people around the world who expressed their condolences to Dench.
The pair later filmed a video about the importance of masking, with Dench telling viewers: “Do what you can, not just for yourself but your loved ones as well.”
For Thanksgiving 2025, Hinton published a series of photos with Dench, writing: “Celebrating 10 years of friendship … and all the memories in between – from selfies to full plates.”
Dench at one point told USA Today: “I didn’t think the media [were] going to keep following us every year. That’s what has surprised me – because I thought people would really tired of hearing out story.”
However, the Thanksgiving Grandma’s executive producer, Jack Leslie, told the Republic he kept being drawn to Dench and Hinton’s story because of how they managed to meet accidentally “and then became great friends” amid “these polarized times that have been going on”.
