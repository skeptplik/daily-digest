The streaming wars, or the battle to be the top streaming company, are ongoing, according to Ted Sarandos, a co-CEO of what many would consider today’s frontrunner.
When it comes to subscribers, no streaming service has more than Netflix, which reported having over 325 million subscribers as of January.
But in an interview with Bloomberg published today, Sarandos noted that people are still spending significant time watching traditional broadcast TV.
“The streaming wars are continuing as we go. Half of television engagement in the United States is still linear, old television,” he said, without specifying the source of the statistic. According to analysis from Nielsen, streaming represented 49.6 percent of TV viewing in August, with 36.4 percent coming from broadcast and cable. That still suggests a sizable amount of viewing time for streaming providers to target.
Netflix wants more hits
As Netflix seeks to strengthen its lead against rivals like YouTube and Skydance, there’s extra pressure for it to deliver hit shows and movies.
During the Peak TV era, Netflix drove subscribers by offering exclusive hit shows and movies, like House of Cards, Orange Is the New Black, and Beasts of No Nation (2015). Like many of its rivals, Netflix’s reputation for consistently delivering must-watch series and movies has weakened in the 2020s as streaming customers have become inundated with content and struggle to navigate the vast streaming landscape to find relevant content.
Sarandos said:
I think as Netflix has grown and matured and the business has grown and matured around us, that hit rate really matters. And you’ve got to get ahead of ‘are we on a path to continue [and] maintain that hit rate in the US particularly, but everywhere in the world?’ And I’d say the business is working everywhere in the world, including the US, but we should be doing better in the US than we were. And we’ve got to really focus on our ability to do that.
The CEO said that Netflix is focusing on “the ambition of storytelling” rather than “chasing everything that people just happen to spend five seconds on television on” or relying on AI-generated content.
