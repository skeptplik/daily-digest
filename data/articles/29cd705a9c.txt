If it feels like Sephora has managed to be everywhere all at once in 2026, that’s by design.
The prestige beauty retailer, which was largely responsible for parent company LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton’s 8 percent growth in selective retailing during the third quarter, has spent the last 12 months firing on all cylinders — and plans to do the same in 2027.
“Any of us who have been in the industry for a long time know beauty is an incredibly resilient category,” said North America chief executive officer Artemis Patrick, opening the 2026 Beauty Inc Catalysts conference in a fireside chat with editor in chief Jenny B. Fine. “Sephora is a place where people can just take a breath. Where they can feel like they belong; can feel like themselves, and that’s important to us. It’s why we have been able to be successful in an industry that already is quite exciting.”
This year, Sephora forged new retail partnerships with Olive Young, TikTok Shop and Marks & Spencer in the U.K.; filmed a viral holiday campaign starring Seth Rogen; brought Rhode to Europe and set new sales records in the process; remodeled nearly half its stores, and became the first prestige beauty retailer to launch its app in ChatGPT.
It also signed on last year as a founding partner of the Golden State Valkyries — who took home the WNBA championship title in October.
“Everything we’ve done, every crazy idea — whether it works or it hasn’t — has been rooted in our purpose and our DNA,” said Patrick, adding that she advises Sephora’s brands and founders to take a similar approach. “Don’t try to be something you’re not.”
With the brand’s holiday campaign for instance, Rogan was the key point of excitement but the underlying message was the expertise of the store’s beauty advisers.
“Our marketing team came back with a lot of data, a lot of studies, and said the number-one barrier to shopping at Sephora for gift givers is that sometimes they can feel overwhelmed,” said Patrick. “Ultimately, what it comes down to is we have over 55,000 employees around the world dedicated to teaching and educating about beauty and just calming the nerves when we’re feeling overwhelmed about what to do.”
The CEO added that she feels “optimistic about how we’re going to end the year,” though in terms of the brand landscape, “we know that the highs are getting higher and the lows are getting lower.
“When you have a social media phenomena, something that’s going viral, it’s critical to slow down and figure out how to capture that consumer and keep them for the long run…you can’t just rely on those viral moments anymore,” Patrick said.
Within its own four walls, Sephora is making sure that each new expansion or partnership can prove its purpose, including its online and in-store induction of an Olive Young-curated Korean beauty assortment. “For us, what Olive Young brings to the table is speed, innovation and the best Korean beauty in the world…what we bring to the table is trust, curation and scale in the U.S.,” said Patrick, adding that Sephora just launched the partnership in Southeast Asia and will soon bring it to Australia, the Middle East, the U.K. and Canada.
The Marks & Spencer launch enables Sephora to stake a bigger claim in the British beauty market, while the retailer’s drop-focused partnership with TikTok Shop has so far drawn 80 percent new and lapsed clients via TikTok Live. “The sales are exceeding expectations, and that’s great, but what’s been more exciting is the new clients,” said Patrick. “Again, it’s about differentiation — because otherwise, it’s not worth putting the effort behind it.”
In addition to its ChatGPT partnership, Sephora teamed with Google Gemini to launch its app via that chatbot, and is plotting its next steps in agentic commerce. “We will continue those conversations with LLMs because we definitely think that tone, and conversational search, is absolutely the future,” the CEO said.
Sephora is also using the data of its community to inform decision-making: 90 percent of the retailer’s sales are driven by its tens of millions of Beauty Insiders, and Sephora advisers conduct some 150,000 skin scans per week, which track and advise guests on what moisturizers, complexion shades and other products would best cater to their skin types.
These scans, executed in store using the retailer’s signature handheld devices, result in a roughly 75 percent conversion rate.
“With [Sephora-]exclusive makeup brands, we actually share that data and say, ‘Hey, this shade or this undertone is coming up a lot and we’re not getting a lot of results’ — and so it helps inform their product development,” said Patrick.
Even as Sephora accelerates in digital, it is continuously auditing how to improve the store experience.
“We know beauty is inherently tactile…that human connection is never going to go away for beauty,” said Patrick, adding Sephora is striving to create an equitable shopping experience in terms of look, feel and care across locations new and old via its remodel. “Sephora is a place to escape and to feel good, and it’s important we continue to invest in our stores for that.”
As for her assessment of what makes a successful brand in this landscape?
“An incredible engaged brand founder, of course; innovative product; a good team around you that supports you and doesn’t have to come from beauty, but at least has a love and passion for beauty and understands it,” Patrick said. “And I think there’s a fourth today: that authentic connection with your community. Whether you’re a Milk Makeup [doing that] through Milk Studios or a Mona Kattan doing it through great content…or Sofie Pavitt leaning into her aesthetician ties…lean into your community, connect with them, and the rest is golden.”
