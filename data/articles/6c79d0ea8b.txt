Tories welcome reports that EV targets to be watered down
In an interview with Sky News, Julia Lopez, who is serving as interim shadow business secretary while Claire Coutinho is on maternity leave, said that the Conservatives would like to get rid of the ZEV [zero emission vehicle] mandate entirely. She said that she did not think there was enough consumer demand for electric vehicles, and that there were not enough charging points.
But she still welcomed reports that Labour will weaken the EV targets. “If Labour wants to be pragmatic and change some of those targets, that’s good,” she said.
Karin Smyth, a health minister, was doing a media round for the government this morning. Asked about reports that EV targets are going to be cut, she just stressed that “on this particular issue, no decisions have been made”.
But the Green party is not minded to wait before speaking out. In addition to comments on social media from Zack Polanski and Caroline Lucas, the party has now issued a statement from the Green MP Siân Berry saying:
The switch to EVs has the potential to create greater emissions savings than any other energy transition policy, while providing drivers with vehicles that can be significantly cheaper to run.
Yet again, this Labour government is heading in the wrong direction. Instead of pandering to corporate lobbying and stalling on the phase out of petrol and diesel vehicles, they should be boosting EV uptake through vehicle scrappage schemes and cheaper, more abundant and more reliable public charging infrastructure.
Burnham urged to think twice about Norwegian options as outcome for EU reset - by Norwegian PM
Andy Burnham says he wants the UK to move to a closer relationship with the EU after the next election and one option would be to join the single market. This would significantly boost trade with the EU, but it would involve Britain accepting free movement rules. Norway is in this position – not in the EU, but in the single market.
But today Burnham is being advised to think twice before copying Norway – by the Norwegian PM.
In an interview with Tim Ross from Politico, Jonas Gahr Støre pointed out that being in the single market but not in the EU means accepting regulations decided by others. He said:
After Brexit, British friends called me and said, tell us about the Norwegian way of doing it. And I said, if you left the EU because there was too much Brussels, don’t take the Norwegian version. Because here, you know, basically we are taking most of their decisions, we implement them.
The Tories have criticised the Scottish government for posting a message on its X social media account about a Sikh festival at 11am yesterday – as the nation was observing the two-minute silence for Remembrance Sunday. Harriet Cross, the shadow Scottish secretary, said:
It is hard to see their decision to post about a Sikh festival at precisely 11am, at the very moment that people across the country were observing a two-minute silence to honour our war dead, as a coincidence.
There is no reason why they couldn’t have recognised both - not least out of respect to the 83,000 Sikhs killed fighting in both World Wars.
This failure to mark Remembrance Sunday shows a total disregard for the sacrifice our soldiers made and continue to make in defence of our country.
Government promises stronger modern slavery protections for goods sold in UK
Businesses which trade goods made with slave labour will have to withdraw them from sale, the government has said, revealing it will change the law to enforce the new ban. The Press Association reports:
Jonathan Reynolds, the business secretary, said he plans to introduce new legislation to introduce mandatory human rights due diligence for companies, which will require them to remove products made with forced labour and modern slavery.
He outlined the move in a letter to the Commons business committee, which has been calling for new business safeguards against modern slavery for nearly two years.
The new duty will apply to British businesses with a global turnover of more than £350m, and non-UK businesses with a UK-generated turnover of more than £350m.
Those who do not comply with the new duty may be forced to withdraw products from sale which have come from forced labour or modern slavery, Reynolds said.
The commitee has been calling for modern slavery protections to be strengthened since it heard evidence from Chinese-owned clothing company Shein in January 2025.
In that session, the firm repeatedly declined to confirm whether its products contained cotton sourced from China, including Xinjiang, where it is believed that the Uyghur minority are subject to forced labour.
Fiona Harvey and Jasper Jolly on impact of cutting EV targets
Here is an article by Fiona Harvey and Jasper Jolly published last month on what the consequences would be if the government were to relax its EV targets, as the Guardian today reports now seems inevitable. (See 9.26am.)
And here is an extract.
The end goal of 2035 is likely to remain, but under a consultation that ended on Friday, the 2030 targets could be weakened to 70% or even as low as 50% of new cars. Taken with previous watering down of the policy to allow more sales of hybrid cars – which use both electric and combustion engines – that change would lead to emissions greater than burning all the gas in the Jackdaw field, according to the ECIU [Energy and Climate Intelligence Unit].
If the mandate were cut more drastically to require only 50% of sales to be EVs by 2030, this could mean an additional 147 megatonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent, which would equate to about 58% of the likely emissions from burning the oil produced by the Rosebank field.
Jess Ralston, an analyst at the ECIU, said weakening the target would not solve the problems for manufacturers, who are already facing intensified competition from China as increasing numbers of people opt for EVs. It would also not benefit the public because EVs can be £1,750 a year cheaper to run, and potentially more with oil prices rising.
