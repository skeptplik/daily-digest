Jim Chalmers has declared “a very serious moment for our economy”, after inflation surged to 4.9% in the year to October, from 4.3%, amid fears Australia could tip into recession next year.
As cost-of-living pressures peak in the lead-up to Christmas and unemployment reaches five-year highs, the Australian Bureau of Statistics on Wednesday said the “temporary shocks” of surging fuel costs and the end of electricity rebates drove the larger-than-expected lift in consumer prices.
The treasurer has been preparing to present a mid-year budget update in three weeks’ time, and he again pledged to deliver “substantial savings” that would help ease inflationary pressures.
Chalmers said 2026 “has been an especially difficult year for people”, but also recognised the limits to how much help his government could give to struggling households.
“There’s no amount of responsible cost of living relief or budget savings, which will make this inflation challenge go away overnight,” he said after the latest consumer price report was released on Wednesday.
Stephen Smith, a partner at Deloitte Access Economics, said the inflation numbers “underscore the difficult balance facing the federal government”.
“The treasurer must attempt to hand down a budget update that cuts enough to take some pressure off inflation, but avoids cruelling an economy on the cusp of a downturn,” Smith said.
The latest ABS figures showed fuel costs climbed by 6.3% in October, to be about 33% higher over the year. Electricity prices were 25% higher than a year before, reflecting the end of government power bill subsidies.
Rachael McCririck, the ABS head of price statistics, said removing the impact of these “temporary shocks” on fuel and electricity provided a better idea of underlying price pressures in the economy.
But even here, the news was worse than expected. The Reserve Bank’s preferred trimmed mean measure of inflation – which takes out the most volatile prices – lifted to 3.8%. Economists had expected it to stay at 3.6%.
Building costs inflation accelerated to 5.1% in the year to October, from 4.7%, as project home builders passed on higher labour and material prices on to their customers.
Childcare, hairdressing and insurance prices all climbed by more than 5% through the year.
Despite evidence of the ongoing inflationary threat, analysts said the RBA would not be tempted to deliver a sixth interest rate hike at its next board meeting on 7-8 December.
Economists remained confident that price pressures have peaked, and would ease through 2027 as the shocks of this year ebb away.
Brendan Rynne, the chief economist at KPMG, said “today’s inflation jump is not the kind of spike the RBA will be wanting to see, but it’s still unlikely to result in a further rate rise this year”.
“Instead, we expect they’ll hold until February to see how the numbers look between now and then.”
Taylor Nugent, a senior economist at NAB, said: “Our view remains that the RBA is done hiking.”
Nugent said he still expected two rate cuts next year, starting in August, and potentially as many as three in 2027 if the labour market deteriorates more quickly.
