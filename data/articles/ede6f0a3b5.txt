Sat, 7 Nov, 1:05 pm, Final score, Cats 52, Demons 56
A North Melbourne victory has been a near certainty heading into almost every AFLW match of late.
The Kangaroos have lost just one game in their last 43, and boast a 96 per cent winning rate since losing the 2023 grand final.
At the opposite end of the scale, struggling teams Collingwood and GWS have winning percentages closer to just 15 per cent.
Tomorrow, North will play Melbourne in its fourth consecutive grand final, aiming to be the first side in competition history to secure a three-peat.
Despite the gap between either end of the ladder, AFL Hall of Famer and former Western Bulldogs coach Nathan Burke said it will benefit the league's competitiveness long-term.
"I think it's outstanding for the competition, because this is still a fledgling competition … to have North Melbourne so far out in front, it just means that everyone has to catch up," he said.
"The downside is that the competition becomes a little bit more predictable … and there are some people that just see that and think, 'Oh, North Melbourne are going to win,' and just turn off.
"They don't look at it closely and see the improvement that's coming from underneath because we're trying to catch North Melbourne."
The Kangaroos have not been the only dominant side in the competition over the past 11 seasons.
This is the first year neither Adelaide nor Brisbane will feature in the AFLW grand final.
The Lions have played in seven grand finals, yielding two wins, while the Crows currently hold the record for most premierships in the competition with three.
That record will be equalled if the Kangaroos salute at Princes Park tomorrow night.
AFLW commentator Chyloe Kurdas said a win would edge the Roos ahead when ranking the best sides across the competition's history.
"[They are] interesting comparisons because of the flags Adelaide have won. You look at the conversion rate for Brisbane [of grand finals into premierships], and you look at North Melbourne's conversion rate into their fourth grand final," she said.
"[To win] Three from four, I guess, on the conversion rate, North Melbourne — should they get over the line — will be the number one of those three sides."
Kurdas said the club is being rewarded for the efforts it put into a women's team well before the AFLW's inception.
Melbourne University Women's Football Club developed a partnership with the Kangaroos so players could train at Arden St, and remained the club's VFLW affiliate until 2019.
"It's the groundwork North Melbourne did in 2009, 2010 to invest in the really early days, even before AFLW was on the radar … they used a lot of that intel to build a culture that was ready for women and girls to come into the game at a professional level," Kurdas said.
"And of course, how they built their list. They prioritised footballers at the time, really prioritised skills, and they were able to leverage the draft rules at the time and bring in a really strong group of players from day one."
Melbourne University alumni Emma Kearney and Ash Riddell joined North Melbourne in its first season in the AFLW and remain key pillars of the side leading into tomorrow's grand final.
This year, North lost its first match since the 2023 grand final and recorded its lowest score since that season, while still clearly remaining the premiership favourites.
"Slowly they're [other teams] catching up; it's not happening as fast as everyone would like," Burke said.
"I think Geelong made the biggest strides getting closest to North Melbourne … we'll see how close Melbourne get this weekend, but I do still think there's a bit of a gulf from those top three teams," Burke said.
The statistic in Melbourne's favour tomorrow is its win-loss record against the Roos.
The Demons are the only team in the AFLW to have a better head-to-head record against North since the two-time reigning champions entered the competition.
