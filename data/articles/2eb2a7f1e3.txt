Germany dominated neighbor and historic rival the Netherlands in their Nations League quarterfinal match-up at Berlin's Olympic Stadium on Monday evening.
It was the team's sixth game under head coach Jürgen Klopp and the 50th ever between the two sides.
Germany's 5-0 shellacking of the Oranje was their most lopsided victory since 1959, when they won 7-0. In all, Germany have now won 19 of those matches, the Netherlands have won 12, and 18 ended in a draw.
Speaking to German broadcaster ARD after the game, attacking midfielder Florian Wirtz — who scored twice in the fixture — said: "We hope that we can make it fun for the fans. It was definitely fun for us, especially in the second half."
Despite the loss, Xavi Hernandez's Dutch side will reach the final eight alongside Germany.
Prior to the match, Klopp had made it clear that he and his team were approaching Monday's showdown as a final after having lost to Greece 1-0 in his first home game in the job on September 27.
By night's end, Derry Scherhant and Nico Schlotterbeck each scored once and Brajan Gruda scored once and added two assists on top of Wirtz's double.
Klopp has been high on Wirtz, who has come under fire for not delivering at Liverpool since his arrival there in June 2025, and his bet seems to be paying off with the 23-year-old now scoring four goals in his last four games for Germany.
"We don't care who we play. We want to play attacking football. We want to score goals and dominate the opponent. And I think we did that perfectly," said Wirtz on Monday evening before adding: "We want to keep going next year. We want to get better. I think we've taken some great steps in our development as a team."
