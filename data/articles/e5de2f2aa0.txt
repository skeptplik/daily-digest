Rights group details 'surge' in violent attacks on Palestinian health-care workers
Violence jumped 350% from 2022-2024, Physicians for Human Rights Israel says
For Yousef Dihir, a paramedic since 2008 in the West Bank, treating his fellow Palestinians has become a daily — sometimes lethal — risk.
Leaning against his ambulance in Ramallah, Palestine, earlier this month, he told CBC News about one of the many incidents of settler violence he's faced after receiving a distress call. On this particular occasion, three years ago, his son had come along as a volunteer paramedic.
"My son was injured, we were hit with glass in our faces when the gunshots broke out on the ambulance," he said. "They shot at the rear glass and the front glass."
In a report published this week, Physicians for Human Rights Israel (PHRI) says settler violence affecting health-care workers "surged 350 per cent" between 2022 and 2024 in the West Bank, from 93 violent encounters to 422.
PHRI documented instances of violence against health-care workers through interviews with 25 paramedics, medics and emergency response workers, Dihir included, as well by accompanying paramedics and ambulance drivers during their shifts to see first-hand how they navigated checkpoints to get to patients.
PHRI says the uptick in violence has been particularly notable since the Oct. 7, 2023, Hamas-led attacks that killed 1,200 people, saw 251 taken hostage and sparked a devastating war in Gaza that has left much of the territory in ruins and its two million people living in dire conditions.
Since then, PHRI says, "restrictions on medical care under the violent occupation have increasingly given way to direct attacks by Israeli forces and settlers on emergency medical services."
They say it's clear that the health-care workers were targeted in particular through "broader policies sustaining Israel's unlawful occupation of the West Bank."

The number of obstacles blocking movement around the West Bank has also increased, from 642 in June 2023 to 925 in December 2025.
"Reaching patients, evacuating the wounded and transferring patients between medical facilities all depend on Israeli military approval," the report says.
"Interviewees describe physical assaults, threats at gunpoint, verbal abuse, humiliating searches and being prevented from reaching wounded or seriously ill patients."
The report notes an uptick in violent altercations between settlers and paramedics in the West Bank since Oct. 7, 2023. (Bashar Qaryuti/PHRI)
In 2025, the World Health Organization recorded a total of 233 attacks on health-care workers in the West Bank. And 38 attacks were recorded between January and April 2026, "35 of which involved obstruction of health care."
It is a violation of international law, said Milena Ansari, director of the Occupied West Bank at PHRI, at a news conference on Nov. 16.
Citing the Fourth Geneva Convention, which guarantees protection of civilians during armed conflict, Ansari said Israel has a responsibility to ensure medical services and public health access to "the occupied population."
"And this explicitly extends to Palestinian paramedics, ambulances and emergency medical teams."
IDF denies claims, condemns 'acts of violence'
The Israel Defence Forces (IDF) denied claims that their security forces were stopping medical teams from doing their work and "condemned all acts of violence against medical teams and ambulances," in a statement to CBC News.
"The IDF enables freedom of movement for medical teams through Judea and Samaria and coordinates the movement of Palestinian ambulances with relevant authorities in advance," the statement says.
Ansari said that's not enough.

"We should be setting a high standard of removing all checkpoints, removing all barriers that are between Palestinian communities and Palestinian territories," she said.
In one case, a paramedic described being kept away from an injured man. "I was standing close to the wounded man. I could have reached him easily, but the soldiers stopped me. He lay there bleeding until he died. Only then did they leave, and we were able to reach him," the paramedic recounted to PHRI.
The United Nations Office for Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs said the highest number of violent attacks in the West Bank has been recorded in 2026 to date with over 2,000 attacks against Palestinians resulting in death or property damage.
Paramedics 'constantly on edge,' report says
PHRI is calling on Israel to end all violence against medical workers in the West Bank and ensure the unhindered passage of ambulances through checkpoints, gates and closed areas.
They are also calling on the government to investigate settler violence and prosecute all attacks against medical workers.
The PHRI report also documents the "severe psychological toll on emergency workers," with many noting feelings of distress, anxiety and sleep disturbances.
"All 25 described being constantly on edge, fearing they could be stopped, attacked or prevented from reaching patients," it says.
Dihir said one of his colleagues died in April 2023 during an altercation with settlers.
"He was shot in the head but he refused to stop," he said. "He was afraid for the lives of the injured." His patients survived the attack.
While Dihir admits he doesn't want to die, he says he's not scared.
"I don't care about religion, race, ethnicity or anything else. ... I only care that a person needs humanitarian help and I can do that."
