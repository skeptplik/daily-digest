David O'Reilly, an environment artist who worked at Rockstar for 12 years on games like Red Dead Redemption 2, Grand Theft Auto 5, and the just-released GTA 6, recently started a YouTube channel called Game World Art where he explores videogame environments. For the last year and a half or so, he's chatted about those games and others from his point of view, sharing tidbits about the tech involved in making them—and now that GTA 6 is out, he's showing off his work and offering some impressions.
"To set expectations, this is not a Rockstar insider-type channel, it's not telling you exactly how Rockstar makes their worlds, this is more about seeing these kinds of open world games—and this one in particular—through my eyes and my perspective as an environment artist," he said in the video.
In his impressions video, he cruises around for a while and talks about his changing relationship to Rockstar games. Given that putting them together was literally his day job, he couldn't enjoy games like RDR 2 and GTA 5 "to anywhere near the extent" that can enjoy GTA 6—since he left the studio in 2023, he's got some distance from the final game. That doesn't mean he's turning the analytical part of his brain off entirely, though, as he stopped here and there to critique the world he saw.
He called the game's art "generally stunning" and "almost flawless," saying it was visually better than Crimson Desert, which was hamstrung by a "slight jankiness." But he also said he preferred RDR 2's rivers, which he worked on extensively to the point that it became a specialty. If you think he's being persnickety, he did note that he's "obsessed with rivers," and has argued in a previous video that Crimson Desert's rivers "just clinch it" over Red Dead's. Hopefully we can get a definitive river tier list from O'Reilly soon to settle matters on a broader scale.
Still, he had almost exclusively positive things to say, and was careful to note that it's hard for any one artist to take credit for anything on a game as huge in scope as a Rockstar open world heist-'em-up. Ultimately, it seems like he's mostly pleased to see his and his friends' work finally out in the open: "I want to celebrate this world that I spent literal years working on. And I have plenty of friends who are still at Rockstar who have worked on this for a long time, and I couldn't be more pleased for you all."
