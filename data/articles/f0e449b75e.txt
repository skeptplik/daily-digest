On Friday, Rockstar Games' parent company Take-Two Interactive announced sales figures for Grand Theft Auto 6 that are so dizzying, I'm still processing them. They have clearly shattered preorder records for video games and launch sales — in fact, total sales – for just about any form of entertainment.
Take-Two said that GTA 6 sold 32.5 million copies during its pre-release period, and that through the end of launch day on Nov. 19, it had racked up $3.79 billion in revenue.
Comparisons are fun to make, but apt ones don't really exist. On its day of launch, GTA 6 had made twice as much money as Taylor Swift's entire Eras tour, and more than any film has ever made across a full theatrical run. Alinea Analytics reckons that by the end of the weekend the numbers were up to 44.9 million copies sold and a round $4 billion in revenue. It took GTA 6 one day to eclipse the lifetime sales figures of The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild.
Here's a more meaningful comparison: Grand Theft Auto 5 took three days to hit $1 billion in sales. GTA 6's launch is four times bigger than its predecessor's. It will definitely crush GTA 5's lifetime record, right? Well… not necessarily.
As amazing as GTA 6's sales are, it has a long, long way to go to catch up with its big brother. GTA 5 is the second-best selling game of all time behind Minecraft (or third-best, depending on how you account for sales of Tetris). It has sold a staggering 235 million copies to date, more than two and a half times as many as the next game in the all-time ranking, Rockstar's own Red Dead Redemption 2.
When it comes to long-tail, long-term sales, GTA 5 is in a different class entirely — a class of two, alongside Minecraft. It's not a given that GTA 6 will necessarily be able to join this class. A few things have to fall into place.
Rockstar has yet to confirm a PC version of the game, for starters, though one is expected in 2027. That will unlock a whole new audience for GTA 6, especially globally, and supercharge its sales again.
Rockstar has also yet to confirm that it will create a GTA 6 version of GTA Online. GTA 5's scrappy multiplayer mode became a live-service behemoth that has driven a huge proportion of those 235 million sales, and that financially sustained Rockstar through the extremely long and expensive developments of Red Dead Redemption 2 and GTA 6. Analysts also seem confident in predicting GTA 6's online mode will arrive in 2027, although I think a degree of skepticism is warranted on that part.
Rockstar hasn't said one word publicly about GTA 6 Online for good reason. Migrating online gaming audiences, who’ve invested massive amounts of time and money in their accounts, from one game to a sequel is notoriously difficult. It's why there was never a World of Warcraft 2, for example, and there are vanishingly few examples of it being done successfully (Destiny 2 being, perhaps, the exception that proves the rule).
This is not to say that Rockstar won't do it, but the studio will need to be extremely careful and consider its strategy. Will it lock the new online mode behind a full purchase of GTA 6, as it initially did with its predecessor? Sell it separately and/or bundle it in a GTA Plus subscription, as it now does for GTA Online? Or maybe even go free-to-play? These approaches all have their advantages and drawbacks when it comes to tempting GTA Online players to upgrade and maximizing the new online game's revenue, but only one of them is going to help GTA 6 sell as many copies as GTA 5.
There's a final but important asterisk to consider with respect to GTA 5's astonishing sales. It has been released across three console generations, and a lot of those purchases will be duplicates or even triplicates (which is especially true for GTA Online players).
In other words, the sales figures are likely flattering to the maximum size of the GTA audience; I don't think there are 235 million GTA players out there. It's going to be challenging for GTA 6 to meet that number unless it, too, spans three generations. I hope we won't be waiting until late in the PlayStation 7's life for GTA 7, but you never know with Rockstar.
One thing's for sure: Rockstar's only real competition is itself. GTA 6 has had the biggest launch imaginable. Can it also go the distance?
