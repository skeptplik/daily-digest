China’s Z.AI has fallen behind schedule on its next flagship artificial intelligence model, while rival Moonshot AI expects that the interval between major releases could double from six months to a year or longer if it loses access to overseas computing power, people familiar with the matter have said.
The previously unreported details show how efforts by U.S. AI companies OpenAI and Anthropic to prevent distillation, a technique in which developers train models using answers generated by more capable ones, have slowed Chinese AI development. Washington’s latest chip restrictions threaten further delays.
The measures jeopardize a key advantage that has helped Chinese AI companies challenge their American rivals despite years of export controls: the ability to develop competitive models at substantially lower cost.
