From corsets to bustles to floor-length skirts, the womenswear of the Gilded Age was known for many things. But Louisa Jacobson’s frayed raw hems and buckled collars were certainly not in the ladies’ 1880s style guides.
While doing press in New York City for the fourth season of her smash HBO historical drama The Gilded Age, Jacobson showed us a bit of her own street style... and it was a far cry from the looks that her character, Marian Brook, has donned on the very same streets.
Leaning instead into a bit of menswear, rather than the pastel gowns we’ve come to expect from her on-screen alter-ego, the actor experimented with suiting and modernized tailoring, thanks to the help of her stylist, Edward Bowleg III.
Last Monday, Jacobson turned to Mel Usine for a light-gray set from the label’s Spring/Summer 2027 collection. Composed of a frayed, collarless jacket and matching inside-out skirt, the ensemble offered an unfinished, deconstructed look you’d have never seen during the 1883 Metropolitan Opera House opening.
It was layered over a black-trimmed white tank with a skinny red tie wrapped around her waist, and paired with peep-toe heels.
The next day, the actor pushed things even further, wearing a black-on-black monochrome look from Commission’s Spring/Summer 2027 collection. This one was all about the buckle accents, with a zip-up blazer topped with a belted collar and little capri leggings trimmed with the same detail. In between was a pleated skirt, and for shoes, Jacobson wore pointed-toe pumps over calf-high socks.
Later in the day, Jacobson was a proper Calvin Klein girl—seen in Hudson Square wearing a dark-clay satin blazer and matching trousers with a heather-gray jacket over both. Her little nude ballet flats featured a contrasting reflective strap, and she grabbed a shoulder bag before heading out for an afternoon of press.
In an age of constant method dressing, it appears Jacobson is doing things her way.
