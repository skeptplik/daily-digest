Sat, 7 Nov, 1:05 pm, Final score, Cats 52, Demons 56
As rain bucketed down, North Melbourne confirmed its AFLW dynasty with an historic third consecutive premiership.
While it may seem repetitive to talk about the Roos' record-breaking run, what they had to compete with in 2026 makes this third flag that little bit more special.
For the first time since 2023 they lost a game, and challengers consistently came closer than in the previous two years.
The looming feeling that senior players could potentially hang up their boots also added a sense of occasion to the victory.
And then the weather showed up. The rain was going to be the great equaliser, allowing fellow grand finalist Melbourne to gather some confidence.
But by dominating three specific phases of the game, North Melbourne made history on Friday night, securing a three-peat.
The fast start that set up the victory
Throughout 2026, North Melbourne hasn't been an overly strong first-quarter team.
Generally, the opening terms were largely arm wrestles, almost like the Roos were playing with the opposition, giving them hope, before taking it away.
But on Friday night, the fast start was necessary. With rain already falling, and more on the radar, any early lead was going to be seriously valuable, so the Roos ensured they were the team in front.
Tess Craven knocked through the first goal of the game within 21 seconds, and captain Jasmine Garner kicked a second six minutes in. They went direct, gained ground with kicks, and got deep entries to unsettle Melbourne's typically staunch defence.
Both goals for the Roos came from stoppage, which was a worrying sign for Melbourne so early on. Last time these sides met, it was off turnover — not stoppage — that North Melbourne had set itself apart.
But this time around, the Roos tipped those scales in their favour.
Best on ground medallist Garner set the tone early in the piece. In the first quarter alone, Garner's stats included seven disposals, four inside 50s, and the goal, laying the foundations for how North Melbourne was going to attack the game.
While it took Melbourne some time to adapt to what the game was throwing up, North Melbourne just got to work.
The Demons over-possessed the footy in slippery conditions, opting for the handball too often, instead of getting boot to ball to win all-important territory.
Holding steady as the Dees fought back
Once Melbourne managed to get into the game, the Roos had to simply batten down the hatches and absorb the blows being thrown their way.
But crucially, just as in the first quarter, North Melbourne had already taken advantage of its early momentum on the scoreboard, with a classy Eilish Sheerin goal 39 seconds into the next term.
The Demons shifted to more of a kicking game in the second quarter, got into more dangerous positions, and Tayla Harris's contested marking reared its head. With this shift, they kicked what would be their only two goals of the match.
That shift in style was borne out in the stats, with Melbourne's ratio of 39 kicks to 14 handballs in the second quarter helping to throw North Melbourne's movement out of whack.
While the second quarter was Melbourne's lowest-disposal quarter of the match, it was the side's most effective. Notably, both of the goals came from intercepts.
Pressure was also a major consideration in the second term, with Melbourne recording a pressure factor of 2.30 across the quarter — a rate at the upper end of the elite category — which rose to 2.38 in the third term.
But, crucially for the Roos, they maintained a lead even when the Demons threw their best at them. And the third quarter became a stalemate, in which North Melbourne maintained its arm's-length lead. The Roos' deep defence was staunch, repelling repeated forward entries and preventing Melbourne from scoring further goals.
Midfielders Garner (eight intercepts) and Ash Riddell (seven) were important in this respect, sliding behind the ball post-stoppage, while winger Amy Gavin Mangan, half-forward Jenna Bruton, and half-back Eliza Shannon (all six intercepts) got busy defensively.
In doing so, they lulled the Demons back into over-possession of the footy, taking their kick rate way back down to 50 per cent.
Essentially, the middle two quarters were a tale of the Roos just taking the hits and remaining on their feet.
Five-minute burst seals the premiership
While the game largely felt like it was in the balance for the duration, a five-minute patch in the last quarter turned a six-point lead into a 26-point victory for North Melbourne. It was a classic demonstration of the Roos' brand, taking the game away in a flash.
Melbourne controlled territory for the bulk of that term, attempting to break through the numbers in its attacking arc but unable to convert for the major that would have really rattled the Roos.
Instead, North Melbourne again absorbed the blows — and then the fast break happened. A long rebounding kick unlocked the Roos' speed, which had been a worry for Melbourne all evening and in the end proved the difference.
It allowed the Roos to set up an attack once again, create a stoppage inside 50, and secure a crucial goal off the boot of Bella Eddey.
And that goal was the dam wall breaking. It put the Roos up by 13 points, and opened the floodgates for two more goals via Blaithin Bogue, taking the game from a thrilling nailbiter to a comprehensive North Melbourne victory.
This is the Roos' style: absorb, absorb, absorb — then land the felling blow.
Throughout the season they have consistently dominated late in games.
They kicked the last three goals in their preliminary final, and continued the trend by kicking the last three goals in the grand final.
Prior to the game, one question for Melbourne was whether it could prevent that final flurry and hold on.
On Friday night, North Melbourne provided an emphatic answer.
