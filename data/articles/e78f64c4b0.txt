In short:
The Tasmanian premier and ministers have been holding meetings with the Commonwealth, the AFL and Cricket Tasmania over progress on the Hobart stadium project.
The ABC understands the government has been preparing for a cost blowout since at least August.
What's next?
The preferred builder has given its final fixed-price cost to the government, but it is not expected to become public for a few weeks.
The Tasmanian government has begun the process of canvassing financial partners to help fund its Macquarie Point stadium, as the $1.13 billion project faces a budget crunch.
The ABC understands preliminary discussions have been held with a number of parties, including the AFL, regarding the prospect of an increased financial contribution towards the stadium, as the government prepares to sign a potential billion-dollar building contract with BESIX Watpac.
Early informal conversations have also been held between Premier Jeremy Rockliff and Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, while private money is also being discussed.
The premier is also aware of cricket's potential role in bringing private money to the project.
But just how much money, if any, the government may be able to secure from the private sector, the AFL and the federal government remains unclear.
The pursuit comes as the stadium project stares down a funding gap, with state funding capped at $875 million, and the project's $1.13 billion cost seemingly in jeopardy.
BESIX Watpac delivered its final fixed-price lump sum number to the Macquarie Point Development Corporation (MPDC) on Friday last week.
But any hope that the stadium project might be completed within its $1.13 billion budget received a blow when development corporation head Anne Beach told a parliamentary committee this month that she was preparing to make a submission to cabinet regarding the project's parameters, which included delivery timelines and overall cost.
The ABC understands the government has been preparing for a cost blowout since at least August, following the submission of tenders by prospective builders.
Approaches to AFL
The ABC can reveal that on August 28 Tasmanian Treasurer and Macquarie Point Urban Renewal Minister Eric Abetz met with the AFL's executive general manager of infrastructure, clubs and finance, Matthew Chun.
The meeting took place two weeks after tenders from the two stadium bidders were received, and in the midst of a technical, commercial and governance assessment of the project.
It was a departure from other progress meetings between the government and the AFL, and the first time the treasurer had met one-on-one with the league's finance boss to discuss the stadium.
It was also particularly timely.
According to the "stadium spending cap" section of the government's stadium "safeguarding commitments" document, which was updated in September, August was earmarked as a "key checkpoint on any threshold budget issues".
In response to specific questions regarding whether a greater financial contribution had been sought from the AFL, a government spokesperson did not respond directly.
The spokesperson said the meeting between Mr Abetz and Mr Chun "provided an opportunity to provide an update on the project and hear the AFL's perspective on progress".
Mr Chun is also the AFL's representative on the Macquarie Point stadium steering committee.
Three other meetings between the government and AFL have taken place this year — in March, April and July.
All involved Mr Rockliff, with one an introductory meeting between the premier and incoming AFL chairman Craig Drummond.
Those meetings focused on the progress of the Tasmania Devils Football Club, rather than specifically about the stadium.
The AFL is already contributing $15 million towards the Macquarie Point stadium, and a further $10 million to the Tasmania Devils Training and Administration facility in Kingborough.
The ABC asked the AFL whether the government had sought extra funds from it, and whether the league was considering providing it with extra financial assistance.
An AFL spokesperson said: "The Tasmania Devils Football Club and Macquarie Point stadium continue to make great progress ahead of the Devils' inaugural AFL season in 2028."
Rockliff sounds out Commonwealth
The hunt for more stadium money ramped up in September, when the premier flew to Canberra to meet with Mr Albanese and Deputy Prime Minister Richard Marles, where the new AFL team and stadium were on the agenda.
The trip coincided with the launch of the Parliamentary Friends of the Tasmania Devils group, which saw Devils bosses Brendon Gale, Grant O'Brien and Kath McCann also travel to Canberra for a meet-and-greet with a number of Tasmanian federal politicians, as well as a meeting with the deputy prime minister, where the stadium was discussed.
It is understood Mr Rockliff informally raised the prospect with Mr Albanese of the federal government contributing more money to the Macquarie Point project over and above the $240 million it has already committed, as part of a meeting about general Tasmanian matters.
Publicly, the federal government has insisted its contribution is capped, but it is understood that privately, the idea of contributing further funds towards broader precinct costs at Macquarie Point has not been completely dismissed.
However, it is understood no formal request or commitment has been made.
In a statement, a Tasmanian government spokesperson said it "continues to engage with its funding partners on the Macquarie Point Multipurpose Stadium and the broader precinct".
Stadium could get private funding
Private money is also on the table, with cricket looming as a dark horse in the search for extra funds to help pay for the stadium.
At least one prospective buyer of the Hobart Hurricanes Big Bash team, which could be partially sold via Cricket Australia's Big Bash League (BBL) privatisation process, is attracted by the prospect of investing in the project, the ABC understands.
Prior to the Canberra trip, Mr Rockliff discussed the prospect of private money for Macquarie Point in a meeting with Cricket Tasmania boss Dominic Baker and chair Tim Scott.
The meeting took place on September 8, the same day Cricket Australia publicly announced it would invite private investment into the BBL.
Any potential stadium cost blowouts would need a creative solution, as the state government previously agreed to a state funding cap for the project in order to secure enough votes for it to pass parliament.
The cap was a condition of upper house independent MLC Bec Thomas, ahead of last year's crucial parliamentary vote.
As part of that agreement, the government was to seek any additional funds required from external partners, namely the AFL, federal government and Cricket Australia if it was in danger of breaching the cap.
With state parliament adjourning next week, a number of crossbench MPs have written to the premier and the Labor opposition leader calling for an extension of sitting days this year so the stadium's final cost can be scrutinised.
