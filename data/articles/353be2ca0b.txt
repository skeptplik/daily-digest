Cyberattack exposes unclassified information from CIA system, US media reports
A recently discovered hack on a CIA analysis system exposed some unclassified information, US media reported on Tuesday. The breach is the latest in a series of cyber attacks that have targeted US government departments and major companies in recent weeks.
A cybersecurity breach at the CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) exposed some unclassified information, US media reported Tuesday.
The New York Times said the recently discovered intrusions targeted an open-source intelligence analysis system, a computer network that sifts through public, unclassified material for insights.
Though the information is generally public or semi-public, open-source information can give intelligence analysts significant insight on its own or in combination with intelligence obtained from classified spying programmes.
It was not clear what specific information was compromised or who was responsible.
The newspaper, citing US officials and people familiar with the issues, said that classified material was not jeopardised.
The issue that resulted in the breach has now been resolved, a person familiar with the incident told AP on condition of anonymity.
The Times quoted an ODNI official as saying that there had been no disruptions to critical capabilities. It said the CIA declined comment.
The report comes several days after the Agriculture Department and FBI were reportedly targeted in cyberattacks, and amid mounting concerns over the security of US computer systems following a wave of major technical outages.
Last week an internal network the National Security Agency uses to communicate classified information was offline for a time and a nationwide ground stop snarled air travel after air traffic controllers struggled to communicate with pilots.
Apple, JP Morgan Chase and Microsoft also had systems go down this month.
The incidents have put a spotlight on the vulnerabilities inside critical infrastructure, banking systems and the digital tools that millions of people use daily.
Asked about the reported FBI breach on Saturday, President Donald Trump said he had only recently learned of it.
"I will say that it's not good. I don't like that," he told Fox News.
The outages in recent weeks have disrupted travel, communications and e-commerce for countless users across the United States.
White House cybersecurity chief Sean Cairncross was fired last week as criticism mounted over the spate of issues.
Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said on Sunday that the outages largely stem from the rush to patch and upgrade systems to stay ahead of hackers who are now empowered with sophisticated artificial intelligence tools to attack at an unprecedented scale.
