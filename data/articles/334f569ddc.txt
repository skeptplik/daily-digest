Good morning. On Friday last week Andy Burnham was in Brussels to announce a reset deal with the EU. Today the debate will be revived as we get to read the small print.
We are getting three documents that should help experts assess the merits, or otherwise, of the deal. They are: the text of the legislation implementing the deal, formally known as the European partnership bill, but which ministers are calling the cutting costs bill; the legal text of the deal on food standards, which runs about 200 pages; and the legal text of the deal on emissions trading.
Faisal Islam, the BBC’s economics editor, has more here on what the legal text will show. He says it will confirm that the food deal has “a 10-year termination clause that would mean if one party ended the agreement, they would have to pay to rebuild border checks infrastructure, which will be significantly scaled back as part of the new deal”. Termination clauses like this are not unusual in trade deals, but this one has been dubbed the “Farage clause” because it is partly inspired by the EU’s worry about Reform UK winning the next election and ripping up the deal. It could be called the Badenoch clause too, because the Tories also want to scrap the deal, but Farage is better known in Brussels, where he is considered a more formidable enemy than the Tory leader.
The government says its European partnership bill will “save industry millions by cutting red tape, lifting bans on iconic British exports, and exempting £6.5bn of British exports from EU carbon taxes”.
But the Conservatives are claiming any savings for customers are minimal. Here is an extract from the news release issued overnight.
 Analysis by the Conservatives of the government’s own estimates shows that the savings from the sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) deal will amount to just pennies on everyday essentials, despite Andy Burnham promising the deal would bring down food prices.
Beef prices have risen by 27.8 per cent since Labour took office, adding £1.22 to a pack of beef mince. Yet the government’s SPS deal would save shoppers just over 1p on a £5.60 pack.
Pork prices have increased by 9.6 per cent, adding 29p to a pack of pork loin steaks, while the deal would cut the price by barely 1p. A six-pack of tomatoes would become just half a penny cheaper, while a head of broccoli would fall by less than a third of a penny.
Currently half of all the pickled gherkins Britain imports come from the EU, yet on the government’s figures its EU deal will take just over half a penny off a £1.40 jar of gherkins. However, this would be on top of the government’s generous 4p cut announced at the budget.
If you don’t fancy reading 200 pages of legal text, there might be some more juicy documents coming from the government today when it publishes a fresh tranche of documents required by the humble address on Peter Mandelson. These are the papers relating to his appointment as ambassador to the US. Most documents have already been released but some – particularly those relating to what Mandelson told No 10 when it asked specifically about his links to Jeffrey Epstein – were held back at the request of the police (who are still pondering whether to charge him with misconduct in public office in relation to leaks to Epstein). Now the government is able to release a further tranche. They will come at some point today, but we are not sure when.
