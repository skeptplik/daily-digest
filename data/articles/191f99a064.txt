For years, influencers have come up with workarounds on social media to figure out how to share affiliate links with followers. A new integration between Instagram and Amazon was announced earlier this month as a way to make it easier for influencers to make money from affiliate links — but some content creators are suspicious about who really benefits.
Some creators are skeptical of Instagram’s new affiliate program
Influencers are now able to link their Amazon and Instagram accounts to promote products. Some are concerned about what they’re opting in to.
The new integration allows creators to connect their Instagram and Amazon accounts and automatically tag products with their affiliate links, from which they earn a commission when followers make purchases. Influencers will be able to embed up to 30 product links in a Reel or 20 in a feed post, but a permissions setting has led to concerns about what creators are actually agreeing to.
In connecting an Amazon account, creators must agree to allow the retailer and its brand sellers to create ads from “any public content” that includes a related affiliate link. Influencers must also agree to acknowledge that brands can edit their content and use their “testimonial, caption or media” for ads.
The wide-ranging permissions are giving creators pause. Some have urged peers not to connect their accounts without reviewing the permissions and understanding how the feature would affect earnings. People making money on social media spend years tweaking their content and strategy to maximize reach, engagement, and, by extension, their bottom line. For some, it appeared that they would be giving Amazon and its sellers the ability to use their content under unclear terms. On Instagram head Adam Mosseri’s post announcing the feature, influencers complained about the lack of clarity, questioned whether using the feature would decrease their reach (which some creators say happens when they link to outside products), and demanded answers to whether their commission rate would stay the same when Amazon or brands use their content for ads.
Linking products through the new integration won’t reduce reach, Jimmy O’Keefe, Instagram’s director of product marketing, said in an email to The Verge. O’Keefe also noted that creators will continue to earn commission through affiliate links when Amazon boosts their posts, and that they can stop the boosting in settings or disconnect the integration entirely. There’s also a separate, manual way to add products without linking accounts, if a creator wants to opt out of having their content made into ads.
Amazon has an enormous influencer program that reaches across social media platforms. It is so lucrative that some content creators are able to make a living almost exclusively by hawking products for sale on Amazon, building empires on the shopping habits of their followers. When it comes to putting those links in front of followers, though, social platforms have historically been a roadblock, hence the cottage industry of link-in-bio tools meant to circumvent limits. Slowly but surely, though, platforms like Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube — perhaps chasing the success of TikTok Shop — have added more and more shopping features. The result is that shopping and social media begin to feel more and more like the same thing.
