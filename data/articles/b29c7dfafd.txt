With a beaming grin from ear to ear, covered in champagne and looking like a man who had a weight lifted off his shoulders, Oscar Piastri summed up his stunning Las Vegas Grand Prix in six words: "It was a lot of fun."
2026 has not been Piastri's year, and of the top drivers in F1, he has had the most disappointing season.
But on the Las Vegas Strip, in a grand prix that teammate Lando Norris called the "most chaotic race ever", Piastri found his mojo, and has allowed himself the chance to finish 2026 with a bang.
Following on from the bitter disappointment of losing out in last year's thrilling world championship fight to Norris, Piastri's 2026 season started horribly, and never really got any better.
The soaring highs of leading the championship in 2025 felt like a distant memory when he failed to start the opening two races this season.
But a second-place finish in Japan and third in Miami — rounds three and four of the season — gave a false hope that Piastri could be in contention again in 2026.
It wasn't to be.
Piastri would not be on the podium in the next 16 grands prix, the longest streak of his F1 career.
Meanwhile, Norris claimed three brilliant race wins and is fighting to finish third in the championship, while Piastri languishes in seventh.
The Las Vegas Grand Prix, on paper, should have been like the rest of Piastri's 2026 — a struggle.
He qualified seventh, took a penalty for adding a new internal combustion engine to his power unit, and started a lowly 17th on the grid.
What followed was tremendous racecraft, as Piastri sliced his way through the field.
In a race where the field was pretty much bunched up for all 50 laps, and with a ridiculous 352 overtakes across the grand prix, Piastri made sure he was going forward more than he was going backwards.
He didn't have it all his own way. He was behind his teammate when the safety car was called and dropped from ninth to 13th while he had to wait for Norris to pit first.
That still didn't matter.
Piastri just went back to work, picking off his rivals one by one, until the most unlikely of podiums was in sight.
He had a bit of luck to get there. George Russell hitting the wall, and Kimi Antonelli losing battery power late in the race cleared the path for Piastri's podium finish — but the Victorian had made his own luck.
And luck is something he has had little of in 2026.
Asked post-race if he had turned a corner, and could eye off bigger and better things in 2027, Piastri, as usual, remained grounded.
"It's hard to say. I think the pace in general was pretty strong, so I was happy," her said.
"But that was such a particular race — the next two weeks are not going to look like that."
He's right. The next two races in Qatar and Abu Dhabi will not look like the frantic, action-packed, drama-filled grand prix in Las Vegas.
But history suggests there could be at least one similarity — Piastri on the podium.
Piastri's first win in F1 was the Qatar sprint race in 2024, and he went on to finish third in the grand prix.
Last year he again won the sprint race, and should have won the grand prix had it not been for a disastrous pit strategy by McLaren, which practically handed the race win to Max Verstappen. Piastri still finished second that day.
Now he goes into a track that, statistically, he is exceptional at, and has confidence following one of the best drives from anyone this season.
Piastri also has success at Abu Dhabi, with a best finish of second, which he achieved last year. He started on the front row there as well in 2024, but was taken out by Verstappen on the first lap.
He is heading to tracks he enjoys, and where he has enjoyed great results.
His Las Vegas podium does not mean he has turned the corner, and Piastri is the first to acknowledge that.
But the terrific performance could not have come at a better time, and could be the first step in his rise back to the top.
