The Binding of Isaac developer Nicalis has removed undisclosed AI-generated music included in Repentance+ after backlash led to a review-bombing campaign on Steam and a response from the game's creator, Edmund McMillen.
McMillen and Nicalis spent months building up to the November 19, 2026, release date for the latest update in their classic action roguelike game, but that launch was quickly soured. Just 8% of The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+'s recent Steam reviews are positive, with a good chunk of comments left by players referencing AI-generated material that was not previously mentioned in any official capacity.
"Thanks, Nicalis, for breaking everything and adding AI slop to an already-existing game!" one popular review said.
"I'm starting to think that adding AI to a game should be valid grounds to refunds to people who have already paid for and played a game," another said. "Shameful they would do this to an Indie gem. Shameful."
The allegedly AI-generated content in question seems to be music exclusively tied to a specific, Grand Theft Auto-themed run meant to coincide with the recent release of GTA 6. What could have been a cute jab at another gaming titan boiled into a full-blown controversy for Nicalis when Reddit user ShweetsStuff reported the questionable tunes just one day after launch.
As artificial intelligence advances, it's become increasingly difficult to discern human-made art from content pushed out by machines, but ShweetsStuff was able to provide evidence backing their claims. Specifically, they pointed to a handful of tracks that mention AI music software Suno in their metadata, which also included links to their Suno pages.
AI-generated content passed off as art has already become controversial on its own in recent years. Adding to the fire for The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ was the fact that the music was featured in the new update without any sort of update to its Steam description. Valve requires developers to disclose how they may have used AI during game development, making the music's inclusion as puzzling as it is concerning for those who felt Nicalis had pulled the wool over their eyes.
Negative reviews poured in following ShweetsStuff's discovery, leading McMillen to issue a statement today. He said he “had no idea" how AI-generated music found its way into the launch build of The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+'s new update.
"Woke up to a s**t show," he said, "obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned."

Just hours later, Nicalis launched a new patch, confirming the "removal of generated music files." The update is currently live for PC, with the studio adding that it has already been submitted to Microsoft, Nintendo, and Sony. When exactly it will go live for those platforms remains to be seen.
No explanation for how or why the AI-generated music was included was provided. Instead, the team simply said it was "working on the game and will continue to address new and old bugs." McMillen also encouraged fans to report issues and bugs to the developers.
In the meantime, The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+'s recent reviews on Steam remain firmly in the "Overwhelmingly Negative" category. The major update also coincided with the launch of Repentance+ on new platforms, including Nintendo Switch 2, PlayStation 5, and Xbox Series X/S.
