Extra-time may soon have a different meaning for fans of FC Barcelona. Supporters are now being offered the chance to keep their ashes at the Spanish football club, under recently announced plans to build a memorial within the walls of the famous Camp Nou stadium.
Liverpool's legendary manager Bill Shankly once famously said "Some people believe football is a matter of life and death. I am very disappointed with that attitude. I can assure you it is much, much more important than that."
Sentiments shared, it seems, by FC Barcelona as the Spanish Liga champions have recently unveiled plans for their fans to remain part of the club well into the afterlife.
Barça want to open up the Camp Nou columbarium complex at the end of 2027, a 1,500-square-metre space where supporters will be able to leave their ashes.
The project, called Memorial FC Barcelona, will have 26,000 columbarium niches and 9,000 commemorative plaques.
Presenting the plans, architect Quique Montoya said the design was inspired by the shape of a "trainer" and that the 26,000 niches represent as many supporters who will accompany the team in spirit.
The Memorial will have its own entrance from the street, open on a permanent basis. On match days and on special dates it will also be accessible from inside the stadium, via gate 35. There is already a first name on the team sheet. Former club captain, Josep Vicenç 'Tente' Sánchez, has said that he wants to be laid to rest there.
There's a price to pay of course for such a coveted spot at a legendary club. Leases run for 10, 25 or 50 years for a niche of your own at the blaugrana stadium with costs starting from €2,490 euros for a decade, €4,990 for 25 years and €5,990 for half a century. To reserve one, you have to pay €500 up front, which is deducted from the total, and after that there is a yearly maintenance fee of €70.
The club is also offering a cheaper option by way of a commemorative plaques, which costs between €490 and €990 depending on how long you want your name on the hallowed walls. But even within these options there are surcharges, for example if you want a specific spot or to be seen close to the legends of the club.
Atlético de Madrid, the original
Barcelona are not however, the first Spanish side to offer fans a eternal place in a stadium. Similar ventures are also available to supporters of Espanyol, at Cornellà-El Prat, and Betis, which opened their own at the Benito Villamarín in 2011 with around 4,000 urns.
The Spanish pioneer in this 'field of dreams' was Atlético de Madrid which opened their Espacio Memorial at their former Vicente Calderón stadium back in 2008. It had space for 4,200 ceramic urns at a cost of €3,600 each, according to a study in the journal Disparidades.
The club itself boasted at the time that it was the first football stadium in the world with a space of this kind. As a result of its stadium's demolition, however, the remains of Atléti's deceased fans have sinced been moved to a final resting place at their new Metropolitano stadium, a process carried out "with the permission of the families".
