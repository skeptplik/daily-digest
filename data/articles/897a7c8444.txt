Luxury development planned on land some Albanians say is theirs, not Jared Kushner's
A company working for President Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner purchased 300 acres of Albanian land for more than $130 million in April. But Albanians in the area tell NPR this is not Kushner's land — it's theirs.
ROB SCHMITZ, HOST:
In the summer of 2021, Jared Kushner and Ivanka Trump boarded a yacht owned by Nat Rothschild, an heir to the European banking family. On the way from Montenegro to Greece, the yacht anchored off the coast of Albania, and as Kushner told an investment conference last year, that's when Albanian Prime Minister Edi Rama arrived at the boat to tell them about his plan for the country.
(SOUNDBITE OF ARCHIVED RECORDING)
JARED KUSHNER: But I was very, very impressed with his vision for what he wanted to do with Albania. I really didn't know anything about the country. I didn't understand the history. And the darkness of the history with the communism and the terrible leadership actually led to it being fully undeveloped as a nation.
SCHMITZ: A year later, Kushner was looking for development opportunities in the Balkans. He returned to the Albanian coast to the city of Vlorë, which was finishing the construction of an airport atop a wetland near an undeveloped coastline.
(SOUNDBITE OF ARCHIVED RECORDING)
KUSHNER: Obviously, a country is going to build an airport by the coast in the place that they think is the most beautiful. So we went there, we looked for opportunities, and we were able to find some incredible, incredible opportunities.
SCHMITZ: And this is where Kushner found these opportunities.
(SOUNDBITE OF WIND BLOWING)
SCHMITZ: Hidrovor Beach is an expanse of caramel-colored sand with the gentle waves of the Adriatic lapping its perimeter. Albanian authorities say that a company working for Kushner purchased this land and hundreds of acres surrounding it for more than $130 million in April of this year. But several people in Albania tell NPR, this is not Kushner's land. They say it's theirs.
GENTIAN MOÇKA: This land here belonged to my family for centuries.
SCHMITZ: Gentian Moçka says his family has owned this strip of beach and wetlands since the time of the Ottoman Empire. He's provided NPR land titles that appear to prove this. After World War II, Albania's communist government seized it, along with all private land. When communism fell in 1991, families like Moçka's embarked on a yearslong process to get their land back.
MOÇKA: But then our problems started with guys that were much more powerful than we were, had a lot of political support, were very rich and very well connected with the politics of that time.
SCHMITZ: In 2006, Moçka learned that someone else was laying claim to his family's land, a man named Artur Shehu, who was a familiar name for those who lived here in the 1990s when the regional capital of Vlorë was outside the control of the country's government. It was instead run by Mafia-style gangs, as captured by news video from the AP at that time.
(SOUNDBITE OF GUNFIRE)
SCHMITZ: Artur Shehu was well known to police in Vlorë for his alleged involvement in a crime syndicate that, according to Albanian prosecutors, smuggled drugs and migrants into Europe. He fled Albania in 1999 after what police say was a shootout with a rival gang. He ended up in the United States, where he received asylum in 2001 and later became a U.S. citizen. The 60-year-old Shehu now lives in a villa in Miami Beach.
But he's continued to manage a growing real estate portfolio back in Albania through an associate in Vlorë named Pëllumb Petritaj. The pair has claimed plots of land, including Moçka's family plot on the beach, up and down the southern Albanian coast, and prosecutors have taken notice. In 2018, Petritaj served three years in prison for forging land titles on behalf of Shehu. And on Wednesday, Petritaj was sentenced to another seven years in prison for falsifying documents to $17 million worth of oceanfront property in the city of Vlorë on behalf of Shehu. The court ordered the land confiscated from Shehu last week.
(SOUNDBITE OF DOG BARKING)
SCHMITZ: The pair has also claimed oceanfront farmland in the village of Zvërnec, adjacent to Moçka's beach.
(SOUNDBITE OF DOG BARKING)
SCHMITZ: The quiet village has only a couple hundred residents. Most of them, like Minela Konomi (ph) and Natasha Kota are past the age of 70. I asked them if they know who Artur Shehu is.
MINELA KONOMI: (Non-English language spoken).
SCHMITZ: "He is from the village of Kaninë, far from here up in the mountains," says Konomi, "but his family has never lived here. He's involved in shady dealings," Konomi says. Then Mrs. Kota speaks up.
NATASHA KOTA: (Non-English language spoken).
KOTA: "There wasn't a single soul of that family living here, going back to the Ottoman times," she says. "We've heard stories about him, and we're very scared of him."
KOTA: (Non-English language spoken).
SCHMITZ: Twenty years ago, Kota, Konomi and dozens of others in this village learned that Artur Shehu had somehow acquired their farmland. They filed suit in Albanian court to reclaim their land, a fight that escalated this past April when Shehu sold the parcel that includes what they say is their farmland to a company developing a luxury resort project for Kushner. That transaction, however, is under review. In court documents obtained by NPR, Albanian prosecutors argue that the land involved in Kushner's development is, quote, "suspected of having been illegally acquired through forgery."
For his part, Kushner issued a statement on his website saying his partnership purchased the land from a private party in a private transaction. Title, he wrote, was reviewed by counsel and verified. NPR has reached out to Artur Shehu, but he did not respond. He has previously denied any wrongdoing.
KUJTIM CAKRANI: (Non-English language spoken).
SCHMITZ: However, a former lawyer for Shehu, a man named Kujtim Cakrani, was willing to talk.
CAKRANI: (Non-English language spoken).
SCHMITZ: Cakrani, dressed in a black suit and tie with slicked back hair, admits that individuals who were authorized by Artur Shehu as his power of attorney did forge documents related to the land that is now tied to Jared Kushner. He says Shehu's associates forged an inheritance certificate that made it appear that Shehu's grandfather had owned the land, which was then passed to Artur Shehu himself. And then last April, he sold that land to a pair of Syrian-born brothers who are now billionaires living in Qatar, Moataz and Ramez Al Khayyat. They are financial partners of Jared Kushner.
CAKRANI: (Non-English language spoken).
SCHMITZ: Cakrani says that when he discovered that Artur Shehu was involved in this forgery, he dropped him as his client. "It was too serious of a matter, and I can no longer defend him," Cakrani says. "And this is why," he says, "prosecutors have put Artur under investigation and have frozen his bank accounts." Cakrani says he is now representing a group of 30 relatives of Artur Shehu who he claims to be the rightful owners of this land.
Thank you so much for your time.
CAKRANI: Thank you.
SCHMITZ: Gentian Moçka as well as Minela Konomi and Natasha Kota in Zvërnec tell NPR that nobody by the name of Shehu - Artur nor his relatives - has any legal claim to what they say is their land, oceanfront land that has now been claimed by several parties, including the son-in-law of the president of the United States.
RODEN HOXHA: Now that it's Kushner in it, everybody is freaking out.
SCHMITZ: Albanian real estate expert Roden Hoxha has seen this kind of land battle many times before. He says land ownership in Albania is complicated by decades under a Stalinist regime that stole all the land from private owners and collectivized it. And when communism fell in 1991, he says Albania became a land-grab free-for-all.
HOXHA: And it was so disorganized - it was basically chaos - that people started claiming land wherever, you know, they threw a stone. It was basically the Albanian version of U.S.'s claiming of the West.
SCHMITZ: Complete, Hoxha says, with wealthy speculators, keen to exploit the chaos of the moment and with multiple levels - locals whose families have farmed the land for generations, well-connected operators like Shehu who aggressively try to claim that land.
HOXHA: And then on the international level, it exploded because it was Kushner and Trump and Arab money behind it.
SCHMITZ: And Hoxha says, what at first appeared to be yet another Albanian land dispute now has ties all the way to the White House. In the end, an Albanian court will likely decide who the rightful owner of this property is. But for now, the corruption this deal suggests has sparked unprecedented public outrage in Albania.
(SOUNDBITE OF EXPLOSIONS)
SCHMITZ: Protesters have taken to the streets of the capital, Tirana, every day since May 31, calling on Prime Minister Edi Rama to resign, but Rama has only vowed to crack down harder on what have become increasingly violent demonstrations against his entire government. Rama has vowed that Kushner's resort project will go ahead as planned, but an ongoing investigation by Albania's state prosecutor into forged land titles could halt the project.
(CHEERING)
SCHMITZ: When contacted by NPR, a spokesperson for Prime Minister Edi Rama sent a statement which read that, quote, "if the seller does not have legitimate ownership of the property, the money will remain within the Republic of Albania. And certainly, it will be used for anyone who proves that his claim over the property is grounded." The statement went on to say that the ambition for Rama's government is to create a new benchmark for sustainable Mediterranean development, a goal that aligns with Jared Kushner's vision for a luxury resort, a five-star ecotourism site, as his wife, Ivanka Trump laid out in a podcast episode called "Ivanka Trump On Building The Authentic Life."
(SOUNDBITE OF ARCHIVED RECORDING)
IVANKA TRUMP: For me, it feels more like a challenge than anything else, the culmination of all of my experience in real estate, all of my travel, a lot of reflection on how I want to live.
SCHMITZ: In the podcast, Trump described both the Zvërnec beachfront property, as well as an island across from it, named Sazan, as part of a development project belonging to her and Kushner.
(SOUNDBITE OF BOAT MOTOR RUNNING)
SCHMITZ: Getting to Sazan from the mainland takes about an hour by boat. Today, tour operator Sharif Durmichai (ph), who insists I call him by his nickname Capitan Bacchi (ph), takes me to the uninhabited island.
SHARIF DURMICHAI: (Non-English language spoken).
SCHMITZ: When we get there, we hike up a trail past foxholes. Signs along the trail warn passersby to stick to the trail lest they step on a landmine. For years, this island was a fortified military outpost. I ask him what he thinks about Trump and Kushner's plans for the region and the protests that have followed them.
DURMICHAI: (Non-English language spoken).
SCHMITZ: "I work in tourism," he says, "and I'm from here. If you've seen how local developers, along with the local Mafia, have built this region, it's a massacre," he says. "They've destroyed so much of what is a beautiful coastline. I support the Kushner project because it's going to be ecologically sustainable, and it will set a good benchmark for future projects. We are a small country," Capitan Bacchi says, "and Kushner and Trump are very big names." Big names that'll be in the crosshairs of an incoming Democratic Congress back in the U.S.
JAMIE RASKIN: This is an individual in the Trump family who has essentially been two-timing on the American people.
SCHMITZ: Democratic Maryland Congressman Jamie Raskin will soon head the House Judiciary Committee and will have power to investigate and subpoena members of the president's family. He says he'll be focusing on Jared Kushner, who he says has been splitting his time between foreign policy and making business deals on the side.
RASKIN: American foreign policy has never seen anything like that before. And in this, of course, he is only imitating his father-in-law, who declared that he got $2.2 billion on the side in his first year in office. That's more than $1 million an hour.
SCHMITZ: Congressman Raskin says the luxury resort project in Albania is one of many Kushner-backed deals that have prompted investigative inquiries from his committee, and there could be more as the new Congress is sworn in.
