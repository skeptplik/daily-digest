This is a round-up of some of the top headlines from and about Germany on November 2, 2026:
Praise and condemnation from Merz following protests
Chancellor Friedrich Merz is to tell a conference of local party leaders of his conservative Christian Democratic Union (CDU) that he saw a positive aspect to weekend youth protests, while condemning violence that occurred in their aftermath.
"I take above all a positive message from the weekend: The young generation is politically engaged and is concerned about its future," he is to say, according to a speech released beforehand to news agencies.
In the planned speech, Merz says the government is making efforts not to burden the younger generation with the costs of an aging society.
"That is why we are carrying out pension reform for young people, why we are concerned about our security, and why we are preparing our economy for the digital future," Merz's speech runs.
'No tolerance' for violence
However, the chancellor has harsh words for those who rioted and attacked police on the sidelines of the mostly peaceful protests.
"Violence has no place on our streets, and my government will show no tolerance in this regard," the speech says.
"Anyone who attacks police officers and emergency service personnel and loots shops is attacking our freedom, mocking society and the legitimate interests of young people," the chancellor emphasizes in the speech.
"The offenders will feel the full force of the law."
Tens of thousands of young people demonstrated across Germany over the weekend, mostly peacefully.
However, several of the protests were followed by sometimes severe riots, such as in Frankfurt, where stones and petrol bombs were thrown, shop windows were smashed and looting took place.
More than 100 police officers were injured.
Major metal and electrical union calls for thousands to strike
The trade union IG Metall, which represents workers in the metalworking, electrical, steel, automotive and industrial manufacturing sectors, has called on thousands of employees to walk out at more than 2,000 workplaces nationwide by the end of the week.
A number of companies are expected to be affected, including carmakers Opel and Rolls-Royce, consumer goods giant Procter & Gamble, e-mobility supplier Vitesco, Daimler Truck AG and precision engineering firm Paul Beier GmbH.
The union, Germany and Europe's largest, is calling for a 5% pay rise over a 12-month period, along with job and site security.
Employers have put forward an offer of a one-off payment next year and a pay rise of 1.3% in July 2028, citing Germany's economic woes and cost pressures on industry to justify not meeting the union's demand.
Riots on weekend raised questions of integration — education minister
Education Minister Karin Prien has said the violence that occurred in the aftermath of youth protests on Saturday evening was in part an indication that the integration of people of migrant background into German society could be improved.
Prien told broadcaster Deutschlandfunk said that the "for the most part peaceful demonstrations" had been followed by rioting by left-wing radicals "but also by others who probably come from migrant milieus."
She said that recognizing this fact was not about stirring up resentments, which she called "politically wrong," but about facing reality and getting a picture of why people resort to such means.
"And if they come from migrant milieus, we should be worried about it and look at the causes," said Prien, a member of Chancellor Friedrich Merz's conservative Christian Democrats (CDU).
She said one question was why integration succeeds less well in some groups than in others.
"And it's not about the question of a passport. It's about the question: Why have people not been integrated into our society and what can be done so it works better in future," she said.
Her comments came despite the fact that authorities in Frankfurt, which saw the worst of the violence, said that most of the people they arrested in connection with rioting over the weekend were German citizens.
Interior minister blames 'migrant anarchists'
Prien's remarks came after archconservative Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt of the Bavarian Christian Social Union (CSU) said the "peaceful demonstrations were hijacked toward the evening by violence-prone left-wing groups and migrant anarchists," without offering evidence for who was behind the violent turn in the demonstrations.
Dobrindt's comment was, however, called into question by Defense Minister Boris Pistorius of the center-left Social Democrats (SPD), the junior partner in Germany's federal coalition government.
"What are migrant groups, then? Are these people living in Germany in the third generation who are German nationals? Where do we stop differentiating here? I think this is going in the wrong direction," he said.
Pistorius said, however, that whoever had been responsible for the rioting had to be punished for it.
His intervention comes as the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) continues, without evidence, to blame migration for most of the country's problems, with Dobrindt seeming to adopt some of the populist party's anti-migrant rhetoric.
Accused in deadly car ramming in Leipzig goes on trial
A 34-year-old man accused of having killed two people while racing with his car through a pedestrian zone in the eastern city of Leipzig in May is going on trial on Monday.
The accused has also been charged with 85 counts of attempted murder.
State prosecutors argue that the man planned the attack with the intent of killing people with his car, which reached speeds of up to 90 kmh (56 mph) during the rampage.
A 63-year-old woman and a 77-year-old man were killed in the incident.
The accused is said to have checked into a psychiatric clinic of his own free will a month before the attack and to have been discharged at his own wish a few days prior to it.
However, a psychiatric assessment has found no indications of diminished capacity on his part.
The trial has been scheduled to last until July of next year.
Germany's gas storage level well below target — AGSI
German natural gas storage facilities are currently only around 61.2% full, according to figures from a European platform that monitors storage levels. AGSI.
That falls well short of the 70% targeted by Sunday.
However, a spokeswoman for Economy Minister Katherina Reiche said there was no cause for concern.
"Germany's gas supply is secure for the coming winter, even with the current storage levels," she said.
"Based on the current assessment, we expect Germany to get through the winter in good shape. Security of supply depends not only on storage levels, but also on actual gas withdrawals, ongoing gas imports and additional precautionary measures," the spokeswoman said.
But Sebastian Heinermann,  Sebastian Heinermann, managing director of the Initiative Energien Speichern (Energy Storage Initiative), a mouthpiece for the sector, said the current storage levels were a "serious warning."
"Germany is entering the heating season with smaller safety reserves. This increases the vulnerability of the gas supply, particularly in the event of exceptionally low temperatures or additional disruptions to gas imports," Heinermann said.
The Economy Ministry has said the war in Iran, which has brought a partial blockage of the Strait of Hormuz, a vital transport route for global gas and oil, was one factor behind the current low storage level.
However, it says Germany now has more options to access additional gas than was the case in 2022, when the gas supply from Russia was cut off amid Moscow's launch of its invasion of Ukraine.
AI on the agenda at German-British talks on military cooperation
The German and British defense ministers are also expected to focus on artificial intelligence as a field for enhanced military cooperation at talks on Monday.
"By jointly testing, developing and, finally, introducing AI in our armed forces, we want to deepen our cooperation and keep pace with those who want to hurt us," UK Defense Minister Wes Streeting wrote in the daily Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung ahead of talks with his German counterpart, Boris Pistorius.
"Our opponents' readiness to harm us is not new. What is new is their abilities to do so. This demands an answer from all of us in Europe," he wrote.
Representatives from government and industry from both countries are to meet in the spring of next year to push ahead with the implementation of these plans, he wrote.
Two years ago, Germany and the United Kingdom agreed to cooperate more closely on military matters in the so-called Trinity House Agreement.
A year later, both countries announced that they would be focusing more on cyber defense as part of this cooperation.
Government to open AI-focused Digital Summit in Berlin
A two-day conference in Berlin that will look at ways of enhancing Germany's digital infrastructure, particularly in the field of artificial intelligence (AI), is to be opened by the minister for digital affairs, Karsten Wildberger, on Monday morning.
More than 1,000 guests, including digital ministers from Europe and Canada, are expected to attend the summit, which this year will focus largely on the uses and safety of AI and new developments in the field.
Chancellor Friedrich Merz is scheduled to hold a speech in the afternoon and to meet with a robot from the German company Neura Robotics.
The company develops robots that can see, hear and feel and are potentially utilizable in both industries and households.
The event is being held for the 19th time since its inception in 2006 as the National IT Summit. It did not take place in 2021, an election year in Germany.
