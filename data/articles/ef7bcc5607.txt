The Food and Drug Administration approved a Bayer drug to reduce recurrence of stroke, making available the first in a novel class of medications that hold the promise of preventing blood clots more safely, STAT notes. Many blood thinners are not used in this setting due to the increased risk of bleeding, but the drug, which will be sold under the brand name Sunharbo, uses a mechanism that is thought to stop the formation of bad clots without increasing the risk of bleeding. Experts say the data showed a significant advance in an area that has seen little innovation over the past few decades. Secondary strokes are estimated to affect about 20% of people who have had a first stroke and can cause severe disability or death.
The FDA warned that GLP-1 drugs carry a potential risk for a rare but serious eye condition and required pharmaceutical companies to inform patients and doctors of the risk, STAT writes. The condition, called non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, or NAION, is sometimes referred to as an “eye stroke” and is caused by insufficient blood flow to the optic nerve. It typically causes sudden vision loss and, in severe cases, can lead to blindness. There are currently no proven treatments for the condition. The FDA said it started looking into this risk after researchers in 2024 published an observational study linking Ozempic and Wegovy to the eye condition.
