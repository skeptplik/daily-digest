Medical technology company Boston Scientific is sending data breach notices to individuals affected by a cyberattack on the firm.
According to a notification sample Boston Scientific shared with U.S. authorities, the hacker used social engineering in a voice phishing (vishing) attack to gain access to the company's cloud infrastructure.
The attack exposed cloud-based applications and cloud-hosted data between May 7 and 1.
The company became aware of the unauthorized access on May 12, and the investigation determined that a data breach occurred on November 5.
Individuals in multiple U.S. states have been impacted, exposing personal details and medical information. According to the Texas Attorney General breach portal, the incident exposed information from 117,789 people, which included:
- Full name
- Physical address
- Social Security Number (SSN)
- Government-issued ID number (e.g., passport, state ID card)
- Medical information
- Health insurance information
- Date of birth
The letter sent to impacted individuals specifies that patient medical records and data from remote monitoring systems, connected medical devices, and clinical case management or workflow applications were not exposed.
Boston Scientific is a Massachusetts-based medical device maker whose products include stents, catheters, pacemakers, defibrillators, and endoscopes.
It is one of the world’s largest medical device manufacturers, with more than 59,000 employees, 13 manufacturing facilities, and a presence in 127 countries. In 2025, Boston Scientific reported more than $20 billion in revenue.
The company said there was no disruption to manufacturing, ordering, distribution, or service to patients and healthcare providers as a result of this incident.
It also said the incident had no impact on operations, performance, remote monitoring, or the safety of its products and medical devices.
Boston Scientific says it contained the incident, investigated with outside cybersecurity experts, and notified law enforcement. The notification does not offer free credit monitoring to impacted individuals but recommends using free credit reports and considering placing fraud alerts and credit freezes.
Second breach in 2026
The breach is the second cybersecurity incident this year. On August 25, Boston Scientific disclosed a cyberattack that caused network outages and a global operational disruption for the company, including to order processing and shipping.
CrowdStrike’s investigation determined that the attacker breached an internet-facing network device and pivoted to a limited part of the on-premises IT environment. However, the investigators found no evidence that the attackers compromised cloud applications.
BleepingComputer contacted Boston Scientific to learn more about the most recent incident, and a spokesperson confirmed that it is unrelated to the August 2026 intrusion.
The spokesperson declined to share additional details.
