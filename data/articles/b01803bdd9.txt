Former White House press secretary Katie Zacharia investigated by California state bar
Watchdog group filed complaint saying Zacharia misrepresented herself as a lawyer
California's state bar is investigating former White House press secretary Katie Zacharia after a complaint she represented herself as a lawyer without having ever passed the bar exam.
The State Bar of California confirmed its investigation into Zacharia, who was removed from her role as press secretary after a rocky five days, in a letter to a watchdog group published Thursday.
In a statement on Thursday, the state bar declined to comment further but noted the existence of an investigation doesn't mean "a non-licensee" has practised law without authorization.
"Our investigations are confidential, so we cannot share anything more, regardless of whether or not a complainant has made their own disclosure," its email read.
The watchdog group Campaign for Accountability filed a complaint last month, asking the state bar to investigate whether Zacharia broke the law by describing herself as a lawyer in interviews.
Zacharia graduated from Pepperdine University's law school in California in 2011, but was never admitted to the bar.
"Zacharia may have thought she was just playing a lawyer on TV, but by repeatedly holding herself out as an attorney on news programs, she was pretending to have authority she lacks," Campaign for Accountability executive director Michelle Kuppersmith said in a statement.
The organization published a response from the state bar on Thursday. In that letter, the bar confirmed the complaint had been "forwarded for further investigation" and would be assigned to an investigator and legal adviser.
Under the state's business code, anyone "holding himself or herself out as practicing or entitled to practice law" without being an active licensee of the bar is guilty of a misdemeanor. The violation is punishable by up to a year in county jail, a fine of $1,000 or both."
Katie Zacharia listens to U.S. President Donald Trump outside the White House after her only briefing as press secretary last month. (Kevin Lamarque/Reuters)
U.S. President Donald Trump announced Zacharia's appointment as press secretary in October, following Karoline Leavitt's resignation from the role in August.
Zacharia held only one, shaky briefing before she was removed from her role, making her the shortest-serving White House press secretary in history.
She had also faced a flurry of headlines questioning whether she had misrepresented herself as a lawyer when she spoke as a commentator on television.
In an October interview, she said she was preparing to take the California bar exam at a time when she had just gotten married and was preparing to move to China to join her new husband.
Zacharia said it was appropriate to use the title when she was offering legal analysis and said she had worked as a lawyer in China. U.S. President Donald Trump had also described her as "an attorney."
