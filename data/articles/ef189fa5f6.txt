Strikes at car makers and other manufacturing in full swing
A massive strike is underway in Germany hitting major car brands another manufacturing factors. Metal and electrical workers are demanding better pay ahead of upcoming wage talks later this week. The strike has been initiated by IG Metall, Germany’s biggest and most powerful union.
Famous car brands Mercedes and BMW were affected as was an Opel plant, which is part of the international Stellantis group.
The strikes began on Friday and are due to continue until Tuesday. Wage negotiations are then set to continue on Wednesday with another round of collective bargaining for some 3.7 million workers across the sector.
The union is demanding a pay rise of 5% over one year while the employers association, Gesamtmetall, has offered a one-time payment and a pay increase of 1.3%.
The tough struggle over wages comes as especially the German car industry is facing serious headwinds because of higher energy prices, US tariffs and mounting Chinese competition.
Report claims Russia 'could' soon launch nuclear-armed satellite
A German investigative media report published on Monday claims that Russia would be able to launch a nuclear-armed satellite, called Sputnik-S, into orbit within the space of about 60 days if it wished.
The report from public broadcasters WDR, NDR and the Süddeutsche Zeitung newspaper, cites a warning from German and other western intelligence agencies that it says was circulated at the start of November.
It said that a launch "could" take place by December, without quoting the document or clarifying whether it was talking only about a Russian capability, or a more firm intention.
The report said the satellite itself had been built several years ago and was being stored in the Moscow area.
German authorities did not respond to requests for comment on the record while the Russian Embassy in Berlin said the claims were part of a "propaganda" campaign.
The report matches longstanding warnings, mainly but not exclusively from the US, that Russia could be preparing a nuclear strike in Earth orbit designed to take out a large number of satellites.
This could have a severe impact on intelligence, communications, logisticts, navigation and other key services.
Russia's war in Ukraine, and the Ukrainian military's reliance on the Starlink satellite internet service, has provided an additional reminder of the strategic significance of satellites in orbit.
German exports to Africa see second year of growth
German exports to Africa have reached a new record. That’s according to a fresh study just as the country's president embarks on a trip to several African countries.
Exports are expected to rise by 6% in 2026 to reach about 30 billion euros ($33bn).
What’s behind the growing demand are especially orders from South Africa, Egypt, Tunisia, Nigeria, Angola and Kenya — countries where inflation has been dropping and exchange rates are more stable.
The biggest single export at the moment is a high-speed train network to Egypt. Other main exports include electrical equipment, optical instruments, plastics and pharmaceuticals, as well as food products, including dairy goods, cereals and baked goods.
The data is being reported by the Reuters news agency, based on the government-owned agency Germany Trade & Invest (GTAI), which promotes foreign trade and investment in Germany.
In 2025, exports to Africa had risen by 8% while in 2024 it had gone down.
Germany criticises oil states over failure to adopt climate resolution
The Germangovernment has blamed oil and gas-producing nations for the COP climate conference ending without an agreement.
"The oil and gas industry is still dragging its feet," German Environment Minister Carsten Schneider said after the UN climate talks had failed on Sunday. "Some oil-producing nations realize that the situation is becoming serious for them — that is why they are blocking it so vehemently," saidSchneider.
Almost two days into overtime, the COP31 climate talks in Turkey ended on Sunday as many countries, including the EU, rejected a final declaration.
Major sticking points at the summit included a commitment to accelerate global electrification and whether or not to explicitly mention plans to phase out fossil fuels. The suggested final declaration put forward by the COP presidency did not mention fossil fuels and according to critics would have undermined the shift away from fossil fuels.
"I understand that this poses a threat to the business model of those countries that sell us oil and gas. But electrification will make the world a more peaceful and stable place," Schneider said.
The inability to agree on a final declaration comes as climate change is increasingly linked to worsening heatwaves, floods and other natural catastrophes.
German President Steinmeier starts Africa trip
German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier begins a five-day trip to Africa that will take him to Ivory Coast, Ghana and Kenya with a focus on economic cooperation and the training of skilled workers.
He begins in Abidjan, where he is due to meet Ivorian President Alassane Ouattara. Steinmeier will also visit a vocational college and the International Academy for Counter-Terrorism before travelling on to Ghana.
In the capital Accra on Tuesday, he is set to open the German-African Business Summit, which according to its website, "brings together senior business executives, policymakers, investors, and industry leaders from across Germany and Africa."
His final stop will be Kenya, where he will meet President William Ruto in Nairobi on Wednesday.
The German president is traveling with a German business delegation and wants to encourage closer economic ties and greater investment in Africa.
The trip is his 11th to sub-Saharan Africa and his final one as president as his term in office due to end next March.
Patient suspected of starting fire in Berlin hospital
Police are investigating a suspected case of aggravated arson after a fire broke out at a hospital in Berlin.
Investigators believe a patient set fire to his bed in a room on the first floor of one of the hospital buildings in the Lichtenberg district on Sunday evening.
One nurse and the suspected arsonist suffered smoke inhalation and had to be treated in hospital. Overall, 15 people were injured with another 13 suffering from minor injuries.
While firefighters managed to put out the flames, smoke spread to other parts of the hospital which then had to be evacuated.
Widespread strikes expected across Germany
The country's largest and most powerful union, the IG Metall, has called for a strike this Monday over an ongoing pay dispute.
The union has called on some 23,000 workers to down tools in as many as 19 factories in northern Germany. Among the companies affected likely to be impacted are Siemens, Mercedes Benz, and shipyard Meyer Werft.
At a similar strike on Friday, more than 12,000 workers had taken part.
IG Metall hopes that the strikes will increase pressure on employers to yield to its demands, which include a 5% wage increase.
Employers have so far offered a one-off payment next year and a 1.3% wage increase from July 2028.
