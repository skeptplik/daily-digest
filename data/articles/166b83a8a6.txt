An online petition to ease the growing burden on student loan borrowers in Japan has amassed nearly 25,000 signatures as of Tuesday.
Fixed-rate interest rates on loans offered by the Japan Student Services Organization have shot up from 0.268% in November 2021 to their current maxed-out rate of 3%.
The petition calls for the government to immediately provide provisional subsidies to students paying loans and to gradually lower interest rates until funding can be secured to eliminate the loans entirely.
“In some cases, student loan repayment can last for nearly 20 years, and there are research findings showing that the burden of repayment affects subsequent life plans, such as marriage and childbirth,” said Hirokazu Ouchi, one of the lead organizers of a group working to reduce financial burdens on students and a professor at Musashi University, during an appeal to the Democratic Reform Party on Nov. 17.
The group also seeks reform of the relief system for those who cannot afford to pay by raising the minimum income requirement for repayment (currently set at ¥3 million) and removing the maximum deferral period.
Students who take out loans in Japan are expected to pay interest rates set at the time of graduation rather than when they take out the loan. “As a result, a high interest rate that was not anticipated when students were in their first year of university is applied, and an increasing number of students are burdened with repayment amounts that far exceed their initial expectations,” Ouchi said in last week’s appeal.
Japan’s current 3% fixed-rate student loan interest rate falls well below the fixed 6.52% federal rate for U.S. undergraduates and the U.K.’s 4.1%, but exceeds South Korea’s rate of 1.7%. Post-graduation starting salaries in South Korea are on average 24.5% higher than in Japan, according to a Korea Employers Federation report of 2024 data published earlier this year.
South Korea’s student loan interest rate has also become a point of contention domestically, as some students have reportedly taken advantage of low interest rates to invest in financial markets and cryptocurrency instead of their education.
