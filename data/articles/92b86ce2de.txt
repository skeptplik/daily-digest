From a Paris Fashion Week toast to a New York City launch party, Dior and Harper’s Bazaar have been honoring the legacy of fashion photographer Richard Avedon these past few weeks. This series of intimate events has been tied to the launch of a new coffee-table book titled Richard Avedon: The Harper’s Bazaar Years, as well as a documentary film, Avedon. Now, the festivities have traveled to Los Angeles.
Avedon, who passed away in 2004 at the age of 81, was one of the most influential fashion photographers of the 20th century. In 1944, at just 21 years old, he started his career at Harper’s Bazaar under the guidance of then-editor-in-chief Carmel Snow, and he spent the next two-plus decades defining the creative vision of the publication.
Richard Avedon: The Harper’s Bazaar Years (available now) explores this artistic period by compiling rarely seen photographs, a never-before-published account from Avedon himself, a foreword by editor-in-chief Samira Nasr, and an essay by Bazaar contributing editor Derek C. Blasberg. The new documentary, meanwhile, offers a look through the Avedon archives from a different lens. It was directed by Academy Award–winning filmmaker Ron Howard (Apollo 13, A Beautiful Mind, The Da Vinci Code).
Focusing on the film, Dior and Blasberg hosted a private screening of Avedon at the William Morris Endeavor headquarters in L.A. Guests such as Emma Watson, Gia Coppola, Lauren Hutton, Devon Lee Carlson, and more gathered to watch the film before heading to the House of Dior in Beverly Hills for a cocktail reception.
