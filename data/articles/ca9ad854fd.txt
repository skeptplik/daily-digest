Nvidia has reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO and president following a unanimous vote by its Board of Directors.
"The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners, and customers," said chair of the board Harvey C. Jones.
"We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Company. We are grateful to Colette Kress for her outstanding service as interim CEO, in addition to her continuing responsibilities as Nvidia's chief financial officer."
In a press release, the firm said that during "the process of reaching its decision," the company's board "received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.
Jensen resigned from his leadership position on November 13 after reports alleged extramarital affairs involving a suspected Chinese spy and two employees.
"I am honoured to return to Nvidia to lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution," said Huang. "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment."
"I want to thank the Nvidia board for its confidence and deliberation through this process. I am especially grateful to Harvey Jones for his leadership as chair, and to Colette Kress and our executive staff for leading Nvidia during this period."
Huang continued: "I am also deeply thankful to our employees, shareholders, leaders across the industry and partners around the world for their extraordinary support. Above all, I am grateful for the love and support of my family. I can't wait to get back to work."
Nvidia clarified that it had formed a committee "comprised solely of independent directors to investigate" reports that Huang was "the target of a foreign intelligence effort."
It stated that the investigation will continue, and that Huang "fully supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating with it."
The tech company also formed a National Security and Export Control Committee, also comprised of independent directors, "to oversee trade compliance and national security risks."
A new chief compliance officer position has been established to "oversee compliance across Nvidia and report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee," it added.
"Nvidia will continue to cooperate with ongoing government investigations and inquiries." This includes an FBI investigation. Huang has also been summoned to testify before US Congress.
