Fri, 30 Oct, 7:15 pm, Final score, Cats 88, Bulldogs 20
Right after Sydney upset Geelong in the AFLW semifinals last weekend, a photo was taken that perfectly encapsulated the moment for the Swans.
In it, injured star Ally Morphett is crying while holding a beaming Lexi Hamilton.
"I mean, [at] first glance, I hated the photo," Morphett laughed to ABC Sport. "I thought I looked absolutely terrible. But there's so much meaning and emotion behind it.
"I just care so much about the girls … I was so proud of them once they won because it was just an unbelievable game. I think it was one of the best games of the year."
With the victory, the Swans booked a spot in their first preliminary women's final in head coach Colin O'Riordan's first year at the helm.
Some AFLW pundits had questioned whether the Swans could go deep in finals without Morphett, who did her ACL in round 8.
"It's very bittersweet to be watching on the sidelines because all you want to do is be out there," Morphett said, adding it had been "probably the most emotional year of my life".
"But I was just so incredibly happy for all the girls. I think the difference in the way we play this year compared to previous years is we do it for each other, rather than worrying about how you personally play."
The success has come while the club was repeatedly in headlines due to male players under a police investigation for allegations of a sexual assault and breaching club standards. The investigation is over and players will not be charged.
The saga has added extra emotional weight, Morphett said, but strengthened their bonds internally in the AFLW program.
"It's been a really tough year. Not just for our program, but also women amongst the whole organisation," Morphett said.
"When you go through adversity like that, it brings you closer together compared to other teams who don't really go through something like that and as significant as that.
"But I think vulnerability is such a massive strength, especially around strong women and so many of us, and that happening has brought us so much closer.
"It's just brought so much care amongst the group between Colin, all the staff and the players. And all together as a whole program, we just care so much about everyone.
"It's been a massive [emotional] roller coaster. As for the football side of things and how we're performing, our connection has gone onto the field and the way that we go about it."
With the rehab group not travelling to Melbourne, Morphett will now have the tough task of watching her side face North Melbourne from home.
When the pair faced each other in round eight this year, the two-time reigning premiers in the Kangaroos won by 51 points.
Morphett knows it will be a huge challenge for the Swans, but also knows finals footy is a different game.
"Yes they're nervous, but I think everyone's more geed up than nervous. It makes us hungry, the fact that they're going for a three-peat, and we want to be the ones to stop them," she said.
"If we played how we did last weekend against the Cats, I think that it is very achievable.
"Finals footy is very much grit and desire, and we aren't the number one pressure team in the comp for no reason. So I think … Bloods [Swans] footy is finals footy, really. So I'm really excited to watch it."
