Britain’s Prince William and Princess Catherine (Kate) has arrived in Mumbai for a three-day visit to India as part of the sixth edition of The Earthshot Prize. They will join 5,000 guests, including environmental innovators, business leaders, philanthropists and celebrities from across the world in Mumbai on Tuesday (November 17, 2026) to celebrate the prestigious environmental awards.
This marks the royal couples’ first joint overseas engagement since Ms. Kate announced her cancer diagnosis in 2024, also her first Earthshot Prize since 2022, when the ceremony was held in Boston, United States.
The couple’s return to India comes nearly a decade after their previous joint visit to the country in 2016, when they also travelled to Bhutan.
What is The Earthshot Prize?
The Earthshot Prize is a prestigious environmental award, a project that was launched by Prince William in 2020, and is supported by a global coalition of philanthropists and organisations.
It aims to encourage innovative solutions to climate change and other major environmental challenges such as air pollution, waste, ocean conservation and the loss of biodiversity. It also aims to recognise 50 winners over a 10-year period.
Every year, five winners are selected from 15 global finalists, with each winner receiving £1 million (approx. ₹12.5 crore) to encourage their work and turn bold environmental ideas into actual impact. In November 2026, the sixth edition of the award arrives in Mumbai, also the first time the ceremony is held in India.
Five categories of Earthshot Prize and 2026 finalists
The Earthshot Awards operate on the belief that human ingenuity, innovation and optimism can help repair the planet. Each year, 15 climate solutions are shortlisted across five categories known as Earthshots: Protect and Restore Nature, Clean Our Air, Revive Our Ocean, Build a Waste Free World, and Fix Our Climate.
Under the ‘Protect and Restore Nature’ category, Bhutan’s Princess Chimi Yangzom Wangchuck and Karma Tenzin, senior forestry officer, have been shortlisted for their work in protecting the Himalayan forests which help the water systems that sustain 1.6 billion people downstream across South Asia.
Sam Murray, CEO of ‘The Indigenous Alliance’, has been shortlisted for supporting rangers caring for 270 million hectares of Australian desert. Dr Jodi Hilty, president and chief scientist, and Chief Harlan Schilling of the Yellowstone to Yukon Conservation Initiative have been shortlisted for reconnecting 3,400 km of wilderness across Canada and the United States.
Under the ‘Clean Our Air’ category, Suman Mishra, managing director and CEO of Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, has been shortlisted for building affordable electric rickshaws.
Franco Rodríguez Viau, CEO and co-founder of Satellites on Fire (Argentina), has been shortlisted for using weather forecasts, cameras and satellite technology to protect 92 million hectares across 21 countries from wildfires.
Prof Engineer Bainomugisha, founder and lead of AirQo (Uganda), has been shortlisted for building affordable air-pollution monitors for African countries.
Under the ‘Build a Waste Free World’ category, Sonia Mendoza of the Mother Earth Foundation, Philippines, has been shortlisted for working with informal workers to remove single-use plastic in the vast archipelago before the waste reaches dumps, rivers or beaches.
Nicolas Thierry, Member of Parliament and lead spokesperson for PFAS-Free France, and Maria Pelletier, president of Generation futures, have been shortlisted for building a national, cross-party movement to ban toxic forever chemicals in consumer products and to make polluters pay for the clean-up.
Kim Mi-Hwa, chairperson of South Korea Zero Waste Network, has been shortlisted for driving environmental and economic revolution due to which South Korea now recycles 95 per cent of its food waste, up from just 2 per cent in the mid-90s.
In the ‘Revive Our Oceans’ category, Coral Pasisi, vice chair of Niue Ocean Wide Trust and co-architect of Ocean Conservation Commitments, has been nominated for her work in protecting the oceans, especially the vast Pacific region.
Ingrid Irigoyen, founder and managing director of ZEMBA, has been nominated for cleaning up the shipping industry. Jaume Collboni, Mayor of Barcelona, and Maria Buhigas, chief architect of the city of Barcelona, have been shortlisted for successfully preserving its beaches, restoring marine habitats and enhancing natural coastal protection.
In the ‘Fix Our Climate’ category, Bihal Brahmbhatt, executive director of Mahila Housing Trust, has been nominated for helping some of India’s most vulnerable communities tackle extreme heat. Thomas Njeru, CEO and co-founder of Pula, has also been shortlisted for using satellite and field data to provide affordable insurance to 24 million farmers across Africa.
Ingrid Reumert and Amanda Dasch of Orsted have been nominated for redefining their business from coal-intensive utilities to renewable energy.
India’s leadership in Earthshot mission
India plays a significant part in the Earthshot Prize’s mission, having produced more finalists and winners than any other country since its inception. Out of nine finalists so far, four have won the prize: Boomitra, Keyti, S4S Technologies and Takachar.
“India is at the forefront of climate solutions, and its innovations have immediate global application, especially when it comes to issues such as heat stress”, Earthshot Prize CEO Jason Knauf said, speaking with The Hindu
Observing that India is undergoing a “massive transformation” about renewable energy, he said it had an advantage over other countries to bring in sustainable climate solutions. He said there was no debate between economic progress and environmental protection anymore. “What is good for the environment is good for business, and Indian companies are showing that,” Mr. Knauf said, adding that the past Indian winners had been able to raise $800 million in commercial international investment after winning the Earthshot Prize.
“For a country that is on the frontline of the environmental issues despite having not caused them, people are more optimistic about their abilities to solve them, than many richer countries. That tells you something. We all have to learn from that example. We are happy that the Earthshot Prize is coming here to celebrate that Indian spirit for the whole world,” he added.
Mumbai event highlights
The awards ceremony will conclude a series of environmental initiatives and discussions scheduled from November 13 to 17, across Jaipur and Mumbai. The programme will focus on climate change, air quality, waste reduction, ocean conservation and nature restoration.
Hosted by Hindi filmmaker Karan Johar, the ceremony features prominent personalities including Sachin Tendulkar, Alia Bhatt, Rami Malek, Sonam Kapoor, and Simone Ashley, alongside musical performances by British pop band Take That, Shreya Ghoshal, Shashwat Sachdev, Wild Wild Women, and Merlin D’Souza.
The ceremony will stream globally on Prime Video, starting November 30 in over 40 languages.
