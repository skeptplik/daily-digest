Rahul Gandhi says India has become a 'side player' in world affairs, BJP hits back
Congress leader Rahul Gandhi told students at the University of Chicago that India has become a "side player" in global affairs, prompting Prime Minister Narendra Modi's ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) to respond sharply.
"India, by its size, naturally has a global voice and India should be actively involved in global conversations. But frankly, India has become a side player," Gandhi said in his interaction with students and faculty at the university, citing neighbor Pakistan's role in US-Iran talks.
He also questioned Prime Minister Narendra Modi's reliance on personal ties with leaders like Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin: "Friendship is not the point. Who gives a damn about friendship? Do you really think that geopolitics is going to work because the guy is your friend?"
When asked by a Pakistani participant about relations between the neighbors, Gandhi said talks could begin if Pakistan stopped backing terrorism. "And it is impossible for us to accept our innocent people being murdered and having a conversation with Pakistan," he said.
Reiterating allegations about electoral rolls, he also ruled out an opposition election boycott: "If we were to say that we are not going to participate in elections, Indian democracy would end that very instant."
BJP spokesperson CR Kesavan took to social media to write, "Rahul Gandhi, the LoP [Leader of Opposition], sounds like an eager cheerleader and a shameless propagandist for Pakistan and his reckless remarks expose how clueless Rahul Gandhi is about global geopolitics."

Cyclone Akvan weakens after landfall
Cyclone Akvan made landfall on India's eastern coast on Friday, bringing heavy rain and strong winds with no major damage reported so far, Reuters said. It is the third cyclone to hit the coast in under a month.
The storm crossed the north Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts near Gopalpur between 7:30 and 8:30 a.m. local time (0230 GMT and 0330 GMT), according to the India Meteorological Department (IMD). IMD said it had weakened to a cyclonic storm overnight and crossed with winds of 70-80 kph, gusting to 90 kph.
IMD said it weakened to a deep depression by 11:30 a.m. IST and is to develop into a depression in the next six hours, according to Reuters.
More than 300,000 people had been moved to relief camps in Odisha, local media reported. Odisha and Andhra Pradesh together evacuated hundreds of thousands.
"No damage or untoward incident has been reported so far," Odisha's Special Relief Commissioner Rajesh Prabhakar Patil told Reuters. Andhra Pradesh also reported minimal impact, disaster management official Prakhar Jain said.
Cyclone Murasu struck a fortnight ago, uprooting trees and electric poles, and Cyclone Arnab last month killed six people, Reuters said.
Pakistani minister in Delhi for SCO forum, first ministerial visit since 2023
Pakistan's Climate Change Minister Musadik Malik arrived in New Delhi on Friday for a Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) cultural forum.
It is the first visit to India by a Pakistani federal minister since Bilawal Bhutto Zardari attended the SCO meeting in Goa in May 2023, and the first since last year's conflict between the two countries.
Prime Minister Narendra Modi is set to inaugurate the Civilizational Dialogue Forum, which runs from November 20 to 22. India invited Pakistan's culture minister in October, but Islamabad sent Malik instead.
Pakistan's Foreign Office said he would present the country's views on dialogue among civilizations and meet other SCO members on the sidelines, local media reported.
Malik said the visit is not a bilateral negotiation, according to NDTV. No breakthrough in frigid relations is expected.
The visit comes after the four-day India-Pakistan conflict of 2025, set off by the Pahalgam attack, which killed at least 70 people on both sides, AFP stated. It also comes two days after Indian forces killed Lashkar-e-Taiba commander Lateef Bhat.
Opposition leaders criticized the government's move to host Malik.
"BJP always says that talks and terrorism cannot happen at the same time. But now, a Pakistani minister is visiting India for the SCO meeting," said Samajwadi Party leader Fakhrul Hasan Chand, NDTV reported.
Congress Member of Parliament Pramod Tiwari also recalled Malik's earlier remarks on the Indus Waters Treaty, calling his language that of a "terrorist," according to NDTV.
Earlier this year, Malik, referring to the Indus Waters Treaty suspension after last year's Pahalgam terror attack, had warned against "water aggression," pressing India to respect the 1960 treaty.
"Whoever touches our water, their hands would be cut off," he said at the time.
US religious freedom panel says India targeted Muslims in voter roll revision
The US Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF) said Thursday that India has escalated its targeting of religious minorities during a voter list revision that struck off tens of millions of names, Reuters reported.
At 130 million names removed, it is the largest deletion in India's history, local media reported.
"The Indian government is targeting Muslim citizens by removing them from voter rolls, calling them 'foreigners,' and now illegally deporting them," USCIRF chair Asif Mahmood said in a statement.
The revision has become entangled with a separate drive to identify and expel suspected illegal immigrants, even though removal from the rolls does not itself establish that someone is a foreigner.
USCIRF commissioner Gene Mills urged the State Department to designate India a "country of particular concern."
The panel is a bipartisan US government body whose recommendations are not binding, and Washington has not acted on its recent ones on India.
India's Election Commission is overseeing a nationwide re-verification of the electoral rolls requiring voters to prove their eligibility to remain on them, removing duplicate, deceased, shifted and otherwise ineligible voters.
But critics say the ECI has gone far beyond that remit.
The Indian Express reported that more than 130 million names were removed from electoral rolls across the country. Before this, India had some 970 million eligible voters.
For scale, Germany has about 60 million eligible voters in total.
India's Foreign Ministry has cast USCIRF as biased, and New Delhi has called such criticism interference in its internal affairs.
