Sony's revealed the second wave of games coming to PlayStation Plus Premium and Extra this month, and there are some genuine big-name attractions, led by Hideo Kojima's futuristic delivery man showstopper Death Stranding 2: On the Beach.
Here's the new games coming PlayStation Plus Extra and Premium in November:
- Hell Let Loose (PS5 - available from 11th November)
- Death Stranding 2: On the Beach (PS5 - available from 17th November)
- Atomfall (PS5, PS4 - available from 17th November)
- Dragon Quest 3 HD-2D Remake (PS5 - available from 17th November)
- Like a Dragon: Pirate Yakuza in Hawaii (PS5, PS4 - available from 24th November)
- Trepang2 (PS5 - available from 24th November)
- MotoGP 26 (PS5 - available from 24th November)
- Cricket 26 (PS5, PS4 - available from 24th November)
And here are a couple of games coming only to PlayStation Plus Premium in November:
- Soul Reaver 2 (PS5, PS4 - available from 17th November)
- Freedom Wars Remastered (PS5, PS4 - available from 17th November)
There's a lot to recommend in that list. Death Stranding 2 was one of the standout games of 2025. "A busier, louder, and more emotionally resplendent take on this singular hiking sim," Lewis Gordon wrote in Eurogamer's Death Stranding 2 review.
Like a Dragon: Pirate Yakuza in Hawaii also shone brightly last year, as ridiculous as it was. "A raucous, absurd spin-off that manages to still feel like a first-rate Yakuza game despite the leftfield setting and delightfully unhinged plot," wrote Matt Elliott in Eurogamer's Like a Dragon: Pirate Yakuza in Hawaii review.
Meanwhile, Atomfall - Rebellion's quirky British take on a Fallout-like post-apocalyptic idea - was awarded three out of five stars. "If you can get over a difficult start and fancy a lean take on the survival genre, Atomfall delivers an intriguing tale worth discovering," Tom Orry wrote in Eurogamer's Atomfall review. It's presumably been significantly patched and smoothed since then.
The Dragon Quest 3 HD-2D Remake also scored three out of five stars. "Some excellent enhancements make this the ultimate version of Dragon Quest 3, but it could still do more to make it wholly welcoming to newcomers," Daniella Lucas wrote in Eurogamer's Dragon Quest 3 HD-2D review.
We didn't review Hell Let Loose, but the popular World War 2 shooter would go on to amass a serious following and spawn a follow-up game in Hell Let Loose: Vietnam recently, which we did review. "The lavish jungles, rigid command structure, and emphasis on communication make Hell Let Loose: Vietnam a fascinating war simulator when it works. But its many variables can all too easily devolve into a miserable snipe-fest," Rick Lane wrote.
There's plenty to be getting on with, then.
Sony also announced in the PlayStation Blog post it's retiring the PS Plus app on PC from 10th December. Why? Because nobody really uses it.
"With a very small number of eligible PlayStation Plus Premium members (less than 0.4 percent) using the PlayStation Plus app on PC, making this change allows us to focus on enhancing high-quality experiences that more of our members want to use," Sony wrote. "As a result, the PlayStation Plus cloud streaming app on PC will no longer be available."
