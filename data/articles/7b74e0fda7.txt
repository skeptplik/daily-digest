An exhibition exploring the history of drugs, the return of boy band Take That, and this year's most anticipated documentary — here's what to look out for this week.
It's finally here — the week where You Can See Everything.
And by that we of course mean Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim's highly-anticipated documentary about convicted American fraudster, Elizabeth Holmes.
Its teaser trailer has already spawned a thousand memes and the delayed European release dates have made it near-impossible to avoid any spoilers; whispers from the hallowed halls of Letterboxd hinting at a three-hour runtime full of unexpected twists and turns.
While it comes out on the 20 November in the United Kingdom and Ireland, those in France and other European territories will unfortunately have to wait until the end of December. But hey, you can still take a peek at our film critic David Mouriquand's (spoiler-free) review this Friday.
Another upcoming highlight is the opening of an exhibition on mind-altering substances at Paris' Musée de l'Homme. Through delving into the origins, mechanisms and historical context of various drugs, it de-stigmatises and re-evaluates their meaning within culture and society.
Keep scrolling to find out more about what else is coming up.
Exhibitions
Cosimo I de' Medici: Art and Dynasty
When: 18 November 2026 - 21 March 2027
Where: The Wallace Collection (London, United Kingdom)
Centred around five key historical objects, The Wallace Collection's latest exhibition dives into the world of Cosimo I de' Medici — the first Grand Duke of Tuscany. From expanding the Palazzo Pitti in Florence, to commissioning masterful Mannerist painters like Agnolo Bronzino, Cosimo I became a key figure within Italian culture for using art and craftsmanship to secure his political powers. The works on display demonstrate this, including a 70 centimetre-long Maiolica wine cooler from 1574 and lion-headed ceremonial short sword. Find out more here.
Drugs
When: 18 November 2026 - 6 June 2027
Where: Musée de l'Homme (Paris, France)
The word drugs has become synonymous with taboo, often conjuring stigmatised pre-conceptions about those that use them recreationally. But the truth behind psychoactive substances is far more nuanced and complex than that — something the Musée de l'Homme untangles in its fascinating and mind-altering new exhibition. From the everyday drugs we consume like caffeine, to vintage vials of opium and botanical specimens, the displays collectively explore the sociological, psychological and economic impact of drugs throughout history, re-evaluating and re-educating in the process.
Bonus highlight: Hosted by Institut Lumière director Thierry Frémaux, 'Un Soir au Temps des Lumières' is a live event celebrating the invention of Cinématographe in Lyon, France. It takes place on the 19 November at 8pm at the Théâtre Antoine Riboud.
Movies
You Can See Everything
Where: Cinemas
When: 20 November
Few documentaries have generated as much buzz as You Can See Everything, which premiered at the Telluride Film Festival in September and is finally getting its release across Europe. And phew! Because avoiding spoilers has not been easy.
Directed by Lance Oppenheim (Primetime) and comedy genius Nathan Fielder (The Curse, The Rehearsal), it's an almost 3 hour-long deep dive into the world of Elizabeth Holmes, filmed in the weeks leading up to her prison sentence for defrauding investors in her blood-testing stat-up, Theranos. But it's also so much more than that — a psychologically tense and darkly comic exploration of perception, performance, media and truth that cleverly subverts your expectations.
Bonus highlight: Mahershala Ali stars as a devoutly religious hitman on a mission to protect his family in Your Mother Your Mother your Mother, released in the UK on 20 November.
TV series
Yellowjackets (Season 4)
Where: Paramount+
When: 20 November
The Emmy-nominated thriller about a high school girls’ soccer team that survived a 1996 plane crash is back for a fourth and final series. Alternating between past and present, and featuring an all-star cast including Juliette Lewis, Melanie Lynskey and Christina Ricci, the upcoming episodes will conclude the teenager's rescue and difficult reintegration into society. Meanwhile, the horrifying impact of their shared ordeal continues to stir conflict 25 years later, as the return of an old flame sparks even more paranoia. New cast members include Molly Ringwald and Eric Stoltz.
Bonus highlight: The Altruists, Netflix's new biographical drama about the rise and fall of cryptocurrency exchange FTX, premieres on 19 November.
Music
Take That: Dreamers
When: 20 November
While we can't confirm that they're back for good, they are back for now! Indeed, British boy band Take That are releasing their 10th studio album this week, 'Dreamers'. Recorded by three of the original five-piece, Gary Barlow, Mark Owen and Howard Donald, it's a return to the band's infectious brand of fizzy, feel-good pop. Covering themes of love and hope, it's exactly the kind of live, laugh, love sounds needed for combatting end-of-year comparisons and burn out.
Bonus highlight: For a uniquely diverse and cinematically retro sound, look no further than London-based band Los Bitchos, whose third album 'Los Bitchos & Friends Vol 1' is out now.
