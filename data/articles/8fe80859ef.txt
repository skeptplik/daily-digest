Opposition lawmakers in Sri Lanka have accused the government of preventing a parliamentary debate on the release of jailed former Prime Minister of Pakistan Imran Khan.
Last week, the state-run Daily News reported that the Sri Lankan Parliament was scheduled to hold an adjournment debate on November 21, 2026, on a motion moved by Opposition MP Ajith P. Perera, from the main Opposition party Samagi Jana Balawegaya (SJB or United People’s Power), calling for Mr. Khan’s release on humanitarian grounds. The debate did not take place. Mr. Perera expressed disappointment and said it was “regrettable that the motion has been rejected even before being debated.”
Defending the government’s position, Deputy Minister of Foreign Affairs Arun Hemachandra said the government did not wish to entertain matters that could create “diplomatic tensions” or affect ties with Sri Lanka’s bilateral partners. However, Mr. Hemachandra observed that Mr. Perera was free to express his personal views on the matter during the time allocated to him in Parliament.
Addressing Parliament on Monday (November 23, 2026), SJB MP and Leader of the Sri Lanka Muslim Congress Rauff Hakeem attacked the government for pursuing a “transactional” foreign policy, rather than one based on principles. Pakistan, he said, had been “a longstanding friend” of Sri Lanka, supporting the island during difficult times. He recalled Pakistan’s assistance during the pandemic and Mr. Khan’s crucial intervention in urging the Gotabaya Rajapaksa government to end its mandatory cremations policy during the pandemic that had caused enormous distress among the island’s Muslim community.
Sri Lanka and Pakistan have historically maintained close bilateral ties, regardless of the party in power in either country. Mr. Khan visited Colombo in February 2021, when he was Prime Minister. Gotabaya Rajapaksa was President of Sri Lanka at the time. Mr. Khan’s two-day visit was received very well by the local media as well as cricketing fans on the island, known for its passion for the game.
Further, Mr. Hakeem slammed the government’s position on the Palestine issue, contending that statements of support at international fora meant little without substantive domestic action. Sri Lanka’s ruling Janatha Vimukthi Peramuna [JVP or People’s Liberation Front] has historically supported the Palestinian cause. After its ascent to power in 2024, the National People’s Power coalition that the JVP leads has maintained ties with Israel and has continued to send Sri Lankan workers to Israel. In an interview to The Hindu in May 2026, Deputy Minister Mr. Hemachandra said: “We separated our Free Palestine stand and our diplomatic relationship with Israel as two different things. I think any country should have that choice. And when we take the opportunity to send workers to Israel, that does not change our stand on Palestine.”
