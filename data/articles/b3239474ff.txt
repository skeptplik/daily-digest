The Princess of Wales opens up about the physical and emotional impact of her cancer diagnosis in a deeply personal BBC documentary. Kate also reflects on her recovery, finding hope amid uncertainty and learning to live with a new perspective on life.
Catherine, Princess of Wales, has spoken candidly about how cancer changed her, both physically and emotionally, in a new documentary about her journey through illness and recovery.
"Cancer does change you. It changes your body, it changes you physically, but there's a much, much deeper part to what a cancer diagnosis shifts in you," Kate says in the trailer for the film, titled Heal.
The 30-minute documentary follows the Princess as she reflects on her own experience and meets other people facing serious health challenges.
Kate also discusses the process of finding her way back to everyday life after treatment, describing healing as a journey. "How to reconnect with the things you once knew and how to find a sense of hope and joy alongside uncertainty," she says.
Three Peaks Challenge became her own way of ringing the bell
The documentary follows Kate as she takes on the National Three Peaks Challenge, climbing the highest mountains in Scotland, England and Wales within 24 hours.
She completed the challenge in June 2026 in support of The Royal Marsden Cancer Charity, the organisation linked to the London hospital where she received treatment.
The film includes footage of Kate embracing her husband, Prince William, and their three children, George, Charlotte and Louis, after completing the demanding challenge. In another emotional moment, Kate visits cancer patients and watches a survivor ring the traditional bell marking the end of treatment.
The Princess reveals that she did not ring the bell herself when she was told she was in remission.
Her brother James suggests that completing the Three Peaks Challenge may have become her own way of marking the milestone.
Kate's cancer diagnosis and recovery
Kate announced in March 2024 that she had been diagnosed with cancer following major abdominal surgery. She subsequently underwent preventative chemotherapy, completing her treatment in September 2024.
In January 2025, she announced that her cancer was in remission, while emphasising that recovery would take time.
The Princess has not disclosed the type of cancer she was diagnosed with.
Earlier this month, she also spoke about the challenges of recovery in an interview with Country Life magazine, whose latest edition was guest-edited by Prince William.
"I was told in hospital that my recovery would not be linear. There will be good and bad days, that it will take time," she said.
Kate also explained how spending time in nature had helped her through the process, describing it as a sanctuary where she could reconnect with herself.
"I think that's because nature offers something unique. It doesn't ask anything of us. It doesn't judge us or expect us to perform. It simply allows us to be," she said.
Documentary aims to help others facing serious illness
Through Heal, the Princess hopes to encourage a more holistic approach to cancer care, taking into account not only physical health but also emotional and psychological wellbeing.
"It's clear we're not meant to carry life's challenges alone," she says in the trailer. "Things can renew and grow. Often it is through pain that we realise there's a greater beauty that lies beyond that."
Kensington Palace said the documentary explores the profound impact serious illness can have on patients and those around them, drawing on Kate's personal experience and the stories of others navigating health challenges.
All proceeds from the documentary's distribution will go to The Royal Marsden Cancer Charity, supporting people living with and beyond cancer. The charity, established in 2002, continues a tradition of philanthropic support for The Royal Marsden, the specialist cancer hospital founded by Dr William Marsden in 1851.
Heal will be broadcast on BBC One and BBC iPlayer on 29 November, with international screenings to follow in the United States, Australia and Canada.
