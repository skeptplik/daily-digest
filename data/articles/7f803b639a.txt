F1 Las Vegas Grand Prix: Oscar Piastri finishes third, Max Verstappen wins
Oscar Piastri has produced his finest performance of the 2026 season, claiming a sensational podium in Las Vegas after starting 17th on the grid.
Max Verstappen proved why he is one of the finest Formula 1 drivers of any era, winning a thrilling grand prix that will rank as one of the best this season. There were 352 overtakes on track, with 35 of those passes for the lead of the race.
Piastri took a 10-place grid penalty for using a new internal combustion engine, and was driving a McLaren described by team CEO Zak Brown as a "sitting duck" on the long straights, due to the car's lack of top speed.
But Piastri picked his way through the field across an action-packed 50 laps, crossing the line third behind Verstappen and McLaren teammate Lando Norris.
Piastri, who is notoriously cool under pressure, had an uncharacteristic snap at his team when they stressed the importance of being careful with his teammate Norris.
With a few laps remaining, and both drivers battling in a tightly packed top-five, Piastri was told he needed to be careful if he was battling his teammate, but Piastri was having none of it.
"Stop telling me to f****** be careful," he snapped.
Piastri would overtake Norris a few laps later, but his world champion teammate re-took the position on lap 48 of 50.
Neither McLaren driver looked set to finish on the podium, but the Mercedes duo of George Russell (off track) and Kimi Antonelli (energy deployment issue) both fell down the order.
"They say Las Vegas is the entertainment capital of the world, and I think that was pretty entertaining," Piastri said post-race.
"Everyone was just in such a big pack; it was bizarre to be a part of."
Verstappen wins after 35 lead changes
In 2025, Max Verstappen won the Las Vegas Grand Prix when there were 17 overtakes on track during the entire race.
In 2026, he won when there were 35 overtakes on track, just for the lead.
Verstappen was at his masterful best on Sunday (AEDT), converting pole position into the 75th grand prix win of his career.
The long straights at the Las Vegas Strip circuit resulted this year with a very powerful slipstream, meaning the car behind at the end of the straight would be going much quicker.
Verstappen drove a near-perfect race, losing the lead at the end of the long back straight several times, only to then smartly deploy his electrical power to retake the lead just a few corners later.
George Russell, Kimi Antonelli and Lando Norris all overtook Verstappen for the lead at the end of the back straight at some point, only for the four-time world champion to snatch the lead straight back.
"Crazy. Really, really surreal and something I have never seen before in Formula 1 myself," Verstappen said post-race.
Verstappen was ever present in the lead, while several drivers battled to be the next challenger to the Dutchman's position.
Russell, who started second, was Verstappen's main threat throughout the race, but while running third behind his teammate Antonelli late in the race, locked his wheels and went off the track.
Antonelli was shaping up to be Verstappen's challenger on the final lap, but an energy deployment issue on the penultimate lap led to the new world champion falling behind Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri. Antonelli would finish fourth.
Isack Hadjar, Verstappen's teammate, looked destined to be a contender as well. However, his race came unstuck when his front wing collapsed, without contact, on lap 26 during a restart after a safety car.
Alpine duo Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto were fifth and sixth, a terrific result to help cement the team into sixth place in the constructors' championship, and give them an outside chance of finishing fifth.
Lewis Hamilton was seventh for Ferrari, while Carlos Sainz secured a vital eighth place for Williams.
Russell was ninth after his late mistake, while Ferrari's Charles Leclerc dropped to 10th following a 10-second penalty for colliding with teammate Hamilton.
