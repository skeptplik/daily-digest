These are the stories making headlines in fashion on Thursday.
Viral products like Miu Miu’s micro-mini skirt or Balenciaga’s Triple S sneaker are a boon for brands, but they must strike a balance between sales spikes driven by virality and long-term brand desirability. Problems may arise when a buzzy product isn’t replaced by another hit once demand fades. If an item is too visible, it also runs the risk of being duped by more accessible players. {Vogue Business/paywalled}
A new wave of contemporary labels are gaining traction in the fashion industry for offering luxury quality without the luxury price point. This cohort includes brands like Kallmeyer, Loulou de Saison, Róhe, Studio Nicholson, TWP, Nili Lotan and Toteme. Luxury houses have alienated consumers with soaring prices, but these brands are increasingly offering the accessibility of contemporary fashion with the brand storytelling seen in luxury. {Business of Fashion/paywalled}
According to a Thursday report from Corriere della Sera, L’Oréal is in talks to buy 10% of Giorgio Armani alongside eyewear maker EssilorLuxottica, which would take a 5% stake. LVMH has reportedly dropped out of the race for the Armani stake. The late designer stipulated in his will that his heirs should sell around 15% of his company between 12 and 18 months after his death. Armani also specifically called out L’Oréal, EssilorLuxottica and LVMH as preferred buyers. {Business of Fashion/paywalled}
Back in January, Pam Kaufman joined Gap Inc. as the company’s first chief entertainment officer. She is leading Gap Inc.’s entertainment strategy, which highlights fashion’s role in culture, across sports, music, gaming and Hollywood. For one of Kaufman’s first big campaigns, the company partnered with American boy band JYT on a mall tour, which calls back to Gap’s beginnings as a mall brand. For its “fashiontainment” approach, Gap Inc. aims to continue partnering with entertainment companies, sports leagues, gaming companies and more. {Vogue Business/paywalled}
Kering has appointed Francisco Ochando to the newly created role of group pricing director, effective Nov. 16. Ochando reports to Marilyne Trebus, Kering’s chief product officer. According to Ochando’s LinkedIn, he will be “leading the group-wide pricing strategy across all Kering houses and geographies, driving sustainable revenue growth, brand value creation and pricing excellence”. {Vogue Business/paywalled}
