Ukraine war spilling into NATO territory
The war between Russia and Ukraine once again creeps onto European territory – from attacks on ships to missiles in Poland and fresh allegations of Russian operations inside Denmark.
ROB SCHMITZ, HOST:
A Russian ship was set ablaze after it was struck by a drone in the Baltic Sea. Yet another sign that the war in Ukraine is, at times, spilling over into the rest of Europe. This comes just days after President Trump called Russian President Vladimir Putin his friend. Teri Schultz joins us now from Brussels. Teri, let's start in the Gulf of Finland. A Russian cargo ship was hit by a drone there Saturday and caught fire. What's the latest on that?
TERI SCHULTZ, BYLINE: Well, a few hours ago, Rob, Russia took control of this cargo ship, the Sparta II, and towed it out of Finnish waters a day after, as you mentioned, it was hit by a drone. The crew was rescued by another Russian vessel traveling nearby at the time of the strike. Now, the attack is presumed to have come from Ukraine, but Kyiv has not yet claimed responsibility. Finnish President Alexander Stubb referred to the incident as an unfortunate reminder that the, quote, "consequences of Russia's war of aggression are also visible in our region." Interestingly, teams from both Finland and neighboring Estonia came Saturday and helped Russian ships try to put out the fire on board. Stubb thanked all of those involved for what he called responsible and professional actions.
SCHMITZ: So if the Sparta II was in the service of the Russian military, why didn't Finland seize the ship to put it out of business?
SCHULTZ: There was a lot of talk about this in Finland because this ship transports weapons and other supplies to Russia to use against Ukraine. And the vessel itself is under U.S. and U.K. sanctions, and its owner is under European Union sanctions. But the conclusion this time was that it would create too big an environmental hazard to keep it in Finnish waters. Here's how it was described in a press conference by Jukka-Pekka Lumilahti, the director of sea operations for the Finnish Border Guard.
(SOUNDBITE OF PRESS CONFERENCE)
JUKKA-PEKKA LUMILAHTI: (Through interpreter) If we would let the vessel burn, it would most likely sink. And after that, it would cause a major environmental risk for Finland and Estonia.
SCHULTZ: So as much as Finland would be pleased to take a military cargo ship out of Moscow's arsenal, in this case, they were basically saying good riddance.
SCHMITZ: So Ukraine is not the only one launching far-reaching strikes. Last Friday, Russia launched missiles into Polish territory. And I understand that you were there covering a nearby military exercise involving nearly a dozen countries, and it was that that was suspected to be Russia's reason for doing this, right?
SCHULTZ: Yeah, that's right. There were three Russian cruise missiles that landed between 30 and 60 miles from this military exercise, which was training forces for what's envisioned to eventually be a peace-monitoring mission in post-war Ukraine. At the time, all we knew on the ground was that there were multiple air alerts, at one time even telling us to take cover. And that happens often in Poland, especially at that location just over a hundred miles from the Ukrainian border. But this is particularly risky for the Russians, as they never know whether they'll kill people from a NATO country on the ground. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte was asked by a crowd of reporters in Warsaw whether the alliance can do anything to stop these Russian incursions.
(SOUNDBITE OF ARCHIVED RECORDING)
MARK RUTTE: I cannot guarantee you that it will not happen. But I can guarantee you is that NATO will defend every inch of Polish territory, that as a collective alliance, we impose immense costs on Russians.
SCHULTZ: Now, Rob, NATO ambassadors will meet Monday morning in a special session to discuss what more their countries may be able to do to protect Poland.
SCHMITZ: That is Teri Schultz in Brussels. Teri, thanks so much for your reporting.
SCHULTZ: You're welcome.
