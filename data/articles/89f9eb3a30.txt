Le Pen's top ally Bardella faces fresh allegations of anti-semitic comments
French investigative outlet Mediapart on Thursday published extracts from private conversations in which far-right leader Jordan Bardella allegedly made anti-semitic remarks as a teenager, prompting fresh accusations that he has denied.
French far-right leader Jordan Bardella on Thursday faced new accusations of having made anti-semitic remarks as a teenager, after French media published extracts from previously unaired conversations online.
The 31-year-old top ally of French presidential candidate Marine Le Pen has denied any wrongdoing after allegations emerged in September that he made conspiracist comments about Jewish people on Facebook in 2013.
Bardella, tipped to become prime minister if Le Pen wins next year, has denied any wrongdoing when he was an activist for the National Front – now renamed the National Rally (RN).
Investigative website Mediapart and daily Le Monde on Thursday published what they described as more antisemitic comments Bardella made in private Facebook conversations with a high school acquaintance in the year he turned 18.

Bardella, through a lawyer, categorically denied the new accusations, denouncing them as "an unacceptable intent to cause harm".
Mediapart said it had already seen these comments when it broke the story in September, but had not published further excerpts to protect the identity of the outlet's source.
The acquaintance then agreed to share the previously unpublished remarks in a bid to show Bardella was lying after he rejected the initial accusations, the investigative outlet and Le Monde said.
In these, he allegedly writes "it is Zionists who head banks" and seek a "new world order with Jerusalem as a capital".

The investigations indicated that elements of the conversations could be attributed to Bardella, including comments about his teachers at the time, as well as other details about his parents, and a stint teaching foreigners French that he also mentioned in his 2024 autobiography.
Bardella denounced what he described as a "witch hunt" in a statement obtained by FRANCE 24.
"I reiterate my categorical rejection of the statements attributed to me by Mediapart, Le Monde, and the media outlets that have echoed these accusations," Bardella said.
"These statements reflect neither my ideas, nor my values, nor my convictions, either then or now," he added.
Bardella said that legal action had already been initiated on grounds including forgery, use of forged documents and defamation.
Le Pen has long sought to rid the RN of the toxic legacy of her father, Jean-Marie Le Pen, a man convicted for antisemitism who once called the gas chambers of the Holocaust a mere "detail" of history.
Bardella has been instrumental in this effort, visiting Israel in 2025 – the first such trip by a French far-right leader – in a bid to demonstrate the RN's new credentials.
(FRANCE 24 with AFP)
