‘A weight off my shoulders’: Haley Stevens defends endorsing Mike Rogers as Democrats slam congresswoman’s ‘betrayal’ – live
Stevens, whose eleventh-hour endorsement of the Republican Senate candidate rocked the Democratic Party over the weekend, attempted to defend the move on CNN late Sunday
Stevens on 11th-hour endorsement of Mike Rogers: ‘This is just a weight off my shoulders’
After Haley Stevens announced her decision to back Mike Rogers in the Michigan US Senate race, she defended her move in an interview with CNN on Sunday.
“This is just a weight off my shoulders. Abdul [El-Sayed] has been getting angrier and angrier,” she said, referring to the Democratic nominee. However, anchor Anderson Cooper pressed Stevens on what had changed, considering there were reports that just hours before pulling her support, the congresswoman had told Democratic colleagues that she was still backing El-Sayed.
After evading the question, Stevens said that she “had some time to, you know, think”, but still didn’t provide a specific answer to what about El-Sayed’s candidacy over the last 12 hours led her to endorse Rogers.
Many of the issues Stevens eventually gave were historic concerns, including El-Sayed’s decision to campaign with leftwing streamer Hasan Piker.
Cooper pressed Stevens further: “Those things that you cited, though, were from a while ago, and it seemed like those were OK for you up until some point yesterday, and then the only really new development was the disinviting to you know a campaign event or a church service that seems to be the crucial kind of turning point? Is that correct?”
But the representative – who narrowly lost the August primary to El-Sayed after a bitter contest that saw her campaign boosted by millions of dollars from the pro-Israel lobby – didn’t expand on her reasoning and sought to downplay her defection.
“I’m just casting my vote this one way. In Michigan, there’s a lot of people out there voting,” she said.
When asked how she squares a vote for Rogers – when she has blasted him for being a “rubber stamp” for Trump’s “Maga agenda” – Stevens instead said that she was unhappy voting for a Democratic nominee who is “injecting” this “anger and lack of kindness”, without offering any specific recent examples.

Immigrants who have been granted release from detention after winning habeas corpus petitions in court are being re-arrested by US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).
The Guardian has identified at least 20 cases nationwide relating to people who were detained after being released on habeas corpus petition in the last year, according to court records, attorneys and media reports.
Increasing arrests and detentions have led to immigrants filing more than 91,000 habeas petitions as they seek to challenge their detentions, according to research by ProPublica. More petitions have been filed since 25 January than during the past three presidential administrations, including Trump’s first term, combined, the outlet found.
A petition of habeas corpus – Latin for “you shall have a body” – challenges in a federal court the government’s right to hold someone in custody while their main legal case is proceeding.
But in a new development, people are winning their release only to find themselves quickly detained again.
“We’ve seen a very, very sharp increase in people who are released on habeas petitions, only to be very rapidly re-detained, often in violation of the federal court’s order,” said Bridget Pranzatelli, an attorney with the National Immigration Project.

El-Sayed: 'At the end of the day, I actually don't think about Haley Stevens'
In his first public comments since Stevens’s endorsement of Rogers, El-Sayed told reporters at a press conference outside of a rally he held on Sunday, the very same rally that Stevens had earlier been disinvited from attending, “At the end of the day, I actually don’t think about Haley Stevens.” He added, “I’m just less interested in the politics of it all than I am in the policy of it all.
“If you are somebody who believes that we should stand up to hate, and you have watched this last campaign, I think we probably would not want to support Mike Rogers and Donald Trump and JD Vance,” he said.
But Stevens’s political pivot earned her praise from Donald Trump, someone whose policies she has an entire page on her website dedicated to denouncing.
Trump, during his rally in Iowa, heaped praise on Stevens for turning against El-Sayed, urging her to join the GOP. “It was an incredible thing,” Trump said. “I’d love her to join the Republican party. I think that could happen.”
Stevens had lost by a close margin in this year’s primary, and for months had refused to openly campaign for El-Sayed, as had his other opponent Mallory McMorrow.
Some of the people who worked for Stevens’s campaign also reportedly quit following her Rogers endorsement.
Her long-time chief of staff, Justin German, and her communications director, Logan Basch, were among those who left, according to CNN correspondent Manu Raju.
German later released a statement on behalf of Stevens’ campaign team endorsing El-Sayed:
As Haley Stevens’ Chief of staff for eight years, I speak on behalf of our team in wholeheartedly supporting Abdul El-Sayed for Senate. This election is too important to sow division or stand on the sidelines.
We are shocked and heartbroken by Congresswoman Stevens’ decision to support Mike Rogers.
Democrats condemn Haley Stevens' 'betrayal' following Republican endorsement
Hello and welcome to the US politics live blog.
Democratic politicians have condemned congresswoman Haley Stevens, who lost the Michigan Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, over her endorsement of his Republican rival just days before the midterm vote.
Stevens’ extraordinary endorsement of Mike Rogers was the culmination of an escalating feud between the former primary rivals – primarily over support for Israel – that has roiled one of the most fiercely contested Senate contests of the cycle.
As one might expect, top Democrats have been quick to express their outrage at the congresswoman’s “betrayal”.
California governor Gavin Newsom said on the political talkshow 2WAY that Stevens “went another 180 degrees overnight, so something was going on there”. “She’s been successful at taking our eye off the ball and that’s why I understand party leadership condemning it ... This may be curiously the October surprise of sorts,” he added.
Senate majority leader Chuck Schumer also responded negatively to the endorsement. Schumer’s campaign account called it a “despicable betrayal” from Stevens in a statement, but continued to push for unity among Democrats. He said:
Senate Democrats are united in our support for Abdul El-Sayed, because we know he will fight for the economic interests of working families in Michigan, unlike Mike Rogers, who is a tool of Donald Trump and billionaire donors. We Democrats equally united in our total revulsion at the racist, Islamophobic, and over-the-top false attacks on Abdul by Republicans.
These attacks are evidence of the total bankruptcy of the Republican Party today, who resort to ugly and un-American fearmongering because they have nothing to offer on the problems that concern people today.
Meanwhile, Michigan’s Democratic party chair Curtis Hertel said:
Haley Stevens’ decision to campaign with Rogers – a man who has repeatedly sold out Michigan’s working class, wants to pass a national abortion ban, and has pledged to be a loyal vote for Trump’s agenda – shows that she has abandoned everything she claims to care about. We reject the attempts by Stevens and bad-faith actors to divide our party and undermine our momentum, and will not allow these eleventh-hour distractions deter us.
Bernie Sanders joined the controversy, attacking Stevens for what Democrats view as an extraordinary betrayal after Stevens accepted El-Sayed’s narrow victory over her in Michigan’s Democrat senate primaries.
“Rep. Haley Stevens’ staff is right to resign in disgust,” Sanders posted on X. “They understand that the election in Michigan is far more important than Haley’s fragile ego and that all of us must stand together to elect Abdul, defeat Rogers and end the Republican rubber-stamp control of the Senate.”
Zohran Mamdani, the New York City mayor, said Stevens had “betrayed” working class people “by endorsing the very Republican she said she wanted to run against”.
