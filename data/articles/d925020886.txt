Spanish design practice Isern Serra combined organic architectural forms with a vibrant colour palette for Circl, a fitness studio and matcha cafe located in Barcelona’s historic Eixample district.
Set just below street level, the 180-square-metre space was designed to offer an escape from the busy city centre and contains an entrance cafe, reception area, changing rooms, bathrooms and a pilates studio.
“The project began with the idea of bringing together two worlds: pilates and matcha,” Isern Serra told Dezeen.
“Although different in nature, both share a common philosophy centred on wellbeing, balance and the connection between body and mind,” he continued.
“Architecture plays an essential role in this experience, accompanying visitors on a gradual transition from the energy of the city towards a more intimate and introspective environment.”
His studio introduced a series of colour-blocked spaces to guide customers through different phases of the interior, with colours that were specifically chosen to correspond to the function and tone of each area.
“This vision of wellbeing takes shape through a carefully choreographed sequence of spaces, where each change in level, colour and atmosphere guides users away from the bustle of the city and towards an increasingly introspective environment,” said Serra.
Serra combined cream banquette seating with dark wooden tables and alabaster sconces to create a calm, meditative environment for the cafe part of the space. A sculptural, mirrored service counter was also incorporated to reflect natural light around the space.
To signal the transition into the pilates studio, the designers introduced a deep-green reception hallway with tiered seating blocks and a spherical light installation.
The bright green circulation area is connected by an open archway and features curved architectural volumes that house the changing rooms, bathrooms and a water fountain.
Another rounded archway then leads to the main studio.
The main pilates studio was designed as a white space to reflect the coloured light from a sculptural LED light installation attached to the vaulted ceiling.
The aim was to create diverse exercise environments and enable the lighting to control the mood and energy of the space.
“The white surfaces act as a neutral canvas for coloured light, allowing different hues to transform the atmosphere,” Serra said.
“This system also offers flexibility for brand collaborations, as the lighting can be adapted to reflect different identities and experiences,” he added.
In the studio, the designers used mirrored walls to create the illusion of a circular room and added full-length perimeter curtains to soften the space’s texture.
Other interior projects recently featured on Dezeen include Matteo Thun designs for Milan’s Torre Velasca skyscraper and SABO’s design of an apartment on Manhattan’s Lower East Side.
