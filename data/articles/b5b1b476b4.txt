Judge declares Meghan Markle's father fit to face charges related to child sexual abuse images
Thomas Markle is being held in a Florida jail after allegedly cutting off his ankle monitor
A U.S. judge in Florida ruled Thursday that Thomas Markle, the estranged father of Meghan Markle, Britain's Duchess of Sussex, is mentally competent to face a federal charge of possession of child pornography filed earlier this month.
Agreeing with the findings of a court-appointed forensic psychiatrist, U.S. Magistrate Judge Lindsay Griffin ruled that Markle, 82, was mentally fit to understand the proceedings against him and assist in his own defence.
Markle's defence lawyer had said they would not contest the finding by Dr. Amie Gerodimos. The exam was ordered after his arrest at a senior care centre in Lakeland, Fla., on Nov. 6.
During a 90-minute interview, Markle denied the allegations against him and blamed his son for his legal troubles, according to Gerodimos's 15-page report.
"Why I am here. What have I done?" she quoted him as saying. "I don't have any child porn. I have no pictures of naked kids."
The FBI, however, said 78 items were seized from two storage units rented for years by Markle in the Los Angeles area, including slides, film negatives and books that contained suspected child sexual abuse images.
Some images appear to depict him with children, the FBI said.
Markle is in custody in a jail while the case remains in federal court in Tampa. It will eventually shift to a similar court in Southern California.

Markle was initially released on bond but was arrested again when authorities accused him of cutting off his electronic ankle monitor on Nov. 10.
He had a stroke earlier this year while living in the Philippines and had to have his left leg amputated. That forced him to return to the U.S. and settle in Florida.
The Sun, a British tabloid newspaper, reported that Meghan's half brother found child sexual abuse images while clearing out their father's home in the Philippines. The discovery led to a search of the two U.S. storage units in California, which Markle had rented since 2008.
"My son hates me. He has never accomplished anything," Markle told the psychiatrist.
Gerodimos said in her report that Markle, who has a number of health problems, understands the charges against him and can assist his lawyer — two key points in any competency determination. The expert said he was "malingering" at times during the interview.
Investigation in the Philippines
Meanwhile, in the Philippines, Markle is under investigation for possession and intent to distribute images.
The Women and Children Protection Center, a branch of the Philippine National Police, filed a formal criminal complaint against Markle on Monday, police spokesperson Brig.-Gen. Allen Rae Co said in a written statement.
The complaint was filed with the prosecutor's office following an investigation into digital devices found at Markle's former home in Cebu City, the statement said. The prosecutor's office will assess the case for possible charges.
Markle mentioned the Philippines in his interview with Gerodimos, insisting "there's nothing incriminating" there.
Markle did not attend his daughter Meghan's 2018 wedding to Prince Harry at Windsor Castle. She said at the time he needed to tend to his health. Meghan is an American citizen who starred in the U.S. television drama Suits.
