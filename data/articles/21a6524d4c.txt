Going into Tuesday’s reveal of the 2026 Game Awards nominees, the question wasn’t whether or not Grand Theft Auto 6 would pick up any nominations. It was more about how many nominations it would get. And now that the dust has cleared, GTA 6’s Game Awards nominations rolled out pretty much…exactly as expected.
But it’s nevertheless an unconventional situation. The game isn’t out yet. Reviews haven’t been published. Still, GTA 6 ultimately picked up eight nominations — the most of any game in 2026, immediately cementing it as a frontrunner for the GOTY race. It was nominated in the following categories:
- Game of the Year
- Best Game Direction
- Best Narrative
- Best Action/Adventure
- Best Art Direction
- Best Score and Music
- Best Audio Design
- Best Performance (for Manni L. Perez in an undisclosed role)
This falls short of records set by Sandfall Interactive’s beloved parry simulator Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, which Polygon’s Oli Welsh predicted was an impossible gambit for GTA 6 anyway.
In 2025, Clair Obscur was nominated for 13 categories and won nine awards. That means GTA 6 could win every category it’s nominated in and still fall short of Clair Obscur’s record. At best, it could tie, depending on how the Player’s Voice category — which is voted on entirely by the public — plays out. As far as sheer number of nominations, purely on grounds of eligibility, it was never going to set the bar. Clair Obscur was up for two categories GTA 6 would never be eligible for (Best Indie Game and Best Debut Indie Game), and its ensemble cast carried it to three Best Performance nominations (Ben Starr, Charlie Cox, and Jennifer English, the latter of whom won).
Whatever happens, GTA 6’s raft of nominations reveals details about a wildly anticipated game that, to date, has only been seen through selected marketing materials and curated media coverage. A Best Score and Music nomination suggests Grand Theft Auto’s pedigree of killer soundtracks remains unshaken. A Best Game Direction nomination reaffirms Rockstar’s pedigree of immaculately conceived game design. Snagging a Best Narrative nomination — in a year full of narratively rich games that nevertheless excluded the sprawling, ambitious, deliciously messy Fire Emblem: Fortune’s Weave from the field — suggests GTA 6 might be something more nuanced than a mayhem-packed sandbox.
But perhaps the biggest reveal is in Perez’s nomination for Best Performance. The Game Awards did not officially say what role she plays in GTA 6, but these things tend to go to lead characters, which aligns with a long-standing rumor among fans that Perez portrays Lucia, one of the game’s protagonists. That GTA 6 only nabbed one Best Performance nomination additionally suggests one of two things: that she’s the only good performance in GTA 6, or that her performance is miles above and beyond everyone else in the game (which includes recent Emmy winner Stephen Root among its officially revealed cast).
All told, GTA 6 ended up nominated in basically every category it conceivably could’ve been nominated in: the top category, the top of one genre category (games are only allowed to compete in one of those), all three technical categories, and all three general artistic categories.
At the top of 2026, much ado was made about the shadow cast by GTA 6 over this year’s GOTY race — if the game was released in time, of course. For a large part of the year, particularly when it was still hard to believe that this thing was actually coming out, when it still felt like it might’ve been delayed, there were hints that this year would fold out in interesting, unexpected ways. On a podium left empty, who clambers to the top? A licensed Star Wars game? A 120-hour tactical soap opera? Another indie darling? None of those ended up nominated for the evening’s top prize. Now that we’re fast approaching the finish line, the race is playing out precisely as many expected a year ago.
