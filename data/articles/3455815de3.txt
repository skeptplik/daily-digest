A year after the Washington summit that brought peace between Armenia and Azerbaijan, the Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity (TRIPP) project has entered its construction phase amid broader geopolitical reshaping in the region and beyond.
Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan announced "the launch of the practical phase" of the Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity (TRIPP), the key regional US-backed transit corridor of the South Caucasus, as Armenia and Azerbaijan continue building together the future of the region after decades of tragic wars.
In his official launch address, Pashinyan described the launch as "a truly historic event in the modern history of the Republic of Armenia,” describing TRIPP as the largest economic project in Armenia since its independence.
"Beyond the benefits of connecting Azerbaijan with the Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic, and the mutual advantages for the Republic of Armenia in terms of developing international and domestic connectivity, the TRIPP project will hold immense regional and international significance by infusing additional vitality into the Trans-Caspian trade route and West-East connectivity," he added.
Announcing that TRIPP will be "jointly implemented by the Governments of Armenia and the United States,” together with Azerbaijan, Pashinyan said that the initiative aims to establish unimpeded, multimodal transit across the South Caucasus based on full respect for the "sovereignty, territorial integrity, and jurisdiction" of participating states.
Armenia’s prime minister added that the project will enable Yerevan to access foreign markets and attract investment to Armenia’s southern Syunik province. It will also contribute to Armenia's security, with its neighbours seeking stability for the regional developments, according to Pashinyan.
A live television broadcast following his speech showed the start of the preparatory construction in Armenia’s Syunik province, linking mainland Azerbaijan to its Nakhchivan exclave.
Syunik governor Robert Ghukasyan confirmed that work began at several locations in the Meghri area, noting that major construction will follow after an initial period of preparatory work.
Pashinyan earlier said electricity transit will be a main component of the project, amid plans to connect Armenia, Azerbaijan and Turkey’s grids in a major geopolitical development for the region.
Another key component is building a railway connecting the project countries, with an agreed completion date of 2028.
Washington's backing and joint development structure
In a video address marking the kickoff, US President Donald Trump congratulated Pashinyan on launching the project and praised his efforts toward peace with Azerbaijan.
Trump announced the establishment of the TRIPP Development Company, stating that the US-Armenia partnership will open new routes for cargo, energy flows, and passenger travel across the South Caucasus.
"Today, we continue our progress with the launch of the Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity and the brand-new TRIPP Development Company,” Trump said, adding that “this partnership between the United States and Armenia will create new trade routes for the flow of goods, energy, and travel throughout the region.”
“We are committed to ensuring it is a great, great success," Trump said.
Under the bilateral framework ratified by Armenia's parliament in October and signed by President Vahagn Khachaturyan earlier this month Washington holds a 74% stake in the joint operating company and Yerevan holds the remaining 26%, but the agreement's provisions allow Armenia’s share to rise to 49% if the partnership is extended.
Armenian officials pointed out that Yerevan retains control over legislation, customs and administration along the route.
Trump also thanked both Pashinyan and Azerbaijan’s President Ilham Aliyev for their cooperation and invited the Armenian premier to visit the White House in 2027, when Pashinyan is scheduled to travel to Washington for the National Prayer Breakfast.
"I once again want to thank Prime Minister Pashinyan and his counterpart, President Aliyev of Azerbaijan, for working with us to make this day possible and for so generously nominating me for the Nobel Peace Prize," he added.
Shortly after the announcement, Azerbaijan's president posted a statement on X thanking Trump for his remarks at the project's launch.
The TRIPP initiative stems from the 8 August 2025 joint declaration signed in Washington by Aliyev and Pashinyan in Trump's presence, where the two countries' foreign ministers launched the two countries’ peace agreement.
Restoring unhindered transport and logistical links between mainland Azerbaijan and Nakhchivan has been a central pillar of the normalisation process between Baku and Yerevan.
The TRIPP project is the key component of the wider so-called Middle Corridor, the emerging strategic transit and trade route between Asia and Europe and a strong Eurasian alternative amid global supply chain disruptions in the Middle East.
The Middle Corridor is a rail and maritime transport network that will connect China, Southeast Asia, and Central Asia with European markets via the Caspian Sea and the South Caucasus to the Black Sea.
Azerbaijan, Georgia and Kazakhstan are stepping up their cooperation to develop the Middle Corridor, as the network will pass through these countries and onwards to Turkey and Europe, through a joint plan aimed at improving transport infrastructure, streamlining logistics, and making cross-border trade faster and more efficient.
The joint plan comes as European companies explore new investment opportunities along the route.
The participating countries are discussing how to connect the energy routes and markets from a so-called strategic-geography perspective, with significant political and economic benefits and growing influence.
The US-backed project is moving forward in a complex geopolitical area marked by tensions, with Iran and Russia following closely.
Both the TRIPP project and the wider Middle Corridor face geopolitical challenges, depending on the ongoing peace process between Azerbaijan and Armenia, notably Armenia’s adoption of its peace treaty through a new constitution, as well as the possible challenges posed by Iran and Russia to the US-backed TRIPP project and the wider interactions of the participating countries within the current geopolitical reshaping processes.
