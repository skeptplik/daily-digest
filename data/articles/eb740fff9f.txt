Andy Taylor, guitarist of new wave icons Duran Duran, dead at 65
Taylor brought 'indelible fire and swagger to our music', band says in statement
Andy Taylor, the original guitarist of the 1980s English new wave band Duran Duran, has died. He was 65.
Taylor's death was confirmed by his family and bandmates in a statement posted on social media and Duran Duran's website.
"From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music," the band said.
"His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed."
On the night he was inducted in the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in November 2022, Taylor revealed that he had been battling Stage 4 metastatic prostate cancer for several years and was unable to attend.
"I would like to thank each of my brothers in this great band," he wrote. "You can dream about what happened to us but to experience it, on one's own terms, as mates, was beyond incredible."
The band's huge pop hits include Hungry Like the Wolf, The Reflex and Rio. Their rise up the charts was inextricably linked to the launch of MTV.
The two-time Grammy Award winners had nine gold records and six platinum ones, with 20 Top 20 singles in the U.K. and 18 in the U.S. They were two-time Ivor Novello Award winners and are MTV Video Visionary Award recipients.

Fashion, synthesizers and glam-pop
The band's imperial stage occurred with a trio of hit albums between 1981 and 1983, with such dance-floor barnburners as Girls on Film and Union of the Snake and the moody ballads The Chauffeur and Save a Prayer. Other hits in the coming years included The Wild Boys, New Moon on Monday and Come Undone.
Their popularity was cemented when they recorded the James Bond theme song A View to a Kill and earned a slot at Live Aid in 1985.
Mixing fashion, pioneering synthesizers and glam-pop swagger, the telegenic band were all over radio and young people's walls, with bassist John Taylor and singer Simon Le Bon becoming heartthrobs and the band being labelled part of the New Romantics. Princess Diana was known to be a fan, which meant a lot to the band.
"Andy never took the easy musical route. His overdrive, overdrove us into being the only funk/rock/punk band in the New Romantic movement, with songs that still survive, decades later when his own life is cut so short," Le Bon said in a statement Wednesday.
The band's success was due in large part to their videos, including their Antigua-set song Rio, which featured shots of the band in pastel suits sailing confidently on a yacht.
For the album Rio, they shot three music videos in Sri Lanka — including Save a Prayer, for which bass guitarist John Taylor was sprayed by an elephant — and two in Antigua, feeding MTV's need to have a stream of visuals, something classic rock didn't have.
"A lot of the success of the album is down to the power of the videos and the way it made the band omnipresent around the globe," Le Bon told the Daily Beast in 2012.
Andy Taylor agreed: "I don't think we could have done it without MTV."
Taylor was the first of the Duran Duran boys to get married, tying the knot with Tracey in 1982 and having four children — Andy, Georgie, Bethy and Izzy, and a grandson, Albie. He is survived by all of them, and they credited his devotion to them in a family statement.
"For all the incredible experiences music brought him, family always came first. Nothing made him happier than having us all together, and no matter how far music took him, his heart was always at home," the family statement said.
In 2023, he released the album Man's a Wolf to Man, his first solo release in over 30 years. On the title track, he sang: "Last night/I dreamt someone loved me/And life was not surreal/Someone's souvenir."
