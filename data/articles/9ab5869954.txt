Consumer prices fell by 0.7% in November 2026, after rising by 0.4% in October, according to the latest figures from Insee, published this Friday.
Prices fell by 0.7% in November after rising by 0.4% in October, the National Institute of Statistics and Economic Studies (Insee) said on Friday.
This slowdown in inflation is largely explained by the movement in energy prices. While they are still showing a sharp increase compared with last year, they rose at a less rapid pace in November than in October, moving from +24,1 % to +19,2 %.
Insee particularly highlights the marked fall in prices of petroleum products, even though gas tariffs continue to rise.
Service inflation also fell back to 2,3 % year-on-year, after 2,6 % in October, a drop that Insee attributes to a "sharp slowdown" in prices for accommodation and transport.
By contrast, Insee records a slight increase in food prices (up 1,3 % over one year after 1,2 % in October), driven by a sharp rise in the fresh products sector (+9,4 % over one year compared with 8 % in October).
Consumption and unemployment stable
Meanwhile, French household spending on goods stabilised this month, after falling in September and August, according to Insee.
The drop in energy consumption (-2,9%) is offset by a rebound in consumption of manufactured goods (+1,3%), while food consumption remains stable (-0,1%).
The report also highlights the stability of salaried employment (0,0 %, that is -12 200 jobs, compared with -0,1 %, or -26 100 jobs, in the previous quarter). However, employment figures continue to fall year-on-year for the fifth consecutive quarter.
Salaried employment nonetheless clearly exceeds its level before the health crisis at the end of 2019 (+4,8 %, that is +1,2 million jobs), the national statistics institute notes.
However, France's gross domestic product (GDP) fell by 0,2 % in volume in the third quarter of 2026, after remaining stable in the previous quarter, Insee says, adding that the growth carry-over for 2026 stands at +0,1 %.
The purchasing power of households' gross disposable income (RDB) per consumption unit rose by 0,2 % (after a fall of 0,5 %), while the household saving rate stabilised in the third quarter at 17,4 % of their RDB.
