The president of the Royal Institute of British Architects, Chris Williamson, has decided to renew his membership with the Architects Registration Board, nearly a year after terminating it.
In a LinkedIn post, Williamson said he was rejoining the Architects Registration Board’s (ARB) register because he no longer wanted “to face the prospect of legal action”.
Specifically, he said he feared the potential “legal proceedings and financial penalties” from referring to himself as an architect, which ARB does not permit if a person is unregistered.
“I have felt the fear of legal proceedings”
Williamson’s post comes nearly a year after he revealed he was not going to renew his membership to draw attention to what he called the “absurdity of the current regulatory framework”.
This ARB framework currently only regulates the protected title “architect” and the competence of those on its register, meaning it does not oversee the competence of unregistered providers of architectural services.
His withdrawal from ARB last year aligned with the launch of a campaign by the Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA) calling for a new regulatory model for UK architects.
Though he has now rejoined the ARB register, Williamson said his position on the need to reform remains unchanged.
“Today, I have decided to rejoin the ARB register,” he posted on LinkedIn on Friday.
“Over the last year, I have sat in meetings and roundtables with Ministers, MPs and Peers, spoken at party conferences and COP31, and had the pleasure of awarding the Stirling Prize,” he continued. “Throughout, I have felt the fear of legal proceedings and financial penalties all for referring to myself, in these settings, as an architect – something that’s so fundamental to my identity.”
“I want to stress this is not a step backwards”
Williamson, the co-founder of Weston Williamson + Partners, began his two-year term as president on 1 September 2025.
In the LinkedIn post – a shortened version of an open letter shared with RIBA members – he said he will now continue to “push for a stronger system” for regulation.
The current RIBA campaign calls for three steps to reform architects’ regulation, including repealing the Architects Act – a UK law that sets the framework for the regulation of the title architect, under which ARB is the regulator.
It also proposes new legislation outlining reserved activities, such as submitting full planning applications, and who can execute them. The final step calls for the introduction of a Built Environment Council to oversee the competence of individuals and companies working in the construction industry.
“I know my decision to return may surprise or disappoint some of you, particularly those who followed my lead,” concluded Williamson.
“I want to stress this is not a step backwards and it does not reflect a change in my views. In fact, the year I’ve spent outside of the Register has only cemented my commitment to reform,” he continued.
“While my title may change, RIBA’s campaign for regulatory reform will not. I will use my experience on both sides to push for a stronger system that protects what we call ourselves – and what we do.”
Williamson was selected as RIBA president in 2024. Prior to this, he held several roles within the RIBA, including the vice president international from 2017 to 2019.
In 2014, ARB gave Dezeen 14 days to amend an article describing John Pawson as an architect, claiming it was “in the public interest” for Dezeen to use an alternative description, as Pawson is not registered with the board.
