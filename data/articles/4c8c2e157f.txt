The final trailer for 'Avengers: Doomsday' becomes the second most-viewed of all time, while the film's director has spoken out on Patrick Stewart's last day on set. Stewart died in October aged 86. His appearance as Professor X in the forthcoming Marvel film marks his last screen performance.
It's been a great year for cinema when it comes to numbers, with Summer 2026 officially delivering the biggest box office since COVID thanks to The Odyssey and Spider-Man: Brand New Day, as well as breakout hits like Obsession and Backrooms. And the year isn't over yet...
In little over three weeks' time, two of the year's biggest blockbusters drop on the same day: Dune: Part 3 and Avengers: Doomsday. When it comes to the latter, it looks like Hollywood's bean counters will be rubbing their hands once more...
The final Doomsday trailer dropped yesterday, and it racked up 563 million views in its first 24 hours – making it the most viewed Disney trailer of all time, beating the Avengers: Doomsday teaser (503 million in 24 hours).
According to data from WaveMetrix (via Deadline), the final Doomsday promo was the second-most-viewed movie trailer of all time behind Sony/Marvel Studio’s Spider-Man: Brand New Day, which was watched by 718.6m in its first 24-hours, and wound up becoming 2026's biggest film.
Considering final trailers usually get fewer views compared to first trailers, it seems like Doomsday is bound to set a box office opening record. Not long to find out now...
Meanwhile, Doomsday director Anthony Russo has spoken out on Patrick Stewart's last day on set.
Doomsday will be Patrick Stewart's final on screen role, having died aged 86 on 28 October. Stewart had been "struggling with his health", Russo revealed to Entertainment Weekly.
“He worked so hard to deliver a performance and a presence - for us as directors, for his other actors, for the crew," said Russo. “You could see it wasn’t just the effort of a regular actor showing up and delivering that kind of presence and performance and experience for others, collaboration.”
Russo continued: “It was someone who was really being challenged in a very deep way on a health level, showing up and doing that. For him to have muscled through and delivered the performance that he did in that movie, and the experience that he delivered for all of us as collaborators, it made it intensely emotional.”
Marvel Studio president Kevin Feige also remembered Stewart’s last day on set, sharing that the actor “stood up and he gave a speech to the crew”.
“We didn’t know necessarily it would be his last day on a set, but he was sort of indicating to us that he thought it was,” he said.
Feige said Marvel was now considering how best to honour Stewart in the finished film, saying: “We’re long done filming that movie, and he did an amazing job for us. We’re looking now at how we can put something together that honors and respects our friend.”
Avengers: Doomsday will be Stewart's ninth and final appearance as Professor X.
The beloved actor first played Charles Xavier in the 2000 film X-Men. He went on to play the character in several instalments of the franchise, including X2 (2003), X-Men: The Last Stand (2006), X-Men: Days of Future Past (2014), and Logan (2017), before making a brief return in Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022).
Avengers: Doomsday hits theatres on 18 December.
