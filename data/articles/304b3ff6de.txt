About 100 football clubs, representing hundreds of thousands of players, have deliberately missed the deadline to sign up for next season amid a dispute with Football Queensland.
The disagreement has forced Football Australia to intervene, after a breakaway group threatened earlier this week to form its own competition.
Representatives of 110 clubs – about half of Queensland’s football clubs – attended a Tuesday night meeting to discuss the issue, a day before a deadline set by the governing body for all clubs to sign up for next season. The group includes nine of the 12 men’s national premier leagues clubs and six of the nine women’s national premier league clubs.
They unanimously agreed to not sign up.
Bardon Latrobe football club president, Gerard Doyle – whose club is part of the breakaway group – said they want to register for 2027, but won’t until their demands are met.
“The most important thing for the clubs is that we get a voice up and into Football Queensland,” he said.
“We have all signed a joint letter via a law firm. We have no confidence in the board. We have no confidence in the executive. We want to affect change, which means a change of who is running Football Queensland.”
Football Queensland hasn’t called elections for its governing board for many years, he said.
Doyle said the clubs are pushing for the central body to call elections this year. It is required by its bylaws to call elections in November, but hasn’t done so, he said.
Clubs have complained for months about what they say is a lack of transparency and governance within the body, and increasing fees with little to show for it, he said.
Some Queensland parents are made to pay $5 gate fees per person to watch their children play football. Doyle said the final straw was a proposal by Football Queensland to charge a $35 per player fee for summer competitions organised by the clubs themselves among other fee increases, a plan that has since been dropped.
“(Football Queensland) hasn’t been consultative,” he said.
“It’s simply been adding costs, adding bureaucracy, and issuing dictums down the line with no avenues for consultation or discussion”.
Clubs continue to plan to play in 2027, according to a statement posted online by the group, known as Football Clubs United.
Football Australia posted its own statement last night, saying it was “working in good faith with stakeholders towards an outcome that serves the long-term interests of the game and the communities it serves”.
It committed to facilitate “focused discussions” to resolve what is said were “unresolved issues concerning Football Queensland’s affiliation arrangements” that had “created uncertainty for players, coaches, referees, volunteers, parents and clubs across Queensland”.
“Recognising the commitment of clubs yet to affiliate to the wellbeing of their participants and the future of Queensland football, Football Australia calls on them to move to affiliation for 2027.
“For 2028, Football Queensland must publish a clear plan and timetable for reviewing ongoing arrangements with all clubs, with Football Australia participating throughout. These arrangements must be subject to meaningful and transparent consultation and agreement”.
Football Queensland has been contacted for comment.
