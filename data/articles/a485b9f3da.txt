Fri, 30 Oct, 7:15 pm, Final score, Cats 88, Bulldogs 20
There was a moment in Darcy Vescio's press conference for their 100th game where they hesitated on a word.
The inaugural Carlton player has long been a voice and face for the competition, including for the LGBTQ+ community, and particularly so after coming out as non-binary in 2021.
They have always championed that AFLW is for everyone — and everyone is welcome.
But while that is true, they do not exactly want everyone to feel comfortable at their home semifinal on Saturday.
As Princes Park, Carlton's HQ, is used as a home venue for many teams, a lot of the Blues' opposition have become very familiar with the ground.
North Melbourne star Ash Riddell spoke very publicly about loving the ground ahead of the Kangaroos' qualifying final with Carlton last week.
"You want [opposition] people to not feel… welcome," Vescio said, to laughter.
"When they come here. Even heading into last week's game … Riddell was very public in saying she loves playing at Ikon Park because they've built great memories here and they're really home here.
"So that's something we do want to wrestle back."
Vescio has said it has been tough to watch other teams play at their home during finals series. Carlton did not make the final eight from 2022 to 2024.
The Blues will have that opportunity when they come up against Hawthorn this weekend in a semifinal.
When Vescio runs out for that game, they will become the first Carlton player to reach 100 games in the AFLW.
Over the course of their career, Vescio has also played a pivotal role in the AFLW collective bargaining agreement negotiations over the years, helping drive the league's pay, conditions and professionalism forward.
"That adds, not weight, but it makes the footy experience a lot more rich," Vescio said.
"I love the game and every challenge that that brings, physically and mentally."
"But when you can extrapolate further and see the way you can shape things and push the game into a better space and I guess impact different communities who might not have always seen themselves in footy, for me, that adds to my experience of being a human playing footy.
"From the outside, it can be tempting to just want footy players to just focus on footy. But the reality is when you step out onto the field, you're a fully formed human with a brain and with things going on in your life.
"So if you're able to, I guess, service all areas of yourself whilst playing footy, it just makes it feel like a lot more worthwhile experience."
A lot has changed over the 11 seasons Vescio has played, including the number of teams in the competition (from eight to 18), season length and start date, and the skills and evolution of the game on-field.
One thing for Vescio themself, is the length of their socks.
"They're a bit of a fidget toy for me at this point. They kind of come up, they come down. But I am enjoying the long socks," they said.
"I feel like for a long time the socks we were given were very small, like I've got size 11 feet, so they were really squeezing in and some games they would be around my heel.
"So I feel like after 10 seasons of that, I'm trying to just hit back with maximum length. But yeah, I know it is uncomfortable for some people to look at."
