Grand Theft Auto 6. It's got cool cars, crime, a big new city to explore, and the potential to hoover up so much player time that many developers have chosen to give it the widest berth possible. It's led to an October uncommonly packed with big-hitters, and a November - ordinarily one of gaming's biggest months - that's near-barren. But not every developer is steering clear. Despite the potential risks, a select few have looked at GTA 6 and seen not a threat but an opportunity, so what's driving these brave souls?
Escape Academy 2: Back 2 School, a first-person puzzle adventure with a popular predecessor, was originally set to launch this September, but like many games this year saw its release date moved. However, its developer bucked the industry trend, shifting its sequel not away from GTA 6's orbit, but closer. "The thing that scares me more than this monocultural event - GTA 6 - is releasing in a week when there are like 15 good games rather than one big one", Coin Crew Games co-founder Wyatt Bushnell tells me when I ask about the studio's decision.
Escape Academy 2's original release date might have kept it away from Grand Theft Auto 6, but it would have instead faced a potential "bloodbath" of competition from the likes of Onimusha: Way of the Sword, Blood of the Dawnwalker, Control Resonant, and more. "That was our rationale," Bushnell says. "We can still cut into the GTA stuff, but it would have been a lot harder in September and October."
It helps, notes Bushnell, that Escape Academy 2 and Grand Theft Auto 6 - one a contemplative puzzle game, the other a blockbuster action spectacle - are serving very different crowds. That's a sentiment echoed by David Lowey, Atari's senior director of sales and marketing. Atari has similarly bucked industry trends and is releasing two games - Godzilla Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered and Barbie Rewind - in the now largely vacant November window.
When we speak, Lowey says there's a clear logic to smaller companies "sneaking" into spots that bigger-budget games can't risk filling, especially if their games differ greatly from Grand Theft Auto 6. "Look at Barbie Rewind coming out on the 12th," he elaborates. "It's a retro game, so it'll pull in people who grew up with these games [and] there's the cosy dreamhouse design at the core ... which is a vastly different game type to GTA 6 and plenty of other games.
"It's not an FPS, not a high-octane action game, there's not a lot of combat," Lowey continues. "It's designed to be the exact opposite. It's a game you're meant to curl up in a chair and play." And while he acknowledges the "reality of modern gaming" means there'll be some audience overlap between Barbie Rewind and GTA 6 players - "These sort of older ideas of what people play by gender and age have been smashed", he says - it "won't be the biggest overlap in the world". That, then, makes going toe-to-toe an easier bet.
But for Bushnell and Escape Academy, there's another consideration making GTA 6's launch window feel less of a risk: longer-tail sales. "The traditional thinking is that you have this big release and make all of your money in those first two weeks," he explains. "I don't think that holds a tonne of water anymore. The vast majority of sales for Escape Academy were made well after its release. The reality is with digital distribution, discounting, and the fact there is this shelf of games, we're in a world where games last far longer than their initial launch." That means it's less of a concern if GTA 6 sucks up everyone's attention for a few weeks.
And for Atari's David Lowey, Godzilla Destroy All Monsters and Barbie Rewind's proximity to GTA 6 has brought added benefits, enabling both games to capitalise on the hype around Rockstar's open-world epic in their own way. "One of the most rewarding things has been the fascination we've seen online around the proximity of Godzilla, Barbie, and GTA," he says.
The community has found it easy to root for games releasing in the behemoth shadow of GTA 6. Godzilla Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered - an updated version of the 2002 GameCube party fighting game, which released on 3rd November - has been dubbed, tongue in cheek, the "GTA killer" by fans, while others have joked that Rockstar is "running scared" from Barbie Rewind. "There's no question that this buzz based on the closeness has created player interest," adds Lowey. "In retrospect the timing worked well for us."
There's one game, though, that isn't just orbiting GTA 6, it's facing the triple-A juggernaut head-on. Edmund McMillen, Tyler Glaiel, and publisher Nicalis are bringing The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ to Xbox Series X/S, PlayStation 5, and Switch 2 on 19th November, the same day GTA 6 arrives. "That was [Nicalis'] idea," McMillen laughs when we speak. "'Wouldn't it be funny if...?' I was like, whatever, that's funny ... We're done, so we've got to release this month ... It's a fun experiment."
"There's a possibility [Repentance+'s release date] wasn't that stupid an idea," McMillen adds, "and if it was a stupid idea, it's a funny stupid idea." He also hints there's a "funny stupid" thing awaiting players who do a Repentance+ daily run on any platform during GTA 6's launch...
McMillen is, though, all too aware of the impact major releases can have on other games. "My heart says it doesn't matter, but my brain has shown me it does," he explains. "When Mewgenics came out it blew up; we went past two million sales almost right off the bat. It was such a blasting, skyrocketing thing for like three weeks. Then Slay the Spire 2 came out, and it sucked everybody out. The same players who liked Mewgenics, they were Slay The Spire players. So they all went woosh and right over ... When a new thing comes out, people all move to the new thing ... You can see numbers go from astronomical to very small, and then if the game is good, it'll trickle back in."
The way McMillen sees it, though, GTA 6 isn't immune to this natural ebb of flow of player interest either, and its pull might not be as long-lasting as some fear. "I know a lot of people buy Grand Theft Auto. I don't know how many people play Grand Theft Auto," he explains. "I don't know how many people are going to be sucked into this game and stay the whole time." And for McMillen, the fact GTA 6 is putting so many players in front of consoles is a positive.
"I think the people who like Isaac are going to play the game regardless," he says. "[But] logically, there's a record number of people who are going to be on the console the game is on at launch [because of GTA 6] ... They might be like me, play [GTA 6] for a while, and say, 'Okay, I have a console with one game, what am I going to play next?' Then, you know, a rising tide will raise all ships. I think Grand Theft Auto will bring people back to consoles, because consoles are abysmal right now. Sales are bad and no one is playing anything. People own next-gen consoles and they're just sitting there..."
