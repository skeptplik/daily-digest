After less than two years, Pierpaolo Piccioli is stepping down from Balenciaga, where he had been tasked with interpreting the house’s legacy of sculptural elegance without alienating the streetwear superfans his predecessor, Demna, courted.
Piccioli entered Balenciaga amidst the creative director roundabout of last year, a moment when many major fashion brands shuffled leadership. His debut, shown in the spring 2026 season, explored Balenciaga’s signature sack silhouette with simple evening dresses and separates alongside sculpted leather outerwear and bubble-hemmed skirts—all styled with space-age sunglasses and long leather gloves.
Throughout subsequent collections, he continued to chase an idea of pragmatic beauty. For Spring 2027, he showed elevated, voluminous versions of familiar ensembles like a T-shirt and jeans. Through that tenure, he courted high-profile fans like Meghan Markle and Tessa Thompson.
Born in 1967 in Italy, Piccioli studied fashion at the Istituto Europeo di Design in Rome. He interned at Brunello Cuccinelli before joining the team at Fendi, working alongside his design school friend Maria Grazia Chiuri, who would become his long-term creative partner. After roughly a decade, the pair went to Valentino to lead the accessories department in 1999; they were appointed co-creative directors of the brand in 2008. In 2016, Chiuri left to helm Dior, leaving Piccioli to lead the brand on his own.
There, he really established his legacy as a beacon of elegance and a maestro with color. Each season, he offered a new palette that encouraged audiences to rethink the rainbow—and how to pair its individual shades. His fall 2022 collection was rendered almost entirely in shocking pink, earning him a Pantone shade dubbed “Pink PP.” His couture collections in particular were celebrations of fashion, often eliciting tears from the audience—most notably from vocal fan Celine Dion. Yes, they were fantastical, but just wearable enough to keep shoppers returning to stores. After roughly 25 years at Valentino, he departed in 2024—then went to Balenciaga in 2025.
Cristóbal Balenciaga founded his namesake label in 1917 in San Sebastian, Spain, reestablishing his couture house in Paris in 1937. Often referred to as an “architect” of fashion rather than a designer, he is remembered for a vision rooted in shape above all. He cast the corsetry of the time aside and dealt instead in the manipulation of volume and silhouette. His creations were both sculptural and fluid, with iconic shapes including the “tunic” and “sack” silhouettes. His legacy was carried on by subsequent directors Michel Goma, Alexander Wang, and most notably, Nicolas Ghesquière and then Demna.
In theory, it could have been a great match for Piccioli. But Demna, who helmed Balenciaga from 2015 to 2025, turned those founding principles upside down. Unlike Cristóbal, he designed with satire top of mind. The designer injected his singular vision from his very early days on the job, creating collections that blended the classic codes of Cristóbal with his own signature high-low, European club-kid aesthetic. In doing so, he transformed the French fashion house into a mega-brand beloved by a spectrum of superfans across both menswear and womenswear. And that is a difficult consumer base to turn away from.
Piccioli had what was undeniably a difficult job, one rather symptomatic of the current state of fashion in which creative-director shakeups have become so commonplace. Three seasons is a very short time to establish a new look for a fashion brand, especially a sales behemoth like Balenciaga. Piccioli is immensely talented, and we look forward to whatever he has in store for us next.
