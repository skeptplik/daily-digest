In short:
The Tasmanian government says the cost of building the Macquarie Point stadium will be $1.23 billion, up from last year's estimate of $1.13 billion.
When "project costs" and precinct infrastructure including a car park and power supply are included, it rises to $1.545 billion.
What's next?
The premier says the stadium and its new cost will not be headed back to parliament for approval.
The total estimated cost of the Macquarie Point stadium precinct has risen to $1.54 billion, the Tasmanian government has revealed.
The 23,000-seat roofed stadium, which is a condition of Tasmania's AFL deal, was approved by the Tasmanian parliament last December.
A new estimate revealed today has put the cost of stadium delivery at $1.23 billion.
But that figure does not include the wider precinct infrastructure costs needed to support the project that the government also announced today.
A car park and power supply for the precinct was budgeted at $132 million, while project delivery costs add another $183 million.
This brings the total to $1.54 billion.
Contingency costs not included
There is also a "client contingency" amount, which has not been revealed or included in costs.
It will cover any potential rise though the final stage of procurement negotiations and, if needed, would bring the project cost even higher.
Until today, the most recent stadium cost estimate had been from September, when the government priced the stadium at $1.13 billion, however this figure included project costs and contingencies.
Last week, the lower house of the Tasmanian parliament passed a motion calling for the "current estimated cost" of both "stadium delivery", and "the broader Macquarie Point precinct-enabling infrastructure", including power upgrades and a centralised car park.
In order to win over the votes needed for the approval order to pass the Legislative Council, the government made a number of commitments, including to limit the state's contribution to $875 million.
Treasurer and Minister for Macquarie Point Urban Renewal Eric Abetz said the government would cover the shortfall from state funds, breaking the promised state contribution cap.
He said the state would then "look to what opportunities there are to gather the funding, and as a result reduce the borrowings that are necessary".
Parliament grills government on costs
In parliament this morning, Tasmanian Greens leader Rosalie Woodruff asked Mr Rockliff if he would keep his promise of a $875 million taxpayer funding cap.
Mr Rockliff responded that the spending cap "was the best estimate at the time".
"I couldn't foresee the conflict in Iran," he said.
"I acknowledge we have to borrow more to see the project through, and we had the best estimate back in September last year so now we're 14 months on with the Iran conflict and cost escalation in everything we do."
Ms Woodruff asked Mr Rockliff if there was "any limit to how much you're prepared to spend or commit of Tasmanians' taxpayer money to get the stadium built".
He did not answer the question, instead saying the government had been "up-front with the Tasmanian people".
A Greens' push for the government to "honour its commitment to cap taxpayer funding for the stadium at $875 million" did not get enough support to pass the lower house.
Treasurer Eric Abetz described it as a "motion designed to kill the stadium". Labor also voted against it.
"We support scrutiny, but we don't support sabotage," Labor leader Josh Willie said.
"The stadium is going ahead and it will be a good thing for Tasmania."
Independent Clark MP Kristie Johnston, who, along with independents Helen Burnet, Craig Garland and George Razay, voted with the Greens and was scathing of Mr Willie and Labor.
"They might as well move across the government benches because they provide no opposition at all," Ms Johnston said.
The federal government has committed $240 million to the Macquarie Point precinct, and the AFL is chipping in $15 million for the stadium.
Despite the US–Iran war pushing up construction costs worldwide, the government has only recently admitted the stadium would likely cost more than estimated last year.
During question time, Ms Johnston asked Mr Abetz whether the government had calculated what the loan financing would cost.
"How much interest will we be paying on the stadium borrowings over the next 10 years?" she said.
But Mr Abetz criticised Ms Johnston and "those that have opposed the stadium each and every step of the way".
"Have you written to federal Labor, have you written to the AFL, have you written to Cricket Australia asking them to make a bigger contribution for the stadium so our borrowings may be less?"
Mr Willie, speaking after question time, said the federal government should pay more, and he called on the state government to go back to the other funding partners.
Oil, material and labour costs blamed for price increase
Premier Jeremy Rockliff called the increases "unavoidable", pointing to high oil and materials prices, higher labour costs and the "complexities driven by the parliamentary-driven order".

He said the state was in conversation with its funding partners about them making larger contributions.
While some MPs had been calling for the parliament to be required to tick off any cost increase, Mr Rockliff said it would not be doing so.
"The parliament had its say last year in early December," he said.
He remained committed to the project.
"Every generation gets a handful of opportunities to shape the future, and the stadium is one of those opportunities,"
he said.
Ms Johnston told 936 ABC Hobart this morning that the state would be "on the hook" for significant amounts of borrowing.
"We will be borrowing $1.29 billion to cover the cost of the stadium … that's a significant impost onto the state's finances, particularly when we are in a budget crisis," she said.
"That's taxpayer's money that will need to be paid back and remembering that the stadium will not be generating sufficient income to even cover the interest payments, let alone the principle."
She said the government needed to explain the different contingencies regarding the estimated costs of the project.
Independent economist Saul Eslake said $1.29 billion would either need to be borrowed directly by the government or by the Macquarie Point Development Corporation (MPDC), which is owned and underwritten by the government.
"The interest cost on that $1.29 billion will, if the interest rate is about 5.5 per cent on average over the period for which that debt is outstanding, is about $71 million per annum, so that's a significant cost," Mr Eslake told 936 ABC Hobart.
"And the government has acknowledged, and I'm not criticising them for this, that there may well be some further increases that are currently still to be negotiated with the company before a contract is finally signed."
Final contract negotiations
MPDC is in the final stage of negotiations with preferred tenderer BESIX Watpac, and hopes to sign a contract for the build by Christmas.
It submitted its final price to the government earlier this month.
MPDC chief executive Anne Beach said a "intensive, rigorous process" had been run to sign a contract this year.
"There are some risks that are carried by us, and some by the builder," she said of the contract.
She said about $100 million had been saved through value management already, and that MPDC would keep looking for efficiencies.
Our Place Hobart, a community group opposed to the stadium project, said the $1.54 billion project figure was not the full picture.
"That's not including a massive interest bill, the northern access road and contingencies," the group's Roland Browne said in a statement.
He said the government was hundreds of millions of dollars above the maximum state funding figure it promised parliament's upper house.
"This is extraordinary. The government and MPDC have lost control of this project," he said.
