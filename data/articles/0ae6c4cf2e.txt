French architecture studio PCA-Stream has completed Centre Pompidou Francilien in Massy, a storage and exhibition space fronted by a gridded timber deck and external stair that echo the high-tech character of the iconic original Centre Pompidou in Paris.
Officially named Centre Pompidou Francilien Fabrique de l’Art (Art Factory), the 30,000-square-metre building is the first Centre Pompidou annexe in Île-de-France. It is scheduled to open to the public in spring 2027.
Overlooking a small lake, the building will house over 150,000 pieces from the Centre Pompidou’s reserve collections made visible to the public through temporary exhibition spaces, alongside private conservation and restoration areas.
It was built around a nine-metre cubic grid described by the studio as “a giant Sol LeWitt” artwork, which creates open, flexible spaces for both storage and exhibitions.
The grid is fronted by an open timber frame and traversed via an external staircase dubbed “the caterpillar”, nodding to the glass-tube escalators at the original Pompidou Centre in Beaubourg, Paris.
“The brief posed a paradoxical question: how do you display what normally remains invisible?” PCA-Stream founder Philippe Chiambretta told Dezeen.
“What truly constitutes the spirit of Beaubourg is not its image but its principle: large, flexible floor plates, made possible by great constructive rationality, which leave the institution free to reinvent its uses over time. That is the heritage we wanted to carry into Massy,” he added.
“The caterpillar, by contrast, is a gesture in the literal sense: a wink, a homage. The great timber stair that climbs the facade quotes the escalator on the outside of the Paris building, like a label of the Beaubourg spirit.”
Across the grid is a shifting gradient of openness, which moves from the more enclosed storage and restoration areas in the centre and eastern half of the building, where the facade is finished with curved metal louvres that control the entry of natural light.
The open timber stair on the western side of the centre connects a cafe and book store on the ground floor to the exhibitions level in the centre of the building and a rooftop terrace above.
“Beaubourg had pioneered the idea of a museum open to the city, with its piazza and its urban, accessible ambience. In Massy, we transposed this generosity of spirit into a different setting, no longer the dense urban fabric of Paris but a landscape of park, lake and trees,” said Chiambretta.
“Visitors never enter the vault, but they experience the whole building as a sequence that leads them from the water up to the canopy,” he added.
Behind the timber grid, the rest of the centre’s exterior has been finished with precast concrete panels, while inside the exhibition spaces sit beneath ceilings covered in black steel rigging.
Several museums in Europe have recently explored combining storage and display, giving visitors varying degrees of access behind-the-scenes. In London, Diller Scofidio + Renfro designed the V&A East Storehouse, while in 2021, MVRDV completed the Depot Boijmans Van Beuningen.
The Centre Pompidou is currently closed for five years as part of a renovation project being led by French studio Moreau Kusunoki Architectes and Mexican practice Frida Escobedo Studio.
