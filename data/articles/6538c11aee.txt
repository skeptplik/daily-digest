A second federal judge has blocked US President Donald Trump's plans to build a massive "triumphal arch" in Washington.
District Judge Leonie Brinkema on Friday said she would issue a preliminary injunction temporarily halting construction of the project - a 250-foot-tall memorial arch inspired by Paris' Arc de Triomphe.
The decision comes after fellow District Judge Tanya Chutkan also temporarily blocked the project from getting underway.
Opponents have questioned the arch's purpose and voiced concerns over its proposed location next to the Arlington National Cemetery - the resting place for hundreds of thousands of US veterans - arguing that it would block the view of the site.
Democracy Forward, which is representing the Arlington County Board in the case, has welcomed Brinkema's ruling.
“We are pleased that the court has recognised the irreparable harm that will come to people who live and work in Arlington and stopped the massive vanity project while our case is heard and considered by the court,” Kate Talmor, a senior counsel at Democracy Forward said in a statement released on Friday. “We are honored to work with the Arlington County Board to stop the proposed arch from jeopardising public safety.”
The National Capital Planning Commission (NCPC) had voted to approve the project earlier this month.
Trump, who has embarked on a campaign to remodel Washington since his return to the White House in January 2025, has said the structure would be turned into a "top grade military complex."
"I have agreed to convert the magnificent Triumphal Arch, planned since the Civil War Era many years ago, at the Receptive Circle adjoining the Arlington Memorial Bridge, into a top grade Military Complex/Triumphal Arch," he wrote in a post on his Truth Social account in September.
He added that it could be used "to house, store, and have the rapid ability to use large numbers of drones, plus Snipers, on both the roof and plaza areas, and additionally have and hold large quantities of sniper ammunition in storage."
