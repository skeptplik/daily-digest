If the past is any indication, the bob may be the most versatile haircut. In 2026 alone, we’ve seen shorter lengths transition seamlessly from spring into summer (think boho-inspired styles and soft, cropped layers), then through to autumn (namely, variations of chic French bobs)—never once wavering in popularity.
Now that winter is here, the same goes. But with wind, rain, and frosty temperatures to reckon with, you might want to make a few swaps to your bob-care routine. “One of the biggest challenges for our hair during winter is the constant change in environment,” celebrity hairstylist Adam Reed observes. “We’re moving between cold outdoor air, which often contains less moisture, and centrally heated spaces, where the air can be incredibly dry. This can leave the hair feeling dehydrated, particularly if it’s already porous, colored, or naturally prone to dryness.”
Reed also names friction from scarves, coats, and hats as a common culprit for static strands and frizzy flyaways, as they disturb the hair cuticle. For him, “winter haircare is about maintaining a balance between hydration, protection, and managing the hair’s natural texture.”
While this doesn’t require a complete overhaul of your typical hair wash and maintenance routine, we’ve compiled four of the easiest, most effective ways to “winter-proof” your bob this season.
1/ Create a Frizz-Free Finish
Winter weather can wreak havoc on a fresh blow-dry. Rich Swindell, co-owner and senior stylist at Dick & Garry’s Hair Salon in Hackney (one of Treatwell’s best-rated salons, and well worth booking into), says balance is key, especially with a bob.
“You want to smooth the hair and control frizz, but you don’t want to overload a bob with product, or it’ll lose all its shape and movement,” Swindell explains. So, use anti-frizz serums with ultra-lightweight formulas—“a little through the mid-lengths and ends helps to keep things looking polished without weighing the hair down”—and always use a heat protectant spray before using a hot tool.
“For anyone with hair that likes to do its own thing, a shaping cream is another great option,” Swindell adds. “Just remember that a little goes a long way, particularly on shorter hair; we want glossy, effortless-looking hair.”
2/ Avoid the Effects of “Hat Hair”
Flat, misshapen hair is a common consequence of removing a winter hat indoors. Swindell adds that this is even trickier with a bob, since there’s already limited length to work with once it’s been sitting under a woolly hat for a while.
“My biggest tip is to make sure your hair is completely dry before putting your hat on,” he recommends, since even slightly damp hair will take on the shape it’s been squashed into. Give a freshly styled bob time to cool completely so it sets before you apply pressure.
Reed adds that before putting on a hat, you can gently move your parting to the opposite side. “When you remove it and return the hair to its usual position, you’ll often find you’ve created a little natural lift,” which helps anyone with fine or thin hair that lacks volume.
“I also love using my fingers to gently massage the roots after removing a hat, encouraging the hair back into shape rather than immediately reaching for a brush. The secret is to work with the haircut rather than trying to completely restyle it every time you take your hat off.”
3/ Consider Your Condition
Garry Swindell, co-owner and fellow senior stylist at Dick & Garry’s, notes that bobs are famously one of the styles where even a small amount of growth can alter the entire shape, especially around the neckline and jaw.
Swindell therefore generally suggests booking a trim every six to eight weeks (depending on the style and how sharp you like your bob) to refresh it. “You don’t necessarily need to take loads of length off; sometimes it’s just about getting that shape back,” he says.
Dryness can also worsen coarse split ends. “The combination of dry indoor air, friction from clothing, and everyday heat styling can leave the ends feeling a little more fragile,” agrees Reed. “I’d also encourage people to pay attention to the condition of the hair, particularly around the nape and sides, where scarves and high collars can create additional friction.”
Consider weekly conditioning treatments to maximize hydration, and don’t forget your scalp, which can also feel the uncomfortable effects of dehydration, by way of irritation and discomfort. Prioritize gentle cleansing and an appropriate scalp-care routine.
4/ Style Strategically
“I’m a big believer that your hair routine shouldn’t become a full-time job just because it’s winter,” continues Swindell. “Nobody wants to spend 40 minutes doing their hair, only to walk straight back outside into freezing temperatures.”
Instead, he suggests placing Velcro rollers around the crown while you’re doing the rest of your beauty routine, then “once you take them out, you’ve got some lovely lift without having to reach for your curling wand.” Or, for a more tousled, “undone” effect, try a texture spray; Swindell says it adds movement without making your hair feel too stiff or overworked.
“Ultimately, the secret to a great winter bob is keeping things simple. Have a good cut, use the right products, and don’t fight your natural texture too much. Your hair should work for you, not the other way around.”
