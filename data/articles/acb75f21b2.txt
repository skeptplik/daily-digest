If you haven’t already caved and bought a fresh set of wireless earbuds, over-ear headphones, or bone conduction headphones this year, Black Friday is rewarding you with a colossal amount of deals, including discounts on the best models available. Apple, Beats, Soundcore, Sony, Nothing, and other major brands are offering some of the best prices we’ve seen on many of their products, whether you’re on a tight sub-$100 budget or willing to spend more (but still want a great deal). We’re seeing some surprisingly great sale prices, especially on noise-canceling earbuds like the AirPods Pro 3 and Soundcore Liberty 5 Pro.
Our favorite headphones and earbuds are cheaper than ever for Black Friday
We’re seeing the lowest prices to date on the AirPods Pro 3, Sony’s XM4C, and even bone conduction headphones.
Bose’s second-gen QuietComfort Headphones build on the original with better sound quality and support for lossless audio over USB-C. They also offer improved noise cancellation and are more comfortable thanks to their lack of “eardrum suck.”
The standard OpenRun are a lighter option than the newer OpenRun Pro 2, with a streamlined wraparound design, up to eight hours of battery life, and an IP67 rating for better water resistance. However, they don’t offer the same bass performance or USB-C charging.
The latest Beats Studio Pro feature improved noise cancellation, sound quality, and comfort. They also support lossless audio over USB-C and offer native support for a range of iOS and Android features like other Beats headphones. Read our review.
We consider the second-gen AirPods Max a worthy successor to the original. The over-ear headphones boast fantastic sound and a few useful features that first-time buyers will likely love. But first-gen owners aren’t missing out on much. Read our review.
The Liberty 5 Pro are Soundcore’s most impressive wireless earbuds yet. At this price, they’re a steal, thanks to their superb call quality and a slew of other features typically reserved for higher-end models. Read our review.
Nothing’s first attempt at pro-grade headphones is a hit. The 1 Pro are comfortable, with great sound from its triple-driver design, and they support Auracast. Their ANC isn’t quite at the level of pricier competitors, but they’re a solid value at the Black Friday price. Read our review.
Sony’s best wireless headphones yet in terms of sound quality and active noise cancellation (plus the return of a foldable design from the XM4), the XM6 also bring a higher price tag. Despite the current (lowest yet) deal, they’re $208 more than their predecessor, the XM5. Read our review.
Nothing’s first over-ear headphones have a retro transparent design and high-end features like active noise cancellation, spatial audio with head tracking, and plush ear pads. They also feature a paddle button that allows you to control music playback. Read our review.
The Studio Buds Plus are priced correctly for the moment. The comfort, sound, ANC, and battery life are fantastic for $90 (and that transparent option is stellar), though some might be best served by spending $60 more for the feature-rich AirPods Pro 3. Read our review.
The Beats 360 are ANC headphones for people who prefer headphones over earbuds at the gym. They’re Beats’ first IP-rated cans, with ear cushions that you can easily remove for cleaning (or style points). They also stay in place during most exercises, while still delivering solid bass. Read our review.
Sony’s XM4 noise-canceling headphones were so good, the company brought them back with a few minor improvements. They still offer great ANC, a timeless foldable design, and performance nearly on par with Sony’s newer flagships at a fraction of the cost. If you don’t need a folding design, opt for the XM5, which are $20 less. Read our review.
Nothing’s second over-ear headphones may not offer everything found on the pricier Nothing Headphone 1, but you do get physical controls, good sound, and more than five days of battery life per charge. Their design is also a bit more discreet, for better or worse. Read our review.
You can keep your favorite jogger or cycler safe while they pump up the jams with these specialized headphones. Shokz’s halo-shaped earpieces pulse sound through the wearer’s jawbone, keeping their ears open. A separate speaker greatly improves bass, while splash resistance and long battery life make them ready for any adventure.
The Powerbeats Pro 2 are some of the best earbuds for fitness. Their ear hook design is comfortable, keeping them snug while you work out, and they have IPX4 water resistance. Like the AirPods Pro 3, they have heart rate monitoring built in. Read our review.
Former Verge reviewer Chris Welch praised this model as being able to go toe to toe with Apple in terms of sound quality, even if they can’t match it with as many features. Still, at $55, you’re getting a fantastic value here. Read our hands-on.
Most companies strictly remove features to cut costs. CMF, Nothing’s sub-brand, kept noise cancellation and great sound in its Buds Pro 2, and added a volume-adjusting dial to its charging case. Novel, yet affordable.
The Momentum 5 support higher-res audio than their predecessors, if you’ve got the right device to connect them to. They carry forth the design and fantastic battery life, but with a Black Friday price cut that cancels out the $50 increase from the last-gen model.
Apple’s AirPods Pro 3 deliver the best noise cancellation and sound quality of any AirPods yet, along with a more comfortable, secure fit and improved water resistance. They also feature built-in heart rate tracking for workouts and support live translations for a handful of languages. Read our review.
Open-style earbuds aren’t just for the sporty; they’re an entirely different way to listen that keeps you environmentally aware without the plugged-up feeling of traditional buds. The OpenFit Pro take a balanced approach, with seamless open-ear performance and even a touch of noise reduction to dampen the din on crowded commutes.
The market is flooded with surprisingly cheap yet good over-ear headphones with active noise cancellation. The Headphone Pro by CMF are certainly some of the best-looking out there, and they come in multiple colors.
Despite having been succeeded by the WH-1000XM6, the 2022 XM5 are still an excellent set of headphones. Their active noise cancellation effect is one of the best out there — competitive with the latest models from Bose and Apple — and the sound profile is tighter and more balanced than the older Sony headphones.
JBL’s Tour One M3 noise-canceling headphones come with a touchscreen-equipped wireless transmitter that lets you wirelessly listen to audio from in-flight entertainment systems and other devices without Bluetooth. They also offer solid active noise cancellation and last up to 70 hours on a charge.
Samsung’s latest high-end earbuds are great, with better ANC and a more consistent sound profile than their predecessor. But unless you plan to pair them with a Galaxy phone, they’re tough to recommend for iPhone or Pixel owners. Read our review.
The latest-gen QuietComfort Ultra Headphones support lossless audio over USB-C and terrific sound with a bit of fine-tuning. They also include a battery-saving feature that powers them down when you lay them flat on your desk. Read our review.
Google’s latest pair of wireless earbuds features crisp sound, a comfortable design, and solid active noise cancellation, which is great considering the budget-friendly price tag. They even offer built-in support for Gemini and, unlike the entry-level AirPods, a purple option! Read our review.
