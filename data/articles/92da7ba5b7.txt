Rakuten Securities, which was hit by a massive data breach earlier this month, has disclosed that the leaked information included the asset balance data of approximately 1.68 million client accounts.
The firm also said Tuesday some 14,000 sets of clients’ self-reported annual income and roughly 2.71 million entries of self-reported assets were compromised. These consisted solely of figures selected by clients from a list of options in questionnaires; it did not contain specific monetary amounts.
The company said it has so far found no evidence of unauthorized access to client accounts or fraudulent transactions related to the incident.
It said that about 4.41 million sets of personal information, including branch and customer codes, login IDs, names, dates of birth, addresses and telephone numbers, had been leaked. The firm initially reported on Nov. 17 that up to 4.47 million data sets were affected by the breach.
Of these, roughly 3.22 million data sets belong to account holders while some 1.19 million were of individuals who have given their information but have not opened accounts.
In addition to the items disclosed in its initial report, the company on Tuesday confirmed seven other categories of information affected by the breach: gender; nationality; fax numbers; information on accounts held with the company; account openings, application processing and cancellation statuses; account application questionnaire responses; and account registration statuses.
The company has also determined that login credentials and branch codes for approximately 11.98 million accounts were leaked during the breach.
It has found no evidence of compromised passwords, PINs or other information required to log in to Rakuten Securities or conduct transactions.
More than 60,000 people had registered their interest in pursuing a class-action lawsuit against Rakuten Securities as of Monday.
Makino Law Office in Tokyo, the firm that is organizing the potential legal action, said that the breach is more serious than other incidents, as it can provide bad actors with a list of people along with their assets and addresses, and can also be used to facilitate scams.
