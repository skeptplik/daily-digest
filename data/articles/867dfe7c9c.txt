Pretxt, with offices in Aspen, CO, and Dublin, Ireland, has emerged from stealth with $5 million seed funding raised from Deciens Capital, Howdy Partners and Service Provider Capital. Pretxt enters the emerging category of firms providing technology to prevent AI agents escaping their guardrails and causing unintended harm.
AI is more like a person than like traditional computer code: it doesn’t simply run; it receives, interprets, misinterprets and responds to ambiguous instructions. It is the combination of misinterpretation and wide agency of response that results in AI agents going rogue and potentially causing harm.
Since it is impossible to categorically exclude ambiguity in language (which is how we instruct the agents), our only alternative method of controlling them is to limit their agency. But too stringent a limitation of agency could become an unacceptable restriction on a potentially huge business benefit.
So, without a new category of supervision control, it is likely that we cannot have beneficial AI agents without also having potentially dangerous rogue agents. This requirement for a new control is leading to the emergence of a type of cybersecurity product that attempts to recognize a potentially harmful agentic action at runtime and control it before it can cause harm.
Pretxt is announcing just such a product which it describes as AI Supervision, “a purpose-built runtime technology to supervise AI agents as they work, guiding them back on course before mistakes become business consequences.”
Rather than neutering the capability of an agent, explains Aaron Costello, Pretxt co-founder and CTO, “We say, ‘Okay, you give an AI agent the same level of access as an authorized employee, and we will continuously monitor the manner in which it interacts with systems and other people. We will validate and ensure that all such interaction conforms with your business standards and operates in the same way that you expect your employees to operate.”
If the agent exceeds its expected authority or deviates from its authorized purpose, Pretxt doesn’t simply shut it down – it ‘steers’ the offender back on track. It’s a bit like having an experienced supervisor permanently on the shoulder of a trainee, who just steps in when necessary, with, “Don’t do it like that, do it like this instead.”
‘Steering’ is governed by context, continues Costello. “We ask new customers to onboard and connect their AI agent to our product, so we can immediately see the agent’s instructions and understand its purpose. As we watch it in operation, we learn how it speaks. We see what tools it can access, and we learn what abilities it has been given – such as ‘send’ an email, ‘post’ to LinkedIn, or ‘update’ a database.”
The customer then explains to Pretxt, I never want my AI agent to do x, or I only want my agent to do y, or see this kind of data. “It could be anything,” adds Costello. “The information is imported into Pretxt’s own AI. It becomes the context for acceptable behavior and is hooked into anywhere within the agent that could potentially violate the agent’s intended purpose and constraints.”
He gives the example of a customer service agent used by a medical practice to help patients book an appointment. Pretxt may have been told by the medical practice, ‘I never want this agent to give medical advice.’ “What we do,” he continues, “is hook into every single occasion the AI agent interacts with the user. Our own agent will look at this interaction and check whether it contains medical advice. This is done in real time. If it does contain medical advice (which we know should not be offered), we prevent it. But not by shutting down the agent, because that would leave patients unable to book appointments. We use a different approach that we call ‘steering’. If we detect medical advice, we invisibly talk back to the agent and effectively tell it, ‘You tried to give medical advice. Please continue to help the user with their booking request, but do not attempt to give medical advice again’.”
This is the purpose of Pretxt’s AI supervision app – not to shut down wanton AI agents, but to keep them safe, and prevent them straying from their predefined constraints.
