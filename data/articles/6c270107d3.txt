Rackspace is shutting down its Rackspace Spot cloud platform after a security breach allowed hackers to access customer Kubernetes clusters and secrets, giving customers until December 8 to migrate their services and download their data.
Rackspace Spot is a cloud platform that allows organizations to deploy and manage containerized applications using Kubernetes.
The company announced the shutdown in a new security notification sent to customers and obtained by BleepingComputer, saying it had decided to discontinue the service after reviewing the incident.
"Following a review of the situation, Rackspace has made the decision to wind down the Rackspace Spot service. This decision is being made to provide a clear path forward for customers," reads the notification.
Rackspace says the service will be permanently discontinued on December 8, 2026, at 5 PM Central Time, when all remaining workloads will be terminated, and customer data will no longer be accessible.
Customers have been instructed to retrieve their data and migrate their workloads to different platforms before the deadline.
The decision comes after hackers breached Rackspace Spot on November 17 using compromised credentials, allowing them to create unauthorized pods in customer Kubernetes clusters, read Kubernetes Secrets, and list nodes and volumes.
Rackspace discovered the intrusion on November 18 following a customer report and disabled access to the platform's administration console while securing its infrastructure. Service was restored on November 21 following an extended outage.
"An unauthorized party gained access to parts of the Spot platform and may have read cluster Secrets and files on storage volumes," Rackspace said.
The company says the affected Spot environment is segmented from its core infrastructure and that it has not identified evidence of unauthorized access to its core systems or the environments of other Rackspace customers.
Rackspace is now instructing all Spot customers to treat their API tokens, refresh tokens, kubeconfigs, and Kubernetes Secrets as potentially compromised, regardless of whether they previously received a breach notification.
Customers have also been advised to review access logs dating back to November 16 and search for suspicious Kubernetes objects associated with the attack, including:
- IP address: 23.224.79.106
- IP range: 23.224.0.0/15
- Suspicious Kubernetes pod and object prefixes: i1, glm-, and probe
Customers urge Rackspace to reconsider
The shutdown announcement has prompted disappointment among Rackspace Spot customers, with some urging the company to reconsider and offering to pay more to keep the platform operational.
In a Reddit discussion, one customer said they believed users would be willing to pay higher prices to keep the service running.
Other customers expressed similar concerns in a separate discussion, with some asking about affordable alternatives and others describing the impact the shutdown would have on their existing infrastructure.
Others praised the service for its affordability, saying alternative cloud providers would be more expensive.
Rackspace has not indicated whether it will reconsider the shutdown decision or offer customers additional time to migrate their workloads.
