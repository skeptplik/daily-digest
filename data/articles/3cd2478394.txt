The Prince and Princess of Wales have said environmental innovators are changing the lives of future generations as they celebrated this year's winners of the Earthshot Prize.
Five award-winners were named at a ceremony in Mumbai, India, and will each receive Â£1m to scale up their projects.
One of the winners was a country - with Bhutan being recognised for its environmental policies that help to safeguard water supplies for nearly a fifth of the world's population.
Indian cricket legend Sachin Tendulkar, actor Rami Malek and Bollywood stars Alia Bhatt and Sonam Kapoor were among those who attended the ceremony, which saw performances from Take That and Bridgerton's Simone Ashley.
The annual event, founded by Prince William and now in its sixth year, was hosted by Karan Johar, presenter of The Traitors India and one of the country's most well-known film makers.
Prince William was joined by Catherine at the ceremony as the royal couple continued their first official overseas trip together in four years. Catherine's cancer treatment has limited the amount she has travelled internationally.
They arrived on the event's green carpet wearing outfits celebrating Indian fashion.
Prince William wore a bespoke Bandhgala, a classic Indian formal jacket, while Catherine was dressed in an Anarkali dress by Mahima Mahajan made from handwoven silk Georgette with traditional Banarasi gold detailing.
During his speech, Prince William said this decade was the moment when positive change becomes irreversible" and remarked that history was "not written by challenges alone", but also by those who rose to meet them.
He added that "around the world, we are witnessing human ingenuity on a remarkable scale".
Separately, Catherine highlighted the "hope" represented by entrepreneurs, organisations and scientists tackling the environmental crisis.
The princess, who presented an award, also said: "Just as the natural world finds ways to adapt and flourish, so too can we, when we nurture new ideas and give them the opportunity to grow.
"The people we're celebrating tonight are a huge part of that story. Their work will inspire and create seismic change that will improve lives for generations to come."
There are five Earthshots, or goals, for which an award is given: Protect and Restore Nature; Clean Our Air; Revive Our Oceans; Build a Waste-Free World; and Fix Our Climate.
Five winners were chosen from a list of 15 finalists who attended Tuesday's awards ceremony.
Earthshot Prize 2026 - full list of winners
- Protect and Restore Nature: Bhutan, the country that helps to protect the water security of 1.6 billion people across South Asia and has a national nature coalition that aims to revive forests and rivers by restoring 50,000 hectares of damaged land
- Build a Waste-Free World: PFAS-Free France, a national campaign that helped secure landmark legislation banning 'forever chemicals' in France by 2030, and hopes to extend the ban to more major countries
- Revive Our Oceans: Niue Ocean Wide Trust, the Pacific island's scheme that has safeguarded marine life by investing in conservation to fund the long-term protection of its vast waters
- Clean Our Air: Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, a scheme rolling out up to one million electric rickshaws across India with the aim to drasitically cut one of the world's largest sources of on-road air pollution
- Fix Our Climate: Ãrsted, a global energy company that has cut its direct carbon emissions by more than 98% by transforming from being highly coal-intensive to a leader in offshore wind
The Earthshot prize, first held in 2020, was inspired by former US President John F Kennedy's Moonshot project, which challenged scientists to get astronauts to the Moon and back safely.
Tuesday's event also saw appearances on stage from Earthshot ambassadors Robert Irwin, the Australian conservationist, and Nomzamo Mbatha, the South African actor.
As well as Take That, there were also musical performances from a number of Indian artists including Shreya Ghoshal, composer and pianist Merlin D'Souza and all-female hip hop collective Wild Wild Women.
The dazzling awards night was brought to a close by musician and composer Shashwat Sachdev.
Earlier on Tuesday, William and Catherine drove in an electric rickshaw to meet the 15 finalists.
As William stepped from the vehicle, which is manufactured by prize winner Mahindra Last Mile Mobility and has a top speed of 55mph, he said: "It was quite good fun, really good fun."
The couple arrived in India on Monday for the three-day visit, which is expected to also include a meeting with India's Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Wednesday.
Their first engagement saw them play a game of street - or "gully" cricket - in Mumbai alongside Tendulkar.
