A threat actor has compromised hundreds of endpoints across dozens of ConnectWise ScreenConnect customers in a fresh campaign, cybersecurity outfit Huntress reports.
The attacks were observed on November 11, when the hackers abused their unauthorized access to cloud-hosted ScreenConnect panels to run commands on managed endpoints.
According to Huntress, the campaign compromised at least 6 MSPs and 288 hosts across 48 organizations. The attackers hit all 288 compromised hosts within 90 minutes.
The attackers’ commands deployed rogue ScreenConnect clients and a malware toolkit called SCKit that provides persistent backdoor access, intercepts session cookies, and can self-replicate.
Initially deployed via ScreenConnect panels, it waits for a technician to connect to the compromised endpoint to infect their workstation. Next, attempts to compromise other sessions the technician has access to and spread itself to other panels.
“However, not all functionality has been observed during observed incidents; some functionality has been discovered through analyzing the payloads,” Huntress says.
As part of the attacks, the hackers exploited a vulnerability in RMM+, a third-party extension for managing ScreenConnect sessions that uses a shared access key to bypass ScreenConnect’s authentication mechanism.
The hackers likely exploited a bug in the extension’s versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32, where the access key defaults to blank if not configured, thus allowing an attacker to bypass authentication with an empty value.
“Our analysis suggests that most RMM+ installations we encountered were legitimate, usually added for a project and left in place. But versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32 contained a serious authentication flaw: if an administrator had never configured the extension’s access key, RMM+ would accept a blank one,” Huntress says.
The cybersecurity firm says its investigation determined that commands reached endpoints within minutes. However, there was no corresponding login activity, and the reviewed logs were linked to RMM+ instead.
In response, ConnectWise disabled five extensions from the RMM+ family across approximately 400 ScreenConnect environments, including Labtech Plus, RMM Plus, RMM+, RMM Plus Password Link, and RMM+ Password Link.
“Their presence alone does not establish compromise. These extensions provide an API capable of executing remote commands. Access to that API is protected by a passphrase configured by the partner during setup,” ConnectWise notes.
Both Huntress and ConnectWise have released indicators of compromise (IoCs) associated with the campaign and recommend that organizations disable the extensions in their on-premises ScreenConnect deployments.
