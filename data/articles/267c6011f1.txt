On Monday, the Department of Health and Human Services issued a long-awaited update to the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act privacy rule. Originally proposed in January 2021, HHS’ Office for Civil Rights finalized several updates to the rule intended to make it easier to share health information — to coordinate patients’ care between health providers, but also to make it easier for patients to access and share their own data.
The HIPAA privacy rule update puts a particular focus on measures to support patients’ access to their own records. “It’s fair to say that two-thirds of the content of the rule is about this problem, the problem of individual access,” said Lucia Savage, former chief privacy officer at HHS’s Office of the National Coordinator for Health IT.
The final rule reduces the maximum time for covered entities to respond to patient requests from 30 to 15 days. It prevents them from imposing unreasonable measures for individuals requesting records to prove their identity. And it establishes standards for accessing those records through a newly defined category of tools called “personal health applications,” which can include wellness apps and tools linked to fitness trackers. Some of those products have been headlined by other federal agencies during the Trump administration, including the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.
