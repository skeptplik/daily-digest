The number of victims of domestic violence was higher in 2025 than ever before, according to new reports from the Federal Criminal Police Office. Violence against women and girls has risen across many categories of offences.
In 2026 at least 125 women were killed in Germany. They were killed by men with whom they were in, or had been in, relationships in which love was supposed to play a role, but which ultimately turned out to be a patriarchal form of gender-based violence.
At least 126 other women survived an attempted femicide in 2025. This is the conclusion reached by the alliance Keine*Mehr alliance, which is calling for protests on 25 November the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women.
The Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) does not record femicide as a separate offence, but reports that 209 women died as a result of domestic violence in 2025, 130 of whom were killed by a current or former partner. In total, 292 people died as a result of domestic violence. There is also a newly established federal-state working group that is developing a definition of femicide and is to collect specific information on it in future.
"Almost every day in Germany a man kills his partner or ex-partner, or tries to," Federal Justice Minister Stefanie Hubig (SPD) told Bild am Sonntag. But not every one of these acts was clearly classified as murder, and there is still no official public statistic on femicides. Hubig wants to change that: the Criminal Code's provision on murder is to be expanded to include femicides.
Femicide to be treated as murder in future
A killing is considered a femicide when a man kills a woman because she separates from him or wants to make her own decisions about her life. There is not yet a legally clear definition. In future, "gender-specific or other inhuman motives" should explicitly be listed as aggravating features of murder, according to the draft bill by the Justice Minister reported by Bild am Sonntag.
Until now, many femicide cases have been classified in court as manslaughter. In legal terms both manslaughter and murder are intentional killings. However, the motives in murder are considered especially reprehensible, including for example killing for pleasure, treachery, cruelty and base motives. This can affect the sentence. Under the new draft bill, killing a (former) partner is to be classified as murder in all cases, no longer as manslaughter.
"Women must be safe from violence and assaults, no matter where and how they are out and about, what opinions they express and what life decisions they make," Hubig stressed at an event on the topic. This is still far from the reality: "Violence and assaults against women are a massive problem in Germany, in partnerships and families, in public spaces and in the digital world," Hubig added.
Situation reports: increases in almost all offence categories
In the past year 54,675 women and girls were recorded as victims of sexual offences, an increase of 2.3% compared with the previous year. Almost half of them were minors at the time of the offence. This is shown by the situation reports published on Tuesday by the Federal Criminal Police Office. In the area of digital violence, such as coercion, threats and stalking, the number of female victims rose by almost 16% compared with the previous year.
"The federal situation reports show that violence against women is a problem in Germany. Far too often women are threatened, abused, humiliated and killed," said Hubig. "We must protect those affected better, hold perpetrators consistently to account and prevent violence at an early stage," said Federal Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt. Concrete support services are to be strengthened so that previously hidden violence also becomes visible.
The figures on domestic violence include both partner violence and violence within the family. Both categories have more victims than in the previous year. Victims of domestic violence are predominantly female at 70.6%, and in cases of partnership violence the share of female victims is even higher at 79.4%.
Federal Women’s Affairs Minister Karin Prien and Hubig both emphasise that further measures, such as tougher penalties for date-rape drugs, electronic ankle bracelets for offenders and strengthening women’s shelters and prevention work, are to be implemented. "And we need a rethink in society, a break with outdated images of masculinity and patriarchal patterns of thought," Hubig concluded.
The proportion of offences reported to the police is below 10% for most of the forms of violence examined, according to further information from the BKA. The study also shows that only 3% of women report sexual assaults.
Sexualised violence against women is everyday reality
The alliance Keine* Mehr and other associations point out that this is a systemic problem. "They know what they are doing," said the organisation Terre des Femmes in a call for protests on Wednesday. According to this, perpetrators drug women in order to rape them while they are unconscious. This sexualised violence and so-called chemical submission is the focus of Terre des Femmes this year.
Just a few weeks ago a perpetrator was convicted at the Berlin Regional Court who, over more than a decade, invited women to his home, drugged them, raped them, filmed the assaults and then shared the recordings on the internet. There was hardly any sign of remorse, on the contrary, the judges spoke of a deliberate exploitation of the imbalance of power.
One in two women experiences partner violence
One in two women experiences violence from her partner at least once in her life, whether psychological, physical, sexual or digital. This was the finding of the LeSuBia dark-figure study by the Federal Criminal Police Office, published in February 2026. While many people picture a partner who beats them, systematically ignoring "no" during sexual acts or devaluing and insulting a partner are also forms of violence.
"Violence against women is not a problem of a lack of caution. It is a problem of violent men," said Nastassja Wachsmuth, Director of Communications and Foundation at Terre des Femmes. Together with the advertising agency KNSK, the organisation has launched the awareness campaign "Every second woman".
"Partner violence is not an invisible fringe phenomenon, it is a reality at the heart of our society," said Kim Notz, owner of KNSK. "We place the socially romanticised picture of relationship happiness directly alongside the bitter reality of violence," Notz continued. Because while some women are given roses, others receive black eyes, as one of the posters says.
Attitudes towards women still poor
According to an international survey, around one in ten male baby boomers believes a wife must always obey her husband. Among Generation Z it is almost one in three respondents, according to a survey by the polling institute Ipsos from March 2026.
"It is worrying to see that attitudes to gender equality are not more positive, particularly among young men," said Julia Gillard of King’s Business School in London, who was involved in the Ipsos survey. According to the survey, 61% of men born between 1997 and 2012 believe that enough has been done for gender equality. Fifty-seven percent of this group even think that men are now discriminated against. In Germany the figures are 55% and 37% respectively.
"This year’s survey shows us that we may be witnessing a major renegotiation of gender roles in today’s society," said Kelly Beaver, Chief Executive of Ipsos in the United Kingdom and Ireland. According to the survey, young men more often endorse traditional hierarchies in relationships and have a problem when women earn more or act independently.
"Digital ecosystems intensify polarisation because social media algorithms reward pointed messages," from masculinity influencers through to feminist counter-narratives, explained Robert Grimm, Head of Political Research at Ipsos in Germany.
By international comparison, attitudes in Germany are more moderate than average. Only one in ten people in Germany believes that women benefit when men stick to traditional male role models. This is the lowest figure among all 29 countries surveyed, according to Ipsos.
