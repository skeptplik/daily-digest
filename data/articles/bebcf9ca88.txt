Florida pastor convicted of U.S. charges in 2021 killing of Haitian president
Moïse killed during nighttime raid on his home in July 2021
A Florida pastor with dual Haitian-American citizenship was convicted Wednesday of U.S. charges arising from the 2021 assassination of Haitian president Jovenel Moïse, a killing that plunged the Caribbean nation into deep turmoil.
Christian Emmanuel Sanon was found guilty on charges including conspiring to kill Moïse after a nearly eight-week trial in federal court in Miami. He had faced a total of eight charges, the most serious one conspiracy to kidnap or kill a person outside the United States.
Sanon showed little reaction as the verdict was announced in a federal courtroom in Miami. He faces up to life in prison at sentencing, which is set for Feb. 10. He remains in federal custody and was taken back to a detention centre after the trial concluded.
Prosecutors declined comment afterward. But Sanon's attorney, Zeljka Bozanic, said the defence team was disappointed with the jury's decision and would file an appeal at the appropriate time.
Haiti has not had a president since authorities said that a squad of mostly retired Colombian soldiers killed Moïse and wounded his wife in a nighttime raid at their home on July 7, 2021. The poorest country in the Americas, Haiti has a history of dictatorship, political upheaval and foreign intervention that has long hindered democratic rule.
Federal prosecutors said at trial that South Florida was a hub for planning and financing a plot to oust Moïse and install a president of the conspirators' choosing. The prosecutors said Sanon was the conspirators' initial choice for Moïse's replacement. They also said Sanon helped raise money for the conspiracy and arranged travel to Haiti and lodging for himself and others in that country.

Sanon had acknowledged in trial testimony that he was in communication at one point with people trying to replace Moïse, but he pointed out that the group had moved on to another candidate long before the assassination. He denied any knowledge of plans to kill Moïse.
Sanon was born in Haiti and moved to the U.S. as a teenager. He testified that he worked with churches in Haitian immigrant communities in Florida and the Midwest.
A general election in Haiti is scheduled next year for a new president. Candidates include the widow of Moïse, a respected economist, a well-known prosecutor accused of multiple summary executions and a former president accused of drug trafficking and sponsoring gangs.

Mercenaries part of scheme: prosecutors
At the trial in Miami, prosecutors said the scheme to kill Moïse included paying about two dozen foreign mercenaries with money obtained from U.S. government programs that were intended to help businesses during the COVID-19 pandemic, according to court records. U.S. prosecutors have charged more than 40 people with crimes related to the assassination and a dozen have been convicted, including five who are serving life sentences.
The plot to oust Moïse began taking final shape in April 2021, when Sanon met in South Florida with a former Haitian senator and several U.S. businesspeople, according to an indictment. The group agreed that Sanon, once in power, would award contracts to two Florida-based companies — Counter Terrorist Unit Federal Academy and Counter Terrorist Unit Security, collectively known as CTU — for infrastructure projects, security forces and military equipment, according to investigators.

CTU initially hired about 18 Colombian nationals with military training to provide security for Sanon, according to prosecutors. Conspirators also spent months obtaining weapons and body armour and attempting to build ties with Haitian gangs, officials said.
By June 2021, the conspirators realized Sanon had neither the constitutional qualifications nor sufficient popular support to become president. They shifted their support to Wendelle Coq Thélot, a former Haitian Supreme Court judge who died in January 2025 while a fugitive.
Sanon was previously scheduled to be tried with four other men who were convicted earlier this year, but his case was separated from theirs and delayed for health reasons.
At the earlier trial, Arcangel Pretel Ortiz, Antonio Intriago, Walter Veintemilla and James Solages were found guilty of conspiring to kill or kidnap the country's elected leader and of providing material support for the plot. They were also convicted of violating the U.S. Neutrality Act and also face possible life sentences. They await sentencing.
Separately, in late September, 18 suspects arrested in the case were extradited from Haiti to the U.S.
