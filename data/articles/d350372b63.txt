Geneva – Countries opened a path Saturday towards finding an international agreement on regulating lethal autonomous weapons systems — though campaigners seeking to stop so-called killer robots were disappointed by the “weakened” outcome.
After five grueling days of talks at the United Nations, which dragged on into overtime, states agreed on building some form of pact over the next two years.
However, with warfare being radically transformed by artificial intelligence, nongovernmental organizations wanted far swifter, legally binding rules on handing power over human life and death to autonomous machines.
