Wallabies fall to Ireland 38-14 in Nations Championship Test to end 2026 on losing note
Wallabies coach Les Kiss admits his side has "work to do" after finishing their 2026 campaign with a 38-14 defeat to Ireland in the Nations Championship at Twickenham.
With the Rugby World Cup in Australia less than 12 months away, the result in London saw the Wallabies end the Test season with a 6-7-1 (win-loss-draw) record.
They finished sixth in the Nations Championship, one spot behind Ireland.
Trailing 17-7 at half-time, the Wallabies were made to pay for several wasted attacking opportunities when camped in Ireland's territory.
They earned repeated entries inside the Irish 22, but a Kalani Thomas try was all they could show for their efforts in the second half.
Ireland, on the other hand, crossed for three second-half tries, a tally which included a double to reserve forward James McNabney.
The Wallabies, ranked seventh in the world, lost five of their last six Tests for the year.
A win over world champions South Africa in September was a rare highlight for Kiss's troops.
"Every team above us [on the world rankings], bar one, we haven't been able to beat," Kiss told Stan Sport.
"We've got work to do."
The Wallabies opened the scoring against Ireland in the 21st minute following Jeremy Williams's converted try.
World number two Ireland, who was coming off a 20-17 win over the Springboks in Dublin, replied on the scoreboard with tries to Robbie Henshaw and Nick Timoney.
Sam Prendergast converted both and added a penalty goal on the stroke of half-time to establish Ireland's 10-point lead at the break.
Robert Baloucoune scored Ireland's third straight try early in the second half and following Prendergast's conversion, the Wallabies were trailing 24-7.
Thomas's try, and Carter Gordon's conversion, closed the gap, but McNabney's brace inside the final 10 minutes of the match helped ice Ireland's winning margin.

Italy triumphs over Fiji
Fullback Tommaso Allan kicked a penalty 13 minutes from the end as Italy avoided the wooden spoon in the inaugural Nations Championship with a 23-21 victory over Fiji in their 11th-place play-off at Twickenham.
It was a first win in the competition for the Italians, who lost all six pool matches, including a 21-16 defeat by Fiji in Udine ‌last weekend.
The result will come as a relief to coach Gonzalo Quesada, who has had to deal with a slew of injuries.
Fiji had its chances to win and will rue ‌a slow start, but there were more signs of improvement under new coach Jake White.
"It was physical, fair play to Fiji," Italy flanker Ross Vintcent said.
"We wanted to end this tournament ‌on a high. We owed it to ourselves, the staff and everyone who has supported us."
We have had a tough six months as a team, but we need to get back to where we were last year.
"It [the win] will ‌be a massive boost in terms of morale and we will have some boys coming back from injury for the Six Nations."
Italy raced into a 17-0 lead thanks to tries from centre ‌Federico Mori and hooker Giacomo ‌Nicotera.
But Fiji’s speed at the breakdown and dominance in ‌the contact areas allowed it to take some control in the match.
The Fijians scored three tries through fullback Ponipati Loganimasi, winger ‌Peniasi Dakuwaqa and number eight Kitione Salawa.
Fiji led 21-20 at that stage but when it conceded a penalty at the scrum, Allan edged Italy back in front.
