Ukraine strikes major Russian bridge with drones and missiles as aerial attacks escalate
KYIV, Ukraine — Ukrainian drones and missiles struck the vital Kerch Bridge linking Russia to the occupied Crimean Peninsula, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Tuesday, calling it an "unprecedented" operation as Russia reported that its forces downed a record number of Ukrainian drones for the second straight day.
Zelenskyy portrayed the bridge operation as a masterstroke following a weekslong effort by Ukrainian forces to gradually wear down Russian air defenses before bombarding the prized target with a barrage of missiles and drones overnight.
Zelenskyy didn't detail the damage, but the Ukrainian General Staff said that an arch and a span of the bridge's road section collapsed, severing the road link, while the railway line was still standing but with a damaged arch.
Russia's Investigative Committee, the nation's top criminal investigation agency, said the bridge was attacked by drones and sustained damage but didn't elaborate.
The Kerch Bridge is a crucial link in moving military and civilian supplies from Russia to Crimea and for supporting troops on the front line. The construction of the bridge, a potent symbol of Russia's illegal annexation of the peninsula in 2014, was inflammatory to Ukrainians.
"The symbol of the occupation has cracked," the Ukrainian General Staff said in a statement.
Moscow's forces, meanwhile, fired ballistic missiles and drones at Kyiv, hitting two of the Ukrainian capital's main bridges and killing at least two people.
A recent escalation in aerial attacks came as Russia and Ukraine await a date for the next round of U.S.-mediated talks aimed at ending the war that began with Moscow's full-scale invasion of its neighbor on Feb. 24, 2022.
U.S. President Donald Trump says he plans to talk about the war in Ukraine during his highly anticipated meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in China this week.
Missiles delivered the final blow in the 7-stage operation
The Kerch Bridge attack was the culmination of weeks of preparatory strikes over seven stages of the operation, Zelenskyy said on social media.
Ukrainian forces knocked out defense systems, radars, electronic warfare equipment and other designated targets before launching Monday night's final attack, he said.
Domestically developed long-range drones struck the bridge's road section during the night while Ukraine's Neptune missiles and other drones aimed at the rail section, he said. Precision-guided Flamingo missiles delivered the final blow, he said.
Zelenskyy and the Ukrainian military published videos showing what they said were the attacks and subsequent damage to the bridge, with part of the deck in the water and a fallen arch. The Associated Press wasn't able to verify the images.
The Russian-installed governor of Crimea, Sergei Aksyonov, said traffic across the bridge was halted but rail traffic was restored by Tuesday afternoon. He didn't mention any damage.
Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said that "there is no doubt that the bridge will be restored and will be even better than it was before."
Zelenskyy noted that Russia has battered Ukrainian infrastructure, especially the power grid, every day. There were significant power outages Tuesday in three Ukrainian regions, he said.
"We have repeatedly offered Russia steps toward de-escalation — and every proposal has been met with loud rejection," he said.
U.K. Defense Secretary Wes Streeting, meeting at NATO headquarters in Brussels with Ukraine's Western backers, said the Kerch Bridge strike was "another example of Ukrainian ingenuity and Ukraine's absolute determination to defend itself against Russian aggression."
The United Kingdom announced £240 million ($317 million) in air defense support for Ukraine this winter, including more than 1,000 short-range missiles and more than a dozen radars.
Russian air defenses downed 1,441 drones during a second straight night of major Ukrainian attacks, Russia's Defense Ministry said Tuesday.
That was a day after Russia said that it downed 1,336 Ukrainian drones in the highest number of interceptions it had reported so far during the war.
Ukraine also attacks industrial areas deep inside Russia
Elsewhere, authorities in Bashkortostan, a Russian region in the southern Ural Mountains, said that defenses repelled a "massive" drone attack on industrial zones in the cities of Ufa and Salavat. All plants there worked as usual, officials said.
However, independent news outlet Astra reported a huge fire at the Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat oil refining and petrochemical complex in Bashkortostan, nearly 1,300 kilometers (800 miles) east of the Ukrainian border. The major petrochemical complex has been attacked a number of times before.
In Moscow, Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said that around 800 drones flew toward the Moscow region overnight. Most were downed far away, and 321 were shot down as they approached the Russian capital.
Russia hits bridges in Ukraine's capital
Seven Russian ballistic missiles hit two of Kyiv's main bridges during the night, Zelenskyy said. Both were closed to traffic.
The Pivnichnyi and Pivdennyi bridges are key commuter routes across the Dnipro River connecting Kyiv's two banks. The left bank is largely residential with numerous apartment buildings and shopping malls. The old town and many government offices are on the right bank.
On Tuesday morning, long lines of traffic formed on a third bridge linking the two sides of the city.
Russian attacks damaged other infrastructure and started fires in four districts of the Ukrainian capital, officials said, as Moscow continued its campaign to make life difficult for Ukrainian civilians.
The Russian Defense Ministry said Tuesday that its forces overnight hit Ukraine's military industrial facilities, energy, transport and port infrastructure, as well as vessels used for Ukrainian armed forces.
Russian forces launched a combined attack on industrial facilities in three districts of the southern Ukraine region of Zaporizhzhia, killing two people and wounding 18 others and leaving a large number of consumers in the region without power, local authorities said.
The possibility of more talks
Meanwhile, the negotiating teams of Ukraine, the United States, and European partners held a working video call Monday in which they discussed the results of recent talks in Abu Dhabi, the capital of the United Arab Emirates, and the agenda for the next stage of negotiations, said Rustem Umerov, head of Ukraine's Foreign Intelligence Service.
"We aligned our positions and our next joint steps," Umerov said on Telegram following the conversation.
Representatives of France, Germany, the United Kingdom, Italy, Poland, NATO and the European Commission joined the conversation.
The United States was represented by envoys Steve Witkoff, Jared Kushner, and Alexus Grynkewich, the commander of U.S. forces in Europe, Umerov said.
