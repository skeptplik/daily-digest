In short:
With Mary Fowler and Kyra Cooney-Cross returning to the squad, Sam Kerr is ready for the Matildas' World Cup preparation to hit the next level.
Fowler and Cooney-Cross have both missed matches recently due to personal reasons.
What's next?
Australia play Colombia twice in the next week ahead of the World Cup draw on December 11.
With no time to waste as the Matildas hurtle towards next year's Women's World Cup, Mary Fowler and Kyra Cooney-Cross's respective returns cannot come quickly enough for captain Sam Kerr.
Australia play world No.19 Colombia in Canberra on Saturday, then again in Geelong next Wednesday.
Fowler missed the last window through personal reasons while Cooney-Cross returns to camp having spent time away for personal reasons since the Asian Cup.
"We only have six games left, so to play together as a team is most important," Kerr said.
"And of course you want to see new faces and you want to see the new talent coming through.
"But you also want to build cohesion and play with the people that probably are mainstays on the team. And Mary, Kyra, all of those girls are.
"And it's obviously harder when you go into a World Cup with less minutes together.
"So we need as many minutes as we can with everyone, and while breeding the new talent."
This month's games mark the final window before the December 11 World Cup draw as the clock ticks down to next year's tournament in Brazil.
Kerr said she had not spoken to Fowler, nor many of her other teammates this window, but was keen to have her back in the fray.
"Of course, Mary's an important part to the team," she said.
"Hopefully she can play well for us."
Facing Colombia, who have established themselves as the second-best South American team behind hosts Brazil, serves as crucial preparation.
"I know it sounds like six months, but we probably spend a month of that together. So we've got to make the most of every game," Kerr said.
"And Colombia is a really good team and a team that we could face at the World Cup.
"They're a different team. We don't normally face teams like this. They play hard. They play a different style of football than we're used to, similar to Brazil and those countries.
"But personally, I want to win the games."
Kerr famously missed most of the 2023 World Cup, where Australia made the semi-finals, with a calf injury.
A horror protracted return from a knee reconstruction wiped her out for almost two years.
"When you see the draw coming up, all of those things, the last few teams of qualifying, this camp over in Europe, it starts to feel real," Kerr said.
"Six months will go like that. The last 3.5 years has gone really quickly, so it seems crazy that 2023 was nearly four years ago, it's gone really quick.
"Just really excited but taking it day by day, kind of focusing game by game. Getting excited though."
Kerr crossed from English powerhouse Chelsea to NWSL team Gotham FC last season and is confident she is primed to deliver for the Matildas.
"I feel good. Obviously, would have liked to have scored a few goals in the play-offs recently, but it didn't work out like that.
"But I've been going for 18 months, so I'm looking forward to a break and a refresh before starting up next season, but two more games to go.
"And I feel confident every time I play for the Matildas because it feels like a really comfortable space for me to come and perform."
