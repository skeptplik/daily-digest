The story so far: Landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW, meaning unexploded or abandoned ordnance) killed or injured more people in 2025 than in any year since 2020, according to the 28th annual Landmine Monitor report, which was released on Tuesday (November 24, 2026) by the International Campaign to Ban Landmines (ICBL). Its findings come as the Mine Ban Treaty, which bans antipersonnel mines, has seen its first-ever withdrawals.
The treaty came into force on March 1, 1999, after a campaign that won the ICBL the Nobel Peace Prize in 1997. Countries that join must stop the use, stockpiling, production, and transfer of antipersonnel mines. They must destroy their stockpiles within four years and clear mines in areas they control within 10 years. A total of 162 countries are now bound by the treaty, while 35 remain outside it.
