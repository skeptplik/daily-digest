The virtual streets of Grand Theft Auto Online’s Los Santos will be spared from their usual explosions and high-speed car chases tonight, as hundreds of players meet for a final journey across the video game’s world before the release of Grand Theft Auto VI at midnight.
Rockstar Games’ multiplayer criminal sandbox normally prompts hostility between its millions of players by giving them ample access to guns, bombs and fighter jets. But fans took it upon themselves to peacefully cruise across the game world together for “one last drive” in virtual California, before the next chapter in the series arrives.
Willy, who goes by zarki_34 online, has played GTA Online for thousands of hours over the last decade. He has organised several of his own final drive events. “[GTA Online] has been part of my life for 13 years … I’m now 26,” he says. “I’ve spent half my life with this game.”
His route took players from the centre of the game’s Los Angeles-inspired city, across rugged coastlines and through so-Cal countryside in one singular trip.
The gamer from southern France livestreams on TikTok and regularly hosts virtual events: from socials where players show off their in-game vehicles to highly choreographed aeroplane stunts. “Some people have been joining for two years,” he said. “Some have become really close friends and I’ve met some amazing people thanks to this game.”
Willy’s social media post about his plans for a final drive in GTA Online quickly went viral. Inspired by the idea, groups across North America and Europe suddenly began to plan their own farewells.
Rockstar is not ending support for GTA Online anytime soon, but as millions of players prepare to move on to GTA VI, Willy wanted to celebrate “an end of era”. “For me, it’s not just about driving cars,” he says. “It’s about bringing people together who share the same passion and creating good memories.”
GTA Online is the multiplayer version of 2013’s Grand Theft Auto V, and was released two weeks after the single-player game. Despite poor performance and lukewarm reviews from critics at the time, years of updates saw the game grow exponentially in popularity and quality while generating billions of dollars in revenue across three generations of video game consoles, starting on the Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3.
Dylan, 34, from north London, remembers playing the game before the kinks were ironed out. “Back then, you’d get kicked out of the server before you could really enjoy it,” he said. After taking years away from the game, the busy dad has recently returned to Los Santos in anticipation of GTA VI. “I love the world building of GTA, their whole atmosphere of the game.”
Dylan has coordinated his own last drive with strangers online, timing it to coincide with the launch of the franchise’s newest entry so that it would “take us exactly to the moment we can press start on GTA VI”.
In GTA Online, players complete missions and activities to grow their criminal empire, working together to plan, prepare and execute high-octane heists. For Jerry, a 20-year-old from Louisiana, this shared experience with up to 30 players at a time is what makes the game special. “People say this community is toxic – it’s not,” he says.
He says he began playing the game alone during Covid, and “started meeting these nice people who would help me along the way”.
Jerry was encouraged to keep playing by a close friend he met on the game, who has since died. “He’s the main reason I’m doing [the last drive], the memory of him,” he said.
While many are preparing to say goodbye to San Andreas after a decade of play tonight, Jerry is holding out a little longer. He has pre-ordered GTA VI, he said, but won’t be leaving Los Santos just yet: “I’m going to stay in GTA Online for at least six months.”
