On Tuesday, midterm general elections were held in the United States to determine all 435 seats in the House of Representatives, as well as 35 of the 100 seats in the Senate. Meanwhile, key governor races were held across 34 states, like New York, Texas, California, Florida, and Michigan.
These specific elections take place every four years during the middle of a presidential term, acting as a measurement of how happy voters are with the president’s current performance. More importantly, they help determine which side of the political spectrum will control Congress, the body of government that creates and passes legislation.
This time, Democrats ended up winning overwhelmingly, gaining seven seats in the Senate and 25 seats in the House, meaning the party is now in control of Congress and has significant power to buck many of President Donald Trump’s measures.
It was a joyous night for the left, it was also a night of many historic firsts for a U.S. election. Keisha Lance Bottoms became the first Black woman to ever be elected governor in American history, while Deb Haaland is the first Native American. Chris Pappas became the first openly gay man ever elected to the Senate, while Abdul El-Sayed is the first Muslim candidate to join it.
Others made history in their specific states. Xavier Becerra is California’s first elected Latino governor. David Crowley is Wisconsin’s first-ever Black governor. Amy Acton is the first woman to ever become governor in Ohio. And that’s just the beginning.
Ahead, we break down all the historic firsts that happened during the 2026 U.S. midterm general elections.
Keisha Lance Bottoms
Bottoms becomes the first Black candidate and first woman to ever be elected governor in Georgia. She also makes history as the first Black woman to ever be elected governor in the United States.
Abdul El-Sayed
El-Sayed will become the first Muslim member of Senate in U.S. history, after he won his election in Michigan.
Chris Pappas
As a current House of Representatives member for New Hampshire, Pappas is the first openly gay man to ever represent the state in Congress. Now, with his Senate seat win, Pappas becomes the first openly gay man to ever be elected to the U.S. Senate.
Deb Haaland
Haaland is set to become the first Native American female governor in U.S. history. Previously, she was one of the first two indigenous women to ever be elected to Congress in New Mexico.
Amy Acton
Acton becomes the first woman to ever be elected governor in Ohio, defeating Republican candidate Vivek Ramaswamy. Previously, she served as the director of the Ohio Department of Health, and was also the first woman to hold that post.
Xavier Becerra
Attorney and politician Xavier Becerra will become the first elected Latino governor of California. The last Latino to serve as governor in the state was José Antonio Romualdo Pacheco back in 1875, who ascended to the role when then-governor Newton Booth was elected to the U.S. Senate.
Allison Montgomery
Montgomery becomes the first transgender state lawmaker in Alabama, in the state’s House District 25 seat.
David Crowley
Crowley is the first Black candidate ever elected governor of Wisconsin. Previously, he served as the executive of Milwaukee County and was the first Black person to serve in that position, as well as the youngest, as he was elected at age 33.
Gina Hinojosa
Hinojosa becomes the first Hispanic candidate ever elected governor in the state of Texas, and she also becomes the first Democrat to hold the post since Ann Richards won in 1990.
Peggy Flanagan
Flanagan, who is a member of the White Earth Band of Ojibwe in Minnesota, becomes the first Native American woman elected to the Senate.
Aaron D. Ford
Ford becomes the first Black governor of Nevada, after previously serving as the first Black attorney general in the state.
Amy Klobuchar
Klobuchar becomes the first woman elected governor in the state of Minnesota.
Melat Kiros
Kiros becomes the first Gen Z woman elected to Congress, as part of Colorado’s first congressional district.
Jaelynn Scott
With her election to the Washington state legislature, Scott becomes the first out Black trans state lawmaker in the country.
