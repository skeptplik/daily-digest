Donald Trump’s defamation lawsuit against the BBC can proceed, federal judge rules
A federal judge has allowed Donald Trump’s defamation lawsuit against the BBC to proceed.
Trump is suing Britain’s publicly funded broadcaster for $10bn over an edited clip that made it appear that Trump told his supporters to storm the US Capitol on 6 January 2021.
In a social media post, Trump called the decision by US district judge Jeffrey Kuntz “A Huge Victory against Fake News BBC, a terrible abuser of the truth”.

Nine members of Kennedy family sue Trump administration over 250ft Trump arch in Washington DC
Nine members of the Kennedy family have sued the Trump administration in an effort to block its construction of a 250-foot triumphal arch.
The family’s complaint states that the arch, which is planned for near Arlington national cemetery, would obstruct the Kennedy gravesites, the JFK Eternal Flame and the sightlines of the cemetery, “permanently diminishing these memorials.” It notes that the arch would also cast a shadow over the Kennedys’ graves, especially in the early morning when the family traditionally visits.
The justice department recently said construction of the arch, which is modelled on Paris’s Arc de Triomphe, would not be completed until after the end of Donald Trump’s second term. On 6 November, a federal judge blocked any construction work from beginning on the arch for 14 days. Trump has claimed that the arch will be converted into a “top grade military complex” replete with drone and ammunition storage space and customised sniper facilities on the roof and plaza.
“This action is about protecting sacred spaces that belong to the American people,” Rory Kennedy and Maxwell Kennedy said in a statement on behalf of the family. “Our parents, Robert and Ethel Kennedy, and our uncles, President John F. Kennedy and Senator Edward M. Kennedy, are laid to rest at Arlington National Cemetery. Ouruncle Joseph P. Kennedy Jr., who lost his life serving our country in World War II, is also memorialized there. They are among the more than 400,000 veterans and family members buried or honored on this hallowed ground. We believe Donald Trump has violated the laws and procedures established by Congress to protect this most sacred national memorial.”
They added that “Americans from every corner of the country visit Arlington to honor those who served and sacrificed for our nation. A towering arch casting shadows across the graves of our father, mother, and uncles, as well as the graves of so many who have served our country, would also cast a shadow over our democratic principles, undermining the safeguards established by our elected representatives to protect this hallowed ground for generations to come.”
The complaint notes that the arch would block the view between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington House, “which were designed to face each other to represent national reunification after the Civil War.”
The family’s lawsuit argues that the National Park Service and Interior Department authorized construction of the arch without needed congressional approval. It asks the court to set aside the authorization and block construction of the arch.

House Republicans block Pete Hegseth impeachment vote
House Republicans on Tuesday blocked an effort to impeach Pete Hegseth, rejecting allegations that the defense secretary had repeatedly violated the constitution through his handling of the Iran war and other conflicts globally.
A motion to refer Thomas Massie’s eight articles of impeachment against Hegseth to committee passed in the House, 211-204.
The vote fell largely along party lines with 211 Republicans voting in favor of the resolution and 203 Democrats voting against it. Massie voted against the resolution alongside Democrats.
A new Reuters analysis shows that the Securities and Exchange Commission brought the fewest enforcement actions in fiscal year 2026 in 35-years.
The SEC said Tuesday it brought 348 enforcement actions in 2026, the lowest number of cases since 1991. According to Reuters, in 2025 the agency brought 456 cases – the lowest number since 2008. The SEC oversees more than 30,000 brokers, funds and other financial firms.
“The new Democratic majority in Congress will closely scrutinize whether [current chair Paul Atkins’] SEC is protecting the American people – or working for scammers and Trump’s billionaire buddies on Wall Street,” senator ElizabethWarren, who is seen as likely to chair the Senate Banking Committee next year, said in a statement about the enforcement slowdown.
