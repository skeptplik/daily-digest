A very specific coat has taken the internet and fashion people alike by storm. Conner Ives’s robe coat looks like a piece pulled from a 1920s opera-house coat check, and everyone from Sienna Miller to Zoë Kravitz has worn iterations of it. First introduced in his fall 2025 collection, it pairs antique silk with reclaimed fox at the lapels and cuffs, and sells for a cool $9,000. And it’s in extremely high demand.
With searches for the designer up 967% on The RealReal, vintage dealers racing to list their versions, and creatives DIYing it at home, it’s safe to call this the coat of the season. While the Ives version might top many of our wish lists (and budgets), its essence is rooted in the feeling of a bygone era—maybe one we’re craving more than ever these days, if the style is any indication.

“The coat taps into a growing appetite for pieces that feel expressive and one of a kind, qualities that have always made vintage compelling,” says Noelle Sciacca, head of fashion at The RealReal. “We’re seeing a real shift toward everyday opulence, rich textures, and a sense of history. The Conner Ives coat captures that mood. It feels glamorous and eccentric but still effortless enough to throw over jeans.”
The proof for demand is in the data. According to Sciacca, searches for fur-trimmed jackets and coats are up 33% year over year, while jacquard searches have increased 60% and tapestry prints have surged 170%. “What’s great is shoppers don’t necessarily need the exact coat to capture the look,” Sciacca says. “They’re exploring vintage and archival pieces across designers and decades, looking for ornate prints, beautiful fabrics, and unexpected details.”
Retailers are also quickly catching on with similar-style coats from Ciao Lucia, Rixo, Dôen, and Maison Essentiele selling out within days of appearing live on their sites. “I had a gut feeling that it [the Taimi Satin Bouquet coat] would be a top seller,” Lucy Akin, founder of Ciao Lucia, shares. “It was validating to not only see it sell out so quickly but also have my entire Instagram feed flooded with these vintage-inspired jackets. They are having a moment, no doubt!”
Stylist Bailey Moon echoes this. “I think there’s a real appetite for clothes that have personality again,” he says. “The Conner Ives coat taps into a sense of nostalgia, but it also feels playful and a little irreverent, which I think is part of its appeal.” That’s also what separates it from the Penny Lane coat. While that style reads bohemian and casual, this one trades suede for silk and leans closer to eveningwear, so the rest of the outfit has less to do.
“Fur and shearling inherently have a sense of drama, so I prefer to let the coat be the centerpiece and keep everything else relatively understated,” Moon shares. “A great pair of trousers, beautiful knitwear, or even a simple dress can make the look feel incredibly modern. I love the contrast of something quite extravagant paired with something very utilitarian.”
But the appeal extends beyond ornament. Memory and timelessness play a part too. Nour Hammour’s Sima coat was designed after founder Hammour’s grandmother’s coat and the way her grandmother carried herself in it. “It is the balance between exceptional materials, craftsmanship, and a silhouette that will remain relevant long after the season has passed,” shares marketing director Zeina El Zein Hammour. “The quality of the shearling, the depth of the trim, and the way the coat falls on the body are what distinguish a piece that you’ll keep for decades.”
For vintage hounds, it’s also a real marker of the shift away from all-neutral everything that’s dominated for so long. “After years of quiet luxury and minimalism, I think people are ready for a little romance and extravagance,” says Susan Kim of Estella NYC, who sells similar vintage iterations on her site and at vintage fairs.
Secondhand enthusiast and influencer Katie Casper tried her hand at re-creating the coat using pieces she already owned: a Shanghai Tang silk jacket from her mother-in-law and a removable fur stole from an old Zara coat. “In its simplest terms, the coat is a colorful brocade base with a fur trim,” she says. “Both are easy to source secondhand.” The trickiest part was proportion, since the stole overpowered the jacket; she said she’d choose a narrower one next time.
Whether the style lasts beyond this season is another question. “I fear we may all be a little exhausted by 2027,” Casper muses. But Moon sees it differently. “A beautiful statement coat can live in someone’s wardrobe for years,” he says. “The question is whether you’re buying it because you genuinely love it or because you’ve seen it on everyone else. The best clothes should still feel exciting to wear long after they’ve stopped being the thing everyone is talking about.”
