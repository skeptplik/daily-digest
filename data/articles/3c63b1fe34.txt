A Canadian astronaut aboard the International Space Station is celebrating the birth of his third child from hundreds of miles above Earth.
Joshua Kutryk and his wife, Heather Kutryk, welcomed their daughter as the astronaut takes part in a six-month space mission that began in October, the Canadian Space Agency announced on Tuesday.
"Even with the distance between us, we feel incredibly connected as we welcome our new little star into our lives," said the couple in a statement shared by the agency.
Joshua Kutryk, currently on his first space mission, is just the fourth astronaut in history to miss the birth of a child while aboard the International Space Station.
The couple have two older children, aged five and seven.
"We are very proud of our growing family and are looking forward to getting our little crew of five all reunited."
In a statement, the couple said that Joshua's spaceflight was a "childhood dream" come true.
"We hope to show our children that big dreams are worth pursuing, that hard work and determination matter, and that with the support of family and a strong community, extraordinary things are possible," they said.
Prior to becoming an astronaut, Kutryk, from Fort Saskatchewan, Alberta, was a fighter pilot and a colonel in the Royal Canadian Air Force. He was recruited by the Canadian Space Agency in 2017.
Kutryk was the only Canadian selected to be part of NASA's SpaceX Crew-13 mission, where four astronauts are conducting scientific experiments and testing new technologies in space.
The crew also includes two US astronauts, Luke Delaney and Jessica Watkins, and one Russian astronaut, Sergey Teteryatnikov.
Before their take off on 1 October, Heather Kutryk told Canada's public broadcaster CBC News that her family had been preparing for years before the mission, saying it was "a long time coming".
Heather added that her pregnancy was "not exactly planned" but that it was "a great thing".
"I'm not that stressed out. I feel like we have a really good community. I feel well supported by both CSA and Nasa," she said.
"It's six months, I feel like we'll be fine."
In a separate interview with BBC's US news partner CBS News, Joshua Kutryk said he hopes his new child will look at her dad's mission as "making some sacrifice, taking some risk, but doing so in the name of public service and the name, in my case, for Canada."
