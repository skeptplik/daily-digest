In the space of just two days this weekend, Bayern Munich were held to a 1-1 draw by FC Cologne, saw Ballon d'Or winner Harry Kane ruled out until the new year with a serious ankle injury and lost first place in the Bundesliga to rivals Borussia Dortmund.
But even those setbacks – as well as the return of head coach Vincent Kompany to the sideline following health issues – have been overshadowed by explosive public comments made by Bayern club bosses regarding young attacker and Germany international Jamal Musiala.
What is wrong with Jamal Musiala?
Back in July 2025, Musiala suffered a fractured fibula and a broken ankle at the Club World Cup.
Since then, the 23-year-old has been battling to recover not only from that injury but also with a debilitating neurological condition diagnosed as "absence seizures" which led him to collapse on the pitch in two consecutive pre-season games in August this year.
Musiala has struggled to replicate the sort of form which led to a well-remunerated contract extension in January 2025, prior to the ankle injury and the seizures.
And on Sunday morning, German media including tabloid BILD and broadcaster Sport1 reported that Musiala had suffered another seizure as recently as last Monday, believed to be a result of an adjustment to his medication.
Musiala was deemed fit to feature against Cologne on Friday but the most recent incident prompted a series of controversial public remarks from the Bayern hierarchy seemingly questioning the validity of that contract.
What have Bayern Munich officials said?
"It is well known that he has a contract which contains some considerable sums," former chief executive and current supervisory board member Karl-Heinz Rummenigge told broadcaster Sky on Friday evening following the 1-1 draw with Cologne, in which Musiala had made a 20-minute substitute appearance.
"It's in all of our interest that he comes back, ideally in top form, but what happens if that's not the case?" asked Rummenigge, adding that Musiala's condition "leads us to suspect, unfortunately, that it will be difficult [for him] to return to the level that he has often shown in this stadium" and that "then we of course have to think about the finances."
Rummenigge suggested a need for Bayern to hold talks with Musiala, his family and his representatives because "such sums are, with respect, no longer justifiable."
Asked whether Bayern would consider selling Musiala, Rummenigge said he considered it unlikely that any club would be prepared to buy the player out of his new contract, which is set to run until 2030 and which reportedly sees Musiala earn up to €30 million per year.
"I cannot imagine that Jamal would leave the club or that any other club would buy him under these conditions," said Rummenigge. "And that is a problem."
Rummenigge was not the first senior Bayern official to express such doubts. Earlier this month, former club president Uli Hoeness had told local broadcaster Bayrischer Rundfunk that Musiala's situation presented the club with a "dilemma."
"He has an illness which cannot be cured but which can be somewhat controlled through medication," said Hoeness, who also sits on Bayern's supervisory board, questioning "whether he will ever regain the ability he once had" and stating that "he continues to receive money."
Bayern chief executive Jan-Christian Dreesen has also weighed in, telling Munich's Abendzeitung newspaper last week: "We all have a responsibility towards the club and we all hope that Jamal will come back. Of course this will take time. We will assess the situation and speak to his family and management. Either we'll all be happy that the old Jamal [will return] or, if he becomes a different [player], we'll have to hold talks."
FIFPRO: 'Bayern should know better'
The comments by Hoeness, Dreesen and Rummenigge landed like a bombshell in Germany and beyond.
The global players' union FIFPRO issued a statement on Saturday slamming what they called "a coordinated strategy to put pressure on Musiala and his representatives ahead of any negotiations," highlighting Bayern's "duty of care" towards their player, and stating: "A club of Bayern's standing should know better."
"Across professional football, uncertainty about a player's future can take a toll on their mental health, particularly when they are recovering from injury," the statement continued. "Making Musiala's health and future a matter of public debate adds to that pressure. Contracts are there to be respected. An injury or a dip in form gives a club no grounds to reopen terms it agreed to, just as a run of outstanding performances gives a player no automatic right to a pay rise."
Former Bayern Munich and Germany defender Lothar Matthäus also criticized the club, saying in his role as a pundit for Sky: "I've been a bit shocked throughout the entire week. It started with Uli Hoeness, continued with Jan-Christian Dreesen, and yesterday Karl-Heinz Rummenigge chose the strongest words of all. I have enormous respect for Karl-Heinz. I've known him for over 30 years. But he chose those statements very deliberately. The person who suffers most from all this is Jamal Musiala."
The comments by the Bayern bosses also came just hours ahead of the club's annual general meeting on Saturday, where club members – who hold a majority 70% stake in the club in accordance with German football's "50+1" ownership rule – took to the stand to criticize the club's hierarchy.
One member, professional neurologist Jonatan Hoffmann, expressed his "absolute respect" for Musiala and said that patients suffering from such conditions require "support."
He said Musiala had received such support from head coach Vincent Kompany and director of sport Max Eberl, but said Hoeness', Dreesen's and Rummenigge's comments were "not helpful" and had "little to do with the much talked about Bayern Munich family" – earning considerable applause from fellow members.
Bayern president: 'Musiala will get time and support'
Bayern president Herbert Hainer, the club's most senior elected official, attempted to deescalate the situation, responding to Hoffmann by saying: "Health and difficult situations are fundamentally always taken into account at FC Bayern."
Indeed, Bayern have recently been praised for their considerate handling of captain Joshua Kimmich following the unexpected death of his father, and of head coach Kompany, who returned to the touchline against Cologne on Friday following a month-long absence after collapsing due to a particularly serious viral infection.
"When it comes to Jamal, we all want the old Jamal back as he was before his injury," said Hainer. "And he will receive all the support and time he needs from the club."
Speaking on the sidelines of the meeting, chief executive Dreesen also attempted to row back on his comments, insisting to reporters that he and his colleagues had not been trying to put Musiala under pressure.
"We all want Jamal to return to the old Jamal. He needs time. We've said that several times. And he must get that. And then we'll see how things look in the new year," he said, adding that there was no need "to talk about the contract."
President Hainer also dismissed questions from journalists as to whether Bayern felt they had been misled by Musiala's agents before offering him a new contract, inferring that the player's representatives may have known about his neurological condition.
"I absolutely do not think that anyone knew beforehand," Hainer insisted.
German media criticize Bayern
But the public criticism has continued. Leading German sports publication Kicker said it was understandable for the Bayern bosses to be frustrated but called their approach "irresponsible" and said their anger "harms the wrong person" in Musiala.
"He can't help his injuries or neurological condition," wrote editor-in-chief Matthias Dersch. "Musiala is only 23 … and is in the most difficult phase of his career. We cannot imagine how he must be feeling, his insecurities and perhaps also fears about his health and his future. And no: the amount of money he earns is no cure."
German football magazine 11Freunde said the Musiala case offered "a glimpse into the abyss of professional football … an unembellished look behind the scenes of a business which is generally portrayed only as glamorous and exciting … into a shadow world where human beings are damned to function … where the value of a person is measured only by their ability to perform over and over again … That's football: capitalism in its purest form."
Meanwhile, the business of football continues. Borussia Dortmund capitalized on Bayern's draw to go top of the Bundesliga with a 1-0 win away at Borussia Mönchengladbach, Harry Kane underwent an operation on a torn ankle ligament but will be out until the new year, meaning he will miss Bayern's trip to Lille in the Champions League on Wednesday.
And the young Jamal Musiala will continue to battle the effects of serious physical and neurological conditions.
