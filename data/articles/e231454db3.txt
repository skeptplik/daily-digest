The late Pope Francis suffered a spiritual crisis during his papacy when he struggled with how to lead the Catholic Church and believed he saw and heard the devil on several occasions, according to an annotated collection of his private diaries published on Tuesday.
Francis was pope for 12 years until his death last April.
Cardinal Victor Fernandez, a compatriot and close collaborator of Francis', collated the diary entries and provides accompanying commentary, in the first set of papal diaries to be published posthumously since those of John XXIII, who died in 1963.
What does the book say about Francis' difficulties as pope?
The diary entries speak of the Argentine-born pontiff not wanting to be someone "who uses others like puppets," according to the accompanying commentary.
"It was all mixed with governance issues, pressure regarding decisions to make, and aridity and desolation," a December 2022 entry from Francis said.
Although the book does not make clear exactly who Francis worried he was using like puppets or the nature of the internal pressures, Francis encountered stern resistance from more conservative forces within the Church as he urged the staid institution to modernize.
Cardinal Fernandez wrote that Francis "ultimately recognized it was the worst trial that had toremented him ... and that he felt that he was in a continual battle not to fall into this demonic seduction."
What does the book say about Francis' religious visions and dreams?
The 160-page selection of excerpts — starting in 1992 when he was still known as Jorge Mario Bergoglio and running until a few months before his death — are arranged by theme and pay closer attention to Francis' internal spiritual experiences than Vatican politics.
But one of the three occasions listed where the late pope believed he had seen the Devil was during a meeting with Vatican officials in January 2021 early in what he termed a spiritual crisis. In that instance he even believed to have heard the figure speak.
"I can't forget the powerful raspiness of his voice," Francis wrote in an entry. "The Lord granted me an opportunity to leave that meeting, and thus he freed me."
Other entries detail how Francis believed that God spoke to him in his dreams, and one from 2018 stated: "Last night I saw the entrance to Hell. I peeked in to look a little, and it terrified me."
What else features in the diary excerpts?
The papers show Francis to be self-critical at times, chastizing himself for "feelings of disdain, envy, pride" or for ignoring a drunken man who approached him "because I wanted to have a serene walk."
At one point in a 2022 entry he refers to himself as the "proud, self-sufficient, cynical, self-deceptive Jorge."
They also show how in later years he felt anxious to make the most of his remaining years, and regretful about some of his deicsions earlier in his life.
"I want to do good during the little time I have left, desiring to be good," he wrote in 1996 on turning 60. "How much time I've used badly! How much badly done!"
The accounts also list his regret after a visit to Chile in 2018 when he faced stern criticism for initially dismissing allegations of sexual abuse and cover-up against a bishop there before later apologizing.
"It was hard for me to admit what happened in Chile; I didn't think it was so serious," a "devastated" pontiff wrote three years later.
Fernandez also recounts comments from Francis on some senior officials in the Vatican including his successor, US Cardinal Robert Prevost, now Pope Leo XIV. According to Fernandez, Francis called him "a great pastor, a generous missionary, and an irreproachable man."
