Russian incursions into NATO territory are increasing
NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte is seeking to reassure the alliance's eastern European members after three Russian cruise missiles landed in Poland on Friday.
SCOTT DETROW, HOST:
Russian incursions into NATO territory are increasing. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte is seeking to reassure the alliance's Eastern European members after three Russian cruise missiles landed in Poland on Friday. While no one was hurt, Rutte was in Germany today, where he addressed growing concerns. And for more, we are joined now by Esme Nicholson in Berlin. Hey, Esme.
ESME NICHOLSON, BYLINE: Hey, Scott.
DETROW: The cruise missiles hit Poland during one of Russia's biggest aerial attacks on Ukraine since the start of the full-scale invasion. Ukraine then hit back over the weekend. So first, bring us up to speed on that.
NICHOLSON: Well, as you say, the risks of the war in Ukraine spilling over were loud and clear over the weekend. It started on Friday morning with what Polish authorities have now identified as three Russian cruise missiles striking a rural part of southeastern Poland, about 30 miles from the Ukrainian border. Now, as you say, there were no casualties, but the incursions happened while European forces were staging a military exercise in the region. And Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk calls the incident an "intentional provocation" - that's a quote.
Then on Saturday, a sanctioned Russian cargo ship in the Baltic Sea was hit by what is suspected to have been a Ukrainian maritime drone. And on Sunday, Ukraine took credit for sinking a Russian warship docked in the Libyan port of Benghazi, thousands of miles from the front line. Libyan authorities have condemned the attack in their port, reflecting broader concerns that the conflict could spread far beyond Europe.
DETROW: Right. And that is a broad battlefield that you're talking about right there. NATO's chief was in Berlin today. Does he view this as the beginning of an escalation beyond Ukraine's borders?
NICHOLSON: Well, Rutte was rather cautious in his assessment of the weekend. He was in Berlin for an annual meeting, talking to parliamentarians from NATO countries. And while he acknowledged the danger of cruise missile incursions, he was eager that Polish authorities finish their investigations to establish whether they were intentional or not. Speaking to journalists, he said the alliance has, quote, "many response options" (ph) but that he can't always be open about them. But he also appealed for public support for Ukraine.
(SOUNDBITE OF PRESS CONFERENCE)
MARK RUTTE: As societies, we have to be resilient and, most importantly, keep that support for Ukraine going. So for every incident in Europe, more support for Ukraine, not less support for Ukraine.
NICHOLSON: And Zelenskyy, who dialed into the meeting in Berlin today, urged for fresh negotiations with Russia, this time involving the Europeans.
DETROW: Let's talk more about the Europeans. What do you think the odds are that they'll end up playing a role in bringing about the end of this war?
NICHOLSON: Well, since last week's coalition of the willing meeting here in Berlin, it's looking a little more likely. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and European leaders warned the U.S. and Russia against going over their heads. And today, Merz stressed that Europe is ready to sit at the table, something U.S. special envoy for peace missions Jared Kushner supports. But Merz also made it clear that no NATO member, quote, "has any interest in an escalation in the Baltic Sea region," which some see as an appeal to Ukraine to do its part to prevent the conflict from spreading.
He vowed that European leaders would discuss boosting defense on NATO's eastern flank at an EU summit later this week. And he was also quite candid in stating that NATO made a mistake back in 2008 when it decided not to make Ukraine a member, saying that if Ukraine had been a NATO member, this war would probably have been preventable.
DETROW: That is Esme Nicholson in Berlin. Thank you so much.
NICHOLSON: Thank you, Scott.
