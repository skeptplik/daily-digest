The government’s independent adviser on antisemitism has said he is launching an investigation into the BBC after hearing “disturbing” accounts by Jewish staff about discrimination against them.
John Mann, the Labour peer and former MP, said BBC whistleblowers had told him that they faced a “hostile work environment” at the broadcaster and that his previous warnings to its management had gone unheeded.
His decision to investigate the BBC comes after a recent attempt to reassure Jewish staff that discrimination against them would not be tolerated, after a former producer said she had faced antisemitism while employed at the corporation.
Lucie Kon, the former commissioning editor for the BBC’s Storyville documentary series, was paid a six-figure sum by the corporation after her departure.
Kon said she had faced antisemitism after the 7 October 2023 attack on Israel by Hamas. The BBC said its internal processes did not uphold her antisemitism claims, but acknowledged “shortcomings, and the personal impact” on her.
The BBC has rejected calls for an independent inquiry in response to the case.
Lord Mann said his decision to launch an investigation came after hearing from BBC staff with concerns. The review does not appear to have been ordered by the government, but the peer said he would report his findings to the BBC and ministers in the spring.
In an interview announcing his review, Mann suggested that diminishing the voices and perspectives of Jewish staff “is institutional”.
“The things that the BBC have done to try and sort things out have not worked,” he told the Times. “People do not feel confident in raising it within the BBC … There is a pressure in the BBC to show that you are a ‘good Jew’.”
However, speaking this morning on the BBC’s Today programme, he said there “may be” an institutional problem and that he was going into his review with “an entirely blank sheet of paper”.
“There is a problem,” he said. “The question is, is the problem just a few people? Is it a problem that’s easily solved? But if it’s an institutional problem, that’s much deeper and more difficult to solve.”
A BBC source said that the corporation had not been notified of any investigation and so could not comment on its specifics.
A BBC spokesperson said: “We take any and all allegations of discrimination extremely seriously – there is no place for it at the BBC. We have introduced compulsory training on antisemitism for all our staff and we actively encourage colleagues to report any incidences or concerns they have.
“We are determined to ensure the BBC is a place where everyone can be themselves and feel accepted.”
Kon said she welcomed the review. She said she had been failed by the BBC’s internal processes.
“I’m not alone,” she said. “Jewish staff at the BBC are struggling, and many are too frightened to speak up. The BBC needs Jewish staff, just as it needs people from every part of the country. I love the BBC. That’s why it has to change.”
