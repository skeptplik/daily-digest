Think you’ll want to replay some of GTA 6’s most exciting missions? Well, you won’t be able to simply do that from any of its menus.
Now, not every game allows you to do this, so Rockstar’s latest is by no means an outlier in this regard, but seeing as practically every one of the studio’s games since The Ballad of Gay Tony expansion for GTA 4 has allowed you to do so, it is a little surprising.
GTA 6 is a little different from other games in the series, though. Its story flows and branches in small and big ways in a fluid manner not quite seen in any of the developer’s other open worlds. I’ve only completed one full playthrough myself, but can confirm that whole missions can play out differently based on decisions you didn’t even necessarily know you were making hours earlier. For example, one of GTA 6’s best missions, a daring high-stakes heist, can go relatively smoothly or turn out to be a much messier operation.
What’s most exciting is that, now knowing this, I’m left wondering how many new things I’ll see in the second playthrough I’ve just started, as much as I am looking forward to replaying the missions I can’t easily access. The decision by Rockstar not to include the option to revisit them at launch may well prove controversial, and who knows if it will be added post-launch, but I can see the argument as to why they’ve decided to do it this way.
With so many moving cogs and gears working away in the background to direct Jason and Lucia’s story down certain paths, both within main missions and seemingly random events in the open world, going back to change how certain things played out may prove problematic for its flow. With no medal system implemented — another Rockstar staple that grades your performance on a scale of bronze, silver, and gold — you’d want to replay these missions purely for the fun of it rather than chase high scores or personal best times.
There is one more traditional way around this problem to an extent, though, if you really do feel the urge to revisit moments: save files. GTA 6 gives you room for 15 manual save slots, and I’d highly recommend making a new one every 7% or so through the story so you’re never a million miles from reaching a point you may want to return to. With a main story that can take anywhere from 50 to 100 hours to complete depending on your playstyle, it’s always handy to have the opportunity to return to specific chapters.
