Jensen Huang’s reinstatement as Nvidia’s chief executive is “exhibit A” of the “political corruption and rot at the heart of the Trump administration’s big tech and AI policy”, Chris Van Hollen, the Maryland Democrat vying to become the next Senate majority leader, told reporters Monday.
Van Hollen said he intends to focus on Huang’s “efforts to increase and accelerate the export of high-end chips to China, how he’s been accomplishing that, how he’s been getting his way with the administration” when the reinstalled Nvidia CEO appears before the Senate banking committee on 3 December.
It will be one of several appearances on Capitol Hill next week where skeptical lawmakers from both parties will grill Huang over his ties to a woman that some in the intelligence community have said was a spy for China. Huang will also sit for transcribed interviews with the House foreign affairs committee, as well as the select committee on the Chinese Communist party and the House intelligence committee.
“It’s completely hypocritical for the Trump administration, on the one hand, to say the reason we have to take all the brakes off AI development here at home is because China will leap ahead if we don’t,” Van Hollen said. “When in fact, it’s the Trump administration that’s helping China leap ahead.”
Nvidia’s board reinstated Huang after Donald Trump intervened on the executive’s behalf, assuring company leadership that he did not pose a national security risk despite questions surrounding his relationship with Alice Huang, a tech executive of no relation who has been accused of acting as a Chinese asset and routing advanced chips to Beijing via her data center company, Megaspeed.
In an interview with CNBC on Monday, the US president defended his involvement in Huang’s reinstatement, arguing it would be a “bigger threat” for Nvidia to lose Huang’s “brain power” in the AI race. Trump also confirmed that he had provided the board with assurances regarding any national security concerns.
“I spoke to them, let me put it that way. And I convinced them,” Trump said.
Van Hollen pointed to a series of ties between Huang and Trumpworld as evidence of the executive’s influence over administration policy on advanced chips, including Nvidia’s $25m donation to Trump’s White House ballroom project, his personal holdings of Nvidia stock, Huang’s contribution to Trump’s inauguration fund and his attendance at a $1m-a-plate dinner at Mar-a-Lago.
The outrage spread on Capitol Hill on Monday. Gregory Meeks, a Democrat congressman from New York and the ranking member on the foreign affairs committee, called the decision “yet another example” of how “the wealthy and well connected are held to a different standard”.
John Curtis, a GOP senator from Utah, said that Huang was “right to step down” when news of his extramarital affair came to light. “Just 11 days after Nvidia announced his departure, the board brought him back while its own independent investigation was still under way. Last week, the board said it didn’t intend to reinstate him. I’d like to know what changed,” Curtis said.
For Matt Pearl, a director at the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) who specializes in national security, geopolitics and technology, unanswered questions surrounding Huang’s conduct remain.
“We haven’t had an investigation,” Pearl said in an interview, noting that such a probe would normally take “at least a year”.
The Nvidia board, he argued, has instead relied on the “representations” of senior US government officials, including Trump himself, and is “essentially outsourcing the investigation”.
“I suspect that we’ll find out a lot more information in the coming weeks and months as Congress investigates, but it just seems that we have no idea what sort of the risks and threats were in this case,” Pearl said.
The episode, he added, could weaken how national security concerns are raised and acted upon inside government.
“It will make it difficult for individuals in the intelligence community to give honest assessments regarding security risks if it involves highly prominent individuals like [Huang],” Pearl said.
He added: “It will make it difficult for senior officials, like Secretary [Marco] Rubio, to bring those to the president, given that they will be potentially sort of disregarded and not received well. The whole apparatus of the US government in terms of detecting and responding to these kinds of threats could potentially be put at risk.”
Several Democrats have demanded more information about Trump’s pressure campaign to reinstate Huang. Senator Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the banking committee, said that the administration “needs to explain to Congress what ‘input’ was given and by which senior officials in the United States government to support his reinstatement”.
Warren argued that the embattled CEO’s return “only increases the urgency of passing bipartisan export control bills into law and making sure Nvidia does not lobby to kill that legislation and put our national security at risk”.
Congressman Robert Garcia, the incoming chair of the House oversight committee, said that Trump’s actions must be investigated further. “No president who has invested in a private company should be involved in their board decisions. Congress must come together in swift bipartisan action and demand answers immediately,” he said.
Another national security concern exposed by Huang’s reinstatement, according to Pearl, who previously served as director of emerging technologies at the national security council during Joe Biden’s administration, is succession planning for senior executives forced to step aside.
Reinstating Huang, “almost regardless of what comes out”, means “it’s very difficult to act on that again,” Pearl said.
“It’s hard to fire someone and then shortly after hire them and then fire them again. At some point you start looking foolish.”
Some Republicans have directed scrutiny toward the White House and Huang in equal measure.
“I have warned the administration from the start about Communist China obtaining our most advanced chips through both smuggling and remote access,” Senator Tom Cotton, the Republican chair of the intelligence committee, said in a statement. “The controversy with Nvidia and Megaspeed is just the tip of the iceberg, and it’s not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back. Congress has much to investigate and we will pass overwhelmingly bipartisan chip export-control bills, including my Chip Security Act, one way or the other.”
Van Hollen said on Monday it was a “good sign” that some Republican members of Congress had expressed alarm over Huang’s reinstatement.
“Frankly, it’s hard not to,” he said. “But given the last couple of years, when Republican senators buried their heads in the sand regardless of what the Trump administration was doing, it does seem to be a good sign that they recognize this as a threat to our national security.
“The fact that this individual was able to use the president to secure his reinstatement at Nvidia is a problem that I think Republican senators can see as well,” Van Hollen added.
Other Republicans have called for Howard Lutnick, the commerce secretary, to testify before Congress and answer allegations that he slow-walked an investigation into Megaspeed, the datacenter company led by Alice Huang that allegedly diverted Nvidia chips to China.
The US will now add Megaspeed to the trade blacklist after the commerce department implemented a rule last week banning remote access to advanced AI chips and servers through data centers in third countries.
“I’m less interested in talking to Mr Huang. You know he did what he did. I hope he had a good time. It was the most expensive sex since Helen of Troy,” Senator John Kennedy, a Republican from Louisiana who also serves on the banking committee, told NBC News on Sunday.
“I’m more interested in talking to Howard Lutnick and to Scott Bessent. It appears to me … that the Department of Commerce has allowed the Communist party of China to circumvent our export controls on advanced chips,” Kennedy said. “So I want to get both of them in front of a committee, put them under oath, ask them not to stab each other because I realize they don’t like each other, and let’s answer some hard questions, and if Secretary Lutnick is giving our chips away, he needs to stop it.”
Earlier this month, Bessent said he had pushed “for months” to sanction Megaspeed, which would prevent the firm from purchasing Nvidia chips. The treasury secretary appeared to blame Lutnick’s department for the delay.
“Commerce has the pen, and you will have to ask them about their internal processes,” Bessent said in a CNBC interview.
If elected majority leader in next week’s closed-door Democratic leadership contest, Van Hollen said AI policy should rank among the new Congress’ top priorities during its first 100 days.
He is backing a Senate rules reform proposal that would allow “expedited consideration” of bipartisan AI legislation emerging from committees, and supports penalties for technology companies that “develop agents that can endanger the public”.
The Maryland senator also called for a joint congressional committee to examine “all of the different elements of how AI will impact our society and our world”.
