A new version of the SYS01 infostealer malware delivered through Meta ads is enrolling browsers into enterprise-style management to force the installation of an extension that steals sensitive data, including Gmail and Outlook emails.
The ads lure Windows users into downloading free animated wallpapers that work as advertised but also deploy malware in the background during installation.
The campaign targets Facebook and Meta business accounts and gives the operator access to recent Gmail and Outlook messages in Chrome, and the ability to permanently delete emails.
Bitdefender Labs tracks the campaign as SYS010 and says the malware is likely a new version of the SYS01 infostealer, observed since at least 2022 in malvertising activity on Facebook.
The name reflects the researchers' assessment that this variant represents a major improvement over its predecessor, leading them to tag it as version 2, represented in binary as 010.
Researchers identified a malicious SYS010 ad on October 16 and found that the campaign was still ongoing, with hundreds of ads created.
Company telemetry shows that requests to the threat actor’s servers came from several countries in Asia, Europe, North America, Africa, and South America.
Meta Ad Library' transparency page shows that one advertisement alone reached 6,275 users in the European Union, targeting anyone above 18.
In a report today, Bitdefender says most requests came from the Philippines, Thailand, Bangladesh, Poland, Mexico, Algeria, Belgium, Mongolia, and the United States, but emphasizes these figures do not reflect confirmed infections.
Working wallpaper installs thieving add-on
The ads impersonate the legitimate Wallpaper Engine (a renamed, digitally signed version of the MeisterTask desktop app built on Electron) and Lively Wallpapers applications, directing users to malicious download sites, some of them using domains designed to appear official.
After installing the malicious Windows app, users get the advertised animated wallpaper functionality, which helps disguise the malware running in the background.
According to Bitdefender researchers, the attacker forces Chrome, Edge, and Firefox to install malicious browser add-ons by abusing management tools designed for organizations.
A script named eclient.php is responsible for installing the malicious extensions by modifying browser management policies.
In Chrome, it enrolls the browser in an attacker-controlled Google admin console, allowing forced installation of extensions.
In Firefox, it uses an ExtensionSettings policy to force-install an add-on while disabling Developer Tools and abuse reporting.
For Edge, it can enroll the victim's Windows device in an attacker-controlled mobile device management (MDM) server to enable forced extension installation.
These changes require administrator permissions, which is solved by triggering the User Account Control (UAC) prompt and repeating it every three seconds up to 99 times until the user approves the action.
The controls prevent users from disabling or removing the add-ons, Bitdefender says. The extensions can then steal session cookies, allowing the malware operator to access signed-in accounts without entering passwords or completing a two-factor authentication challenge.
Bitdefender determined that the malicious add-ons pose as document tools, Docs Helper in Chrome and Document Hub in Firefox.
They note that the fake Docs Helper extension for Chrome uses an icon resembling the Google Docs logo but is distributed outside the Chrome Web Store.
The extensions also collect information about Facebook Pages, advertising and business accounts that the victim can access, including spending and payment details.
Gmail and Outlook email access
Bitdefender’s research also notes that the Chrome extension can read recent messages in Gmail and Outlook accounts and send their content to the attacker, as well as permanently delete selected messages.
This capability could let attackers intercept login codes and erase security alerts before victims see them, Bitdefender warns.
Furthermore, access to the same inbox could also make it harder to recover compromised accounts.
Similar behavior has been observed recently in another infostealer named Remus, which also uses a malicious extension to read messages in Gmail inboxes.
Remus infostealer takes a different approach to installing the extension in Chrome: it registers the add-on directly in the victim's browser profile and enables Developer Mode to allow the unpacked extension to run.
Although malvertising is often considered a lesser risk, it can still cause significant damage, as stolen advertising accounts are typically used to fund malvertising campaigns under the identity of trusted businesses.
Affected companies incur unauthorized advertising charges, and may lose access to their social media accounts, and have customer conversations exposed.
Bitdefender’s recommendation is to avoid downloading software from social-media ads and to refuse administrator prompts with an unclear purpose.
Additionally, an unexpected "managed by your organization" message in a browser should be a red flag. Individuals using a work-managed device should check with their IT team, while such a message on a personal computer warrants further investigation.
The cybersecurity company advises businesses to review who has access to their Meta accounts, remove permissions that are no longer needed, and check for unfamiliar ads, payment changes, or spending.
