This fall, fashion has been in the thrall of a freaky shoe—the freakier, the better. Designers have been putting forward unorthodox footwear, and people—especially celebrities—have been going totally mad for them. Remember just earlier this year when Alexander Skarsgård rocked clear-PVC, toe-exposing Saint Laurent dress shoes? Or when Margaret Qualley donned Chanel’s “barefoot sandals,” which are merely a luxurious leather cap for your heels? The mood of the season is all about slipping into a pair of shoes that instantly make everyone around do a double take.
And now, as we near the end of a year rich in unhinged accessories, a new freaky shoe contender has entered the ring. . . courtesy, once again, of Chanel’s Mathieu Blazy. Here’s a hint of the unexpected breakout hit: You can wear them rain or shine, but they’re especially useful in a downpour—and even more so in a full-on flood.
And so, meet Blazy’s thigh-high take on the classic rain boot. Back in April, the designer unveiled his resort 2027 collection in Biarritz, and what came stomping down the runway were two pairs of the tall wader-style rubber boots. Chanel has long designed Wellington boots, of course—but these felt especially fresh and unexpected, thanks to their elongated silhouette and bright, primary-colored trims.
They were a perfectly thematic shoe offering for the show, given Blazy was inspired by the beachy town of Biarritz, which put him in a decidedly nautical mood. The thigh-highs, then, evoked the very same boots that many fishermen wear while on the job searching for their catch of the day. But if you think that the dramatic, slightly-cartoony shoes were a gimmick relegated solely to the runway, well, think again. In the same way that people are flocking to the Chanel stores to secure their barefoot sandals, many have been snapping up the rain boots this month, the very moment they hit the shelves.
First things first: the polarizing rain boots retail for $2,800 and are made of caoutchouc—a natural rubber. They come in black or white, and despite their extreme silhouette, shoppers have already been enthusiastically posting their outfits of the day, with the boots as the pièce de résistance. On TikTok, personal shopper and buyer Marci Hirshleifer Penn tried on the white versuib, and went instantly viral. “Channel 4 News said there’s a light drizzle today,” she jokes, adding, “Don’t think twice about them—they’re a total collector's piece.”

TikTok creator @Yofancyyy tried on the same colorway, adding, “Y’all ain’t got these”—which divided the comments. (One user comment: “And I don’t want them.”) Influencer Bryanboy even took the black version into a lake, in an attempt to see if they can withstand hefty water conditions. “I’m like The Little Mermaid,” he says, wading into the sea and perching on top of a rock. “A little Chanel Mermaid.”

It may seem surprising that so many are willing to embrace what is essentially a pantaboot, but Penn said it best: They are to be treated as more of a collector’s item than a day-to-day shoe. Blazy’s more-wearable pumps have already been a commercial best-seller, but it’s impressive that his more daring runway styles—from the barefoot sandals to, now, the rain boots—are proving just as desirable. Clearly, kitschy luxury is proving to be just as investment-worthy as, say, a timeless cashmere crewneck from Loro Piana or The Row.
The question is: Would you spend rent on a pair of thigh-high rain boots? Scott not: In ten years from now, they could be worth double. And at least you’d have something to wear for the next nor’easter. After all, finding non-hideous climate-ready boots is hard.
