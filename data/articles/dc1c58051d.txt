CASHMERE ON CONCORDE: As temperatures drop in Paris, the Hôtel de Crillon has imagined a way to soften winter’s bite.
The Paris palace has tapped French cashmere brand Kujten, turning its Terrasse Concorde into a plush cold-weather retreat as part of its festive-season theme, inspired by Jules Verne’s “Voyages Extraordinaires.”
Through Jan. 15, the space overlooking Place de la Concorde is dressed in the hotel’s signature deep “Discovery Green” paired with beige and Kujten’s bandana motif, with illuminated greenery as well as custom cushions and blankets designed for the occasion.
Cédric Waterkeyn, manager of Hôtel de Crillon, a Rosewood Hotel, described the property as “deeply rooted in French heritage” while “continually inspired by the creativity of contemporary Paris.”
“What drew us to Kujten was its ability to reinterpret a timeless material with a distinctly modern sensibility, an approach that resonates with our own expression of French art de vivre,” he said. The bespoke pieces bring “a sense of warmth and comfort” to the grandeur of the historic setting.
Carole Benaroya, chief executive officer and founder of Kujten, told WWD that the brand “has been an invitation to travel” since its inception in 2012.
“Hospitality is an integral part of our DNA and a natural extension of our brand universe,” she continued. “Dressing the [terrace], an iconic landmark of Parisian heritage that welcomes travelers from around the world, therefore felt like a natural fit for our maison.”
For those who would want to take some warmth with them, a bandana created for the occasion in two colorways, and a matching throw are available exclusively from the hotel’s boutique.
And should those not be enough to combat the chill, the terrace under the hotel’s historic arches is host to a pop-up bar dedicated to the Espresso Martini, created in partnership with Diageo and Cafés 1895 by Lavazza. The cocktail menu includes a customizable version, allowing guests to tailor their own serve.
“Our hope is to bring guests and Parisians alike together to experience cherished holiday traditions in new and unexpected ways,” Waterkeyn said.
