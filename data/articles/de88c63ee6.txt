Recognised as one of the 2027 European Capitals of Tourism, Calvià is an ideal destination for travellers looking for more than sun and sand. Here’s what you can discover.
Calvià, a Mallorcan setting of cliffs, bays, beaches and islets, has been named the 2027 European Capital of Tourism in the cities with between 25,000 and 100,000 inhabitants category.
The coastal destination was awarded the designation for its “ambition to transform its tourism model, moving away from high-volume mass tourism towards a more sustainable and year around approach”, the European Commission said.
Jury members also recognised its work to have tourism contribute to long-term economic, environmental and social wellbeing, while also “creating a more balanced relationship between visitors, residents and the local environment”.
But what makes this Balearic municipality worth a visit in 2027? From historic landmarks, hiking trails and its coastal cuisine, here's where to go and experience Calvià.
Must-see attractions
Sprawled over 145 square kilometres and surrounded by the mountains of Sierra de Tramuntana, this Spanish municipality’s history dates back to the 13th century, with estates still standing from the 16th and 17th centuries.
Can Verger, which now hosts a library and facilities for young people, and Can Ros are among the best known old Mallorcan manors still standing.
There is also a selection of markets to visit. The traditional market in Calvià village is the city’s oldest and takes place every Monday morning, where more than 60 stalls offer visitors food, clothes and handicrafts.
Calvià has also frequently been the destination of choice for both national and international film productions, including the 2019 comedy “The Hustle”, starring Anne Hathaway and Rebel Wilson. Filming took place across various locations, including Puerto Portals.
There is also a dedicated “Calvià, Film Set” route where set-jetters can explore other locations that have graced silver screens.
Adventures on land and water
Several certified hiking trails are ideal for exploring Calvià on foot.
The PR-MA 1 route connects the town of Peguera and the village of Es Capdellà. Landscapes of pine forests, farmland and views of the Torrent des Gorg mountain range are part of the itinerary. While for history buffs, the ancient medieval tower-house, Torre d’En Boira, which dates back to the 14th-century, can also be seen along the way.
Other certified routes include PR-MA 2: La Vall Verda, PR-MA3: Sa Vinya, and PR-MA 4: Sa Font de Sa Cometa.
Away from dry land, the Mediterranean waters are great for activities like motor and jet boating, windsurfing, as well as for banana boat rides, scuba diving and surfing.
Coastal cuisine
Unsurprisingly for an island destination, dining in this Mallorcan municipality features plenty of fresh sea catches alongside traditional fayre.
Leña Mallorca, Spanish steakhouse at the Mandarin Oriental Punta Negra, is inspired by local culinary traditions. With open-fire cooking and a display of dry aged meats, guests can sample an array of vegetable, seafood and meat dishes, as well as Spanish-Japanese fusion “yakipinchos” grilled skewers.
Matsuhisa, the restaurant by Chef Nobu Matsuhisa, is also located in the Mandarin Oriental Punta Negra. Set against sea views, it offers diners sushi paired with coastal ambience.
For paella, Paco’s Santa Ponsa offers authentic Spanish cuisine, alongside tapas, fresh fish as well as steak, pizza and sweet treats – all within easy reach from beaches like Platja de Santa Ponça.
Meanwhile, Lucy Wang in Portal Nous blends Japanese with French and Mediterranean cuisine. Dishes on offer range from edamame to ceviche, alongside sushi and sashimi.
Where to stay
After a day packed with hiking, water activities and dining, a luxurious stay awaits at the five-star Hotel Golf Santa Ponsa. The property offers views of the Puig de Galatzó peak and is nestled in a golf club surrounded by six kilometres of coastline, beaches and a marina.
Indoors, the hotel offers an array of rooms and suites, including some with terraces or balconies. Guests can choose to unwind in its dedicated space for wellness, which includes private massage cabins. There is also a restaurant serving guests seasonal Mediterranean cuisine.
