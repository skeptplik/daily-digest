Initial demand for Fujitsu’s new Monaka server chips is exceeding expectations, fueled by clients seeking alternatives to U.S. and Chinese suppliers, according to the company’s chief technology officer.
Shortly after the Tokyo-based company began commercial sales of its latest 2-nanometer central processing units this month, Fujitsu already is in discussions with its manufacturer Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing about securing enough production capacity, Vivek Mahajan said.
Demand for sovereign data security in areas including defense, finance, manufacturing and the public sector is boosting interest from customers in Japan, the U.S. and Europe, he said.
“The biggest challenge is — if the demand really takes off, can we make sure that the supply keeps up?” Mahajan said in an interview on Tuesday. Most of the 45 companies that received Monaka product samples in August are likely to place orders, he said.
The Fujitsu-Monaka processors — named after a traditional Japanese confectionary — may face supply constraints if orders exceed the company’s current topline estimates of sales of about 500,000 units over two years, Mahajan said. A single manufacturer shipping about 30,000 servers would need more than 200,000 Monaka chips, he said.
Fujitsu, which supplies the Japanese government with computing, artificial intelligence, networking and security products, has mostly restricted use of its processors to its own systems, such as for use in the Fugaku supercomputer. With Monaka, Fujitsu seeks to sell its processors — which use Arm Holdings architecture — to other server makers and turn the business into a new revenue driver.
Fujitsu expects Monaka chips to generate ¥35 billion ($220 million) in sales in the next fiscal year, with annual sales reaching ¥500 billion by 2035. Fujitsu may hit the longer-term target a couple years earlier, Mahajan said. Analysts on average expect Fujitsu’s revenue to stay flat this fiscal year to March at ¥3.6 trillion, and for the company to clock about 5% growth each year through 2030.
AI data centers need more CPUs to deal with the rising volume of everyday tasks and general-purpose computing that users now demand of agents. Intel and Advanced Micro Devices now dominate the server CPU market but the two face pressure from Arm-based chips as well as from Nvidia that tout energy efficiency.
Monaka is targeting companies and governments’ need for better performance, memory access and security as well as energy efficiency, Mahajan said.
“We are the only ones in Japan that make a CPU today and probably only ones outside the U.S. that make such a cutting-edge CPU,” he said. “That’s an alternative that we give to our customers.”
