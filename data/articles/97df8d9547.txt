The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) is warning about a critical vulnerability in Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA Series controllers used by food retailers to manage commercial refrigeration, HVAC, and lighting systems.
Danfoss controllers have been compromised recently in attacks at various supermarkets and food retailers across Europe where hackers remotely shut down cooling systems or raised their temperatures.
The security issue is tracked as CVE-2026-129713 and received a severity score of 9.8. It is a stack-based buffer overflow in the controller’s server.
CISA says an attacker can trigger the flaw without authentication, cause the server to restart, and potentially achieve remote code execution with the web server's privileges.
Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA controllers are central control units for supermarket and convenience-store refrigeration. They are used to manage equipment such as refrigerated display cases and compressor racks, to log temperatures, and to alert when changes occur.
Danfoss calls the product line the AK-SM 800A Series, which includes the 820A, 850A, and 880A models. Depending on the model, they can also control store lighting and HVAC.
CISA notes that the controllers are deployed worldwide, but their exact number remains unclear.
On November 20, Danfoss published a security advisory saying that the CVE-2026-129713 vulnerability would be addressed in the upcoming software version 5.5.x for Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA Series.
The company said that the update would also remove the option that allows customers to turn off ‘Strict mode’ in the product.
Currently, there is no patch available for CVE-2026-129713, and potentially impacted system administrators are advised to take the following actions:
- Keep controllers off of the public internet and minimize network exposure where possible.
- Segregate control systems from business networks and place them behind firewalls.
- Use up-to-date Virtual Private Networks (VPNs) when remote access is required.
Prior Danfoss incidents
Danfoss systems were recently targeted in high-impact cybersecurity incidents across Europe. Between October 11 and 12, several French supermarkets and food retailers suffered significant losses after attacks attributed to Iran’s IRGC-linked Shahid Kaveh unit compromised Danfoss AK-SM/AK-SC and Copeland/Dixell XWEB refrigeration controllers and remotely altered their defrost settings.
On November 2, at least 115 supermarket stores in Belgium, the Netherlands, Sweden, Luxembourg, Ireland, Romania, and Estonia, had their refrigeration equipment shut down or their settings changed remotely.
Supplier Sabcobel said the attackers used the same vulnerability previously seen in the October attacks in France, indicating that Danfoss systems were involved again.
On the same day, attackers suspected to be linked to the same campaign targeted a refrigeration supplier for the French national blood service, altering cold-storage settings at three facilities.
