A BBC report says Arion Kurtaj, the Grand Theft Auto 6 leaker who was given an indefinite sentence in hospital in 2023 because he kept hacking into things or expressing intent to, has been released with a stern warning not to do it again.
Kurtaj was sentenced to seven years in prison, according to the report, but was released on license—similar to parole in North America—the same day, with his hospital stay and time in prison while awaiting retrial credited as time served. He was given a five-year "Serious Crime Prevention Order" restricting his allowable online activity, including bans on using Telegram and Discord.
"Kurtaj knew what he was doing when he carried out these crimes and has finally admitted his involvement," Detective Inspector Mike O'Sullivan of the City of London Police said following Kurtaj's sentencing and release. "We hope this case serves as a lesson that these types of acts will not be tolerated.
"Although cyber crime is borderless, we will seek to utilise the co-operation of partner agencies and international law enforcement to ensure that offenders are brought to justice no matter where they offend from."
Kurtaj carried out hacks against multiple companies when he was 16 and 17 years old, including Microsoft, Samsung, Nvidia and UK telecom company BT&EE, and stole millions in cryptocurrency from individuals.
But it was his hack of Rockstar and leak of GTA 6 materials more than a year before it was formally revealed, that earned him notoriety, not least because of how it was carried out: While under police protection in a Travelodge hotel room, using an Amazon Fire Stick and a mobile phone.
A psychiatric assessment determined that Kurtaj had "acute autism," and that he "continued to express the intent to return to cybercrime as soon as possible" while he was in custody. That led to his hospital sentence, but earlier this year he was found fit to stand trial and subsequently pleaded guilty to all charges.
That struck me as odd—why plead guilty to crimes carried out at a time when your mental state was such that a court decided you required multiple years of institutionalization?—but this may be the answer: Kurtaj, now 21, is a free man. The judge on the case told Kurtaj he is "a very talented young man," but also warned him: "Don't let this happen again."
