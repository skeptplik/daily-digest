Russia has bombed an aid distribution centre and neighbouring shelter in southern Ukraine, killing at least 29 people, with 48 still missing, as a wave of overnight strikes targeted power plants across the country, damaged hospitals and wounded scores of people.
Russia had launched 289 attack drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles across nine regions, triggering emergency power cuts, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said in an online post that noted the barrage coincided with US elections.
“The Russians think America won’t notice this signal of theirs,” he said. “But we need to notice and stop such terror. We need to help Ukraine defend its skies; otherwise, Russia will never stop its missile strikes.”
The guided-bomb attack first targeted a school building used to distribute humanitarian aid in Komyshuvakha, a village in Ukraine’s southern Zaporizhzhia region, and then took aim at a nearby shelter.
The dead included four police officers and two rescue workers, Mykhailo Semikin, acting head of the regional military administration, said on Tuesday. At least five people were hurt. Semikin said 48 people were considered missing.
In the north-east region of Kharkiv, a combined Russian missile and drone attack had wounded at least 62 people, local authorities said. A strike damaged a medical facility in the Nemyshlianskyi district, shattered windows in four apartment buildings and damaged cars. More than 200 patients had been transferred to other medical facilities, said the head of the regional military administration, Oleh Syniehubov.
In the Dnipro area, rescuers pulled a woman alive from the rubble on Tuesday morning, after a Russian attack killed one person and wounded 17, local authorities said.
As night fell in Komyshuvakha, emergency responders used torches to search through the rubble of collapsed buildings, according to photos and video posted online by local officials.
Efforts to rescue people on Monday evening were hindered as Russian drones launched additional attacks in the area that had been bombed, according to Mykhailo Semikin, a military official in Zaporizhzhia.
Zelenskyy said the Russians could not have been unaware that residents remaining in Komyshuvakha gathered each week to pick up humanitarian packages from the site.
The attack, he said, had shown that Moscow was intent on killing people instead of building on the past week’s ceasefire in the embattled southern city of Oleshky, brokered by US first lady Melania Trump.
“Russians continue to demonstrate, time and again, their hatred of Ukraine and everything that exists here,” Zelenskyy said.
“If Russia genuinely wanted de-escalation, there was an opportunity following the situation in Oleshky to continue taking humanitarian steps. We proposed this. But Russia continues to kill people.”
The ceasefire last week enabled an evacuation of the Russian-occupied Oleshky. It was the longest pause in fighting between the neighbours in the war, now in its fifth year, which has killed hundreds of thousands of people and displaced millions.
The Kremlin said that no other similar missions were being planned “at the moment”.
US-led talks between Russia and Ukraine were due to resume next week, the US special envoy Steve Witkoff said on Monday. Donald Trump has pushed for a settlement in the dragging war but there has been no breakthrough.
The talks – to be held on 10-11 November in Abu Dhabi, Witkoff said – come as Kyiv and its European allies worry that Trump has increasingly sided with Moscow.
