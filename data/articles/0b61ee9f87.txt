A contemporary take on Ukrainian vernacular buildings and a preschool in Estonia are among the winners of the Thermory Design Awards 2026, which celebrate the versatility of thermally modified wood.
Of 78 submissions from 23 countries, four winning projects have been selected in the competition’s fifth annual edition, covering the categories of interior, tiny and private house, residential, commercial and public building, and wellness and spa.
Thermory thermowood is wood that has been modified using heat and steam with no added chemicals.
The treatment enhances the material’s dimensional stability and reduces moisture uptake compared with untreated wood, according to Thermory.
The winner of the interior category is a trio of Ukrainian guesthouses collectively named Under the Reed Roof, designed by Kyiv-based studio YOD Group.
Each guesthouse comprises a glass-walled volume at the base, topped by a tapered thatched roof with exaggerated proportions.
While the exterior of these roofs is made with traditional reeds, the interiors are lined with thermo-ash shingles, arranged to create a tactile effect that is repeated on the front doors.
These highlight the interplay between the interior’s traditional and contemporary design elements, contrasting materials, and the balance between the structure’s openness and enclosure.
“The project stood out to the jury for its exceptionally coherent concept, in which architecture and interior elements come together to form a harmonious whole,” said Thermory.
Winning in the tiny and private house category was Marker Architecture‘s Flooded Gums project, a flood-resilient dwelling built on working farmland.
The building features thermo-ash cladding and thermo-spruce decking, owing to the materials’ robustness and aesthetic merits.
“What particularly impressed the jury was the honesty and clarity of the architecture,” said Thermory of the project.
“Rather than concealing its construction, the project makes its structural logic visible, with every element serving a purpose: the result is a modest yet highly refined building that strikes a compelling balance between aesthetics, functionality and its natural setting,” it added.
Estonia’s Rakke Kindergarten secured the win in the residential, commercial and public building category, thanks to what Thermory describes as local studio Arhitekt Must‘s “cohesive and imaginative design”.
Thermo-spruce cladding, thermo-pine posts and thermo-pine decking all feature in the design, enhancing both its functionality and visual appeal.
The building’s function as an educational environment is made distinctive by the space’s inventive ceiling, which is composed of the short thermo-pine posts, arranged in a way that creates an engaging optical illusion.
“This unexpected architectural gesture elevates a functional space into something distinctive, demonstrating how thoughtful detailing can shape the character and experience of an entire building,” said Thermory.
Finally, the winner of the wellness and spa category is a sauna within the Estonian Embassy in Berlin, designed by interior studio AERIS.
The interiors feature statement walls covered in custom-made oak shingles, which, being circular and slightly overlapping, resemble fish scales.
These are joined by thermo-alder benches and walls, creating a cohesive scheme of timber details that integrate neatly with the lighting design.
“The harmonious interplay of materials, surfaces and light imbues the space with a contemporary edge while retaining the intimacy and warmth of a traditional sauna,” said Thermory.
“Although vastly different in character and purpose, the winning projects share a creative and unconventional approach to design,” it added.
“Each places wood at the heart of its architectural expression, combining imaginative thinking with meticulous attention to detail.”
The 2026 instalment of the competition was judged by a diverse international jury of architects and Thermory representatives, including Kärt Kanarbik, Thermory’s architectural project manager; Lars Skjølstrup Østergaard, lead design architect at Henning Larsen; Sami Rintala, professor and architect at Rintala Eggertsson Architects; Jaan Port, architect and partner at PIN Arhitektid; and Tõnis Kalve, architect at ByKalve and Thermory project sales architect.
