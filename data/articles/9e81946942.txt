From former OpenAI researchers to a British technology journalist, the people behind the Claude chatbot could each see their fortunes soar even further into the billions when the company goes public.
When shares in Anthropic go on sale for the first time this week, it will be a major moment not just for the company behind the Claude AI chatbot, but for the wider AI industry and the US stock market.
The company's stock market debut – or initial public offering (IPO) – could become one of the world's most valuable companies overnight
Anthropic plans to sell shares for between $70 (€63) and $78 (€71) each, potentially giving the company a total value of more than $1 trillion (€911.61 billion).
That would put it in the same league as retail giant Walmart and banking heavyweight JPMorgan Chase. It would also make Anthropic more valuable than Coca-Cola, IBM, Starbucks, Delta Air Lines and General Motors combined.
Just five years ago, seven people left rival OpenAI and put their faith in Anthropic, which went from a tiny tech start-up to a giant of the industry.
If the shares sell for $78 (€71), each of the seven founders’ shareholdings will be worth at least $18.5bn (€16.86 billion) each, making them among the richest people on the planet.
Here’s what you need to know about each of them.
Dario Amodei, CEO
Dario Amodei was working at OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, when he decided he wanted to take a different approach to developing artificial intelligence.
In 2020, he left the firm, taking his sister Daniela – now Anthropic’s president – and five other colleagues with him to set up Anthropic.
Raised in San Francisco, California, Amodei showed an early aptitude for both academics and athletics, impressing school friends with his intelligence and speed on the running track.
He was also fascinated by big questions around technology and philosophy, including whether machines could one day become conscious and how science might extend human life.
Amodei initially studied physics at Princeton and Stanford universities but switched to biology and biomedical sciences following his father's death from hepatitis C.
Before joining OpenAI, Amodei worked at Chinese technology giant Baidu and Google.
The 43-year-old has said his decision to leave OpenAI was driven by growing concerns that some within the company were putting commercial interests ahead of AI safety.
Daniela Amodei, President
Dario and his sister Daniela were close growing up and later shared a flat in San Francisco with a group of friends.
Daniela followed her brother into OpenAI and joined him when he left to set up Anthropic.
Daniela studied English literature at the University of California, Santa Cruz, before working for Democratic representative Matt Cartwright.
The 39-year-old also worked as head of risk operations at financial services company Stripe.
She now acts as Anthropic's president, a role which allows her to oversee its executive team and lead work across research, engineering and product development, as well as governance and commercial operations.
Tom Brown, Chief Compute Officer
Before joining Anthropic, Tom Brown built his reputation in the AI industry at Google as part of its DeepMind project and OpenAI, where he was part of the team responsible for developing GPT-3, one of the early large language models that paved the way for ChatGPT.
The 39-year-old also has a background in start-ups, having helped launch Grouper, a social app designed to bring groups of friends together for drinks.
Today, Brown oversees Anthropic's computing resources, a crucial part of the business given the enormous processing power needed to train and operate its AI systems.
He has also voiced support for Donald Trump's push to expand data centre infrastructure in the US, arguing that it represents "an enormous source of prosperity".
Jared Kaplan, Chief Science Officer
Jared Kaplan oversees Anthropic's scientific research and technical development as its chief science officer. He also holds an academic position as an associate professor at Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore.
Kaplan, 42, has a PhD in physics from Harvard University and is best known for his work on AI scaling laws. These are mathematical principles which help explain how AI systems tend to improve as they are trained using larger models and greater amounts of data.
Anthropic says his research has helped give companies a way to predict how their AI models will perform as they grow.
Chris Olah, Interpretability Research Lead
Chris Olah, 34, takes a different approach to AI research, focusing on understanding what happens inside artificial intelligence systems and how they arrive at their decisions.
While his role is technically ‘interpretability research lead’, Canadian Olah is known to ask ethical and philosophical questions surrounding the technology.
According to The New York Times, he has spent much of the past year bringing together religious leaders from different faiths to discuss the links between morality and AI.
Before joining Anthropic, Olah worked as a research scientist at Google Brain before moving to OpenAI, where he led research into how AI systems function.
He left the University of Toronto before completing his degree but went on to receive a Thiel Fellowship, which supports promising young people pursuing research and other projects outside traditional university education.
Jack Clark, Head of Public Benefit
Unlike the other Anthropic founders, Jack Clark comes from a journalism rather than a technical background. The 37-year-old is also the only one born outside North America, having grown up in Brighton, UK.
Clark studied English literature and creative writing at the University of East Anglia before moving into technology journalism. He wrote for publications including ZDNet UK, The Register and Bloomberg before joining OpenAI, initially working in communications and later becoming its policy director.
At Anthropic, he serves as head of public benefit and leads the Anthropic Institute, which examines the wider implications of artificial intelligence and its impact on society.
Sam McCandlish, Chief Architect
36-year-old Sam McCandlish is arguably the least visible of Anthropic's co-founders, but, like the others, he’s expected to own around 1.6% of the company once it goes public.
As Anthropic's chief architect, McCandlish works on the technical design of its AI systems as well as the policies intended to reduce the risks posed by the technology.
He previously worked on AI safety at OpenAI.
He has a PhD in physics from Stanford University and an undergraduate degree in mathematics and physics from Brandeis University in Massachusetts.
