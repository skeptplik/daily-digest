A US jury convicted Chinese tech giant Huawei on a slew of charges on Tuesday including stealing trade secrets and violating sanctions.
The jury in the Brooklyn federal court found the company guilty on 10 of the 12 criminal counts that it faced, which also included bank fraud and wire fraud.
"For decades, Huawei and three of its subsidiaries conducted their business in the United States on the basis of lies and theft," US Attorney General Todd Blanche said after the verdict.
However, the jury acquitted Huawei on two charges of racketeering and conspiring to obstruct justice.
The verdict is the latest blow to the Chinese tech giant which has for years been banned from selling networking devices to telecom carries in the US and other Western countries. However, its consumer electronics business has continued to grow as its networking business in other countries.
Stolen source code, antenna technology and robot tech
Prosecutors detailed a number of ways Huawei stole trade secrets from competitors in order to undercut them in the market.
In once instance, a Huawei employee slipped into a competitor's booth at a Chicago trade fair in order to photograph circuitboards inside another company's networking divice.
Huawei was also found to have once sold routers containing source code from a rival company.
Prosecutors also said a Huawei employee once removed a robotic arm from a competitor's lab so it could be photographed and measured.
According to court records, the victim companies included Cisco Systems, Motorola Solutions, T-Mobile, Quintel USA and Fujitsu.
"The misappropriated intellectual property included trade secret information and copyrighted works, such as source code and user manuals for internet routers, antenna technology, and robot testing technology," the Department of Justice said.
False statenments about Iran operations
Prosecutors also alleged that Huawei did business in countries subject to US sanctions including Iran and North Korea.
Huawei then made false statements to both US government officials and banks in order to hide this activity, prosecutors said.
"Few crimes are as serious as Huawei's decades-long pattern of endangering US national security, violating the sanctity of US commerce, and impeding the ‌enforcement of US laws," US Attorney Joseph Nocella said.
Huawei to appeal verdict
Huawei continued to deny the allegations even after the verdict on Tuesday.
"We believe the government's remaining charges are equally without legal or factual merit," the company said in a statement.
"The five trade secret-related disputes cited by the government were all civil matters that were resolved over the past two decades. No court ever found Huawei liable for willful intellectual property misappropriation, and the company was never ordered to pay damages for trade secret misappropriation."
The company's lawyer, Brian Heberlig, said: "We fully expect to appeal."
Huawei is set to be sentenced next September and could face billions of dollars in penalties.
