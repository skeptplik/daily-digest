These are the stories making headlines in fashion on Monday.
Luxury brands are forging closer ties with the fashion sourcing world as a growing number of wealthy clients hire sourcers to track down hard-to-find products. Fashion sourcers like Gab Waller and Erica Wright believe sourcing should be integrated into brands’ operations to fulfill orders for sold-out merchandise. The unique service already helps satisfy demand for existing customers willing to pay anything for a designer’s merchandise. That said, the rise of sourcing has made it difficult for brands to identify the difference between professional sourcers and opportunistic resellers. {Business of Fashion/paywalled}
Ralph Lauren CEO Patrice Louvet believes the brand’s 60-year success is due to its distinct all-American identity and unwavering focus on universal values such as optimism, authenticity, quality, entrepreneurship and family. The business also primarily sells core products available year in, year out (rather than releasing completely new products every season). These strategies have positioned Ralph Lauren to generate steady sales regardless of changing trend cycles and geopolitical tensions. {The New York Times/paywalled}
Hearst Magazines launched Hearst Creator Studios, a new platform that taps editor-picked creators to co-produce and distribute editorial and branded content on and off Hearst platforms. The studio debuts with a slate of seven original series and 50 creators across five publications. {Fashionista inbox}
World Team Tennis (WTT) has announced Kallmeyer as its Exclusive Apparel Partner and Official Outfitter. The multi-year partnership includes courtside branding for Kallmeyer across all three season venues, integration across future marketing, promotional materials and content opportunities and Kallmeyer creating custom on-court uniforms for WTT’s officials, umpires and ball people. {Fashionista inbox}
On Monday, Awake Mode founder and creative director Natalia Alaverdian stepped down, effective immediately. Her resignation comes after her decision to sell her majority stake to Another Community in 2025. She now plans to focus on filmmaking and independent creative direction. Another Community will announce the brand’s new creative director “in due course,” per the press release. {Fashionista inbox}
Brands such as Anthropologie and SharkNinja are hiring content creators to ideate and produce episodic micro-series to roll out across all social media platforms. This strategy offers a new way to capture consumers’ attention compared with traditional sponsored influencer posts. But as brands invest in produced, long-form content, they must also refigure out payment deals, including creators’ fees, production and usage rights. {Modern Retail/paywalled}
