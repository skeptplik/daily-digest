In short:
Former junior middleweight title holder Tim Tszyu is aiming to become a two-weight world champion.
Just two Australia-born boxers have won world titles in multiple weight divisions: Jeff Fenech and Liam Paro.
What's next?
Tszyu fights Erislandy Lara for the WBA middleweight title on the Gold Coast on December 13.
Tim Tszyu knows all about the highs boxing can deliver.
He knows a fair bit about the lows, too.
Three years ago, Tszyu, the WBO junior middleweight world title holder, was eyeing up unification and US domination.
Three defeats in four fights later, his career was on the trash heap.
"You learn more about your character," Tszyu told ABC Sport from his Rockdale gym.
"You realise life isn't just going to go on an upward scale. It goes up and down. You've got to ride through the waves.
"One thing I learned was don't get too high from your wins, don't get too low from your losses, maintain a stable way of living and keep approaching every day with the foundation of something to work towards.
"That's how it's always been for me."
Three wins later, including against American legend Errol Spence Jr, the 32-year-old is fighting for another world title, this time at the 160-pound-limit against WBA middleweight champion Erislandy Lara.
Not everyone is happy about Tszyu's return to the top.
"We all know he don't deserve a world title fight," American fighter Jermell Charlo told media on Wednesday via Zoom link from the United States.
"You didn't work your way up [at middleweight] for real. He fought a 147-pounder [welterweight] that just happened to overlap 154 [junior middleweight].
"Errol Spence wasn't the Errol Spence we all know, right?
"He can't beat Lara. Styles make fights, and this is just stylistically a bad fight for Tim.
"Lara's going to destroy Tszyu."
Charlo, a former unified champion in Tszyu's former weight division, light middleweight, may have a vested interest in shooting down Tszyu's chances.
Tszyu was supposed to face Charlo in January 2023, but the bout collapsed after Charlo broke his hand in sparring.
Despite Tszyu still being keen, Charlo wanted to hunt bigger fish and stepped up two weight divisions to take on Canelo Álvarez in a big-money blockbuster.
The WBO were not amused, stripping Charlo of his title the second he stepped into the ring, elevating Tszyu.
Tszyu has rebuilt his reputation since, while Charlo has stagnated.
Charlo has not fought since losing to Canelo, returning as the third-billed fight on December's spectacular Gold Coast card.
"Don't forget that, Charlo," Tszyu said, mockingly.
"You're on my undercard."
Tszyu's resurrection into a world title challenger has been nothing short of remarkable.
And although Lara will pose an enormous test of Tszyu's resilience, "He's going to present a lot of challenges," Tszyu noted, with the Australian backing himself to claim victory.
A big part of Tszyu's recovery has been his partnership with former three-weight world champion Jeff Fenech.
"He just came on board at a time where I really needed it," Tszyu said.
Despite that, Fenech has been dealing with several health challenges of late, including a third heart operation in October.
"I wouldn't say it impacted my training camp. I was more impacted personally because I felt like a lot was missing from Jeff," Tszyu said.
"But then when Billy [Hussein] came on board, we just started flowing with things, and we've just been working ever since."
Fenech is one of just two Australian male fighters to win championships in different weight classes (IBF bantamweight, WBC super-bantamweight and WBC featherweight), the other being Liam Paro (IBF super lightweight and welterweight titles).
"Playing it safe all the time is not the way I do things," Tszyu said.
"You got to chase the hard stuff. The tricky stuff. The legacy-defining type stuff."
