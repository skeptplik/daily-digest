PokéPark Fishing Rally DS was a 2005 temporary fishing game obtainable through Download Play on the Nintendo DS. To play it, gamers had to get it from Download Play stations at PokéPark in Nagoya, Japan. The game would remain on their console for 12 hours or until the console was shut off. After this, it would delete itself.
The limited release was a simple fishing minigame, in which players competed for prizes by catching Pokémon and reaching the top of the leaderboard at events. PokéPark Fishing Rally DS was only available for a few months in 2005. It disappeared off the map once PokéPark closed because it could only be accessed with a staff-exclusive DS test cartridge. In July, a PokéPark Fishing Rally DS test cartridge was listed on eBay for $9,000. However, it was later discovered that it only contained the leaderboard ranking software.
Collector finds playable PokéPark Fishing Rally DS test cartridge
Although the first test cartridge remains an important piece of history, it doesn't actually contain the game, which disappointed some fans. Thankfully, this fragment of Pokémon lost media has finally been found. Collector Daniel Quadri was able to get his hands on a PokéPark Fishing Rally DS test cartridge, which he can use to access and play the game.
PSA Video Games has verified its authenticity, and it appears to be the same type of test cartridge as those used at the Download Play stations. In an exclusive statement to IGN, Claire Shelton, head of PSA Video Games, underlined how remarkable it was for this piece of media to have surfaced.
                        "We are always proud to play a role in documenting, authenticating, and encapsulating unique pieces of video game history such as this. Our involvement allows the item to continue existing and potentially transact with full confidence to any collector, assuring them that this unique piece is assuredly one of the only ways to interact with this formerly lost media piece of Pokémon history."
The cartridge is currently in the possession of Daniel Quadri, and he couldn't be happier. He told IGN:
                        "What fascinates me is that visitors at PokéPark could download the game wirelessly to their own Nintendo DS back in 2005. With this cartridge in my DS, I can recreate that experience today. I can send the full game directly to someone else’s DS through Download Play, without needing a download station."
With it, he can wirelessly share the game through Download Play at any time, but for now, it remains encapsulated for its protection. It is reassuring to know that at least one copy of this previously lost game is still out there and is safely preserved.
