Brussels‘ former Citroën Garage has been transformed into Kanal, a 40,000-square-metre museum for modern art and architecture with an exhibition programme developed with the Parisian art institution Centre Pompidou.
The transformation was designed by Atelier Kanal, a consortium made up of architecture studio EM2N, NoAarchitecten and Sergison Bates Architects, with a team of construction workers, who restored the 1930s industrial building and added three new volumes within its structure.
Claiming to be “Europe’s largest museum development”, Kanal – sometimes referred to as Kanal-Centre Pompidou – hopes to provide space for people to gather to enjoy visual art, performance, dance, music, film and architecture.
Opening to the public on 28 November, the canal-side building was originally built in 1934 as a garage and showroom for the car company Citroën.
Atelier Kanal aimed to keep the industrial character of the building by implementing a “light touch” approach, restoring most of the building fabric, repairing and upgrading parts where needed, and replacing other parts to improve climatic and acoustic conditions.
The garage’s former showroom, a modernist curved glass building with 20-metre-tall interiors, was converted into a gallery space topped with a publicly accessible rooftop.
“Rather than a spectacular gesture, our proposal offers an attitude of ‘radical optimism’ – open and non-prescriptive, spontaneous and instinctive – much like the spirit of modern progress in which it was created,” said Atelier Kanal.
“The interior is a fine example of elegant industrial engineering, while the glass facades clearly express early modernism,” it continued.
“Our ambition has been to trust the building as we found it, embracing its industrial character and many layers of use.”
Three new buildings were added inside the existing structure, including a four-storey volume with a basement level that contains art galleries, and another volume housing a 378-seat auditorium and the Kanal offices.
The third volume is the new home for Brussels’ museum and archive of architecture, Kanal Architecture, which was formerly known as CIVA.
Rising nine stories on the north-east side of the campus, across from the art galleries, the structure includes exhibition spaces, a library, archives, research centre, bookshop and workshop spaces.
Architecture collective Assemble designed an indoor playground on the first floor of the museum, which features playscapes informed by volcanoes, planets, and piles of coal that were once transported on the canal.
Elsewhere in the building are a library, bookshop, rooftop bar, restaurants and an in-house bakery.
Kanal’s year-round programme of exhibitions was developed in collaboration with the Centre Pompidou art museum in Paris.
“Kanal invites citizens, organisations, collectives and communities to appropriate and inhabit large parts of the building, for production, performances, events, informal gatherings or simply to spend time,” said Atelier Kanal.
“The museum extends beyond curated art, becoming part of the city and its fabric,” it continued. “In this sense, Kanal departs from the traditional museum and instead offers a place for civic gathering, responding to the growing absence of shared public spaces in our cities today.”
Other museums that have recently opened in historic buildings include the London Museum, located in a transformed Victorian market building, and a bank in Santander that was transformed into the Faro Santander museum.
