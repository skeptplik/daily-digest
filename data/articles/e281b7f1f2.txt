The government is considering adding organic chemical products to the list of “specified critical products” under the economic security promotion law to ensure their stable supply, sources have said.
The move is aimed at strengthening the domestic supply chain for organic chemical products, which are used in a wide range of items, including high-performance materials essential for semiconductor manufacturing, the sources said Friday.
The government plans to begin discussions at a closed-door panel of experts, aiming to amend the relevant Cabinet order by the end of fiscal 2026, which ends next March.
Organic chemical products such as ethylene and propylene, which are used as raw materials for plastics, are manufactured from naphtha and other materials.
The U.S.-Israeli military conflict with Iran has effectively disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, raising concerns about the stable supply of domestically produced organic chemical products.
The government designates items deemed crucial to people’s daily lives and corporate activities as specified critical products in a bid to support to private-sector companies responsible for ensuring stable supplies through subsidies and other measures.
Semiconductors, storage batteries and ventilators are among the items designated under the system.
Japan relies on imports from the Middle East for most of the petroleum used to produce naphtha. Domestic chemical production has been declining, while dependence on China is increasing.
