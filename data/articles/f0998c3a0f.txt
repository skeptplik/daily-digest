The Hungarian prime minister and the Ukrainian president met in Transcarpathia. Both called the talks substantive, and said they open a new chapter in relations between the two countries.
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar agreed that a new chapter had begun in relations between their two countries after years of hostility, as they met under maximum security on Tuesday in Ukraine's Transcarpathia region.
Both leaders said good-neighbourly relations were being restored.
The two men began by laying wreaths at the graves of fallen soldiers in a cemetery in Uzhhorod, western Ukraine. Zelenskyy said 316 soldiers had been buried there since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
After the ceremony, Magyar said the Hungarian government delegation had not come empty-handed. It brought patient-monitoring equipment, medicines and energy equipment such as generators, worth 1 billion forints, which may be needed during the cold winter months. Magyar stressed that Hungary has not supplied weapons to Ukraine.
Zelenskyy thanked the delegation for the aid. The two sides then moved on to the Old Continent hotel in Uzhhorod, where officials including Hungarian Foreign Minister Anita Orbán discussed further support for Ukraine and its possible European integration.
The two leaders had already met in person at EU and NATO gatherings, but Hungary had made an intergovernmental meeting conditional on its demands being met.
Magyar signalled that the meeting was imminent in a speech to the Hungarian parliament on Monday. In it, he recalled the hate-mongering election campaign that the government of his predecessor, Viktor Orbán, had run against Ukraine and Zelenskyy.
By contrast, Magyar told lawmakers, his new government stands by the Hungarians of Transcarpathia and the Ukrainian people under attack, even if Moscow objects.
The Hungarian government is no longer blocking Ukraine's EU accession
According to media reports, Hungary's government withdrew its objections to the last two groups of negotiating chapters, known as clusters, in Ukraine's EU accession process at last week's meeting of EU ambassadors. This means Ukraine and Moldova can now begin talks on every area of EU membership, including particularly sensitive ones such as the environment and agriculture.
Agriculture is a especially delicate issue for Kyiv. The European Commission, under pressure from Poland and France among others, has proposed limiting the access of certain Ukrainian farm products to EU funds and the European market.
Speaking to Euronews, Ukrainian Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said Kyiv cannot accept its farmers being permanently shut out of the European market, and that any measures introduced must be temporary and proportionate.
Opening the negotiating clusters is only the beginning. Candidate countries must then bring their laws and practices into line with EU rules, and during that process member states can use their veto more than 100 times, halting progress.
The change of government in Hungary has given a big boost to EU support for Ukraine
Budapest held up Ukraine's accession process for a long time by blocking the opening of the negotiating clusters. Its veto also stalled Moldova's membership bid, which remains tied to Kyiv's.
Hungary's position began to change after Magyar replaced Viktor Orbán, who had been in power for 16 years. The first negotiating cluster was opened in June, the second in July and two more in October.
Last month, Kyiv and Budapest reached an agreement on the rights of the ethnic Hungarian minority in western Ukraine, which the Ukrainian parliament then adopted.
Although the Hungarian government was ready to move forward, it still waited a few weeks before agreeing to open the last two clusters. Years of anti-Ukrainian rhetoric from the Orbán government had made the step politically difficult at home.
On 16 October, on the sidelines of the European Council summit in Brussels, Belgium, the Hungarian side and Zelenskyy held their first bilateral meeting, another step towards better relations between the two neighbours.
