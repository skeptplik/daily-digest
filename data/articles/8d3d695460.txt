In short:
Springbok coach Rassie Erasmus has been banned for six matches by World Rugby after questioning the officiating in South Africa's last match against France.
South Africa lost 19-21 in Paris, after which Erasmus suggested the incorrect calls were equivalent to match fixing.
What's next?
Erasmus has set up a website for supporters to watch Saturday's game with him from his hotel room.
Banned South Africa coach Rassie Erasmus has invited Springbok supporters to join him virtually on Saturday to watch the Nations Championship test against Ireland.
The 54-year-old has been barred from attending the match in person after receiving a six-match suspension.
Erasmus was sanctioned after questioning the integrity of match officials at a press conference in Dublin on Monday following South Africa's thrilling 21-19 defeat by France in Paris last Friday.
The master coach, who has lifted South Africa from sixth in the rankings when he first took over in March 2018 to being the dominant force in world rugby today, said there had been potential "manipulation of the game" and that officials had missed 17 knee-drop tackles by the French in the Nations Championship fixture.
"The TMO [television match official] doesn't come in once. I'm not saying he's wrong. I'm saying if he didn't see it, it must be a technology problem," Erasmus told reporters in Dublin.
"If he saw it and he decided not to act on it, is that not match fixing? Is that not interfering with the outcome of a game?"
Erasmus also suggested his side were treated differently when it came to disciplinary matters compared to the Northern Hemisphere teams.
South African governing body SA Rugby confirmed Erasmus will request a full misconduct hearing in order to "contextualise his comments and assure the panel that it was not his intention to allege that any match official had acted with ulterior motives".
It is worth stating that Erasmus, a native Afrikaans speaker, was speaking in his second language.
However, this is far from Erasmus's first sanction for speaking out against officiating.
In 2021 he was suspended for 10 months after criticising Australian referee Nic Berry in an hour-long video during the British & Irish Lions tour.
Not long after he returned the next year he was banned for two further matches, having attacked Wayne Barnes's decisions in a test against France on his Twitter account.
But the impacts of his attacks have been profound.
Berry said Erasmus's "character assassination" of him caused "immeasurable damage," while Barnes's wife and children were subject to vile threats over social media.
In the wake of these latest comments, SA Rugby appealed to their supporters to not abuse the officials.
"Robust, respectful debate is natural, but teams have access to well-established and supported channels and protocols for raising officiating matters before and after matches," World Rugby said in a statement.
"Challenging a decision is not the same as questioning an official's integrity or making comments that expose them to personal abuse."
Erasmus has been unapologetic, saying he will happily watch the game with his 230,000 followers on X from his hotel room in Dublin.
"Can sit and moan or make the best of it," Erasmus posted on X yesterday.
"I'll be watching the match at the hotel. Not keen to watch on my own.
"So let's give it a go and hope I get the tech sorted — TV on, my laptop camera on, and you're welcome to join me," he said, telling interested adult fans to sign up at a newly created watchwithrassie.com website.
"This is personal, not official," he added.
A disclaimer on the website states: "This is a personal fan event, not an official Springbok, SA Rugby or broadcaster event, and it does not show or stream the match."
