Mary Peltola wins U.S. Senate seat in Alaska for Democrats
Democrats now emerge with a 56-44 margin in Senate after Alaska win
Democrat Mary Peltola defeated two-term Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan in Alaska, overcoming allegations that members of her campaign helped recruit a candidate who shares the incumbent's name and completing a political comeback in a state U.S. President Donald Trump repeatedly won by wide margins.
The Alaska race is the last U.S. Senate contest to be resolved in this year's midterm elections, and Peltola's victory expands the Democrats' majority after they retook control of the chamber. The party already won campaigns in conservative states including South Carolina, Texas, Kansas and, most recently, Mississippi.
With Peltola's victory, Democrats flipped nine Senate seats, the most since 1958. Republicans entered the midterms with a 53-47 majority, while Democrats now emerge with a 56-44 margin.
Messages seeking comment were left with both campaigns Tuesday night.

Peltola, in a statement on social media, said there "is no greater honour than having Alaskans' support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs and put Alaska first. Thank you, Alaska. Let's get to work."
Peltola previously held the state's lone House seat. She lost her bid for a second full term to a Republican in 2024, but she became central to Democrats' plans to retake the Senate in 2026.
Two candidates named Sullivan
In the campaign's final stretch, however, she faced allegations that her team helped recruit a retired teacher named Dan J. Sullivan to run in order to siphon votes from the incumbent.
Trump called for Peltola's prosecution after the New York Times first reported the allegation less than a week before the Nov. 3 election. Peltola denied any involvement.
"I knew nothing about any other candidates on the ballot except for me and the incumbent," Peltola told supporters in Juneau days before the election.
Dan J. Sullivan has denied any contact with Peltola's campaign or running at the Democrats' behest. He has not responded to messages from The Associated Press since the Times article was published.
The Times report was based partly on text messages and other documents and interviews with Peltola's former campaign manager, who was fired shortly before this year's open primary and later sought to recant her statements. But the second Sullivan had already been on Trump's mind. He referred to him multiple times during a rally in support of the senator in mid-October.
Later, the U.S. Department of Justice filed a lawsuit that Trump said he ordered. It sought to block certification of the race if the incumbent were to lose, pending an investigation into possible voter confusion. Peltola called the lawsuit "flagrant corruption."
The case was dismissed by a federal judge, but the Justice Department appealed. Attorneys for the state sought dismissal, saying officials should be able to conduct the election "without unwarranted interference by the United States."

They also argued the ballot was designed to distinguish the two Sullivans as much as possible and that Alaska's ranked choice voting process would allow any voters who might be confused to rank them.
The Justice Department also confirmed the existence of a criminal investigation, but Alaska's U.S. attorney would not say what potential crimes authorities were looking into.
Peltola finished first in the primary with 49.5 per cent of the vote, followed by the incumbent with 41.4 per cent.
Dan J. Sullivan finished a distant third with about 2.5 per cent, but he advanced to the general election as one of the top four finishers. He was listed on both ballots as Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., with no party affiliation, while the senator was listed as Dan S. Sullivan, Republican and incumbent.
Emphasized affordability
Peltola, who is Yup'ik, emphasized her Alaska roots and resurrected the "fish, family and freedom" tagline from her prior campaigns. She spoke about growing up in western Alaska and fishing for salmon on the Kuskokwim River with her father and spending parts of the summer at fish camp with her children and late husband, who died in September 2023.
She said the fish and wildlife that Alaskans long relied on to fill their freezers for the winter were becoming harder to find. The changes were forcing people to buy more food and other staples at the store — supplies that must be barged or flown in — and deal with "crushing prices," she said.
Peltola also said she saw members of Congress who were "more focused on their stock portfolios" than helping ease those costs and vowed to push back against the billionaire class.
Peltola will be just the fifth Democrat since statehood to represent Alaska in the Senate. The most recent was Mark Begich, whom Sullivan defeated in 2014.
