Instagram announced a change on Monday that may spell the end for users being nudged to visit a creator’s “link in bio.” At least when they’re promoting products sold on Amazon.
Starting today, creators and businesses can attach multiple Amazon affiliate links to their Instagram content—up to 30 per video or 20 per regular feed post. (Affiliate links let creators earn a commission on any resulting sales.) To participate in this Amazon program, creators must be US-based adults with a professional account that has over 1,000 followers.
Instagram’s parent company, Meta, dropped this shopping-focused change right before the holiday season really kicks off. As creators look to cash in on Amazon's sales, you might expect an avalanche of affiliate posts as you sip hot cocoa and scroll through Reels. But Instagram claims this won’t be the case.
“We don’t expect a sudden influx of shopping content from this launch,” Instagram spokesperson Megan Buckley tells WIRED. “Instagram’s recommendation systems will continue to recommend content based on people’s interests and what we think they’ll enjoy and find valuable. This update makes creators’ product recommendations easier to shop.”
For years, Instagram users could attach a single external link to their bio information. This led to the rise of bundlers like Linktree, where creators could create simple one-page sites with multiple links to content, like shopping recommendations or news articles. In 2023, Instagram expanded the number of links allowed in profiles to five. This most recent update could change how it feels to scroll Instagram, moving more shopping nudges from Amazon storefronts hidden in the bio to direct links on the viral videos.
In an example shared by Meta, a woman recording a Reel leans into her camera as she applies makeup. The caption reads: “My favorite lip tint these days!” Above the creator’s name, a small image tile pops up with the Amazon product. Tap the product and a page opens inside Instagram with the price. Tap one of the listed items, and Instagram will bump you over to the Amazon app or website to complete your purchase.
This change comes as social media shopping habits continue to expand and evolve. TikTok, one of Meta’s primary competitors, has been quite successful in expanding users’ purchasing habits through the app. Around this time last year, an analytics firm estimated that $19 billion worth of products were sold globally through TikTok Shop, putting it at roughly the same size as eBay. Unlike TikTok, however, Instagram will not take a cut of the sales (at least for now). Reddit is also experimenting with how users shop, heading into this holiday season, recently announcing Reddit Reviews, which uses generative AI to summarize user sentiment about products.
For over a decade, Instagram developers have searched for ways to streamline how users spend money through the app. In 2016, WIRED wrote about how the social media app added select commerce links from brands like Warby Parker, a fairly novel attempt at the time to get people to buy stuff through social media. A few years later, the Instagram experience morphed into something like a “digital mall” with in-app checkouts.
This latest attempt moves links from creator bios and places them directly in users' feeds. While this could be a boon for creators, the platform’s implementation could annoy users, who are already inundated with ads online. We’ll see if this change clicks.
