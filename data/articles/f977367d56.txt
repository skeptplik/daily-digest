Books make a perfect gift. They offer the chance to introduce a friend or family member to a story or world you love and adventures you can share together. You can even use a book as a seed for future gift ideas by getting someone hooked on a series. Black Friday sales offer plenty of bargains on fantasy and sci-fi classics and recent genre standouts for anyone looking to get an early start on holiday shopping. Even if you’re just looking for some reading material for your vacation, you should check out the 10 best Black Friday book deals.
10 Defy the Dusk (2026) by Danielle L. Jensen
The New York Times-bestselling author of The Bridge Kingdom kicks off a new duology putting a fantasy spin on Mad Max and Snowpiercer. Lirien’s magic broke the world 20 years ago, unleashing dangerous nocturnal creatures. While the most powerful survivors live on the Dawnrider, a high-speed rig ruled by a tyrant, Lirien seeks redemption by waging war against the night.
Price: $18 (normally $36) at Barnes & Noble
9 Other Worlds Than These (2026) by Stephen King and Peter Straub
Stephen King and Peter Straub co-wrote The Talisman in 1984 and teamed up again for the sequel, Black House, in 2001. While Straub died in 2022, King used his ideas to complete the trilogy and bring his work in The Dark Tower’s Mid-World to an end. Other Worlds Than These continues the adventures of Jack Sawyer as he tries to stop a rampaging gang of murderous teenagers from ending the world. Artist Gabriel Rodriguez brings the story to life with 36 black-and-white illustrations.
Price: $17.50 (normally $35) at Barnes & Noble
8 Scion (2026) by James Islington
The Will of the Many author James Islington has moved from fantasy to cyberpunk for his new series. Scion is an action-packed fast read about an enhanced assassin who targets people who would otherwise live forever. When Azure is hired as a bodyguard instead of a killer, the extremely challenging job reveals secrets about his employers and his own past. Along with the AI companions and shady dealings you’d expect from a cyberpunk story, Islington introduces the clever wrinkle of biosecurity – genetically modified monsters designed to protect people who can’t trust technology.
Price: $14 (normally $28) at Barnes & Noble
7 The Handmaid’s Tale (1985) and The Testaments (2019) by Margaret Atwood
Set in a dark future version of the United States where women are forced to bear children for high-status men, Margaret Atwood’s The Handmaid’s Tale took on new significance as advocates for women’s rights and reproductive freedom adopted its imagery at protests. The book became a hit Hulu series, and Atwood’s 2019 sequel, The Testaments, inspired a new show that premiered in April and has been renewed for a second season. Take advantage of this deal on a box set to read the intense books, which offer more perspectives on life in the theocracy of Gilead.
Price: $12.52 (normally $35) at Amazon
6 The Familiar (2024) by Leigh Bardugo
A standalone novel set in 16th-century Spain, The Familiar follows Luzia Cotado, a servant with a knack for magic. That talent takes her from toiling in the kitchens to being embroiled in court politics and the world of the occult. As she trains to control her powers and learns to navigate a world of alchemists and seers, Luzia has to conceal her Jewish heritage in order to avoid persecution by the Inquisition. It’s a perfect intro to Bardugo’s work before diving into her Ninth House modern fantasy series.
Price: $9.99 (normally $19.99) at Barnes & Noble
5 A Court of Thorns and Roses (2015) by Sarah J. Maas
The adventures of Feyre Archeron in the faerie realm Prythian helped turn romantasy into one of the biggest literary subgenres. A Court of Thorns and Roses is a steamy story of a huntress who falls for Tamlin, her faerie lord captor, and has to find a way to protect him and his magical world from an encroaching evil force. This is a gift that can keep on giving as there are five more books in the series. A new entry, A Court of Forgotten Melody, is releasing in January.
Price: $9.50 (normally $19) at Barnes & Noble
4 The Fifth Season (2015) by N. K. Jemisin
N. K. Jemisin’s Hugo Award-winning novel is extremely bleak, beginning with the world being destroyed and following three women dealing with the aftermath. The first book in the Broken Earth series explores the devastating effects of climate change and intolerance, as people with the same sort of power that shattered the world have to hide their abilities for fear of being killed by angry mobs or their own families.
Price: $7.72 (normally $19.99) at Amazon
3 The Last Wish (1993) by Andrzej Sapkowski
Andrzej Sapkowski began building the fantasy world of The Witcher’s Continent with his short story collection The Last Wish, which puts dark spins on classic fairy tales like Beauty and the Beast and Snow White. While his Witcher novels focus more on the brutal cost of war, The Last Wish is the perfect mix of humor, action, and tragedy. The monster hunter Geralt of Rivia has to face plenty of threats from mythical creatures and cruel people alike, while trying his best not to get too tangled in politics.
Price: $7.31 (normally $18.99) at Amazon
2 I, Robot (1950) by Isaac Asimov
One of the foundational books of modern science fiction, the short story collection I, Robot introduced the term robotics and the idea of laws governing artificial intelligence to prevent the robots from harming their creators. The book is very different from the 2004 movie starring Will Smith, weaving together complex tales about morality, psychology, and unintended consequences. Many of the principles Asimov first explored in I, Robot were further developed in his Foundation series and Robot novels, making I, Robot the perfect place to start with his extensive bibliography.
Price: $6.77 (normally $19) at Amazon
1 The Power (2016) by Naomi Alderman
This provocative novel is framed as a history of the 21st century in a world where women develop electrical powers and establish dominance over men. Interweaving the stories of four characters around the planet, Alderman imagines how the abilities spark new religious, political, and military movements, even as disempowered men look to hang onto the status quo.
