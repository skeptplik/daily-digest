Russian television presenter Nailya Asker-Zade, who has been described as the longtime partner of VTB chief Andrei Kostin, invested in Telegram founder Pavel Durov’s ill-fated cryptocurrency project, the exiled investigative outlet IStories reported, citing internal Deutsche Bank documents.
A 2019 report by the German bank’s financial crime unit listed Asker-Zade among investors it flagged as raising concerns, according to IStories.
The report did not disclose how much she invested, but the minimum investment was $20 million in the first round and $1 million in the second.
Telegram raised $1.7 billion in two private rounds in 2018 to build a blockchain platform called Telegram Open Network, launch a cryptocurrency known as Gram and develop its messaging app.
Investors were selected individually, and some prominent Russian investors were turned away, the Vedomosti business daily reported at the time.
Telegram abandoned the project in 2020 after the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission challenged the token sale in court, saying it violated U.S. securities laws. The company agreed to return more than $1 billion to investors. In 2021, VTB Capital helped arrange a $1 billion bond sale for Telegram.
Asker-Zade’s reported ties to Kostin drew public attention in 2019 when late Kremlin critic Alexei Navalny’s Anti-Corruption Foundation (FBK) said she had used a $62 million yacht and a private jet previously owned by an offshore company linked to VTB.
The FBK also reported that she had acquired two luxury apartments in central Moscow, one previously owned by the state bank and the other by one of its senior executives.
The Deutsche Bank documents also identified Olga Gruzdeva, the wife of billionaire and former Tula region Governor Vladimir Gruzdev, as a $40 million investor, IStories reported. Lamesa Holding, a company linked to billionaire Viktor Vekselberg, was also listed among the buyers.
Previously reported investors included billionaires Roman Abramovich and Yuri Milner, Wimm-Bill-Dann co-founder David Yakobashvili, Qiwi founder Sergei Solonin, former government minister Mikhail Abyzov and former Wirecard executive Jan Marsalek.
