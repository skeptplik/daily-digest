Indonesia's former police chief, fired this week from his latest post as Presidential chief of staff, may have committed "treason" through his alleged involvement in wiretapping the head of state's telephone, the government said on Wednesday (November 4, 2026).
"What was done may have fallen into the category of being considered as leading to treason," Presidential spokesman Prasetyo Hadi told reporters in Jakarta of Tuesday's (November 3, 2026) arrest of Listyo Sigit Prabowo.
"This also constitutes actions that endanger the security of the interests of the state, including jeopardising the safety of the country's leader," he said.
On Tuesday, the national police announced it had arrested Mr. Listyo and two other senior officers for allegedly tapping the phone of President Prabowo Subianto, government officials and politicians, journalists, NGO representatives and businesspeople.
Top detective Asep Safrudin said this was done “without any legal basis or without any investigative or inquiry process in accordance with applicable laws”.
Mr. Prabowo, who took office in 2024, has come under increasing pressure for his government's handling of the economy and destructive wildfires in the Southeast Asian archipelago.
The former general reshuffled his cabinet for a seventh time last month, appointing Listyo, 57, as his chief of staff.
Mr. Listyo had until then served as national police chief during the period of the alleged wiretapping that investigators said started in 2025.
On Wednesday, Mr. Prasetyo said the reason for the wiretap was not yet known.
"When we talk about the head of state... there is a great deal of information there that is naturally very sensitive, which could endanger... the interests and the strength of the nation and the state," he said.
Mr. Prasetyo added the President had expressed his "profound concern" about the case.
