A handful of tech CEOs, including Elon Musk, are celebrating Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia. Despite being caught in three affairs — including one with an alleged Chinese spy that has triggered multiple congressional investigations over national security concerns — Huang was reinstated as CEO and president of Nvidia on Monday morning.
“The mistake has been corrected,” Tesla and SpaceX leader Elon Musk wrote on X about Huang’s return.
Musk had been among the most vocal tech leaders to advocate for Huang.
“I think it’s like kicking Michael Jordan off your team when you’re on a winning streak, and it just doesn’t seem like a good thing for shareholders,” Musk said last week at the DealBook Summit.
OpenAI CEO Sam Altman had taken a more neutral stance, commenting last week, “It’s hard to imagine the AI industry without Jensen.”
Altman had experienced a similar situation when he was ousted as CEO of OpenAI, then reinstated days later. After the announcement that Huang would return, Altman simply posted, “Jensen!” Many other tech CEOs have similarly refrained from commenting on the matter, given the shifting politics surrounding Huang’s ouster and reinstatement, as well as the national security concerns at the center of the controversy.
In addition to Musk, Binance co-founder and former CEO Changpeng Zhao (CZ), who served prison time for failing to stop criminal activity on the Binance crypto exchange, is also supportive of Huang.
“Glad to see @JensenHuang back,” CZ posted on X with a clapping hands emoji.
Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, who had been among 200,000 signatories on an open letter calling for Huang’s reinstatement, wrote on X, “Jensen’s return will be a demonstration of American resiliency to corporate, political and journalistic missteps.”
Another signatory on the open letter, former Uber CEO Travis Kalanick, who had similarly been driven out of the company he founded after scandals, also voiced his support for Huang on X.
When Nvidia’s stock tanked in reaction to Huang’s ousting, billionaire hedge fund manager Bill Ackman said he believes that the Nvidia board was “not acting in the best interest of shareholders nor in a manner consistent with their fiduciary duties.” As Nvidia’s stock began to rebound Monday morning, Ackman wrote, “The wisdom of the crowd prevails.”
The attitude toward Huang’s return is not as celebratory outside of the tech industry. Across the political spectrum, some elected officials are skeptical about President Donald Trump’s claims that Huang’s return does not threaten national security.
“No national security risk? Based on what?” wrote Senator John Cornyn (R-TX).
Senator Pete Ricketts (R-NB) added, “Nvidia’s decision today doesn’t excuse Jensen Huang from testifying before Congress. It also doesn’t absolve Nvidia of answering for national security concerns and unaccounted-for chips. I look forward to Mr. Huang coming to the Senate in December.”
Huang is still expected to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, as well as privately before three House committees.
