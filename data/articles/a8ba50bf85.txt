The sporting world is littered with legends.
Few have done more to earn that label than Geoff Henke, who died on Sunday in Melbourne. He was 99.
To describe Henke as a visionary sports administrator would barely scratch the surface of what he achieved on behalf of Australia's elite snow and ice athletes.
"Geoff Henke was the founding father of Australian winter sport," Australian Olympic Committee (AOC) president Ian Chesterman said in a statement.
"Every winter sport Australian athlete has benefited from Geoff's vision, his persistence, and his refusal to accept that winter sport did not have a place in this country.
"Geoff gave his life to winter sport and to the Olympic Movement.
"He fought for winter athletes at a time when few others would and never stopped.
"He saw what Australian winter athletes could achieve long before anyone else did, and he spent decades making sure they had the chance to prove it."
Henke, an Australian ice hockey representative for 13 years, was denied the chance to compete at an Olympic Games himself.
In 1956, the Australian team that he was a part of was set to head to Cortina to take part in the Games, only for the then-chairman of the AOC to refuse to sanction them.
Four years later, Henke did not travel to Squaw Valley, a decision he told ABC Sport he regretted.
But it was his snubbing that meant Henke would fight tooth and nail to ensure that everyone who had a chance to compete at a Games could.
He served as Australia's Chef de Mission at six consecutive Winter Olympic Games, starting in Innsbruck in 1976 and going right through to Lillehammer 1994.
At that 1994 Olympics, Australia won its first ever Winter Olympic medal, a bronze in the men’s 5,000m short track speed skating relay.
The nation has won at least one medal at every Winter Olympic Games since, culminating in the stunning, record-breaking performance in Milan-Cortina earlier this year.
While so many people at those Games wondered how a sun-burned country such as Australia could ever compete on the same level as established Winter Olympic powerhouses, the answers could find their root cause with one name: Geoff Henke.
"Geoff was a dream maker," Milano-Cortina Chef de Mission and two-time Olympic medallist Alisa Camplin said.
"He always had a sparkle in his eye and a project on the go — his belief in what Australia's winter athletes could deliver was unparalleled."
Henke, who was inducted into the Sport Australia Hall of Fame, was instrumental in the establishment of a high-performance centre for Australian skiing at Mount Buller, which helped Zali Steggall to bronze at the 1998 Nagano Olympics, Australia's only Olympic medal to date in Alpine skiing.
He then went on to establish the Olympic Winter Institute of Australia in 1998, and served as its chairperson for 24 years.
During those 24 years under his stewardship, Australia won 17 Olympic medals, 16 World Championship titles, 38 World Championship medals and 364 World Cup medals.
Henke's staunch and repeated advocacy resulted in the building of the water ramp in Brisbane, which opened in 2021, something Henke described as a "miracle".
Twelve of Australia's 2026 Winter Olympians train there, and four of the record-breaking six Olympic medals were won by those athletes, including gold medals to Jakara Anthony and Cooper Woods.
Henke was also a huge advocate for the Ice House in his home town of Melbourne, with its main competition rink named after him.
"Winter athletes of the last 30 years profusely thank Geoff for setting up the engine room that's enabled our Winter Olympic dreams to come true," Camplin said.
"Geoff's legacy goes beyond the results we enjoyed in Milano Cortina early this year. He showed us how to live a passionate and purposeful life, and how important it is to give back to the winter community and our country."
Henke's joy at the development in winter sport was evident to anyone who spoke to him.
Even this past weekend, it is said he was deeply enthused by the top-10 World Cup finish of slalom skier Madi Hoffman.
"Every winter sport Australian athlete who competes at the Olympic Winter Games today does so from the foundations he built," Chesterman said.
"His legacy is measured not only in medals Australia has won, but in the sport systems he built, the facilities and opportunities that exist today for young Australians who dream of competing at the Winter Games.
"Personally, Geoff started as my boss, became my mentor and we finished as the greatest of friends. Geoff was loved and admired by many and will be sadly missed."
Henke is survived by his wife, Gweneth, and their daughter, Joanne, both of whom are Winter Olympians.
