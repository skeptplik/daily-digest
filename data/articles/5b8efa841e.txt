If your TV looks like an off-cut from Stonehenge, isn’t quite as smart as your microwave or, heaven forfend, is boring old HD rather than 4K HDR (high dynamic range), it could be time to invest in something more up to date.
But even a cursory look at the latest TVs available will confirm a bewildering array of options, from super-slim models with unfeasibly large screens to high performers that rival the picture quality at your local cinema.
It’s difficult to know where to begin. But don’t despair. I’ve tested nine models and whittled them down to my six recommendations to help you find the best TV in time for the festive family binge-watching season.
This buyer’s guide takes a clear, no‑nonsense look at the latest models worth considering across brands, budgets and platforms. There’s an entry‑level option from Samsung, a feature‑packed mid‑range Hisense model that surprises for the price, and a high‑end LG OLED for those who want the best possible picture quality. I also highlight other sets that stand out for design, value, performance and smart connectivity.
The aim is simple: help you choose the right TV for your home without undue jargon, hype or guesswork. Whether you’re upgrading your main living room screen or buying a second set for the bedroom, this guide will steer you through the options, so you can buy with confidence.
Why you should trust me
I’ve been reviewing TVs since the glory days of cathode ray tubes, and over the years have scrutinised every panel technology under the sun. I’ve also talked to engineers behind some of the top brands and sought advice from Hollywood creatives who work on your favourite shows and movies about the best way to enjoy their hard work at home.
How I tested
All the TVs here were tested straight from the box. I documented installation, ease of use and features before measuring each set’s performance using industry-standard metrics. I then sat back and watched a shedload of TV, from streaming services, Blu-rays and regular Freeview.
I compared the screens in daylight and darkened rooms, and assessed their colour and contrast. I looked for vibrant but natural-looking colours, deep blacks and high brightness, as well as the ability to show shadow detail without reducing darker areas to a black splodge.
I also paid close attention to audio. Today’s thinnest TVs can often struggle to produce rich, engaging sound, which is why so many of us end up adding a soundbar.
Usability was another key factor. Most 32in-plus TVs these days come with some form of smart TV operating system, but they’re not all the same. The better-equipped platforms offer more than just the top streaming services; they’ll often have some form of smart home control. Ease of use is paramount.
I also compared the various panel technologies, as these have a fundamental impact on picture quality. OLED screens produce perfect blacks and look great when you dim the lights. Some QLED models make great entry-level screens, while mini LED TVs generally offer higher brightness, making them a top choice for daytime viewing and, depending on the model, gaming.
All nine TVs were returned to the manufacturers after testing. Three didn’t make the final cut – although there was nothing intrinsically wrong with them.
On the cutting room floor were the Philips 55in OLED761, an extremely well-equipped budget OLED that you can pick up for £1,199; the Samsung 65in S83H, which is a fine entry-level OLED bolstered by a superb Tizen smart TV OS; and the 48in Panasonic Z90B, a great small-screen OLED option.
The best TVs for your home in 2026
Best budget 4K TV:
Samsung M80H
The Samsung M80H makes perfect sense if you want a feature-packed 55in TV without spending the sort of money you’d need to buy the brand’s leading Neo QLED. It’s perfect for everyday viewing and streaming, and is well equipped for gaming. You get a lot of TV tech for the money.
Why we love it 
An affordable option in Samsung’s mini LED TV range, the 55in M80H sells for well below the brand’s more ambitious Neo QLED sets, but it doesn’t skimp on features.
There’s 4K 120Hz gaming support across all three HDMI inputs, AI-powered picture processing and Samsung’s excellent Tizen smart TV service, which is home to streaming services, catchup TV players and Samsung TV Plus.
Picture quality is good, albeit not as bright or dynamic as the brand’s more expensive mini LED screens (well, something’s got to give). But it’s perfectly suited to daytime TV and sports – and particularly good for games, as it’s super responsive. I measured input lag at just 10.2ms (with 1080p/60Hz games), which is very good, and it looks great with fast-moving action titles.
Sound comes via a built-in 20W stereo system, which is great for dialogue. The banter in Slow Horses (on Apple TV) really sparkles. It’s worth considering a soundbar, though. In the meantime, take advantage of its Bluetooth connectivity to pair your wireless headphones.
It’s a shame that … the M80H doesn’t quite have the brightness to do full justice to 4K HDR programmes. If you want the best image quality, it’s worth spending a little more.

Best for Sky TV:
Sky Glass Air
Sky Glass Air is a great way into Sky’s all-streaming ecosystem: it’s a smart-looking television with a simple setup and a large amount of live and on-demand content. Its quantum-dot picture is colourful, and while stereo sound is basic, it’s easy enough to add a soundbar. My 65in model is also available on a four-year contract from £15 a month with a £20 upfront cost, plus your chosen Sky subscription package (starting at £15 a month for new customers, or £18 for existing).
Why we love it
The Glass Air is Sky’s second all-streaming TV, after the Sky Glass, which is now in its second generation, and offers the company’s wifi channel experience at a more affordable price than its flagship Glass Gen 2 model. It lacks some bells and whistles, but is lighter and more fashionable by design.
Available in grey, green or white, Sky Glass Air comes in 43in, 55in and 65in screens. My test model was the 65in; it comes with a colour-matched central pedestal stand and remote control.
Sky Glass Air has a QLED panel, but doesn’t match the high brightness or deep blacks of Sony’s Bravia 7 II model. But it’s not directly comparable. That’s because Sky Glass isn’t your typical telly. Sky’s premium TV channels can be streamed straight to the TV: you don’t need a dish, as it gets everything over wifi. The TV is available on contract, much like your smartphone, but can also be bought outright.
Connectivity comprises three HDMI 2.1 inputs, with HDMI 2 supporting eARC for an easy soundbar connection. That’s enough to add a soundbar, alongside a Blu-ray player and games console, although it won’t support 4K/120Hz gaming.
Picture-wise, I found the Sky Glass Air to be bright and colour rich. It uses a 4K quantum-dot LED panel and has an Auto picture mode that adjusts the image according to the type of content you’re watching. This makes it particularly easy to live with.
I loved the way it presented Formula One, with car liveries looking vibrant and glossy, before becoming more cinematic for blockbuster TV shows. The moody science fiction series 3 Body Problem (Netflix) can be tonally quite dark, what with all of its creepy interior shots, but Sky Glass Air made sure there was enough contrast to follow the action.
Sound comes via a 2 x 15W stereo system. It’s punchy enough, but I think a soundbar upgrade is inevitable if you’re a film buff who wants to appreciate the best scores.
The real reason to opt for Sky Glass Air is the Sky OS. Along with all the familiar Sky channels are key streamers such as Netflix, BBC iPlayer, Prime Video and Disney+. The basic Sky Essential TV bundle starts at £15 a month and includes Sky Atlantic, Netflix Standard with Ads, Discovery+ and free-to-air channels including BBC, ITV, Channel 4, Sky News and Sky Arts. You’ll pay extra for UHD, movie channels and sports. If you switch from Sky, the TV can still be used with apps such as Netflix, YouTube and others as long as the TV has been activated with a free Sky iD. The HDMI ports also continue to work, and you can hook up a TV aerial for Freeview channels – although you can expect a disjointed user experience. And cancelling Sky TV does not cancel any finance agreement you have on the set itself.
Where the set falls behind rivals is in its gaming performance. It’s not great if you’re into console shoot-’em-ups. The panel runs at 60Hz, which means it lacks the super-smooth, high-frame-rate gaming support of screens that offer 4K 120Hz, such as the LG C6. It’s fine for casual fun, but serious gamers should play elsewhere.
It’s a shame that … the set doesn’t go out of its way to offer gamers a great user experience. You can still hook up a console, but the input lag is high (I measured 67.5ms) even in its Game mode.

Best mini LED TV for gaming:
Hisense U7S ULED mini LED TV
Nails the essentials, with Freely and HDR for movie fans
Only two out of four HDMI inputs support 4K 120Hz gaming
The Hisense U7S makes a persuasive case for stepping up from a standard QLED screen to a feature-packed mid-range mini LED TV. Its picture performance gives films and games real visual punch, and gamers will appreciate the higher-spec HDMI ports. The provision of Freely makes it a lot easier to place about the house, as TV is delivered over wifi and doesn’t need a traditional aerial connection.
Why we love it
If you’re looking for a mini LED TV that combines a bright, impressive 4K picture with strong gaming credentials, the Hisense U7S is an easy recommendation.
It’s available in sizes from 50in to 100in. I tested the 50in, and it makes a great first impression.
Colours are rich and well saturated without appearing overcooked, and its HDR performance is extremely strong. Reflections sparkle, lights have convincing intensity, and darker areas retain appealing depth. Black levels are impressively inky, which really helps when watching letterboxed movies – the picture doesn’t look like it’s sandwiched between uneven grey cardboard.
It’s great with games, too. Console play is smooth, with fluid action and outstanding colour. Overwatch is always my go-to when it comes to fast-moving, colour-rich games, and it looked peachy here.
Sound is above average for a thin LED-based TV. The set’s audio system has been developed with French audio specialist Devialet and delivers 40W of amplification, and includes a 20W rear-mounted subwoofer. Speech is nice and clear while action movies pack a punch. When I watched Ride or Die (on Prime Video), the audio had just the right amount of explosive thump, although I did find that if I had the set at full volume, there was some cabinet rattle.
Design is clean and understated. A slim black bezel surrounds the panel, and the central pedestal includes cable management, making the 50in model reasonably easy to position on narrow furniture. The main limitation is connectivity: only two of the four HDMI inputs support 4K at high frame rates, and one doubles as the eARC connection for a soundbar. So if you want to play games on both a PlayStation 5 and Xbox at 120fps and use a soundbar, your options are limited. Of course, you can connect consoles to any of the HDMI inputs if you’re happy to play 4K games at 60Hz.
As mentioned above, the set also has the wireless TV service Freely. You can watch BBC, ITV, Channel 4, Channel 5 and others without connecting a traditional aerial.
It’s a shame that … Vidaa, Hisense’s smart TV system, doesn’t quite have all the same fancy features of rivals, such as Google TV or Samsung’s Tizen. But if you’re just looking for the big-name streaming services, it’ll do the job.

Best gallery-style TV:
TCL A400 Pro QLED mini LED TV
If you want a TV that can occupy a prominent place on the wall without looking like a black hole when switched off, this is a great buy. The QD mini LED panel delivers high brightness, rich colour and convincing HDR for the money, and Google TV is an easy smart OS to live with.
Why we love it 
Samsung started this style of TV, but now gallery-style TVs are all the rage – beloved because you can slap on a picture-frame bezel and hang them on the wall, so they look like a work of art (that also happens to show EastEnders).
Now TCL has entered the market, with a twist on the theme. Rather than make the picture frame an accessory, it comes with a walnut-effect one fixed, as standard – one less thing to worry about. To maximise the art effect, TCL supplies free displayable artwork images, many of which are recognisable museum pieces, no subscription required. By contrast, Samsung Frame owners can subscribe to the Samsung Art Store, which offers many thousands of artworks, for a monthly subscription.
My TCL A400 Pro was the 65in, but it’s also available in 55in and 75in sizes. The screen itself has a matte, non-reflective finish designed to reduce distractions from ambient light. This works reasonably well, although it can diffuse black-level performance. I struggled to make out what was happening during a particularly dark scene on the drama Paradise (Disney+).
It features four HDMI inputs, with two supporting high-frame-rate gaming. Streaming and catchup TV come via the Google TV platform, which also offers plenty of lesser-known services available from the Google Play store.
Image quality is very good, with dynamic contrast and high average brightness. The A400 Pro is particularly comfortable for daytime TV, but looks good with movies too. The audio system uses rear-facing speakers, but these still manage to deliver clear dialogue and punchy bass, which really drew me into a binge watch of Clarkson’s Farm on Prime Video – although I still couldn’t understand anything Gerald said.
Bluetooth headphones can be paired. The A400 Pro can also double as a Bluetooth music display, showing artwork and lyrics while music plays, which fits neatly with its lifestyle remit.
Obviously, if you leave the set running in art mode, it’s powered up, and the backlight remains on; energy consumption would be higher than if the set was left in standby (0.5W). But the costs will still be modest; its energy rating is D when running artworks in SDR (standard dynamic range).
It’s a shame that … this stylish gallery TV looks good on the wall and performs better than its price suggests, but the walnut-effect frame is fixed, so you can’t change it if you want to switch up your living room style. Sound quality is solid, but the rear-facing speakers lack the scale and immersion of a dedicated soundbar.

Best OLED TV:
LG C6
Excellent picture quality with deep blacks and good contrast
Virtual surround sound modes aren’t very convincing
If you want a premium TV with superb image quality and great gaming credentials, the LG C6 doesn’t disappoint. Its Alpha 11 Gen 3 processor brings a tangible increase in HDR brightness over last year’s C-series model, which was well received when first released, while OLED’s inherent black levels and excellent shadow definition remain hugely cinematic. Not cheap, but well worth the investment for serious home cinema fans.
Why we love it
In the world of premium TV screens, LG has carved a serious reputation. Its C6 model boasts advanced picture processing and brilliant gaming features. The 65in C6 tested here has an RRP of £2,499.99, but the range spans 42in to 83in, with RRPs running from £1,299.99 to £4,799.99. Prices can be volatile, so be sure to look out for sale discounts.
Build quality is excellent, although the glossy screen lacks an anti-glare, matte-style filter found on gallery TVs and the rival Samsung S90H OLED. This makes the screen quite reflective, so it might be best to avoid placing table lamps in front of the set. You may want to consider the room’s lighting before you install it.
Picture quality is excellent, courtesy of that new processor, and because it’s OLED you can rest assured images are highly dynamic with inky black levels. This is the kind of TV that makes everything look good, from bright, colourful daytime TV fare such as Tipping Point to moody blockbuster TV shows like House of the Dragon.
There’s plenty to watch, too. The webOS 26 operating system has all the key streaming services built in, plus LG’s own free-to-watch channels. It’s all controlled with the latest slim version of LG’s mouse-like Magic Remote, with customisable buttons and voice control.
The screen is ideal for gamers, as all four HDMI inputs support 120Hz gaming from PlayStation 5 and Xbox. Input lag is low, so games play fast and look slick.
The TV’s audio has 40W of total amplification spread across a 2.2 configuration (2 x full-range drivers and 2 x woofers). While LG claims a Virtual 11.1.2 up-mix of stereo courtesy of its Alpha 11 AI Sound Pro processor, I didn’t find the effect very convincing. On the plus side, it plays loud. Consequently, I didn’t feel the immediate need for a soundbar.
It’s a shame that … this polished OLED all-rounder delivers superb pictures, but the glossy screen lacks an anti-glare filter, so reflections can become distracting in bright rooms. And while the C6 represents great value for a premium OLED, it’ll still set you back some serious change.

What you need to know
Do I really need a new TV?
Way back when, you only really bought a new TV when the old one gave up the ghost. That’s because tellies were all very much of a muchness. Things are different now: a constant cycle of upgrades, feature improvements and evolving smart platforms serves to constantly tempt us. But how do you know when it’s actually time to make the switch?
As a general rule, when your smart TV stops receiving updates from the manufacturer, and streaming apps stop or disappear entirely, it might be time to consider a new model.
Perhaps you’re investing in a new games console and want a TV that really shows off your system, with advanced motion handling, low input lag and high-frame-rate gaming? A new set could give you a competitive edge.
Perhaps the best reason to buy is the arrival of new panel technologies, such as RGB mini LED, which offer significant leaps in picture brightness and colour depth.
Of course, you can always upgrade your viewing experience without splurging on a new set. If it’s a better smart TV experience you’re after, then adding an HDMI streaming stick is an easy fix. Amazon’s Fire TV Stick 4K Plus is a plug-and-play solution that features all the key streaming and catchup services (Netflix, Apple TV, Disney+, Prime Video and so on) through an easy-to-use interface. It is, however, tightly integrated into Amazon’s shopping portal.
