Church of England clergy could be banned from joining or supporting the hard-right Restore Britain party under a motion put forward for debate at the General Synod.
The move is an attempt to replicate the decision by bishops 17 years ago prohibiting clergy from belonging to or supporting the British National party, declaring its policies incompatible with church teaching on racial equality.
It comes against a backdrop of concerns about Christianity and Christian symbols being appropriated by the far right, including the anti-Muslim activist Tommy Robinson, and support for him from some pastors.
A vicar from a historic west London parish is among those fronting a “carol concert” next month organised by Robinson, who is expected to be jailed days later after his conviction for harassing two journalists.
Those behind the Church of England private members’ motion on Restore Britain membership are gathering signatures. It could be debated next year if 100 are collected, and would be expected to pass with support from many in the synod’s largest bloc of members, the liberal-leaning Together for the Church of England network, and others including some in the Evangelical Group.
Rupert Lowe, the leader of Restore Britain, described the motion as “outrageous”. “We will fight them in the court,” said the Great Yarmouth MP, who formed the party after falling out with Nigel Farage and leaving Reform UK. “I am not tolerating it.”
Sam Wilson, a lay member of the synod and youth worker in Altrincham, Greater Manchester, said he had proposed the motion after witnessing what he described as an upsurge in far-right activity on the streets and racialised language online.
Restore has been polling at as much as 5% and has become a magnet for far-right activists, including some who have explicitly drawn a link between Christianity and British identity. Members include Calvin Robinson, a cleric and former GB News presenter who was pictured along with Lowe at a Restore Britain event in London this month and is facing calls for his deportation from the US after saying Jews should be “driven out of America”.
Wilson told the Guardian: “My motivation is not that there is a large number of vicars who are supportive of Restore, but more that I think it is incumbent on the church to really establish a position and draw a line in terms of what is acceptable and not acceptable debate.”
The motion states that the synod, the national governing and legislative assembly of the church, affirms that “all people are created in the image of God and that racism is a sin”. It calls on bishops to exercise their power under section 3(3) of the Clergy Conduct Measure 2026 to make a declaration that the policies, objectives, activities and public statements of Restore Britain are incompatible with the Church of England’s teaching on the equality of persons or groups of different races. Section 3(3) allows for clergy to be suspended and ultimately defrocked.
Wilson added: “The last time anything like this was debated was in relation to the BNP and it passed with near unanimity, so there is confidence that this will also pass. A fear back then was that the church debating these parties could give them undue oxygen and publicity but that has changed now with X and other platforms.”
Robinson, whose real name is Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, has increasingly made Christian nationalist rhetoric part of his brand and is planning a “carol service” in central London on 12 December. It will be addressed by a range of far-right supporters. Those billed to speak include Father Luke Dobson, a Church of England priest and vicar of St George’s Headstone in Harrow, London.
Dobson has previously appeared alongside far-right activists, including members of Restore Britain, where he has said that deportations are about “maintaining our Christian culture”. At other times he has spoken of the need for the Church of England to rediscover “the lost art of excommunication” and apply it to those who refused to renounce “deceptive doctrines” in order for the church to be “spotless and pure”.
He also said that issues such as abortion and immigration were interconnected, adding that immigrants would not be needed if abortion was not allowed. “Immigration brings a degradation to our culture as English because other cultures are coming in who are not in a Christian mindset or culture,” he added.
