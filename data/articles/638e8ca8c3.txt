A record share of Japanese people views ties with China and Russia negatively, a government survey showed, underscoring the diplomatic challenges facing Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi.
Some 93% of respondents described Japan-China relations as poor or somewhat poor, the highest since the government began posing the question in 1986, according to preliminary results of a Cabinet Office survey released Friday. The figures aren’t fully comparable as the survey methodology changed in 2020. A record 93.7% also viewed ties with Russia negatively.
The findings come at the end of a week of high-profile diplomacy in Asia, where Takaichi came face to face with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen. Her relations with both have been strained. A yearlong standoff with China persists, and ties with Russia worsened after Moscow arrested a Japanese journalist in October and seized assets of Japan Tobacco this month.
Tokyo’s relationship with Beijing has deteriorated since Takaichi told parliament last November that a Chinese attack on Taiwan could threaten Japan’s survival, potentially allowing Tokyo to exercise its right to collective self-defense. Beijing demanded she retract the remarks. China also imposed export restrictions and launched personal attacks against the Japanese leader.
At this week’s summit in Shenzhen, Takaichi exchanged handshakes and smiles with Xi, though the two held no formal talks. Her reception in China was otherwise distant, with a cool welcome at the airport and a place in the back row of the leaders’ family photo.
Takaichi also spoke with Putin for 30 minutes and raised Russia’s seizure of Japanese assets and the arrest of the Japanese journalist, but Russia has yet to acknowledge that the meeting took place. Moscow’s takeover of Japan Tobacco’s Russian operations is expected to push the company into a net loss for the year ending December, the company said Thursday.
The souring view of China and Russia contrast markedly with Japanese sentiment toward South Korea. Some 62% of respondents expressed affinity toward the country, up from 35% in 2020. South Korea is an important partner in addressing global challenges, a Foreign Ministry official who oversaw the survey said, adding that bilateral ties have become increasingly vital amid a difficult regional security environment.
The survey targeted 3,000 adults nationwide from Sept. 17 to Oct. 25. Preliminary results were based on responses received by Oct. 16, with final findings due next year.
