L’Assiette Champenoise in Tinqueux, France, earned a 99.5 score, reserved for the best restaurants in the world, for the first time this year.
LaListe’s Top 1,000 World’s Best Restaurants 2027 list is out now – and more than half of the spots earning the very highest rating are in Europe.
Created by pulling together reviews from food critics, restaurant guides, other media sources and bloggers, each one is rated with a score out of 100, which is then weighted based on its reliability and reach. These are brought together, along with online reviews from guests, to create an overall score.
Only 13 restaurants received the highest rating – 99.5 – this year, with seven of these places found in Europe, including two in France, and one each in Switzerland, Italy, Sweden, Spain and Germany.
“L’Assiette Champenoise and Sushi Saito are new at the top, and Frantzén is back,” LaListe founder and CEO Philippe Faure said. “But great gastronomy is not defined by the size of the bill: with our new European selections, we show that it is possible to eat remarkably well at reasonable prices.”
Outside of the continent, the US and Japan had two restaurants apiece, with the final two coming from Macao and Hong Kong.
The very best restaurants in Europe, according to LaListe
L’Assiette Champenoise, the new addition to LaListe’s top step, is in Tinqueux, a suburb just outside of Reims.
Owned by chef Arnaud Lallement, the restaurant has had three Michelin stars since 2014. Signature dishes include the blue lobster, created in homage to his father, who passed the restaurant down to him, and the caviar with smoked haddock and potato.
Cheval Blanc by Peter Knogl in Basel, Switzerland, also has three Michelin stars, as well as 19 Gault&Millau points. There, you can expect “French haute cuisine meets Mediterranean and Asian influences”.
In Brusaporto outside of Bergamo, the family-run Da Vittorio (also three Michelin stars) offers several tasting menus, with one centred around truffles, another around seafood, and one that gives free rein to the chefs.
The first Swedish restaurant to earn three Michelin stars, you’ll find Frantzén in Stockholm. Owned by Björn Frantzén, he is the only chef in the world to have three restaurants with the top accolade – the other two being Zén in Singapore and FZN in Dubai.
In Paris, Guy Savoy has been among the best restaurants in the world since its opening in 1980, earning two Michelin stars in 1985 before going up to three in 2002 (although it has since been demoted as of 2023).
Martín Berasategui, whose various restaurants have a total of 11 Michelin stars, making him the most decorated Spanish chef, has his eponymous restaurant in Lasarte. It has held three Michelin stars since 2001, and serves Basque cuisine.
Last, but not least, Schwarzwaldstube in Baiersbronn has held its three Michelin star rating since 1993. Offering views of the Tonbach Valley, guests can enjoy French cuisine with a modern twist.
