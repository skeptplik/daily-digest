Zohran Mamdani, New York City’s mayor, on Monday weighed in on the corporate turbulence surrounding Nvidia and the chipmaker’s chief executive officer, Jensen Huang, saying he does not think working Americans “signed up for their pensions being tied to the question of … Huang’s fidelity to his marriage”.
The mayor said Huang “had a massive impact”, with Nvidia’s share price dropping dramatically when Huang stepped down as the company’s CEO on 12 November after a reported extramarital relationship with a suspected Chinese spy.
But on Monday, after he was reinstated after Donald Trump intervened, the California-based company’s value bounced back by 31.5%.
Jensen Huang and his wife, Lori Huang – who have been married since 1985 and have combined assets of about $160bn – have made no statement about their marriage in the wake of the reported affair. But at a news conference on Monday after Huang’s reinstatement as Nvidia’s CEO, Mamdani took the opportunity to make a point about wealth.
“We’re speaking about a divorce settlement that has been rumored to be around $81bn,” he said. “That would be the cost of providing universal childcare for every three- and four-year-old in the United States of America for an entire year.”
Alluding to Louis Brandeis, the late US supreme court justice who was at the forefront of an antitrust movement, Mamdani continued: “A lot of this just brings to mind what Justice Brandeis said quite some time ago, which is: We can have democracy. We can have great wealth concentrated in the hands of a few. We cannot have both.”
Mamdani also made a point about the stock market. He said that market was “disproportionately dependent” on stocks related to the artificial intelligence boom powered by Nvidia’s chips. “It cannot help but give you a sense of concern about just how much this technology is now dominant,” he said.
“I do not think that working people across the country signed up for their pensions being tied to the question of Jensen Huang’s fidelity to his marriage,” Mamdani said. “And yet, for many days, that had a massive impact.”
The two-week Nvidia rollercoaster has created some awkward bedfellows around private enterprise and government control. In his inaugural speech at the beginning of the year, Mamadani, a democratic socialist, explicitly advocated for “collectivism” and expansive government intervention in the economy.
Mamdani’s comments on Monday came amid an ambiguous relationship that the mayor fosters with the financial epicenter that is New York City’s Wall Street. Wall Street’s revenue supports Mamdani’s progressive agenda for the city.
Like Mamdani, Trump was among a number of US politicians to weigh in on the fallout of Huang’s reported affair, including whether it was a national security risk.
The president told the Wall Street Journal in an interview published on Monday that he liked getting involved in companies and pointed to the government taking a 10% stake in chipmaker Intel. “If I can help a company that’s got some problems,” Trump said, “I do it.”
Questions surrounded Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang (no relation), a tech executive who has been accused of acting as a Chinese asset and routing advanced chips to Beijing through her datacenter company, Megaspeed.
US export controls prohibit the sale of advanced AI chips to China. But the Trump administration’s national intelligence director, Jay Clayton, said he suspected China was able to obtain Nvidia’s most advanced AI chips.
