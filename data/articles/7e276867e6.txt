Molly Meldrum, the Australian music journalist, broadcaster and producer, has died at the age of 83.
He died peacefully in the early hours of Monday morning at a hospital in Melbourne, his longtime friend and manager, Mark Klemens, confirmed to the Guardian.
“We have lost a true Australian icon, a man who opened doors for so many and entertained millions of people here and around the world,” Klemens said.
“He was unconventional and wonderfully unpredictable, but always caring, always funny and always down to earth, with a passion the rest of us could only envy. He made the most of his life, and boy, was it a full one. Rest in peace, Molly.”
Meldrum’s partner, Yan Wongngam, and family said in a statement issued by Klemens: “Affectionately dubbed ‘the Guru’, Molly was the heartbeat, the champion and the spirit of Australian music. He shaped not only the sound of a nation but also the dreams of generations who grew up with his voice guiding them..
“Yan cared for Molly with great love and devotion, especially through his final years. The family is deeply grateful to him, to Molly’s many loyal friends, and to the staff at Epworth Richmond for their wonderful care.”
The Stetson-wearing Countdown presenter is credited with engineering the breakthrough of Abba, Blondie and Madonna in Australia. During his time on the wildly popular Sunday evening ABC music show from 1974 to 1987, he championed songs including Blondie’s In the Flesh, John Mellencamp’s I Need a Lover, Duran Duran’s Planet Earth and Madonna’s Holiday, transforming then unknown tracks into Top 10 hits in Australia.
Meldrum also passionately promoted Australian performers, giving exposure on Countdown to dozens who went on to local or international fame, including INXS, Kylie Minogue, Skyhooks and Mental as Anything.
In a post on X, the Australian prime minister, Anthony Albanese, paid tribute to Meldrum: “With an interview style that was inimitable and an enthusiasm that was uncontainable, Molly Meldrum loved music almost as much as we loved him. On Countdown and beyond, he championed Australian artists and brought international acts to our shores. He gave new artists a leg up and established artists a welcoming embrace. And over the years as he implored us to do ourselves a favour, he became a central part of the Australian soundtrack. We were lucky to have him.”
Victorian Premier Ben Carroll said Meldrum will be given a state funeral. “All of our thoughts as Victorians and indeed Australians are with Molly Meldrum’s family and his friends, and we know how wide that network was,” he said.
Elton John once described Meldrum as the “single most important influence in Australian pop music”.
Meldrum never disguised his unabashed fervour as a fan, even while forming decades-long friendships with an array of global stars.
He developed an obsession with the Beatles after their explosion on to the world stage, and eventually interviewed his pop idol, John Lennon, who fed him the scoop of the band’s impending break-up. But Meldrum did nothing with the incendiary information because he “was so in awe” of Lennon.
Born Ian Meldrum in Orbost, Victoria, to a family he called “quite dysfunctional”, the larger-than-life character moved to live with an aunt and uncle in Shepparton from the age of 12. He went from wanting to be a conductor to becoming entranced with the idea of producing music and later lived with the family of his close friend, the singer Ronnie Burns.
He was one of the first openly gay personalities on Australian television and was known for his bumbling interview technique and for his chaotic and colourful scuffles. On the 40th anniversary of Countdown in 2014, Prince Charles recalled a shambolic 1977 interview with Meldrum, telling the ABC: “There is an old show business saying which warns never to work with animals or children, but nobody prepared me for Molly Meldrum.”
Meldrum began appearing on camera on Countdown in mid-1975, delivering the weekly Rock Report, redubbed Humdrum by a guest host, John Paul Young. While reviewing albums, Meldrum would often hold LPs up awkwardly, making them hard to see under the glare of stage lights. ABC policy prohibited hosts endorsing products, so “do yourself a favour” became Meldrum’s catchphrase.
In the 1980s Meldrum landed various presenting gigs on television: the 1985 Oz for Africa concert, the Australian leg of Live Aid; the ABC interview show The Meldrum Tapes and a regular music segment, Molly’s Melodrama, on the variety show Hey Hey It’s Saturday.
In 2011 Meldrum fell from a ladder in the back yard of his Melbourne home and was placed in intensive care in a critical condition, undergoing surgery for head and spinal injuries. After four months he recovered enough to begin appearing in public again.
In 1986 he was made a member of the Order of Australia, with a citation for “service to the fostering of international relief and to youth”. In 2012 he was inducted into the Logie Hall of Fame. In 2014, when Meldrum became the first non-recording artist to be inducted into the Aria Hall of Fame, Minogue said the Australian music industry was “blessed to have had you in our lives for over four decades”.
Elton John was similarly unreserved in his praise, saying in 2019: “I don’t think anyone has done more for music in Australia than Molly Meldrum.”
Meldrum is survived by his partner, Yan Wongngam, his adopted son and his two younger brothers.
