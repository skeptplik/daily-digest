What's next?
The Spaniard plays Ben Shelton in the semifinals.
Seven-time major singles champion Carlos Alcaraz has eased concerns about a hamstring injury by cruising past Italian fifth seed Flavio Cobolli 6-4, 6-1 at the ATP Finals in Turin.
Wearing an elastic black band on his injured left thigh and a strip of tape on his right knee, Alcaraz completed an unbeaten group-stage campaign ahead of the semifinals.
The Spaniard had already booked his place in the last four after victories over American seventh seed Frances Tiafoe and Daniil Medvedev.
But his fitness came under scrutiny after he left the court with heavy strapping on his left leg following his win over Medvedev, who was the 2020 ATP Finals champion.
Any doubts were quickly dispelled as Alcaraz broke the already-eliminated Cobolli in the opening game and surged into a 2-0 lead.
The 23-year-old delivered an efficient display, keeping rallies short and dictating play without putting unnecessary strain on his body.
He carried that momentum into the second set, breaking for a 2-0 lead and again to move 4-1 ahead before closing out a 65-minute victory.
Alcaraz finished with 29 winners to Cobolli's nine.
"It was a difficult match to see how I was going to feel on court and playing against Flavio is not easy," Alcaraz said.
"He is a great player and person off the court.
"I tried to play as aggressively as I can, hit the ball as hard as I can.
"The way I felt on court today was incredible. I am getting more confident after every match and hopefully I can keep it going."
Alcaraz had already secured a place in the semifinals against Ben Shelton at the elite eight-player event.
The semifinal will be a re-match of the US Open quarterfinal between Shelton and Alcaraz.
While Shelton won that five-set clash in New York, Alcaraz recently got revenge in the Paris Masters final.
In the other semifinal, top-ranked Alexander Zverev will face either Medvedev or Tiafoe.
