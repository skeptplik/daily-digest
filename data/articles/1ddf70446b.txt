The number of investigator-initiated trials, a Chinese regulatory pathway that allowed investigators to start clinical trials without sign-off from central regulators, have slowed considerably, STAT reports. Loosened rules had enabled Chinese companies to test drugs in humans faster and cheaper and prompted U.S. companies to either come to China or push the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to adopt a similar approach. But since May, when the Chinese government issued new restrictions, 164 have been approved under the new regulations and just over 1,000 were submitted for review.
India proposed rules requiring regulators to immediately suspend manufacturing licenses for medicines that fail specified quality standards in government testing, tightening oversight of one of the world’s biggest pharmaceutical industries, Reuters writes. The rules would apply if government testing finds the drug fails quality standards for impurities or sterility, the amount of active ingredient, or dissolution, which assesses how the drug releases that ingredient. The license would remain suspended until the manufacturer conducts an effective root-cause analysis and implements corrective and preventive action, and could be restored only after regulators verify those measures have been implemented.
