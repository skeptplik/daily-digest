In public, Andy Burnham’s recent focus has been on securing better trade terms with the European Union and reforming Britain’s welfare system. In private, the prime minister has been preoccupied by a more global problem: what happens next in the Iran war.
For several weeks, Burnham and his most senior Cabinet colleagues have been consumed by the possibility Donald Trump could resume major strikes on Iran, according to people familiar with the matter. While the U.K. has taken hope the Republicans’ defeat in the midterms might temper Trump’s foreign policy, ministers see the U.S. president weighing options for a fresh attack, they said, a prospect that appears to have grown in recent days.
Burnham on Friday sealed a deal for closer EU ties that he said could boost the economy by 0.5% a year, bring down food prices and raise the prospect of lower electricity prices. But looming in the background is Iran. Even if Trump decides against resuming his war, the grim reality for Burnham is his officials have told him all plausible outcomes will have a negative impact on the U.K.’s economy and security.
