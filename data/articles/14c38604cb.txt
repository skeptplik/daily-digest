A GP surgery has suspended all routine appointments due to what it calls a "tsunami" of demand.
It is the second time in a week that Aberfoyle Medical Practice in Londonderry has told patients that it is not offering routine appointments - either in person or online.
Dr Donna Mace, who is a partner at the practice and also a member of the Northern Ireland General Practitioners' Committee, said the practice was currently dealing with unprecedented demand.
In real terms, that means their doctors are seeing around 60 patients a day, which she said was not safe.
BBC News NI has contacted the Department of Health (DoH).
"Aberfoyle is one of the biggest practices in Derry and we are really fortunate in that we have 11 doctors, social workers, physios, mental health practitioners and counsellors but unfortunately the last few weeks have just not been manageable at all," Dr Mace told BBC Radio Foyle's North West Today programme.
"Every single day the appointments have been filled up, so much so, that every on-call doctor is dealing with 60 patients per day.
"In my on-call, that is dealing with emergency mental health patients, patients in palliative care and very ill children and it was just not manageable," she said.
"I am a partner at the practice and we employ four salaried doctors and there was a risk of losing those doctors If we lost them due to stress or resignation because they couldn't cope, that would mean we would lose 100 appointments so we had to manage the risk.
"They couldn't go on and there was a total crisis."
The doctor said the decision was taken due to concerns about patient safety.
"It was not manageable and it was not safe, no patient is going to appreciate if we miss something because we have less than the usual 10 minutes with them," she said.
The doctor said they could only deal with urgent appointments and apologised to all those affected.
In October, the practice saw more then 4,000 patients face-to-face, handled 1,500 telephone consultations and dealt with nearly 9,000 prescription issues.
The practice would review the situation on a "day-to-day basis" to determine when it could reopen routine appointments.
