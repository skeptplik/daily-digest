JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard have disclosed incidents involving outside service providers after the N0n extortion group claimed it stole their customers’ data.
JPMorgan said in a post on X on Thursday that some of the companies providing services to the bank had been hit by a cyberattack.
“Based on our investigation so far, we believe the attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses. There is no disruption to our services,” the bank said.
Where appropriate, the bank plans to reach out to impacted customers and give them free credit monitoring. It said the incident has nothing to do with the problems some customers saw earlier this week.
In a statement to the media, Visa said the claims are “associated with an incident involving a third-party service provider in Asia Pacific.” The payments giant said neither its own systems nor payment data such as card numbers had been impacted.
Mastercard said in a statement to Reuters that it had recently learned of an incident “at a third-party provider that resulted in the exposure of limited amount of email addresses — non-payments information — from a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program.”
Mastercard said its payment network was not affected and that cardholders can still use their cards.
On November 16, N0n claimed to have obtained JPMorgan-related engagement data spanning “hundreds of thousands of campaign interactions for tens of thousands of distinct cardmember email addresses,” according to Reuters. It made comparable claims about Visa and Mastercard the same day.
SecurityWeek has seen N0n’s leak site, where all three companies are marked as not having paid, and the allegedly stolen data is offered for download.
Reuters’ review of the JPMorgan-related data found over 16,000 customer emails, some accompanied by phone numbers and postal codes, along with what appear to be 124 email addresses belonging to bank employees.
The Visa files hold thousands of purported customer emails, phone numbers and marketing campaign records, mostly from Asia-Pacific markets, Reuters said.
None of the three companies has identified the affected providers or said how many customers were impacted.
N0n, which emerged in September, currently lists roughly 200 alleged victims on its leak site.
