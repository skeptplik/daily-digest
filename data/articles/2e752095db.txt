These are the stories making headlines in fashion on Tuesday.
Following Amazon MGM Studios’ announcement of Robert Pattinson as the next James Bond, the fashion and watch brands he represents could benefit. Pattinson has been a Dior ambassador since 2013 and became a face of Jaeger-LeCoultre in September. The 007 franchise has historically been shaped by brand partnerships, from Pierce Brosnan’s Brioni suits to Billy Reid, whose peacoat Daniel Craig wore in “Skyfall” boosted the smaller label’s visibility. {Business of Fashion/paywalled}
On Monday, four women who said chemical hair straighteners caused their cancer reached a confidential settlement with L’Oréal’s U.S. and other companies during a trial in Chicago. More than 12,700 related cases are consolidated in federal court in Chicago, according to court records. A L’Oréal spokesperson said they remain confident in the safety of its SoftSheen-Carson products and that all their products are “always subject to a rigorous scientific evaluation of their safety.” Arizona and Minnesota also sued L’Oréal this year, alleging it misled women about the products’ safety. {Reuters/paywalled}
LVMH has opened applications for the 14th edition of its Prize for Young Designers, an incubator for talents such as Simon Porte Jacquemus, Grace Wales Bonner and last year’s winner, Julie Kegels. Designers aged 18 to 40 with at least two collections can apply at lvmhprize.com until Jan. 10. The winner will receive €400,000 ($440,000) and a year of coaching from LVMH experts. Runner-ups will receive the Karl Lagerfeld Prize and Savoir-Faire Prize, each awarded €200,000 ($220,000). LVMH is also accepting applications for its graduate program through April 4. Three fashion school graduates will be selected to spend a year at one of the group’s houses, with each graduate and their school receiving €10,000 ($11,000). {WWD/paywalled}
Nike offered a first look at its upcoming footwear and apparel innovations at a “World of Athletes” showcase in Beaverton, Oregon. The centerpiece is Tuned Air, a digital manufacturing method that Nike says opens “unlimited possibilities” for how its Air cushioning feels, performs and looks. Among the new roster of products are the Nike Free and Nike Mind platforms, the Vaporfly 5 and Vomero 19 running shoes, Devin Booker’s Book 3 basketball shoe, and Nike Morpho X. Much of the lineup arrives over the next year, per Nike’s press release, with some innovations coming later. {Nike}
David Beckham’s commercial company, DB Ventures, is suing fragrance maker Coty in New York for at least $41 million, alleging the German beauty group breached its licensing agreement by letting Beckham-branded perfumes end up in discount stores. Coty has asked a judge to dismiss the case, arguing New York courts lack jurisdiction and saying DB Ventures is using the claim to “manufacture its early exit” from the deal so the contracts can go to Interparfums. {AP News}
