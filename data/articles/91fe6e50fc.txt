The Scottish government has ordered a review into hepatobiliary surgery at NHS Grampian after a whistleblower leaked a document showing 19 avoidable deaths at the health board.
Dr Mohammed Habib claimed he had contacted the Scottish government on four separate occasions and had gone to the police and the lord advocate with his concerns about patient safety at the board.
In a report from 2022, which was only released by Habib on Thursday, it was found 19 of the 31 deaths in liver and pancreatic surgery cases were avoidable.
NHS Grampian chief executive, Laura Skaife-Knight, said the board would be "open and transparent" in co-operating with the review.
Habib also said two people had died after having the wrong procedure performed on them and has called for the health board to face a corporate homicide inquiry.
Health Secretary Angela Constance said the review would assess how concerns raised about liver, gallbladder and bile duct surgery, including those by Habib, were handled by the board and the Scottish government.
She said it would identify weaknesses in governance, missed opportunities, unreasonable delay, and gaps in accountability.
Constance said Habib's concerns were "deeply concerning and of the utmost seriousness".
"Following discussion with the first minister, I am today commissioning an urgent and fully independent review of how concerns about hepatobiliary (HPB) surgery in NHS Grampian was handled, including the 2022 report into the deaths of patients who underwent HPB surgery," she said.
"Patient safety and high-quality care must always be the first concern of all those who work in, and with, the NHS."
She said that if any patients had questions or needed advice and support they should contact the NHS Grampian helpline on 08085 202030.
One patient told BBC Scotland News he "technically died" after undergoing liver surgery at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary.
Derek Appleton said he believed mistakes had been made during a procedure to remove 60% of his liver after being diagnosed with stage four cancer.
He said he lost 11 litres of blood and went into cardiac arrest, but was never given a proper explanation for what happened.
Constance will make a statement in Holyrood on Tuesday, where she is expected to lay out the timetable and announce who will lead the review.
She said: "My thoughts are with anyone who has lost a loved one and I am acutely aware of the courage it has taken Dr Habib to come forward.
"I recognise he has sought to do this repeatedly, over a number of years, to both to the Scottish government and several other bodies.
"Patients and staff in Grampian have the right to expect full transparency and independent assurance that when valid concerns are raised, they are properly and robustly pursued. That is what this review will seek to establish."
Skaife-Knight said the review was the "best way forward".
She added: "We know the allegations which have been the subject of significant media interest in recent days will cause further concern for patients and families, our communities and staff.
"We will take immediate steps to secure and preserve relevant information, files and records as part of this process."
'Dramatic action'
Humza Yousaf was health secretary at the time of Dr Habib's report.
He told BBC Scotland News he supported "further dramatic action" being taken in relation to NHS Grampian.
A spokesperson for Yousaf - who went on to become first minister before resigning in May 2024 â said: "Humza supports full transparency about what happened at NHS Grampian and will co-operate fully with any review that takes place.
"He has made it clear that he supports minutes from the period in question being released.
"As health secretary, Humza met whistleblowing champions in health boards across Scotland, and he remains clear that staff who raise patient safety concerns must be listened to and protected.
"It takes real courage to do what Dr Habib has done, and he should be commended for refusing to let these concerns go."
The Scottish government has said action will be taken quickly when required as part of the process to refer any issues â including potential criminality â to the relevant authorities without waiting for the final report.
The announcement comes after First Minister John Swinney announced a review of how the government handles correspondence, after he claimed he first heard of three letters sent to him by Habib on Thursday.
On Tuesday, Constance appointed an expert health team to oversee NHS Grampian improvements.
She said the "external intervention support team", comprising experienced senior NHS leaders, would work alongside Grampian's health team to provide "intensive turnaround support".
Healthcare Improvement Scotland has also been asked to complete an early review of NHS Grampian within three months to assess progress, followed by ongoing reviews, to provide independent assurance.
'Excruciating' wait for answers
Scottish Labour's health spokeswoman, Jackie Baillie, said the review was "welcome", adding that a similar situation "must never happen ever again".
She said: "Were it not for the bravery of Dr Habib in this week speaking out, we may never have known just how far this went.
"Grieving families who lost loved ones in the care of NHS Grampian deserve the full truth of what happened at the health board, including who knew what was going on, when they knew, and why it has taken this long for these concerns to come to light."
Yi-pei Chou Turvey, Scottish Liberal Democrat MSP for north east Scotland, said the wait for answers for the families of affected patients had been "excruciating".
She added: "SNP ministers ought to have been battering down the door of NHS Grampian after receiving multiple warnings from Dr Habib and a report uncovering 19 avoidable deaths.
"This independent review must quickly and thoroughly establish why John Swinney, Humza Yousaf and successive health secretaries failed to grasp what was going on and intervene."
Scottish Conservative health spokesman Miles Briggs said the review should not be used as a "vehicle to shirk responsibility".
"This review must determine exactly what information reached Scottish ministers and NHS Grampian executives and what action was taken," he said.
"For patients and their families, the review must follow the evidence wherever it leads and ensure full accountability at every level."
