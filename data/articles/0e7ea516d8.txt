My friend Alison Walton-Robson, who has died aged 61 of ovarian cancer, was a tireless campaigner for the disability arts movement. Latterly she was the creative director of Headway Arts in Blyth, Northumberland, a pioneering inclusive organisation she co-founded in 1995. Headway offers people from all backgrounds the chance to contribute to the cultural life of their communities and to realise their full creative potential.
The company, which became an Arts Council England national portfolio organisation in 2023, pays particular attention to providing opportunities for artists who are neurodivergent or have a learning disability, and has built an impressive portfolio of projects, including international collaborations. Allie was proud to be an Erasmus+ ambassador and contributed a case study to the European Movement’s successful campaign for the UK to rejoin the EU Erasmus+ education and training programme.
Before working with Headway Arts, Allie had been a freelance artist, working with organisations such as the Durham-based Jack Drum Arts as a maker, performer and puppeteer. She was born and bred in Blyth, the daughter of Jean (nee Reid), who worked for the Prudential insurance company, and Alan Walton, a safety official for the National Coal Board. Allie studied first at Carlisle College of Art and later at Northumbria University in Newcastle, returning to her roots when the opportunity arose. In her early 20s she helped to run the arts programme at Northgate hospital near Morpeth, where she developed her passion for disability rights, accessibility and inclusion.
Allie was a performer, director, producer, writer, dramaturg, singer and ukulele player, and an accomplished painter, puppet-maker and textile artist. In recent years she designed a new banner for Crofton Mill Pit in Blyth, which received its first outing at Durham Miners’ Gala in 2024. She made an indelible mark on the cultural ecosystem of the north-east region.
She is survived by her husband, Andrew Robson, whom she married in 2007, and her brother, Keith. Allie was also devoted to her wider family – her sisters-in-law, nephews and nieces, adopted “young ‘uns”, colleagues and creative collaborators, a network of friends across the globe, and her cat, Mags.
