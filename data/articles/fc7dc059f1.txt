U.S. President Donald Trump and NATO chief Mark Rutte issued a joint statement on Wednesday in which they called on Russia to avoid any actions that would further widen the war in Ukraine.
The statement, issued after a meeting in Florida, followed Trump’s meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping last week in Asia, where he called each his “friend.”
“We also discussed the importance of Russia avoiding any actions that intentionally, or unintentionally, expand the war, and the importance of obtaining a peaceful resolution to the war as rapidly as possible,” Trump and Rutte said in their statement.
Trump’s meeting with Putin last week triggered a backlash from domestic Democratic opponents and European allies, the latter of whom are pushing to be included in U.S.-mediated talks to end the war in Ukraine.
Rutte, who has repeatedly tried to soothe tensions within NATO and keep Trump on board with other members of the alliance, has said in response to that criticism that it is normal for the leaders of major nuclear powers to meet.
On Wednesday, Rutte and Trump also talked about boosting defense spending and investment in the United States and across NATO, according to their statement.
The conflict in Ukraine and the U.S.-Israeli war against Iran were the primary topics of Wednesday’s meeting, it added, but did not say what was discussed on Iran.
Trump has often criticized U.S. allies over issues such as NATO spending, trade tariffs and what he calls their inadequate support for the war against Iran.
