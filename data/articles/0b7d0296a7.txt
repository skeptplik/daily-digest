I’m writing this from Paris as the Spring 2027 shows are wrapping up. One glorious night, I had the chance to see Céline Dion during her residency at the Plénitude Arena. What really struck me about her performance was her unique ability to unite an entire stadium. It was just her up there—no background dancers—using her voice to bring us along on her journey. It was deeply moving. I was reminded of how vital performance can be. I’m sure there were people in the arena whose lives and experiences and politics differ from my own, but we could all come together to revel in the sheer joy of an incredible show.
That is the spirit in which we celebrate this issue’s theme, performance. A performer can entertain us, transport us, and, crucially, connect us.
Our cover star, Natalie Portman, photographed by Carlijn Jacobs and styled by our style director at large, Malina Joseph Gilchrist, has consistently transported us over the years with great performances. In the coming months, she will deliver two more, in Cathy Yan’s biting art-world satire, The Gallerist, and in Lena Dunham’s hyped new rom-com, Good Sex. Both reveal a lighter, more humorous, though by no means less committed side of Portman, and that’s by design. “Right now,” she tells our executive editor, Leah Chernikoff, she’s focusing on “a lot of joy. I think it’s both for myself and what I hope to offer an audience. There’s a lot of really heavy stuff going on … and so it does feel like the biggest service you can do right now as an artist is to deliver some joy.”
I have—we all have—watched Portman grow up. What I admire most about her is her integrity: She is deliberate about the choices she makes, not just in her work but in how she chooses to live her life, including the privacy she keeps for herself and for her growing family. (When we photographed her, she was pregnant with her third child, whom she welcomed in August!)
Elsewhere in this issue, I am thrilled to feature a range of dancers, musicians, writers, and actors whose work I have long revered. The dream team of John Legend, Lynn Nottage, and Whitney White came together in conversation to discuss their new stage adaptation of the classic American novel and film Imitation of Life, which also marks Legend’s first musical. “At the core, this is a story about the American dream, with all of its contradictions and complications,” says Nottage.
Sarah Hoover brings us the story of one of ballet’s great modern partnerships—American Ballet Theatre’s Isabella Boylston and James Whiteside—as they prepare to dance their final performances together. Boylston, who welcomed her first child this summer, returned to the barre six weeks after giving birth in order to be ready to partner with Whiteside before he retires next summer. “If I had to go onstage with him tonight in Swan Lake, with zero rehearsal, I’d still feel good about it,” Boylston tells Hoover.
When this issue is on newsstands, our podcast, The Good Buy, cohosted by Chernikoff and our executive digital director, Lynette Nylander, will be back for its fourth season, available wherever you get your podcasts and on YouTube, featuring another incredible lineup of folks whose style we’re obsessed with, including Paloma Elsesser and Kim Kardashian. Each episode is a reminder that getting dressed is the ultimate performance.
