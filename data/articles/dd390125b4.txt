The explosion of extreme wealth in the world is the defining social fact of our times.
When Forbes magazine began ranking the world’s largest fortunes in 1987, the wealth of the richest 0.0001% of our planet’s population equaled 3% of global GDP. Since that time, the wealth held by this tiny group of people – just 3,000 households – has ballooned and now reaches 17% of global GDP.
This extreme wealth explosion has upended the balance of power, leaving choices that affect all of humanity to a few billionaires at the helm of a handful of corporations. The economic power of the ultra-wealthy is being used to consolidate their political power, creating a dynamic that is threatening democracy.
This dynamic of wealth and power is nothing new (the word “oligarchy” comes from ancient Greek), but its velocity and the scale of its cross-border impacts are unprecedented. A single decision made by the controlling shareholder of a multinational firm can wipe away jobs, deplete resources, or determine the course of a war.
Choices made by the ultra-rich ignore national borders as they disrupt work and home lives, inject their ideas into the public discourse, bend public policy to fit their desires, siphon revenue away from states, and damage our earth.
The power dynamic of this international oligarchy must be countered with a transnational coalition to defend democracy. To this end, we affirm three principles.
1 Fair taxation
The ultra-rich should pay taxes in proportion to their resources. As studies in many countries have shown, this is currently not the case. Myriad loopholes allow the wealthiest in the world to pay little or no income tax.
A tax floor, expressed as a percentage of personal wealth, would put an end to this situation. This idea was first developed in a report commissioned by the G20 in 2024, under the presidency of Brazil, which proposed setting an individual tax floor equal to 2% of wealth for all fortunes more than $100m.
Such an initiative would ensure that the ultra-rich would pay at least as large a share of their income in tax as the middle class – the bare minimum required to ensure that fiscal law complies with the principle of tax fairness.
This annual tax floor should be accompanied by a one-time “catch-up” tax on the wealth of the super-rich to settle the outstanding tax inequalities that have fueled the explosion of extreme wealth. If this exceptional levy took into account any taxes paid during the time period covered by the catch-up, only those ultra-rich individuals who had avoided meeting their fiscal obligations would be affected.
2 Media independence and pluralism
Information is not just another commodity, and access to it forms the bedrock of our democracies. The power of oligarchs to control information must be curtailed.
The purchase of media outlets and social platforms is one of the most glaring ways in which the ultra-rich capture power, ensuring themselves control over what is said, heard, learned and read. It is crucial to provide stronger support for public media and implement anti-concentration policies. In the age of big digital platforms, these anti-concentration policies should be designed on an international scale.
3 Decisions to develop and deploy artificial intelligence technologies must be democratized
Knowledge, artistic creation and works of imagination assembled over centuries are being captured, processed and repackaged (often without permission!) by a handful of corporations to be resold as artificial intelligence to the very humanity that produced them. There is still time to refuse that hijacking.
Technologies associated with artificial intelligence come with astronomically high risks: the possibility of cyber-attacks with lethal consequences, rapid and profound transformations of the labor market, the emergence of new forms of manipulation and disinformation, even the risk of human extinction. It would be folly to leave decisions about the deployment of these technologies in the hands of a few Silicon Valley billionaires.
In the field of information technology, the non-profit model has a proven track record. Some of the most popular applications and websites in the world, such as Wikipedia, Signal or Firefox, were developed by non-profit organizations. To build AI without oligarchs, companies working in this field should consider adopting the model of the non-profit foundation, with decisions piloted by those who understand them best: their own workers – the same people who, week after week, have been stepping down from their posts to warn us of the looming risk.
Today, around the world, voices are being raised to protect our democracies from the threats of oligarchy. We are calling for a transnational coalition to defend democracy because, as history has shown, our voices are most powerful when we raise them together, as interconnected citizens. It is possible for democracy to reverse the dynamic of oligarchy. It is possible to work for a world that is no longer defined by exploding inequality. Now is the time to reorient our trajectory. We hope you will join us.
