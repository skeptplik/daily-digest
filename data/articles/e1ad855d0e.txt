The music streaming service Tidal has announced it will ban entirely AI-generated music from its platform and remove millions of songs, saying AI music “diverts streams and royalties away from the artists, and confuses fans”.
“To ensure Tidal remains the best place for artists to share their work, and for listeners to enjoy it, we’re removing all tracks that we determine are 100% AI generated,” the company announced on Tuesday.
Tracks that were made with assistance from AI will still be allowed but tracks will be rejected if their metadata reveals they were generated solely using AI tools. Tidal’s ban will go into effect on 9 December.
The Norwegian-US streaming service, which promises higher quality audio fidelity as a point of difference from streamers like Apple Music and Spotify, revealed it has seen a huge increase in the number of AI-generated tracks on its platform over the past several months.
In the past 90 days, 43% of tracks distributed on the platform were identified as 100% AI-generated, Tidal said, with 27m songs on the platform – 6.3% – tagged as entirely AI.
On a single day – 6 November – 60% of songs uploaded on a single day were 100% AI.
“Yet Tidal subscribers are not listening to these tracks: 96% of them have received zero streams in the last three months,” the company said, adding that it had taken down 65,000 tracks that were flagged as suspected impersonations of real artists.
“Wholly AI-generated music intended to mislead listeners and impersonate artists is another serious problem that has only increased in scale,” Tidal said. “It’s true that not all songs catch on immediately, and artists should have time to build their fanbases. But this situation is different – at this scale, the authentic music and artists that Tidal subscribers are looking to listen to and discover get buried.”
The company said it would “always fight for artists to get paid more”.
“Everything in this update comes back to that,” the statement said. “If you make music, you should be able to put it on Tidal knowing it won’t be buried under tracks no one asked for, or copied by someone pretending to be you.”
Tidal’s policy is the most stridently against AI among all the music streaming services. Bandcamp announced its ban on music “generated wholly or in substantial part by AI” in January. Spotify and Apple Music, the two biggest music streamers, label AI tracks and artists as such but still allow fully generated songs to be distributed on their platforms.
Spotify has promised users a tool to make AI-generated remixes and cover songs but in October it also revealed that it had removed about 50m “slop” tracks from its recommendations to users and outright deleted 75m “spammy” tracks.
