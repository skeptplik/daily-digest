Paris – North Korean money launderers are moving closer to cashing out some of the crypto stolen in last month’s record Binance hack, turning to little-known online services that can swap the tokens for others and help hide their tracks, blockchain researchers say.
THORChain, an online exchange urged by the FBI last month not to help the hackers, and a “coinjoin” service which helps mask tokens’ origins, have been used by the launderers to move the stolen crypto further out of reach, according to the researchers.
U.S. authorities have blamed North Korean hackers for the largest heist in history after $6.3 billion worth of crypto was stolen on Oct. 29. About $2.85 billion of the tokens were frozen by their issuers, but the rest remains in circulation. North Korea denies involvement in the hack.
