Puma is creating a new emerging markets region with the aim of strengthening its position across important growth areas around the globe.
According to the German athletic brand, the new region will comprise Southeast Asia and Oceania, South Africa, Turkey and the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC), with its regional hub in Singapore.
Puma noted that Singapore was selected as the regional hub due to its connectivity across Asia, Africa and Oceania, its access to international talent and its stable business environment. Existing offices, including Dubai, Istanbul and Cape Town, will continue to support the respective markets and the wider region. India, Japan and Korea will continue to operate as standalone markets, Puma noted.
Arthur Hoeld, chief executive officer of Puma, said in a statement that the company’s new emerging markets region has “significant long-term potential” and will “play an important role” in its ambition to become a top three global sports brand.

“With this new set-up, we want to bring our markets closer together, build regional capabilities and benefit more from their combined strengths, while preserving the local expertise and entrepreneurial mindset that make them successful,” Hoeld noted. “Will brings vast experience in scaling businesses and leading teams across Asia Pacific, which will be invaluable as we bring these markets together and unlock their full potential.”
In tandem with the creation of this new region, Will Newton has been appointed managing director of emerging markets, effective Dec. 1. He will report to Arne Arens, Puma’s new chief commercial officer.
Newton joins Puma from The Lego Group, where he served as head of regional commercial development in APAC. Prior to that, he spent more than 20 years at Adidas, holding several senior leadership positions across Asia Pacific, including general manager of Southeast Asia, vice president of wholesale in Japan and vice president of Asia Pacific wholesale.
“I am looking forward to joining Puma and shaping the emerging markets region together with our teams and market leaders,” Newton added. “Each of these four markets brings its own strengths, expertise and opportunities. Our task is to preserve what makes them successful locally while connecting them more closely, building regional capabilities and creating new opportunities to grow together.”
The news comes amid a barrage of executive changes at Puma. In September, the company named Fokko de Rooij as its new managing director of Europe, and Daniel Pustina as managing director of Cluster Central. Earlier that same month, Puma’s first female North American president, Tara McRae, left the firm for a new role at Skechers.
