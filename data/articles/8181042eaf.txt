Euronews Next spoke to Jen Wong about the threat of AI to the open internet and the importance of human connection on the platform.
In the 21 years since its founding, Reddit has played host to some of the strangest, most niche corners of the internet — from knitting groups to discussions about romantic relationships between humans and artificial intelligence.
AI has dramatically transformed the internet for Reddit users in those two-plus decades. Even so, the technology still has the potential to deepen the value of human conversation on the platform, as it “has no inherent taste… and doesn’t know anything,” Reddit’s chief operating officer, Jen Wong, told Euronews Next on the sidelines of Web Summit 2026 earlier this month.
She described Reddit as "the taste layer of the internet", arguing that AI models will always need human communities to understand new products, culture and ways of speaking.
“Reddit — it's fun, it's a place to build together, it's a place where there's something different about getting advice from a group of different people versus having it synthesised by an AI agent,” she said.
Reddit operates on the basis of individually themed groups called subreddits. Users can post links and create their own rules, such as a ban on overt self-promotion or guidelines around authenticity. There are also human moderators who can report extremist or violent behaviour on the platform.
Reddit users increased to 443.8 million weekly active users as of the third quarter of 2025, up from 365.4 million during the same period a year prior. In October, the company said its net income had soared more than 74% on an annual basis in the third quarter of this year.
Still, Wong acknowledged that AI search is harming Reddit's traffic. Search referrals declined in the company's most recently reported quarter, she said, adding that AI search is "not a material traffic driver" in the way traditional blue links were.
Demand for the platform, though, has not disappeared. Reddit remains a top-15 search term, but AI products make it harder for people to reach it. Reddit’s focus now, Wong said, is on logged-in users and those on its application, while also driving traffic to the platform directly.
‘The arms race’
In the interview, Wong argued that AI search is partly to blame for making the internet more closed and siloed than it was 20 years ago.
“I think we have been real believers and beneficiaries of what was an open internet but I think that's changed. I think the internet is not as open,” she said.
Wong also faulted AI for "intensive data stealing and abuse and scraping", which she said has made publishers more guarded.
Reddit currently has partnerships with OpenAI and Google for data sharing. Rather than feeding AI with user content, Wong said the partnerships allow for the company to “lay down guardrails on how our data is being used”.
Data scraping is against Reddit’s terms of service and Wong said it has built-in defences against it. But she acknowledged that “over time that will be an arms race, frankly”. Amid a wave of AI agents and assistants, it could become increasingly difficult to ensure that Reddit users are, indeed, human.
Reddit's approach to AI agents has been to create "clear, sanctioned avenues" for them so their activity is transparent. Bots are acceptable, she said, if they are labelled, and some, such as news feed bots, could even be useful.
When asked about criticism that AI models trained on Reddit data inherit bias, particularly against women, Wong defended the platform as now being "one of the most balanced" on diversity of opinion.
Wong added that while the site's early audience skewed male and tech-oriented, Reddit is now "very gender balanced," with Gen Z women its fastest-growing demographic in some countries, including the United Kingdom.
Reddit in the future
Looking ahead, Wong predicted that over the next five years AI agents will transform Reddit’s business across the board, from user experience to advertising buying. But she insisted the company will maintain its core principles.
“When it seems like everyone's zigging and you're zagging,” she said, “believing in communities, moderation, privacy and anonymity has served us really well.”
Wong added that Reddit’s commitment to human connection would ultimately allow it to adapt to a new technological reality. “When people come together, as we see in real life, it is an incredible trove of lived experience and knowledge,” she said.
