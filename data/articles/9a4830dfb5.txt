The Associated Press has declared Democrat Mary Peltola the winner of Alaska's Senate race after making it just over the 50% mark on first-rank votes.
Alaska was the last outstanding Senate race to be declared in the US midterm elections 2026 and makes it the ninth Republican-held Senate seat to be flipped.
The latest victory means the Democrats now hold a 56-44 majority in the US Senate. The 53-year-old is the first Democratic senator from the state in almost 20 years.
Peltola promises to deliver for 'Alaska's working families'
"As our next United States Senator, I will stand up to anyone to take on the rigged system in DC and deliver results for Alaska's working families," Peltola said in a statement.
"As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my life to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans' support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first," Peltola said.
Peltola beat Republican Dan Sullivan's 131,844 with 144,302 votes, the victory coming after an update in the vote count pushed her to 50.1% of the votes.
Peltola will now become one of the first two Native American women to ever be elected to the US Senate, where she will join Minnesota Senator-elect Peggy Flanagan.
Alaska's vast geography, remote rural communities and lengthy absentee ballot process contributed to a protracted count, with the Senate race taking over two weeks to produce a winner.
Vote siphon allegations
The Alaskan Senate race garnered a high degree of national interest after a New York Times report that aides to Peltola allegedly tried to use a second candidate named Dan J. Sullivan Jr, in an attempt to siphon votes from the Republican Dan Sullivan.
The Trump administration had approached the courts to block certifying the results in the event of there being a potential mix-up.
The legal bid was dismissed by a federal judge ahead of the election.
"My opponent's campaign is literally focused on misleading Alaskans, lying and cheating," Senator Sullivan told reporters in Anchorage on election day.
Peltola has denied any knowledge of an alleged plot. In any case, the margin of victory was greater than the number of votes won by the challenger Dan Sullivan, at just 4, 760.
The Democrats' majority had already been secured, allowing them to block legislation and begin investigations into members of President Donald Trump's administration, but the Alaska win will likely also have a morale impact on both sides.
