'Breast bowl' from Marie-Antoinette's pleasure dairy sets new record at auction
Portion of proceeds to benefit breast cancer research
A rare porcelain "breast bowl" that France's Louis XVI commissioned for Marie-Antoinette back in the 18th century sold for about $4.5 million in Paris Thursday.
The unconventionally shaped drinking vessel (known as a jatte-téton) is a delicate porcelain bowl that imitates the shape of a woman's breast, complete with rosy pink nipple, and sits atop a tripod decorated with scrolling foliage and a trio of goat heads.
It sold for 2.925 million euros via the French auction house Coutau-Bégarie, marking a record high price achieved at auction for a piece by Sèvres — the famed porcelain manufacturer favoured by European royals in the latter half of the 18th century and early 19th century.
Long believed lost, the bowl turned up by chance in a crate at the Galerie Meyer in Paris, the auction venue l'Hôtel Drouot said in a social media post touting the record-setting sale.
Organizers had estimated it would sell for between one and two million euros (about $1.5 million to $3.1 million).
French auction venue l'Hôtel Drouot touted the record-setting price online. Three other such bowls from the original set are on display in public collections. (Hôtel Drouot/Instagram)
The identity of the buyer was not disclosed.
Proceeds of Thursday's sale will benefit breast cancer research, with the seller, auctioneer and venue each earmarking a portion of the sale's revenue for the French non-profit l'Association Ruban Rose.
Created for 'pleasure dairy'
Marie-Antoinette wasn't a fan of their Rambouillet estate, southwest of Paris, when Louis purchased it. So, to win her over, the king built her a luxurious "pleasure dairy" on the estate where she could entertain nobles and enjoy treats like fresh milk, cheeses and ice cream.
As part of the lavish gift, he commissioned Sèvres artisans to create a sumptuous set of porcelain servingware — including a quartet of jatte-téton, which were inspired by mastos, Greek breast-shaped drinking cups used at banquets.
Though an urban legend purports the shape was moulded from Marie-Antoinette's own breast, Sèvres experts have dismissed the notion.
The three other Rambouillet breast bowls can be seen in the collections of France's National Ceramics Museum in Sèvres and Duca di Martina Museum in Naples.
