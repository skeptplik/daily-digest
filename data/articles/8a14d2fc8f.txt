In short:
Four Sydney Swans players involved in a police investigation into an alleged sexual assault will not face charges.
Player Isaac Heeney welcomed the news, saying the past three months had been "incredibly difficult" for his family.
What's next?
Lawyers for the three women involved have confirmed they are still pursuing civil lawsuits related to the allegations.
Sydney Swans players embroiled in a police investigation into an alleged sexual assault in a Melbourne hotel will not face criminal charges.
Isaac Heeney, Chad Warner, James Jordon and Nick Blakey had been under a police investigation over the alleged sexual assault at the Pullman Hotel in late August.
Several adult entertainers are understood to have joined players at the East Melbourne hotel after being hired in the hours after the Swans' win against Essendon on August 17.
The ABC understands the investigation centred on a player allegedly touching one of the women without her consent.
Multiple police sources previously told the ABC they understood an arrangement was reached in the hotel room for players to pay extra to take drugs, believed to be cocaine, from the women's breasts and buttocks.
It is unclear how many players, or which players, had allegedly participated.
It is understood a woman was seen leaving the hotel room in a distressed state in the early hours of the morning, with a Triple Zero (000) call made to police soon after.
This morning, Victoria Police confirmed no charges would be laid over the incident.
"Following a thorough investigation and based on legal advice, Victoria Police will not proceed with criminal charges due to insufficient evidence," a police spokesperson said.
Victoria Police said due to an "ongoing civil matter", no further comment would be made.
Sydney 'stands by' decision to suspend players
Sydney Swans chief executive Matthew Pavlich told a press conference today it had been a difficult period for everyone involved and affected by the hotel incident.
"We appreciate and understand that everyone has been dealing with it in their own way, and we thank them for their support during that time," he said.
"We've been in constant contact with the players through the whole process.
"Today's a moment for them to move forward. It's a time for everyone to move forward, and at some point, they'll be back here at training, back with their teammates, and that's going to be an important step."
The players were sidelined by the Sydney Swans in the days after the incident, for staying out late drinking and inviting women back to the team hotel in breach of club standards.
Fellow Swans player Riley Bice, who was also stood down for breaching club standards, was earlier cleared in the police investigation.

In an earlier statement today, Sydney Swans management said they stood by the decision to stand down the players "because their conduct fell short of the standards of behaviour expected at the club".
"The club was clear from the outset that this decision was separate from the police investigation, and it stands by that decision.
"The club has commenced a comprehensive examination of its AFL program and is committed to acting on the findings and implementing meaningful change to build an environment that players, staff, members, supporters and corporate partners can be proud of," the statement said.
Pavlich said that work was the club's express focus.
"This is not a day for anyone to really celebrate — we're still looking very hard at the program and understanding where we need to get better for the future and examining that in real thorough detail," he said.
"I can guarantee everyone out there that we'll be doing everything we can to restore the standards and behaviours that this club has held for a long period of time."
Blakey last month moved from the Swans to North Melbourne football club, apologising for "the events that led up to my exit from Sydney".
"I know I have to win back the trust of the broader community, football and otherwise," he said at the time.
Women to proceed with civil claims, lawyers say
The police update came as lawyers confirmed three women were still pursuing civil lawsuits related to the allegations.
Lawyers representing one of the women who alleges she was sexually assaulted said she was "devastated" by the decision.
Slater and Gordon lawyer Jackson Pannam said what had occurred had a "profound impact" on her.
"She reported the incident immediately, provides a formal statement and cooperated fully with the investigation," he said.
"She has shown enormous courage in coming forward."
Mr Pannam confirmed the woman would proceed with a civil case, which must be proved on a "balance of probabilities" rather than the higher criminal standard of "beyond reasonable doubt".
"The absence of criminal charges does not prevent a civil claim from proceeding,"
Mr Pannam said.
"Slater and Gordon Lawyers is continuing to prepare a civil claim against the relevant parties and will continue to support our client through this extremely difficult time."
John Karantzis from Carbone Lawyers, who is acting for two of the women in a civil claim against the club, said his clients were "very disappointed and distressed" over the outcome of the police investigation.
He said the women would still forge ahead with their civil claim.
"My clients' civil claim is based on a different burden of proof, it is based on the balance of probabilities," he said.
"We will vigorously pursue this claim."
Asked about how the club might handle related civil claims, Mr Pavlich contained his comments to the outcome of the police investigation.
"Any future aspects, we'll deal with that as it comes," he said.
Isaac Heeney welcomes police update
In a statement, Heeney welcomed the police announcement.
"The past three months have been incredibly difficult for my family, Steffie and those close to me," Heeney said.
"Watching my family carry the weight of this has been heartbreaking.
"To my family and friends, your love and loyalty has meant more than words can do justice.
"To those that have never doubted me and stood by my side in solidarity — thank you. I know it has not been easy in the face of such intense public scrutiny. I will never forget your support.
"To the entire Sydney Swans Club — my teammates, coaches, the board, our sponsors, and our loyal members and fans — thank you for keeping faith in me.
"The Swans have given me an opportunity to live out a childhood dream and I am fully focused on repaying that faith by being the best person and player I can be."
