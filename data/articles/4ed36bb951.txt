TikTok has changed the game for beauty brands, both established and emerging. At The Catalysts conference, Laura Geller, founder of her eponymous makeup line; Malena Higuera, chief executive officer of Noyz, and Darrell Spencer, founder and CEO of Crowned Skin, took to the stage to discuss the different ways TikTok is impacting brand strategy today with WWD beauty editor, news, feature and social, Noor Lobad.
	For Spencer — an entrepreneur with a tech advertising background — TikTok has been the ultimate discovery platform, a place where most consumers find out about his men’s grooming brand, which is then leveraged into search traffic and sales.
Higuera said that TikTok is “applicable to real life.” Stressing the importance of shifting digital relationships to in-person ones, Higuera said that Noyz is trying to “push the envelope of a two-dimensional TikTok” and “create spaces where people can meet.”
	Real-life interactions are important to Geller, too, who stated that “there is no better platform to learn about new product development” than TikTok. Hosting a number of Laura Geller Beauty’s TikTok affiliates at a Los Angeles brand event, Geller said that she learned to think differently about her own products. “It’s like having an in-house focus group,” she said. “We have a product that’s been around for three decades, and I was watching a TikTok affiliate use it, and thought: ‘I’ve never explained it that way.’ It helped to understand another way of using a product that needed a little reinvigorating.”
Spencer said that creating content is an “organic” start to promoting the brand and testing out which marketing angles work best, later using that intel to build Crowned Skin’s structure on TikTok Shop.
	The products have to make influencers want to create content about them, said Higuera, whose team uses interactive mailers when shipping products. Another key factor is finding creators who resonate with an audience. “It’s more about double-digit engagement rates, viral communities and people that are going to create stronger content” than just their follower counts, Higuera said.
TikTok videos are also repurposed for other platforms, fulfilling agreements with Meta in Geller’s case or simply maximized for outreach in Spencer’s.
Spencer said that Crowned Skin uses AI to sift through the vast amounts of data. “AI is able to help us figure out what to experiment on and push for creators,” said Spencer, who uses that intel to shape briefs for content creators. “You need to know what angles work for your company. What are the best selling points on TikTok, specifically? What’s working? What’s not working?”
What undoubtedly does work for Crowned Skin is being active on TikTok in the first place, which Spencer credits for providing smaller brands with a platform and the opportunity to be discovered.
	Higuera also uses the app to advertise events amongst beauty communities online, like the launch of Noyz’s bestselling scent, Only Human, resulting in 1,000 attendees at the New York City pop-up in Sept. 2025.
For Geller, who founded her beauty company in 1997, there’s a magic to people not knowing who she is, but knowing her brand. Hosting a booth at BravoCon in 2025, Geller interacted with numerous customers throughout the day. “Nobody knew who I was, and I was so happy about that because TikTok opened up a whole other audience for us,” she said. “That’s the overarching benefit for us.”
