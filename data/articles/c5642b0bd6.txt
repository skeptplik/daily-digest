Last night, a glamorous cross section of the art, film, fashion, and entertainment industries gathered at Los Angeles County Museum of Art for its annual Art + Film gala, which fundraises for the museum’s film initiatives, exhibitions, acquisitions, and educational programs.
This year’s guests decided to embrace a bit of play with their looks. The event is sponsored by Gucci; so fittingly we saw a lot of the brand on the red carpet. Rosie Huntington-Whiteley, who walked in the Spring 2027 show, embraced drama in a slinky, backless black gown with a slit not at the front, but up the back to reveal just a bit of bum cheek. When Demna spoke about this collection this past fashion month, he said he was most proud of the structural feats of his jersey dresses, light as air but engineered to hang just so. Whiteley walked the show alongside additional LACMA guests including Alex Consani, in a dramatically twisted full-skirted gown, Elsa Hosk, who wore a gray wool gown with a thigh-high slit, and Candice Swanepoel in a dress with full-length silver fringe and slits up to her hips.
Anya Taylor-Joy, styled by Law Roach, pulled from the Pierre Cardin archives, selecting a embroidered silvery-white slip with a ruffled organza evening coat. Kylie Jenner wore a look from couturier of the moment, Ashi Studios, that featured a halter neck gown with an allover deer-like animal print and an oversize tassel at the back. And Julia Garner wore a sculptural black taffeta gown from Loewe, which she paired with oversized earrings from Lisa Eisner.
Perhaps the most fitting look of the evening, Nicole Kidman pulled a much-talked-about dress from Chanel’s Spring 2027 collection by Matthieu Blazy. The look features a bodice designed to resemble burlap and a layered tulle skirt with darker panels added to create the appearance of age and wear. It references Degas’ 1881 sculpture “Little Dancer of Fourteen Years,” and is evocative of what this gala celebrates: Kidman, a movie star, in Chanel, one of the most important French fashion houses, in reference to Degas, one of the most renowned impressionist artists.
