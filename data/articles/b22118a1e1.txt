Polymarket pledges to give $3.9 million back to victims after site compromise
Users of cryptocurrency-based prediction market Polymarket will be compensated after the site was hacked last week.
In a post-mortem on the incident, Polymarket said it would give $3.9 million to those impacted by the theft — with $2.7 million used to cover the initial losses and another $1.2 million to cover losses related to the platform’s hours-long shutdown.
“No claims or tickets needed: we identified eligible wallets from on-chain and trading data,” the company said.
Polymarket did not respond to requests for comment about how many people were impacted by the attack.
Anyone who thinks Polymarket’s calculations are wrong were urged to reach out. As of Saturday, 95% of the affected wallets were refunded in full.
On October 29, the company suffered outages that were eventually attributed to a compromise involving DebugBear — a company that provides website performance services.
Cybersecurity firm Hudson Rock previously said it found evidence pointing to what it called “wallet-draining code” that was designed by someone to discover accounts, select assets and transfer funds to a designated account.
Polymarket — one of the largest prediction markets in the U.S. — confirmed that in the post-mortem. The company said an attacker replaced the performance-monitoring script loaded onto polymarket.com with another designed to steal from the site’s users.
“For some people who had the site open during a small window … it used their active session to sell positions and move funds out of their wallet. The vendor has confirmed the compromise publicly,” Polymarket said.
The New York-based company said it removed the vendor’s compromised script, paused trading, and reset all active login sessions.
It redeployed the vendor’s script but added “additional integrity controls and continuous monitoring and verification” that included several more security measures.
Polymarket added that it has contacted law enforcement about the incident and is conducting its own investigation into the threat actor.
DebugBear released its own follow-up message saying it is continuing to investigate the incident but initial findings say it was compromised through its domain registrar.
“We are currently unable to receive emails sent to our domain. Based on our initial investigation this attack targeted the website of a specific customer,” the company said. “We have not seen any indication of a compromise of our internal systems, though we will continue to investigate this.”
