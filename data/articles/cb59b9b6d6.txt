In short:
Tasmania's premier says it will come as "no surprise" if the price of the planned Hobart stadium pushes past the $1.13 billion estimate, citing the war in Iran as one factor.
Jeremy Rockliff says a higher price would be "no reason to kill this project".
He says the cost will be made public early next week.
Tasmanian Premier Jeremy Rockliff has told parliament the final cost of Hobart's planned stadium will be made public next week, while avoiding questions about whether he'll stick to the $875 million public spending cap.
The last cost estimate of the stadium put it at $1.13 billion, but that was from about a year ago.
Since then, world events have resulted in prices going up, and it seems Tasmanians can expect the same for the waterfront, 23,000-seat, roofed stadium.
Under questioning in parliament, Mr Rockliff said he would be "very surprised" if the cost hadn't escalated.
"I'm not aware of the cost, however everything has gone since the 28th of February where there was an invasion in Iran," he said.
"Houses have gone up, buildings have gone up … there'd be no surprise if there was some cost escalation.
"I don't know the figure … but that is no reason to kill this project."
Over the past two days, Mr Rockliff has repeatedly faced questions as to whether he will stick to the $875 million public spending cap or bring it back to parliament for approval to exceed it.
It was a commitment he made to Upper House MLC Bec Thomas last year to secure her support for the project.
But Mr Rockliff was evasive, saying only that budgets go before the parliament.
Rosalie Woodruff: "I just want a simple yes or no for Tasmanians. Will the premier come back to parliament to ask … or are you just going to break the promise you made?"
Jeremy Rockliff: "We have budgets alright and the budget's gone through the parliament. There'll be a budget go through the parliament as well."
Asked again later in the sitting, Mr Rockliff said the parliament had already spoken on the project.
"The parliament has overwhelmingly voted on this project and made that call, and I know you don't like it."
The government has also been under pressure to reveal the final cost of the stadium before parliament rises for the year.
It is facing a motion, which is likely to get up, from independent Kristie Johnston to release the cost by tomorrow evening.
The preferred builder, BESIX Watpac, has given Macquarie Point Development Corporation (MPDC) its updated cost, but the government says that is not the final price.
That final costing was due to be made public in early December, but in question time today, Mr Rockliff said the MPDC is now working to have it ready by Monday.
"This is the most scrutinised project in Tasmania's history, and we need to get on with it," he told parliament.
"There is a view it should be released before this house rises, so we are working with the MPDC to condense the next two weeks' work into one week.
"Notwithstanding that, it's likely to create significant challenges, and possibly more costs, but we will have the updated costings by Monday next week.
"We'll get those out, and I'm sure we'll give everyone in this house plenty to talk through next week."
Independent wants details of contract
The government says the final cost is likely to be made public Tuesday, but for Ms Johnston that's not enough.
"It's important we get [the BESIX Watpac] lump sum design and construct offer price,"
Kristie Johnston said.
"It's also important that we get the full project number, which the premier has committed he will deliver to the parliament.
"Combined these two prices tell us a lot. It tells us about what's included in the contract, what's not included in the contract and importantly what's still yet to come when it comes to the final project number."
Labor appears likely to support the motion.
"We support scrutiny and transparency. That's why we said we'd support the motion," Labor Leader Josh Willie said.
"The government has said there are probity issues, but the figure will be revealed.
"We need to make sure people's intentions are clear. Kristie Johnston does not support the stadium. She wants to kill the stadium."
Funding uncertainty
Even once the figure is revealed, the question of how much taxpayer money is going towards it will not go away.
Ms Thomas said she expected the government to honour her agreement.
"The government made a clear commitment, a written commitment, to the parliament and the people of Tasmania that it would cap the state's contribution at $875 million," she said.
"If any additional funding is required, the government is committed to seek it from additional sources," she said.
"If the government can't seek that funding from additional sources, the contract ought not be signed."
Greens Leader Rosalie Woodruff said if the taxpayer spend was more that $875 million, the premier had to bring parliament back and put the question again.
"Parliament can exercise its right to make the government bring it back for approval if we had the numbers," she said.
"We've seen signatures from across MPs who voted for and against the stadium, wanting the premier to bring it back to parliament and consider whether the stadium bill needs to be reassessed."
