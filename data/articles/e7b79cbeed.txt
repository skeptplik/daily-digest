Save $200 on Meraki
This sleek, design-oriented semiautomatic ("a steampunk gasworks of a thing," according to former WIRED reviewer Matthew Korfhage) is a dual boiler, meaning you can froth milk at the same time you're pulling a shot, and it comes with an onboard conical burr grinder. It just narrowly missed a spot in our top picks because of concerns about maintenance, including an overly complicated descaling procedure.
Diletta Mio Is $200 Off
This is our favorite traditional espresso machine, classic and Italian-made. It’s built to last and a joy to use, according to its reviewer. Though the construction is decidedly analog, there’s a PID (proportional-integral-derivative) temperature controller that keeps brew temps stable. Note, however, that you will need a separate grinder.
Casabrews’ 5700 PRO Is $50 Off
WIRED contributing reviewer Pete Cottell is a longtime former barista, and this is the machine he uses at home. It’s a simple semiautomatic, with an onboard grinder, accessible controls, and a perfectly serviceable milk frother. (Though it’s a single-boiler machine, so you can’t froth while you’re pulling a shot.)
Save $50 on the Nespresso Vertuo Pop+
No learning curve here! This is our favorite pod machine for espresso, and while the Vertuo pods aren’t “real” espresso, our reviewer was still impressed by the resulting drink’s body and mouthfeel. This bundle comes with the Aeroccino 3 milk frother, a $100 value in and of itself.
