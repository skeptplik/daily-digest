A consultant surgeon at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary has called for a corporate homicide investigation into claims of avoidable deaths among NHS Grampian patients over many years.
Dr Mohammed Habib, who is still a surgeon at the hospital, said he had been raising concerns for a decade, including with government ministers and police, but he had been ignored.
The whistleblowing medic also leaked an unpublished expert review from 2022 which found 19 avoidable deaths among 45 patients in liver and pancreatic surgery, including some who bled to death in the operating theatre because of mistakes by surgeons.
The allegations come a week after a scathing report found poor leadership and culture at the NHS Grampian board. In response to the latest allegations, it said a thorough investigation had already taken place and any new evidence would be carefully considered.
Habib, who has been a consultant with NHS Grampian since 2014, held a media conference on Thursday with his representative Aamer Anwar.
The lawyer and human rights campaigner said Habib had taken the step of speaking out because he was taught when he started as a doctor 36 years ago that taking care of patients must be his first concern.
Anwar said: "Catastrophically, the operating theatre he worked in at times could be described as a slaughterhouse, which killed through incompetence and negligence whilst not giving a damn for accountability."
Dr Habib said he was putting his career on the line to demand accountability from the health board over its failings.
The consultant said he formally reported serious concerns about dozens of avoidable deaths in 2021.
In 2024, he took 20 cases from the previous decade to the police but at the time no charges were brought.
Following Habib's news conference on Thursday, Detective Superintendent Carron McKellar confirmed that Police Scotland had received a report in 2024 "following several deaths".
She added: "Inquiries are being carried out, and a situational report will be submitted to the Procurator Fiscal."
A spokesperson for the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS) said the concerns raised by the surgeon were "taken seriously".
They added: "Given the complexity and breadth of the issues raised, enquiries remain ongoing."
Whistleblower Habib had also leaked two reports by external experts which examined surgical care in NHS Grampian.
A 2022 review looked at 45 patients in the care of liver and pancreatic services at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary (ARI) between 2015 and 2021.
It said 31 had died, and 19 of them were considered to have died of avoidable consequences of surgery or intervention.
The 2022 review found that mortality rates for liver and pancreas surgery at NHS Grampian was very high.
It said that between 2017 and 2020 the overall death rate for major liver resections was 12% after 30 days and 18% after 90 days, compared to an expected rate of 2.2%.
The review criticised decision-making in liver surgery for occasionally being "overly aggressive", with surgery going ahead even if the disease was thought to be inoperable.
In pancreatic surgery, patients were regularly rushed into theatre without adequate planning or consideration of non-surgical alternatives, the experts said.
The review said blood losses in both surgeries was "unacceptably high" and said there was a lack of leadership and no clear standards for planning before operations.
It said poor decision-making and aggressive strategies were adopted despite high levels of deaths being predicted.
A second report earlier this year raised similar themes, with poor judgement about the risks and benefits of operating and repeated examples of massive blood losses and deaths during and after surgery.
It said it was clear that problems in the unit were "long standing and recurrent", adding that NHS Grampian had tried to improve outcomes but had not made the structural changes needed.
It noted that the unit did not have enough specialist staff to deliver the service safely, and that despite concerns being raised repeatedly since 2010, the issues had not been dealt with.
It strongly recommended that no complex or major HPB (Hepatopancreatobiliary) surgery should take place at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary.
'Indifference to patient deaths'
Dr Habib, who has worked in General Surgery & HPB (liver, pancreas and bile duct) since 2014, said he thought several hundred patients had come to harm from an excessively high rate of complications.
He said he had tried his best to use the whistleblowing procedures but the institutions he had contacted had failed to act as robustly as they should have.
He said he first became aware of avoidable surgery-related deaths in 2016.
In July 2021, he formally raised serious concerns about what he believes were dozens of avoidable deaths.
In February 2024, he spoke to Police Scotland.
Habib said he believed NHS Grampian was guilty of "wicked recklessness and an indifference to patient deaths" and called for a corporate homicide investigation.
The consultant said the 2022 external expert report, which was never made public, concluded that 19 deaths out of a case study of 45 were avoidable (42%).
He said he had contacted the first minister and health secretary on numerous occasions since June 2023 but the Scottish government had said it had "no jurisdiction" to investigate whistleblowing claims.
Habib also said that the Independent Whistleblowing Officer (INWO) refused to progress his complaints.
He also wrote to the chief executive of NHS Grampian, Laura Skaife Knight, in December 2025.
She replied six months later saying she needed Healthcare Improvement Scotland to complete its enquiries.
In response to the latest allegations from Habib, Skaife-Knight said: "A thorough investigation has already taken place into these matters, with some elements of that work ongoing.
"Any new evidence or information will be carefully considered, and NHS Grampian will continue to respond through the appropriate clinical, governance and legal processes."
A Scottish government spokesperson said they recognised the "courage shown by this whistleblower" and reiterated that a "very significant level of intervention and an unprecedented level of support" was being given to NHS Grampian.
