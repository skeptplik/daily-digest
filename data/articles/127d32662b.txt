Another two people were killed in a Russian attack on the Sknyliv train station in the western Ukrainian city of Lviv, authorities said.
At least three people were killed in Russian strikes on Kyiv overnight, after Russian President Vladimir Putin warned of a "painful" response to Ukraine's attacks on the Crimean bridge earlier this week.
A further 11 people were injured in the strikes on the capital, where fires were reported across three districts, according to Ukraine's State Emergency Service.
Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said three bridges crossing the Dnipro River had also been hit, disrupting traffic across the city.
"As a result of a ballistic attack on the capital, the Podil-Voskresensky Bridge has sustained damage. Traffic from the right bank to the left bank is currently closed on the bridge," Klitschko wrote in a post on Telegram. "The Darnytskyi bridge has also sustained damage."
He added that the Southern bridge had also been hit but did not clarify whether it had been damaged.
Another two people were killed and four were wounded in a Russian attack on the Sknyliv train station in Lviv, authorities said.
Russian forces launched a "massive strike" against Ukraine overnight, targeting "enterprises of Ukraine’s military-industrial complex, warehouses, energy facilities, transportation and port infrastructure, and naval vessels," the Russian defence ministry said on Friday morning.
It added that the assaults had been carried out using "high-precision land-based, naval, and air-based weapons," as well as drones.
It comes after Putin said there would be "no negotiations" on a partial ceasefire with Ukraine following strikes on his prized Crimea bridge this week, which destroyed a large part of the structure.
The multi-billion-dollar bridge links mainland Russia to the annexed Crimean Peninsula and is a symbol of Putin's rule. It has been repeatedly targeted by Ukraine since Moscow launched its full-scale invasion of the country in February 2022.
