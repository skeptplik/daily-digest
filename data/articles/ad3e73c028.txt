EET Fuels said it regretted the "historic incidents" and had invested more than Â£24m to improve environmental performance.
Ten charges related to wastewater discharges containing oil, suspended solids and chemicals, including cyanide, into Thornton Brook, the River Gowy and the Manchester Ship Canal.
Two other charges concerned incidents in 2019.
In February 2019, between 15 and 20 tonnes of a chemical leaked into the ship canal after a vent was left open and a written procedure was not followed.
A month later, a contractor's vehicle ruptured an effluent pipe. During repairs, about 15 tonnes of partially treated sewage entered Thornton Brook, which leads to the protected Mersey Estuary.
Environment Agency officers reported a sheen of oil on local waterways, while vegetation downstream of the refinery's discharge point was blackened by oil in January 2020.
The company was fined Â£80,000 for each of the 10 offences, with no additional penalty for the two 2019 incidents. Three breaches from 2022 and 2023 were taken into consideration without further punishment.
Nigel Glasgow, the Environment Agency's area environment manager, said the case showed "a repeated failure to comply" with permit conditions.
An EET Fuels spokesperson said: "We take our environmental responsibilities extremely seriously and regret these historic incidents."
The company said its investment included rerouting wastewater to United Utilities' Ellesmere Port treatment works, resulting in "a significant improvement in environmental performance", including fewer discharges and reduced freshwater use.
