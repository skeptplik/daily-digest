It was the most simple of questions. On Monday afternoon, during a NASA news conference about the International Space Station, Space News reporter Jeff Foust asked about an upcoming launch of a Russian spacecraft.
“It’s my understanding there’s another cargo mission going to the ISS this week, a Russian Progress,” Foust said. “Can you confirm that, and give the date and time that will launch?”
This Progress MS-36 cargo launch, mind you, is presently scheduled for fewer than 16 hours from now, at 10:13 UTC (05:13 am ET) on Tuesday. It is going to a space station co-operated by the US space agency. But no, NASA would not provide this basic information about the upcoming Progress launch.
“Regarding the Progress launch, I would ask you to please reach out to Roscosmos directly for any questions tied to that vehicle,” said the senior NASA official on the call, Gaurang Patel, operations integration manager for NASA’s Low Earth Orbit Program.
Effectively, this is a “Go f- yourself” response from NASA. Roscosmos typically does not reply to Western media outlets, and everyone knows this.
So why is a bog-standard Progress launch suddenly a state secret? It seems to be a recent policy change, because, historically, NASA has televised every one of these cargo flights from Russia’s main spaceport in Baikonur, Kazakhstan. But there is no information about the upcoming launch on NASA’s television schedule for upcoming “live” events.
As recently as September, NASA provided live television coverage of Progress launches from Baikonur. It even issued a media advisory promising to update its broadcast schedule once Roscosmos provided launch details. Two months later, NASA officials apparently are unwilling to confirm that another Progress launch is taking place.
Who made this decision?
The question is whether this decision was made by NASA or was a request from Roscosmos, and, ultimately, whether any of this matters. We can make some educated guesses.
It is probably safe to surmise that the decision here was originally made by state-sponsored Russian space corporation Roscosmos. The company has not been advertising the preparation of the Progress spacecraft as it customarily does, some local sources say. Russian space reporter Anatoly Zak confirms that Roscosmos has “dramatically curtailed” its coverage of all launch campaigns in recent months. There is no mention of the launch on Roscosmos’ Telegram channel.
