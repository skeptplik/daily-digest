Mark Carney has warned that US tariffs could push Canadian carmakers towards China, as Ottawa weighs alternatives to an increasingly strained North American auto market.
Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said on Monday his country's carmakers could turn to China if the North American auto industry becomes less integrated due to US President Donald Trump's tariff hikes.
"We have the best auto workers in the world, and we want them fully deployed... in an integrated North American market," Carney told reporters ahead of a meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping at this week's APEC summit in southern China.
"That market has become less integrated because of US trade actions."
Carney said at a summit of French-speaking nations in Cambodia the current system was "the best result for Canadian workers, for Canadian business, and for US consumers".
"It's part of the trade negotiations, as you know - depending on where that settles, there'll be other options, European options, Asian options, of course, Chinese, Japanese," he added.
Trump imposed punishing tariffs on Canada's auto sector last year, and has repeatedly said he wants autos sold in the United States to be made exclusively by American workers.
Carney walked away from trade talks with Trump in August, but Ottawa and Washington have sounded optimistic about prospects for a deal in recent weeks.
Carney said looking to China was "not leverage, but action, reaction".
