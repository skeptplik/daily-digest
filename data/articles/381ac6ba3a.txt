Former police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon has been found guilty of murdering Jesse Baird and Luke Davies with his service weapon.
A New South Wales supreme court jury delivered its verdicts on Wednesday, finding Lamarre-Condon guilty of two counts of murder after two hours of deliberation.
Lamarre-Condon was accused of entering Baird’s Paddington home in Sydney on 19 February 2024 and shooting the 26-year-old TV presenter and his new partner.
The jury also found Lamarre-Condon guilty of aggravated break and enter over an incident on 17 August 2023, when he broke into the terrace house and went through Baird’s phone.
Baird and Davies’ loved ones held each other as they returned to the courtroom. As the guilty verdicts were read out, they gasped and sobbed.
The families of Baird and Davies hugged and cried in each other’s arms after the verdicts.
Meanwhile, Lamarre-Condon stood and stared straight ahead as the verdicts were read out.
Addressing the murderer, Justice Natalie Adams formally convicted him.
“I formally convict the accused Beau Lamarre-Condon of all three counts. Count one being aggravated break and enter, count two the murder of Jesse Baird and count three the murder of Luke Davies,” she told the court.
Applause sounded out from the public gallery as the court was adjourned.
In a statement, NSW police said it acknowledged the verdict.
“Our thoughts are with Jesse and Luke’s family and friends, and NSW police will continue to support them,” the statement read.
The officer in charge of the investigation, detective Sasha Pinazza, was seen embracing the victims’ family members after the verdicts.
The trial
Lamarre-Condon and Baird began casually dating in late 2022, when the prosecution argued that the police officer developed an “infatuation and obsession” with the 26-year-old, the prosecution told the court.
They argued this manifested in compulsive behaviour, which escalated when the pair stopped seeing each other.
Lamarre-Condon and Baird broke up for the second and final time in November 2023 when Baird asked the officer to stop contacting him.
The 26-year-old began dating Davies, a Qantas flight attendant, in early 2024.
On 19 February 2024, Lamarre-Condon dressed up as an Uber Eats rider and entered Baird’s home with what the prosecution argued was a surreptitiously copied key, the prosecution said.
Lamarre-Condon then shot Baird in the back before firing an “execution style” shot through his temple, according to the prosecution case.
The prosecution said Davies, who was in the bedroom, attempted to call triple zero when Lamarre-Condon shot him in the head through the door.
During the following days, Lamarre-Condon cleaned up the crime scene and eventually left the bodies near a Greek monastery in Bungonia, about 150km away.
He did not hand himself in to police until 23 February 2024, and Baird and Davies’s bodies were found with his help four days later.
The defence did not dispute the clean-up or body disposal, but argued the men were shot as Lamarre-Condon acted in self-defence.
During the 10-week trial, more than 50 people gave evidence from the witness box – including Lamarre-Condon – while hours of CCTV and numerous exhibits were shown to the court.
Lamarre-Condon will be sentenced in 2027.
