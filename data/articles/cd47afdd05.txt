“Lab-grown diamonds are the great equalizer,” says Jean Dousset, the great-grandson of Louis Cartier who pivoted his namesake label to exclusively lab-grown stones in 2023. “People don’t have to compromise anymore to own something exceptional.” Once considered the understudy to mined diamonds, these stones (and therefor, the best lab-grown diamond brands), have officially taken center stage—and they’re not just performing, they’re dazzling. Lab-grown diamonds are real diamonds. Not simulants, not stand-ins. We’re talking pure carbon, identical in chemical, physical, and optical makeup to what’s been pulled from the earth for centuries—just made in a lab, not dug up from a mine.
So why the lingering hesitation? Stigma, mostly. The kind passed down like an heirloom from an era when “real” luxury was defined by scarcity and secrecy. But times—and tastes—have changed. “I started working with lab-grown diamonds because it was a smarter, more conscious choice,” says Jennifer Fisher, who now incorporates them across her fine jewelry designs. “They offer the same brilliance and clarity—but they align with how I want to shop and design today.” In an article that ran earlier this year, Jessica Sailer, former Vogue editor and founder of the new lab-grown line Pietro offered this: “Imagine if Hermès introduced an AI technology that could produce a perfect Birkin bag using the same materials in the same atelier, but in a fraction of the time. The craftsmanship would be identical. Would you want one?” Why, yes; yes, of course!

That sentiment is echoed by a growing number of designers (and their clients) who love that you can go bigger, bolder, and cleaner—ethically and aesthetically—without inflating the price tag.
Of course, the Four Cs still matter—color, cut, clarity, and carat—but so do the other Cs: conscience, customization, and carbon footprint. Whether you're buying a diamond ring for a fiancée, a friend, or yourself (because yes, self-gifting is a love language), lab-grown diamonds offer a compelling—and undeniably glamorous—case for the future of fine jewelry. In the realm of fine jewelry, Kinn stands out as a purveyor of modern heirlooms designed to transcend fleeting trends. Founded in 2017 by Jennie Yoon, Kinn was born from a deeply personal mission: to recreate and reimagine her family’s stolen antique jewelry. This intimate origin story infuses each piece with a sense of continuity and timelessness, aiming to be cherished across generations.
Kinn’s collection seamlessly blends classic elegance with contemporary minimalism. From meticulously crafted engagement rings and wedding bands to everyday essentials like the Solis ring—a bestseller featuring an elegant ribbed design inspired by Yoon's mother’s vintage pleated ring—each piece embodies a narrative of heritage and modernity. Crafted in Los Angeles, the brand works with recycled gold and offers both lab-grown and natural diamonds.
Nearly a decade after launching the business, Yoon continues to build on the idea that a family heirloom needn't be inherited to have sentimental value. The result is jewelry that feels as though it might have belonged to your grandmother, while looking entirely at home in your own jewelry box.

Brilliant Earth has long been at the forefront of ethically minded fine jewelry, and in the world of lab-grown diamonds, it was an early adopter. The brand first introduced lab-created stones in 2012—well before most retailers caught on—and has continued to push the category forward with collections that prioritize innovation and transparency. “We started Brilliant Earth to raise the ethical standards of the diamond industry and to create a new way to buy beautiful fine jewelry,” said co-founder and CEO Beth Gerstein in 2024.
In recent years, the brand has introduced collections like Capture, which features diamonds grown using captured carbon dioxide, and its Renewable collection, which emphasizes renewable energy in diamond production. A partnership with Dr. Jane Goodall also resulted in a limited-edition jewelry collection supporting conservation efforts.
But perhaps the most glamorous development arrived in 2026, when Brilliant Earth joined forces with the House of Taylor for a collection inspired by Elizabeth Taylor's legendary jewelry box. The 17-piece assortment reimagines Old Hollywood extravagance with lab-grown diamonds and colored gemstones, drawing on the actress's famously expressive approach to jewelry. Think Taylor-level glamour, minus the Richard Burton-sized budget.
Alongside these more adventurous collections, Brilliant Earth continues to offer classic engagement rings, wedding bands, and everyday diamond jewelry, with an emphasis on customization and information about the origins of its stones.

Dorsey, the cult-favorite jewelry label founded by Meg Strachan at the end of 2019, has redefined heirloom jewelry for the modern era. Inspired by her grandmother's trove of vintage gems, Strachan launched the direct-to-consumer brand with a mission to craft archival-inspired designs using lab-grown stones. Its signature Rivière necklaces and Georgian-inspired Lucien earrings have become recognizable favorites among a fashionable clientele.
Taylor Swift famously wore Dorsey earrings during her All Too Well short film, giving the brand a front-row seat to pop culture history. Hailey Bieber, Bella Hadid, and Emily Ratajkowski are also admirers, layering Dorsey's white sapphires and lab-grown diamonds with enviable ease.
Now, Strachan is expanding her jewelry vocabulary. In September 2026, Dorsey introduced earth-toned lab-grown diamonds, dramatic vintage-inspired drop earrings, and solitaire rings in seven different shapes. The collection is part of a broader exploration of colored stones and unexpected materials, from enamel to floating emeralds.
The effect is jewelry that looks more collected than coordinated—as though its wearer has spent years haunting antique shops, even if the pieces arrived in the mail last Tuesday.

Long before buying an engagement ring online felt second nature, Blue Nile was helping convince a generation of shoppers to take the leap. In 2026, Blue Nile welcomed another pioneer of online diamond shopping into the fold: James Allen, whose standalone website closed in May. Known for its pioneering 360-degree diamond imagery, James Allen made it possible to scrutinize a potential stone from virtually every angle. Its designs now live on as a proprietary collection at Blue Nile, bringing together two of the original names in digital diamond retail.
Today, Blue Nile offers everything from classic solitaires and wedding bands to tennis bracelets and diamond studs, with both natural and select lab-grown diamonds available. For the bride-to-be who knows exactly what she wants—or the one who'd rather explore a thousand possibilities before deciding—there's something rather appealing about having the jewelry counter at your fingertips.

Jean Dousset may be the great-grandson of Louis Cartier, but his career has never been about resting on pedigree. With early training at Chaumet in Paris and an apprenticeship under Alain Boucheron, he quickly established himself in the world of high jewelry, eventually working at Van Cleef & Arpels. But it was when he opened his own showroom in Los Angeles that Dousset began rewriting the rules on his own terms.
In April 2023, he made perhaps his boldest move yet: transitioning his entire brand to lab-grown diamonds. The pivot wasn't just about ethics—it was about creative freedom. “The fact that it's cheaper doesn't make it cheap,” Dousset told Vogue. Fine jewelry, after all, still requires imagination, creativity, design, and craftsmanship, whatever the origins of the stones.
That creative license is on full display in Dousset's recent work, including the bespoke engagement ring he designed for Paris Jackson, featuring a portrait-cut diamond and distinctive sculptural metalwork. Rather than simply recreating a conventional engagement ring, the design reflects Jackson's own aesthetic and personal symbolism.
Today, Dousset's lab-grown diamonds appear in designs that reflect both his Place Vendôme roots and a thoroughly modern sensibility. It's jewelry that honors tradition—while giving it a long-overdue update.

Price range: Pieces start at $1,200
Special services: GIA-certified stones, custom designs, complimentary consultations, free shipping, 14-day returns

Jennifer Fisher has always approached jewelry as deeply personal—and deeply powerful. The designer, known for redefining modern essentials with her signature hoops and dog tags, entered the lab-grown diamond space not to jump on a trend, but to offer something meaningful. “I have a mine-cut stone that is of significant size,” she told Vogue, “and I don’t know who, if anyone, was harmed mining it. That doesn’t feel great.”
Her lab-grown collection, launched in early 2023, is a sharp departure from the micro pavé-drenched engagement offerings flooding the market. Think pinky rings with major emerald cuts, simplified martini-set studs, and substantial tennis necklaces made to sit perfectly whether your hair is up or down. "I stopped wearing my engagement ring on my wedding finger three years ago,” Fisher explains. “Now I wear it on my pinky—and people literally stop me on the street."
Designed in 18k gold, her pieces favor substantial, single-stone silhouettes over sparkle overload. “The market is already saturated with pavé,” she says. “People want one visually extraordinary stone—not a bunch of tiny ones shoved into a setting.”
Though she doesn't plan to expand into traditional engagement rings, Fisher has found that women—particularly those buying for themselves—are gravitating toward her lab-grown pieces as milestone markers: for anniversaries, divorces, or just the joy of wearing a serious rock on any finger they please. “They’re not buying them to be subtle,” she says. “They’re buying them because they want a stone that feels bold, modern, and truly theirs.”
Styles offered: Rings, tennis necklaces, diamond studs
Price range: Pieces start around $3,000
Special services: Simple, signature settings; lab diamonds set in 18k gold; direct-to-consumer model with personal consultation
Think of Rare Carat as your diamond-savvy best friend—only armed with AI, GIA gemologists, and access to over a million diamonds. Launched in 2016 as a search engine for comparing diamond prices (“Kayak for diamonds,” as it was dubbed early on), the platform has since evolved into one of the most trusted managed marketplaces in the lab-grown diamond space.
The brand’s lab-grown offerings lean into value and transparency without sacrificing quality. Their expansive inventory includes diamonds of every cut, carat, and clarity imaginable—many available with 360° HD imaging and grading reports from trusted labs like IGI and GIA. Every customer is paired with expert concierge support (yes, even via text), making it easy for first-time shoppers to navigate the process with confidence.
In November 2026, Rare Carat added more than 70,000 fancy-colored lab-grown diamonds to its inventory, including pink, blue, and yellow stones. For anyone who has ever admired a spectacular colored diamond at auction, it's an intriguing opportunity to experiment with some rather extraordinary hues without the extraordinary auction-house estimates.
What sets Rare Carat apart? Its tech-forward model. “We’re using AI to analyze diamonds, predict value, and detect overpricing,” says founder Ajay Anand. Whether you’re shopping for a classic solitaire or a custom engagement ring with a personal twist, Rare Carat offers a data-driven path to diamonds—no jargon, no pressure.
