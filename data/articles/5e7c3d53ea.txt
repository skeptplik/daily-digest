Hackers are actively exploiting a newly disclosed N-able N-central zero-day vulnerability dubbed "4EverAble2Shell" to compromise servers and deploy cryptocurrency miners, with researchers reporting at least 21 affected systems, including 19 running the company's latest security update.
The attacks follow security researcher Sean Bales' release of two new zero-day exploits today, dubbed 4EverAble2Shell and Agent2Shell, which he says allow attackers to gain root-level access to N-central appliances and SYSTEM-level access to Windows systems.
N-central is a remote monitoring and management (RMM) platform used by managed service providers (MSPs) and IT teams to monitor, manage, and secure customer devices and networks remotely.
The new vulnerabilities were disclosed on November 12, just two days after N-able released emergency security updates to address critical N-central zero-day vulnerabilities that Bales had publicly disclosed earlier this week.
Bales published proof-of-concept exploits for both the 4EverAble2Shell and Agent2Shell vulnerabilities, saying they were tested against N-central Appliance 2026.4.1.26 HF2, the security update N-able released on November 10.
The 4EverAble2Shell exploit allows unauthenticated attackers to execute commands as root on vulnerable N-central servers. However, the researcher says exploitation requires the platform's Legacy API functionality to be enabled.
The attack combines an Apache Axis vulnerability, tracked as CVE-2026-90436, with a separate N-central privilege escalation weakness to gain root-level access.
The second exploit, Agent2Shell, targets N-central's agent functionality and is claimed to allow attackers to execute commands with SYSTEM privileges on Windows devices.
The disclosures came after N-able took its hosted N-central environments offline earlier this week and urged on-premises customers to shut down their servers after Bales published more than 30 alleged zero-day vulnerabilities.
Bales has criticized N-able's handling of the vulnerability disclosures and the company's slow response to patching responsibly disclosed flaws.
"It's much much easier for me to just release the research I have and the vendor can decide what they want to do with it, what they do and don't want to fix, etc. :)," Bales told BleepingComputer.
Bales said N-able has not patched two unrelated RCE vulnerabilities in a different N-able product that he previously disclosed to the company in July.
In a November 12 security advisory, N-able said it was implementing additional protections in response to the new disclosures, including restricting certain N-central features and integrations while it develops further fixes.
"We are restricting certain N-central features and integrations," N-able said.
"These measures may affect some functionality, but we believe taking additional precautions while remediation is completed is the right action to protect our partners and their customers."
The company says it has disabled the Intune integration as a precaution and is urging on-premises customers to ensure Legacy API access is disabled, which is already the default.
The protections have already been applied to hosted environments, but customers are also advised to continue upgrading their Agents and Probes under the previous Hotfix 2 guidance.
In its advisory, N-able said it had seen no signs of compromise using the new flaws, although security researchers have since reported successful exploitation of the vulnerability against production N-central servers.
Hackers already exploiting 4EverAble2Shell
Cybersecurity company Previdian told BleepingComputer that attackers began probing for the 4EverAble2Shell vulnerability on November 12, shortly after the public release of the proof-of-concept exploit.
The company first observed suspicious requests targeting its N-central honeypot at approximately 08:30 UTC, using the same Java class referenced in the publicly released exploit.
While the initial requests were limited to probing for vulnerable servers, Previdian later confirmed that attackers were attempting to execute the complete exploit chain against its honeypot server.
The attacks initially exploit Apache Axis CVE-2026-90436 to execute commands as the 'nable' service account, then exploit a separate N-central privilege escalation vulnerability to obtain root privileges.
This attack chain matches the POC Bales released on GitHub.
According to Previdian, the privilege escalation stage abuses N-central's privileged setLDFileOwner helper and a symbolic link to make a normally root-owned script writable.
The attacker then modifies the script and invokes N-central's serverControl functionality to execute commands as root, while attempting to restore the modified files afterward.
The payload attempted to download and execute an additional shell script from an attacker-controlled server at 157.20.241[.]16, while sending diagnostic information and command output back to the same IP address.
Previdian is tracking the N-central privilege escalation flaw as VULN-2026-0009 and has added it to its own Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog.
Cybersecurity company Huntback also reports that attackers have already successfully compromised at least 21 production N-central servers using Bales' publicly released exploit chain.
Huntback saw the same attacker IP address as Previdian probing its N-central honeypots at approximately 08:30 UTC on November 12, with the first attempts to execute the complete exploit chain beginning at 09:16 UTC.
The attacks seen by Huntback exploited the 4EverAble2Shell exploit chain to download a shell script that installed an XMRig Monero cryptocurrency miner.
The miner was configured to use about half of the available CPU cores, likely to make the mining harder to detect.
Huntback identified other compromised systems through the attacker's cryptocurrency mining pool statistics, where infected systems were assigned identifiers beginning with nc_ followed by the server's IP address.
At 12:50 UTC on November 12, the researchers found 21 active miners using this naming convention, all of which had submitted mining shares within the previous five minutes.
The researchers then checked the associated IP addresses and confirmed that all 21 hosted N-central installations, including 19 running version 2026.4.1.26, the latest Hotfix 2 release issued on November 10.
The compromised servers were located across multiple countries, including the United States, Canada, South Africa, the United Kingdom, Germany, France, and Sweden.
While the attacks observed so far have been primarily used for cryptojacking, attackers could use compromised N-central infrastructure for additional malicious activity targeting managed customer environments.
Organizations running N-central should ensure that Legacy APIs are disabled and investigate suspicious requests to /dms/services/ServerUI, especially those referencing jline.internal.TerminalLineSettings.
Huntback also recommends checking for unexpected xvd.service systemd services, cryptocurrency mining processes, and files created under /tmp/.xvd or /var/tmp/.xvd.
Because the 4EverAble2Shell attack grants full root access to exploited servers, organizations that detect these IOCs should treat the breach as a full compromise of the server.
