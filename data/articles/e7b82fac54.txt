A large-scale search operation for a suspected lion sighted in woodland north of Istanbul has ended, with the animal in question found to be neither a lion nor any other type of big cat, according to the Istanbul governor's office.
The governor's office first issued a statement on Sunday evening saying that security cameras had captured footage of a lion on the loose in Sariyer, north of Istanbul's European side. It said "the lion has been located and the efforts to capture it were underway."
The parks authority also said in an online statement that police officers in the area had spotted "an animal resembling a lion" on surveillance cameras.
Turkish media published grainy images purportedly showing a lioness walking through a grassy area. Some media reports suggested it had escaped from a nearby home.
The Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor's Office also issued a statement saying it had launched an investigation focused on identifying the lion's owner.
After a day of searching, however, the Istanbul governor's office said "no concrete evidence indicating ‌the presence of a lion was found."
It said based on new footage, "it has been determined that the animal in the footage is neither a lion nor any other species of big cat."
The governor's office did not provide further information about what the animal seen in the footage was.
Outcry among activists
The earlier reports about a lion on the loose had sparked an outrage among activists.
Northern Forests Defense, an independent ecology advocacy movement, called for "the individuals who illegally held this lion captive to be identified and brought to justice."
"Removing wild animals from their natural habitats and keeping them in captivity in villas is a crime," the organization said on X.
Wildlife conservationist Gokce Coskun said on X that "there are thousands of people keeping lions illegally in Turkey" and "licensed 'lion parks' have sprung up everywhere."
Last year, a lion that escaped from a "theme park" near the southern city of Antalya was shot dead after attacking a man.
Doganay Tolunay, an ecology professor at Istanbul University, said questions must be asked about how a globally threatened species was brought into Turkey, how it passed through customs and why those keeping it have not been identified.
Although keeping wild animals as pets is forbidden in Turkey, the country has become a hub for the illegal wild animal trade as traffickers exploit its air links to Africa and Asia.
In December 2024, a young gorilla was rescued after being discovered in a crate at Istanbul Airport.
