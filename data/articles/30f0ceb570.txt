In 2026, wellness hit new heights. We maxxed protein and creatine and leveled up our supplement stacks. GLP-1s were seemingly everywhere, so was the topic of peptides. As optimization dominated the conversation, a backlash simultaneously grew. Longevity remained a core pursuit, on land and at sea, aided by innovations in tech.
“The tectonic plates in culture are shifting under our feet,” says Lestat McCree, co-founder and CEO of UK-based wellness retailer Healf. “We say that the run club is now becoming the new nightclub, and weekends are full of saunas and ice baths. [Wellness is] the cultural mainstream, and people are really seeing this as the new way to live.”
Young consumers are gravitating towards in-person micro-communities. It’s an opportunity for agencies and brands that get it right.
The global wellness market, now valued at $2.7 trillion, according to McKinsey, shows no sign of slowing down. McKinsey’s Future of Wellness 2027 report, released in November 2026, found wellness is increasingly important across generations, including for consumers previously less engaged. (Forty-nine percent of baby boomers now say wellness is a top priority, up 11 percentage points from 2024, compared with an average five-point increase across other generations.)
Looking ahead, 2027 is poised to be a breakthrough year for many of the nascent shifts that have steadily gained traction in recent years. The 2027 wellness consumer is notably discerning: they’re more informed, more intentional, and more questioning than those who came before. We spoke with analysts, forecasters and industry leaders to unpack the consumer shifts set to define wellness for the year ahead.
Personalization levels up
In 2027, wellbeing will get personal. Personalization is the defining wellness trend for Healf’s McCree, a thematic shift echoed across almost every report analyzed. This means that instead of stocking up on a slate of generic supplements and products made for a one-size-fits-all approach, wellness regimens will be increasingly informed by an individual’s unique biology, lifestyle, and behavioral preferences.
Tracking and diagnostics are on the rise, but notably, it’s moving beyond wearables. First-time orders of personalized products, like those that track blood, vaginal microbiome and fertility hormones, and at-home test kits for blood, genetics and microbiomes, are all on the rise.
McKinsey’s Future of Wellness 2027 report similarly identified diagnostics as a key trend, spanning at-home lab kits, advanced imaging and biomarker panels. Of the 10,000-plus consumers surveyed across China, Germany, the US, and the UK, 34% entered the category this past year, with 51% planning to increase spending.
“[There’s] a shift from tracking to targeted, measurable outcomes,” says Sara Hudson, partner at McKinsey and one of the report’s authors. She expects this approach to move from niche to mainstream next year: consumers will want proof that something works, rather than more data for its own sake.
Environmental cues take center stage
In 2027, expect wellbeing to be increasingly supported by our environment. With light predicted to be used “as a supplement” in Healf’s What’s Next in Wellbeing: 2027 report, and silence forecast as a luxury trend by strategic foresight consultancy The Future Laboratory, consumers are tipped to use environmental elements as strategic levers for wellbeing.
“[The trend of using] light as a supplement points to how consumers’ understanding of wellbeing is growing ever more holistic,” says Daniel Teweles, head of communications at Healf. “As humans, we evolved in relationship to light, and we’ve largely forgotten that, living largely indoors, particularly in the Northern hemisphere.”
Light products are gaining traction: SAD (seasonal affective disorder) and bright-light therapy lamps, dawn-simulating wake-up lamps and alarm clocks like the TikTok-viral Hatch, and blue light-blocking glasses are all seeing a rise in interest. The growth coincides with rising interest in circadian rhythms, up 72.5% year-on-year, per Spate data.
Silence is also set to take the spotlight. This is anticipated to be visible in an increase in spaces designed for minimal sensory stimulation. “Experiences such as Silent Koli’s guided walks in Finland and the UK’s Sacred Tree darkroom retreat hotel signal an emerging appetite for sensory relief,” says Seyi Oduwole, foresight analyst and author of The Future Laboratory’s Future Forecast 2027 report, set to be released in December. “For 2027, I expect more hotels and wellness spaces to make reducing noise, light and stimulation a core part of their offer.”
Hormone health and male fertility go mainstream
Hormone health and male fertility may have been the breakout themes in recent years, but in 2027, they’re set to become mainstream. For women, hormone health has moved beyond fertility and menopause into a core consideration of overall wellbeing.
For Lucy Goff, founder and CEO of MedTech company LYMA, this is a key trend. “In 2027, I expect women’s health to become an increasingly important area of investment [for brands], not simply because it represents a commercial opportunity, but because there is a genuine need for better research, better products and more effective whole system solutions.”
Oura is one such example. Following the introduction of new hormonal health features in recent years, including menopause insights and contraceptive tracking, its planned acquisition of AI-native healthcare platform Counsel Health deepens its investment in women’s health. Expected to close by the end of the year, the acquisition is set to further integrate biometric data with physician-led care, with reproductive health identified as a key area of focus.
Demand in hormone health is already visible. Cycle and PMS supplements, inositol supplements, the compound often used to support hormonal conditions like Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome (PMOS), and perimenopause supplements are shaping the market.
Male fertility was also predicted by Healf as an emerging trend for 2027, as men increasingly take a more active role in contraception. For many, there’s an increased correlation between fertility and overall wellbeing, with male fertility supplements like Wild Nutrition’s Fertility Support for Men and WeNatal’s For Him supplement seeing sales rise.
The GLP-1 ecosystem booms
If GLP-1s and weight loss medications dominated the wellness conversation in 2026, in 2027, expect to see a second wave. Not necessarily through GLP-1s themselves, but the products used alongside them.
McKinsey forecasts this will grow into an established GLP-1 ecosystem in 2027. “It’s essentially all the products and services that support someone taking GLP-1s. Sometimes these products are specifically designed or developed for GLP-1 users, but often they’re existing products that address the same needs and are increasingly being marketed to GLP-1 users (as well as more broadly).”
While this year saw a boom in the hair loss category and collagen-boosting skincare (with #Collagen amassing 4.8 million views on TikTok), elastin is anticipated to be the next “it” player in skincare. Beyond elastin, Spate forecasts KPV and KLOW peptides to surge in 2027. While these are often associated with GLP-1 use for addressing gut and skin inflammation, tissue repair, and elasticity, Spate suggests this shift points to a broader trend of peptides moving from biohacker forums to the beauty shelf in 2027.
Consumers are also anticipated to increasingly look to what’s next: while general social interest in GLP-1s is down 22.1% from its mid-2025 peak per Spate data, searches for GLP-1 supplements and next-generation compounds like retatrutide are surging, up 265.4% and 501.3% year-on-year, respectively.
Fiber, which supports satiety, is also forecast for growth. Pinterest searches for “high-fiber meals” increased 310% year-on-year, while psyllium husk was named a “breakout ingredient” by Healf. “We see a lot of people using psyllium husk for satiety,” notes Healf’s Teweles. “[This] is likely a response to GLP-1s and how much they’re increasingly dominating the cultural conversation.”
Wellness for the fun of it
For years, wellness has been defined by data and outcomes. But in 2027, the push against quantification — a trend that’s been steadily gaining traction in the past year — may experience its cultural breakthrough. “Wellness brands have spent years selling people more things to do, buy and measure,” says Tom Garland, strategist and founder of brand consultancy Edition+Partners. “The next competitive advantage will be how much they take off your plate.”
For Garland, “subtraction” is the defining word for wellness in 2027. In practice, this looks like wellness offerings that integrate with life, instead of consuming them. “I expect belonging, demonstrable usefulness, and better design around real bodies and life stages to matter more. The opportunity is to help people live well without making wellness their whole life.”
“We’re past peak-optimization,” says Anu Lingala, strategist and author of trend strategy newsletter What’s Anu. “Over the past couple of years, [there’s been] more of an anti-tech attitude culturally in response to AI.” As consumers seek analog options — evident in viral TikTok hashtags like #SlowLiving, #Analogue and #Journalling — presence and experience hold greater weight.
The Future Laboratory also anticipates this shift, as Oduwole explains: “We expect greater emphasis on bodily awareness, pleasure and real-world connection, creating opportunities for wellness experiences that help people feel grounded.”
She points to emerging spaces that prioritize presence and connection as a core way this trend may be expressed. “Photography-free events such as Playbody’s encourage participants to reconnect with how an experience feels… For 2027, I expect presence itself to become a more valuable part of the wellness offer.”
This appetite for presence and pleasure extends to wellness products themselves. Spate predicts grown-up comfort objects as an emerging trend for the year ahead, with interest in weighted stuffed animals up 177.9% year-on-year and weighted eye masks surging over 1,000%, largely driven by TikTok. “Adults are embracing weighted, tactile products as tools for calm,” explains Atkinson. “We see this as part of a broader shift toward sensory wellness, where how a product feels is as important as what it does.”
It’s a sentiment echoed by Healf’s McCree. “2027 will be the year where wellness is less prescribed, less prescriptive and more personal,” he says. “[It’s] less rules and more joy.”
