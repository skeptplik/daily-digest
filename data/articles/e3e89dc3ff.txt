Like many comics aimed at children, Charles Schulz's Peanuts doesn't have too many actual villains. It has a number of antagonists, such as Lucy Van Pelt, various other kids, or the impersonal cloud of failure that constantly hangs over Charlie Brown's head, but it's rare to see a genuinely malevolent force in Peanuts — with one exception.
One of Charlie Brown's long-standing hobbies in Peanuts was kite-flying, despite not being very good at it. Whenever he tried it, he invariably got his kite caught in trees and/or himself wrapped up in the kite string. Like every other hobby in Charlie Brown's life, he was devoted to it despite his bad luck.
That hobby eventually introduced Charlie Brown to his nemesis. The Kite-Eating Tree was an otherwise unidentified tree in Charlie Brown's neighborhood that seemed to deliberately grab and eat his kites, to the point where Charlie Brown developed a one-sided rivalry with it.
According to Schulz in the autobiographical collection My Life With Charlie Brown (2010), the inspiration for the Kite-Eating Tree was simply a bit of observational humor. Schulz had noticed that a kite that got stuck in a tree would slowly disappear over the course of several weeks, as wildlife and the elements took their toll. He decided that must've been because the tree was eating the kite, and so the Kite-Eating Tree ended up in Peanuts.
The Kite-Eating Tree was first specifically identified in March 1965, which marked the beginning of a recurring story that would last for the next 30 years.
Didn't think I'd do it, did you
In many of its appearances, the Kite-Eating Tree doesn't actually do much of anything. In fact, it's noted to have fallen down at one point, but Charlie Brown's subsequent run-ins with the tree suggests that either there's more than one or it's some kind of elected position. Having said that, it’s shown on more than one occasion to be actively devouring its “prey.”
The two major Kite-Eating Tree storylines are from the late 1960s and 1970s. One involves Charlie Brown escalating the stakes in the conflict and subsequently having to run from the Environmental Protection Agency, while the other involves Lucy feeding Schroeder's piano to the tree. Both stories were adopted into episodes of the short-lived '80s cartoon The Charlie Brown and Snoopy Show.
In January 1969, Lucy's one-sided obsession with Schroeder reached a boiling point. Fed up with Schroeder's refusal to pay attention to her instead of constantly practicing, she threw his toy piano into the closest tree. Unfortunately, it was the Kite-Eating Tree, which was entirely willing to broaden its diet with a piano. Despite an attempt by Snoopy to rescue the piano, it was soon gone, and Schroeder had to mail-order a replacement.
The second major Kite-Eating Tree storyline is more surreal, and lasted an unusually long time for a Peanuts arc. On Feb. 25, 1977, Charlie Brown demonstrated an unusual amount of jaw strength when he chose to retaliate against the Kite-Eating Tree. If it was going to bite his kites, he reasoned, he'd just bite it right back, and took quite a piece out of the Tree’s side.
A few days later, Charlie Brown received a letter from the EPA about the tree. While we're never told what the letter said, Charlie Brown decides to run away from home to escape the charges. Over the next few weeks, he reaches another town, sleeps in a cardboard box, ends up as the new coach of a pee-wee baseball team, and finally gets talked into going home, as the EPA has no case. Besides, the Kite-Eating Tree has since fallen over.
That didn't keep the Kite-Eating Tree from making future appearances, but it was primarily a one-joke wonder for most of the rest of Peanuts' run.
Its final appearance came in 1995, when Charlie Brown's attempt to teach Rerun how to fly a kite ended in disaster. Charlie Brown would continue to unsuccessfully fly kites from time to time until March 1999, but the Tree was out of the picture for good.
With one last kite for the road, the Kite-Eating Tree was never seen again, but one must assume that it's still out there somewhere. Waiting. Hungering for fresh kites. Annoyed that kids don't really seem to fly kites as much as they used to.
