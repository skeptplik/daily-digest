New Zealand Prime Minister Christopher Luxon’s right-wing coalition lost its parliamentary majority, final results from this month’s election showed on Friday (November 27, 2026), plunging the country into political uncertainty with an insurgent centrist party set to play kingmaker.
Preliminary results of the November 7 poll had Mr. Luxon’s National Party-led coalition clinging onto power.
But margins were so tight that his party lost two seats in the final results announced Friday -- leaving his coalition with the populist New Zealand First and the libertarian ACT one seat short of a majority.
Mr. Luxon declared he had a mandate to form the country’s next government in the wake of this month’s vote, bitterly fought and dominated by spiralling costs of living in the South Pacific country.
He has run a caretaker government since the November 7 poll.
But he will now need the backing of the centrist newcomers Opportunity, led by Qiulae Wong, if he wants to govern.
And if a deal isn’t struck, the country could be headed for another election.
Pledging a kinder, gentler politics as well as action on climate change, her party became the first in decades to make parliament without a former cabinet minister leader.
Wong has vowed to work with whichever party has the largest number of seats.
But she has insisted that her party’s “bottom lines” on climate change action, cost of living and cleaning up politics are non-negotiable.
On the campaign trail, she ruled out working with a government in which NZ First leader Winston Peters was foreign minister, after he told a Chinese-born lawmaker to “go back to your own country”.
Centrist kingmaker
Both Mr. Luxon and Opposition Labour Party leader Chris Hipkins have held lengthy meetings with Wong in the wake of the election.
Ms. Wong’s rise to kingmaker in the next New Zealand government caps a remarkable rise since the 38-year-old former KPMG consultant took over a party polling at about one percent in August last year.
She preaches “pragmatic politics” as a counter to growing polarisation, saying she wants to “bring the heat down a little bit and actually just focus on moving the country forward”.
She said following the election this month that her win represented a sea change after a decade of rising populism and extremism worldwide.
“I think we are seeing a push globally for more pragmatic politics,” she told journalists.
“We’ve seen that populism doesn’t deliver results,” she said.
With the official count in, it could take weeks or even months for the shape of New Zealand’s new government to be decided.
Following the 2023 poll, it took more than six weeks for Mr. Luxon to form his coalition government with NZ First and ACT.
