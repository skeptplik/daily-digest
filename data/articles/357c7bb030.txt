Wallabies defeat Wales 29-26 in Nations Championship Test to end four-match losing streak
The Wallabies have snapped a four-match losing run with a tense 29-26 win over Wales in their Nations Championship Test in Cardiff.
The result gives the Wallabies an injection of confidence and relieves some of the pressure on coach Les Kiss, although Wales did its best to deny the visitors in the shadows of full-time.
Wales scored a try through reserve hooker Ryan Elias in the 79th minute of the match, cutting the Wallabies' lead to three points.
Following the re-start, the home side launched an attacking movement from deep inside their 22, knowing it was the final play of the match.
But it came to nothing, allowing the Wallabies to celebrate victory after defeats to All Blacks (twice), England and Scotland.
The Wallabies had entered the match low on morale, but their hopes were boosted when they established a 12-0 lead in the first half on the back of tries to Taniela Tupou and Brandon Paenga-Amosa.
But poor discipline proved costly, with the Wallabies giving away nine penalties to four, including seven inside their half.
From one of those penalties, Wales scored its first converted try via captain Dewi Lake to reduce the Wallabies' lead to 12-7 at the half-time break.
The two sides traded tries early in the second half before Wales hit the front.
Fullback Blair Murray's converted try gave Wales a 21-19 lead, but the Wallabies struck back with Len Ikitau scoring a five-pointer and Carter Gordon adding the extras in the 65th minute.
Leading 26-21, the Wallabies extended their advantage through Gordon's penalty goal with six minutes remaining in the match.
Wales refused to roll over, however, setting up a grandstand finish with Elias's late five-pointer.
The Wallabies finished third on the Southern Hemisphere standings in the Nations Championship.
They play Ireland at Twickenham next Saturday, AEDT, as part of the Nations Championship's Finals Weekend.
Wales, fifth in the Northern Hemisphere standings, faces Japan on Sunday, AEDT.
South Africa and France will face off in the Nations Championship grand final at Twickenham on Monday, AEDT.

Springboks fall to Ireland
Two first-half tries from Rob Baloucoune helped a rejuvenated Ireland beat South Africa 20-17 in Dublin.
But a losing bonus point was enough to book the world champions a place in next weekend's Nations Championship final.
The Springboks were without coach Rassie Erasmus, who was watching on from his hotel as he serves the first of a six-match ban.
Ireland dominated the first half and led the subdued Springboks 17-10 at the break thanks to Baloucoune's tries and one from Ronan Kelleher.
A Sam Prendergast penalty stretched the lead to 10 points.
A Malcolm Marx try for the Springboks cut the Irish lead to three just before the hour, but the visitors could not breach the brilliant Irish defence and fell to back-to-back defeats.
Ireland, who began the day with an outside shot of topping the Northern Hemisphere standings, cannot catch England.
But Ireland is guaranteed a top-three finish and one of the marquee matches at the November 27-29 finals weekend at Twickenham.

All Blacks smashed by England
England has outclassed New Zealand 37-12 at Twickenham.
The record victory margin over the All Blacks and a bonus point has given the English a chance of making next weekend's Nations Championship final against South Africa.
The hosts dominated the first half, with Henry Slade going over inside five minutes, before tries from Jack van Poortvliet and Ben Earl made it 19-0.
But Ethan de Groot's try on the cusp of half-time gave the All Blacks hope.
Ollie Chessum powered over for England following the restart, however, and after New Zealand's Beauden Barrett marked his 150th cap with a superb try, Seb Atkinson coasted in to effectively end the contest just after the hour mark.
Replacement fly half Marcus Smith kicked two late penalties to give England a 25-point winning margin, surpassing its 17-point victory over New Zealand in 2012.
"I love the way the team is growing and we look forward to the future," England captain Maro Itoje said.
England moved to the top of the Northern Hemisphere table on 25 points.
Prior to kick-off, England's former captain Lewis Moody, who last year revealed he was diagnosed with motor neurone disease, joined the current squad in facing the pre-match haka.
