Russia said on Tuesday it was aware of French troops deployed to Ukraine, calling it "direct participation" in the war and saying a message had been issued to Paris. France has rejected the claim troops are directly involved in fighting as "absurd."
France's Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot has denied allegations of direct participation in the war in Ukraine, in response to comments made by Russia's Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov on Tuesday.
He said that France was not involved in a military operation, but in "training and maintenance, carried out far from the front lines, with very limited scope and no involvement in warfare", in an interview with French talk show "C à Vous".
He added that the majority of training had taken place in Poland.
"We support the Ukrainian army and the Ukrainian resistance, and this is the least we can do, given that it is a people fighting for their independence and democracy, on the doorstep of Europe", he stated.
Barrot also accused the leader of the hard-left France Unbowed party Jean-Luc Mélenchon of "relaying Russian propaganda", after the presidential hopeful shared an X stating "Alert. We must under no circumstances become a co-belligerent in Ukraine."
Barrot's comments come after Lavrov told reporters that "foreign military personnel" had been in Ukraine "for more than six months. And not just the French."
Lavrov declared that Russia had communicated concerns "through available channels", informing relevant countries that this amounted to "direct participation in the war, including target designation assistance, satellite data provision, and other forms of support."
His remarks follow revelations by French media, based on revelations from a source who disclosed that soldiers had been deployed on a secret training mission in Ukraine for several months to train personnel and help maintain equipment.
The source said the mission of a small contingent of French troops excludes any combat role. Speaking to AFP on condition of anonymity, they described the rotation of "French trainers supporting Ukraine in France, Poland, or occasionally in Ukraine" as a reality.
"But this is not a military operation. There is absolutely no intention of engaging in coercive actions", said the source.
Earlier on Tuesday, Deputy defence minister Alice Rufo told parliament that there was a "very limited" deployment of French troops in Ukraine for training and equipment maintenance.
She added that was no "intention to fight, contrary to what the Russian foreign minister said this morning, which is absurd."
In November, the European Council adopted a decision extending the mandate of the European Union Military Assistance Mission in support of Ukraine(EUMAM Ukraine) for a further two years, with a budget allocation of nearly €300 million for this period.
