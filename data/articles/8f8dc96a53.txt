The Vogue Business AI Tracker keeps a record of the most important AI developments that will influence our industry and our world, each week. From venture capital investments and startup launches to product drops and regulatory updates, we’ll make sure you never miss a beat when it comes to the AI news that matters.
Nov 3, 2026
The news: Prada launches ChatGPT plugin.
Why it matters: Prada has launched a ChatGPT plugin allowing users in the US, the UK and Italy to discover products, explore collections and find stores using information sourced directly from the brand. As AI assistants become a gateway to product discovery, the move gives Prada a more authoritative presence in conversations it might otherwise struggle to influence. “Conversational experiences represent a natural evolution of traditional e-commerce,” said Prada Group digital director Laura Campanella. “Through this pilot initiative, we are exploring a new territory for customer engagement.”
The news coincides with the launch of Prada’s Christmas campaign this week, featuring hand-drawn animation by Disney veteran John Pomeroy. These two contrasting moves reflect a broader distinction emerging in luxury: AI for product discovery, and human craftsmanship for consumer-facing brand storytelling.
The news: Anthropic’s IPO filing reveals the huge costs and risks of the AI boom.
Why it matters: Anthropic’s blockbuster IPO filing offers a rare look inside the economics — and extraordinary risks — of the AI industry. As the Claude AI maker prepares for a potential $2 trillion valuation, the Financial Times reports that its revenues exceeded $33 billion in the first nine months of 2026, while its computing costs have fallen dramatically relative to sales, all suggesting a path to profitability. Yet Anthropic has also committed more than $800 billion to computing infrastructure through 2045, much of it supplied by technology giants including Amazon and Google, who are also major investors.
Crucially, the prospectus devotes 92 pages to risks, warning that future AI models could resist shutdown, manipulate information or cause harm too large for insurers to cover. For businesses embedding AI into everything from creative production to customer service, the filing exposes a central contradiction: the technology is becoming commercially indispensable even as its developers acknowledge profound uncertainty over its long-term risks.
The news: Anthropic plans European AI consultancy.
Why it matters: Anthropic is reportedly partnering with private equity firm Hg to launch a European consultancy that’ll place AI engineers and advisers in external businesses to help them integrate Anthropic’s technology, according to the Financial Times. Expected to launch by the end of 2026, hubs are currently planned in London and Berlin, with the Claude developer likely using the venture to accelerate corporate AI adoption — and even capture some of the work traditionally handled by consultancies such as Accenture and the Big Four.
Fashion and luxury businesses, many of which are still grappling with how to deploy AI, could be fertile ground for these services. But the model raises an obvious tension: while companies may welcome direct access to AI expertise, how independent can the advice be when the consultancy is backed by a company with a commercial interest in driving adoption of its own technology?
Oct 27, 2026
The news: Anthropic launches Claude Duet AI agent to rival OpenAI and Meta.
Why it matters: Anthropic has released Claude Duet, an AI agent rivaling Meta’s consumer-focused Muse and OpenAI’s more enterprise-oriented Dots; the Duet has both brand and consumer use cases for fashion. It can purchase products on consumers’ behalf using Shopify’s Shop Pay and Stripe Link, while merchants can instruct the agent to set up, design and manage their Shopify stores, including inventory and orders, Anthropic announced in a blog post. Meanwhile, a “richer visual editor” — which allows brands to design and preview their storefronts within the agent’s interface before anything goes live — is “coming soon”.
It was only a matter of time until Anthropic followed Meta and OpenAI in launching a personal AI assistant. Now, the battle is on between these tech companies to capture both consumer and brands’ trust, so that the integration these agents depend on across stores and payment providers is realized. The growing competition here signals a shift towards AI-mediated purchases, potentially reducing direct customer interactions for brands and making product visibility and seamless checkout across AI platforms increasingly important.
Oct 20, 2026
The news: Major banks and retailers block AI shopping agents like Meta Muse.
Why it matters: A growing list of US banks and retailers are blocking AI agents like Meta Muse and Instinct from acting on their websites. Amazon and Ebay have both prohibited these models from browsing or purchasing items on their respective marketplaces, while Citigroup, Bank of America, JPMorgan and Charles Schwab have each moved to prevent outside agents from logging into customer accounts and transacting on their behalf.
“We have to make sure we protect our customers in the right way [and] we’re building tools to do that,” JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon said on the company’s third-quarter earnings call last week. “In the meantime, we’re not going to allow just any outside agent to come inside the company, because that’s too risky for clients and for JPMorgan.”
While much of the fashion industry runs its direct-to-consumer sites via platform providers like Shopify — which has opened its network to AI agents unless a brand explicitly opts out — the growing calls from prominent banking officials to introduce legislation point to a bigger unresolved question: when an AI agent makes a purchase that a consumer later disputes, who is responsible for putting it right? For now, experts say much of that burden may still land with the retailer.
The news: OpenAI UK employees received £2.3 million on average in pay and shares in 2025.
Why it matters: OpenAI employees in the UK received an average of £2.3 million in pay and shares last year, according to newly filed UK accounts. The ChatGPT developer distributed £255 million in salary and equity compensation among 110 staff — more than double the previous year’s average package. The figures reflect fierce competition for specialist AI talent, although remuneration varies significantly between employees, with leading researchers commanding the largest pay.
It’s another sign that the AI bubble is far from bursting, with these salaries incentivizing employees for longer and reflecting the budgets of the venture capitalists funding the company. (OpenAI’s most recent funding round reached $122 billion in March 2026, at an $852 billion post-money valuation.)
OpenAI’s UK revenue more than tripled to £293 million in 2025, as London becomes an increasingly key international AI research and commercial hub.
Oct 13, 2026
The news: Pinterest launches AI-powered beauty guides.
Why it matters: Pinterest has launched Beauty Guides, an AI-powered feature that analyzes hair and nail Pins to generate personalized salon instructions, at-home tutorials and product recommendations. Available first to US iOS users, Pinterest says the tool aims to close the gap between discovering a beauty look and recreating it.
While competitors like Google Lens already use AI-powered visual search to identify and shop products, Pinterest is extending the concept to practical beauty advice. The move reflects a broader shift from AI-powered discovery to AI-assisted decision-making, while helping Pinterest turn its vast repository of visual inspiration into greater shopping intent and potential commercial opportunities for beauty brands. It also builds on the platform’s recent push into visual search advertising, giving it more ways to connect inspiration with purchases.
The news: OpenAI releases virtual try-on in ChatGPT.
Why it matters: OpenAI is introducing AI-powered virtual try-on and product-saving features to ChatGPT, deepening its push into fashion e-commerce. Users can upload photos of themselves to visualize how clothing and accessories might look on, while a new Favorites feature lets them save products and try-on images for later.
Powered by ChatGPT Images 2.5 (OpenAI’s latest image generator, released in September) the tools will roll out globally across consumer plans, with purchases still completed on merchants’ websites. The launch puts ChatGPT in more direct competition with visual shopping platforms such as Google and Pinterest, as AI assistants evolve from product discovery tools into personalized shopping destinations.
For fashion brands, it raises the prospect of ChatGPT becoming an increasingly influential touchpoint in the customer journey. Luxury brands have been hesitant to adopt virtual try-on on their own channels, largely because the tech hasn’t met their visual standards — but the latest advances in AI are rapidly improving the realism of these digital models.
The news: TikTok launches AI shopping assistant and one-click checkout.
Why it matters: TikTok has launched an AI-powered shopping assistant that provides personalized product guidance, answering questions about sizing, availability and delivery directly within the app. Alongside this, a new one-click checkout feature allows users to purchase directly from brands on their FYP, without leaving TikTok.
Developed in partnership with commerce and payment providers including Shopify, Salesforce and Stripe, the features mark an expansion beyond TikTok Shop’s existing marketplace model, more deeply embedding AI-assisted shopping and direct brand purchases into the social platform. Its aim is to create a more seamless journey from inspiration through discovery, to purchase.
The launch comes against a wider shift towards AI-powered, end-to-end shopping experiences, alongside Meta’s Muse AI agent, Pinterest’s growing shopping investment, and ChatGPT’s new virtual try-on capabilities. For brands, the challenge becomes how to surface in these AI products, where checkout is handled outside their own channels.
Oct 6, 2026
The news: Trump orders the US government to stop calling AI “AI”.
Why it matters: President Donald Trump has signed an executive order instructing the US government to replace “artificial intelligence” and “AI” with “super intelligence” and “SI” across all official communications and policy documents, arguing that the new terminology better reflects the tech’s rapidly advancing capabilities and economic potential. The language notably echoes Mark Zuckerberg’s vision of “personal super intelligence”, which he made central to Meta’s AI strategy last year.
The rebrand comes amid a much bigger debate over who should be responsible for keeping increasingly powerful AI systems in check. Ahead of signing the executive order, Trump convened some of the industry’s most powerful figures — including Meta’s Zuckerberg, xAI’s Elon Musk, Google’s Sundar Pichai, Nvidia’s Jensen Huang, OpenAI’s Greg Brockman, and Anthropic’s Dario Amodei — to sign a voluntary AI safety accord.
The accord puts the onus on companies to develop internal safeguards, subject their systems to independent audits and provide board-level oversight, in line with the Trump administration’s preferred “self-policing” versus a coordinated slowdown. Amodei has been among the most vocal advocates for slowing down, while OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Microsoft and xAI have backed calls for greater restraint.
The bosses at Anthropic, Google, xAI, Meta, OpenAI, and Nvidia all signed Trump’s voluntary accord.
The news: Oura delays IPO, citing market uncertainty.
Why it matters: Smart ring maker Oura has postponed its hotly anticipated IPO, citing “uncertainty in the IPO market” despite strong demand for its wearables offering. The company had been targeting a valuation of as much as $14 billion, and expected to raise as much as $2.2 billion in the public listing.
The timing points to a broader AI-driven shake-up in public markets. Investors are increasingly scrutinizing the vast cash being poured into AI infrastructure and lofty tech valuations, even as prospective mega-listings from AI companies such as Anthropic and OpenAI threaten to command an outsized share of investor attention and capital. Oura’s delay offers an early glimpse of the potential divide between the tech companies attracting extraordinary sums and those competing for what’s left.
Plus, as of last week, OpenAI announced that it won’t be joining the IPO rush this year. CEO Sam Altman has said going public now would be ill-advised given the safety work still ahead.
Sept 29, 2026
The news: Meta unveils kawaii-coded wearable pendant to house its Muse AI assistant.
Why it matters: Just when we thought Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg had finished announcing his slew of smart glasses and AI updates during the annual connect conference, there was “just one more thing”: another wearable device designed to house its new Muse AI agent, the Muse Charm. It’s a tamagotchi-esque, palm-sized totem on a looped cord designed to attach to keychains or fit into the wearer’s pocket.
The Muse Charm has a small screen to display the user’s selected Muse AI avatar. It’s unlocked via fingerprint and can be spoken into for instructing their agent to carry out tasks. Zuckerberg is still “finalizing” the components, but promised that the device will be ready to ship before the holidays.
The Muse Charm is Meta’s latest attempt at a new form factor designed to get its AI agent into the hands of as many consumers as possible. “We packed the whole muse experience, including the real-time voice and avatar stack into something that fits on a key chain and is always available to talk to,” Zuckerberg said. “So if you’re not wearing glasses, this is going to be by far the fastest way to talk to your Muse and show [it] what’s going on around you.”
It feeds into Zuckerberg’s promise of “personal superintelligence”, and may offer itself as a successor to the smartphone.
The news: OpenAI pauses training of new AI models, following further AI agent hacks.
Why it matters: ChatGPT maker OpenAI has paused training of its new AI models, just hours after disclosing that its AI agents had gone “rogue”. Over the summer, there were reportedly dozens of incidents where OpenAI models unexpectedly breached their system guardrails and compromised organizations’ security controls, including the US government’s.
In July, OpenAI revealed its autonomous AI agents, developed during safety trials, escaped their testing sandbox, accessed the internet, and independently hacked the startup Hugging Face back in July — the first example of an major AI-driven attack that moved faster than the humans trying to contain it.
“We have not been as fast as we would have liked, but we are trying to balance our desire for transparency with gaining a clear understanding from petabytes [1 million gigabytes] of agent activity logs [digital diaries of what the agents do], and working with impacted organizations,” CEO Sam Altman said in a post on X. “Hugging Face is still the most severe event we’ve seen.” He added that OpenAI will “be as transparent as can be”, while hinting that OpenAI’s agents had also discovered vulnerabilities in “other companies” like rivals, which he said would “be their call to disclose or not”.
OpenAI said it will resume training of its most capable AI models only when “confident that we have additional safeguards and alignment improvements in place, which we are working on now,” a spokesperson told Vogue Business. “People want to know AI is being developed safely, and that starts with what companies like ours do. This is not the first time we have hit pause to take such measures, nor do we expect it to be the last as capabilities continue to advance.”
Sept 22, 2026
The news: OpenAI and Anthropic staff question how to put AI slowdown pledges into practice.
Why it matters: Employees at OpenAI and Anthropic are questioning how their leaders’ recent calls to slow the development of increasingly powerful AI models will work in practice. According to the Financial Times, staff were blindsided by the proposals, which include bringing independent evaluators inside the AI labs to scrutinize models and safety practices, leaving them to grapple with questions around how much access outsiders will receive and IP protection.
The uncertainty highlights the gap between the growing consensus that safety measures need to keep pace with rapidly advancing AI models, as well as the practicalities of implementing those commitments. It also raises questions over whether last week’s unusual show of agreement between AI leaders will translate into meaningful change around model development and release.
Sept 15, 2026
The news: AI’s biggest rivals unite behind calls to slow development.
Why it matters: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei is calling on the AI industry to slow the rate at which its most powerful models become more capable, warning that safety measures are struggling to keep pace. His proposals include embedding independent evaluators inside AI labs, greater coordination on safety standards, and international cooperation. Anthropic has committed to bringing independent evaluators inside the company, giving them employee-like access to scrutinize the safety of its models.
What’s more, some of Amodei’s biggest rivals are backing him. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said the tech company would also introduce independent evaluators, while xAI’s Elon Musk endorsed Amodei’s call and Google DeepMind’s Demis Hassabis said the proposal was “heading in the right direction”. The rare consensus comes after a series of AI safety incidents and high-profile researcher resignations intensified concerns over increasingly autonomous systems.
What’s yet unclear is whether the industry’s newfound agreement will translate into a meaningful slowdown, or how the biggest AI companies reconcile it with the fierce commercial race to build more powerful models.
The news: Estée Lauder Companies partners with AI startup to boost brands in search.
Why it matters: Estée Lauder Companies (ELC) is partnering with AI marketing platform Profound to track and improve how its portfolio of beauty brands appears across generative AI platforms like ChatGPT and Gemini. It comes as more fashion and beauty groups invest in recalibrating their marketing strategies to prioritize generative engine optimization (GEO); ELC said the strategic partnership marks a move to “operationalize GEO and AI marketing at scale”.
Profound’s software will show ELC when — and how — its brands surface or become recommended by AI assistants, and identify opportunities to improve their visibility and accuracy. The beauty conglomerate will then use Profound’s agentic tools to optimize product pages, blogs, social media, and other components so that information is easier for LLMs to comprehend and pick up.
“The way people discover beauty is being rewritten, and we intend to shape that shift rather than react to it,” said ELC global chief digital and marketing officer Aude Gandon, adding that the sector is undergoing an “industry-wide transition”, as consumers increasingly use AI for product discovery and recommendations.
The news: Daydream adds Siri and camera roll search via Apple’s new iOS 27.
Why it matters: AI shopping platform Daydream is integrating deeper into the iPhone with the launch of two features built on Apple’s new iOS 27 tools. Users can now find shoppable matches for clothing in photos saved to their camera rolls, or ask Siri to search Daydream’s catalog using conversational prompts without opening the app. Daydream is one of the early co-developers of tools built with Apple’s latest on-device AI foundation models, using its new image-context capabilities.
