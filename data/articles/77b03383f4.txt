The incoming Democratic chairs of key committees have taken a new step toward investigating the most-favored nation drug pricing agreements between drugmakers and the Trump administration, STAT reports. The drug pricing deals are expected to be a top health care oversight priority for Democrats once they take the majority in January. Earlier this year, the top Democrats on the Senate Finance Committee and the House Energy and Commerce, Ways and Means, and Education and Labor committees requested that companies disclose the details of the deals they struck to tie Medicaid drug prices to levels in peer countries.
The Trump administration last year came up with a plan to combat high drug prices, making European governments pay more, and the result so far is that drug prices in the U.S. remain high and there are fewer new drugs in Europe, The Wall Street Journal says. There has been a 27% drop in new drug releases across European countries over the last 17 months, compared with the same period before President Trump announced the plan, according to new data from market-research firm GlobalData. Meanwhile, drug approvals in Europe have held steady over that time period.
