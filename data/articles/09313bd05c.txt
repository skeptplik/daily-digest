The tide of history cannot be stopped, Chinese President Xi Jinping said on Thursday (November 12, 2026), as he urged complete "reunification" of Taiwan with the motherland and pledged to do so peacefully, making no mention of the use of force.
China views democratically governed Taiwan as its own territory, despite Taipei's strong rejection of that claim, and has repeatedly offered a "one country, two systems" model of autonomy not supported by any major Taiwanese political party.
Resolving the Taiwan question is an "unwavering historical mission" of China's Communist Party, Mr. Xi said in a speech in Beijing's Great Hall of the People.
"The tide of history cannot be stopped," Mr. Xi added. "The complete reunification of the motherland must be realised, and it will certainly be realised."
Mr. Xi's comments came in a speech on the 160th birthday of revolutionary hero Sun Yat-sen, who helped lead a 1911 revolt that ended centuries of rule by emperors and empresses and ushered in the Republic of China.
Mr. Xi said China adheres to "peaceful reunification and the policy of 'one country, two systems'" and is willing to talk to Taiwan while upholding the "one-China principle", or Beijing's position that both sides of the Taiwan Strait are one country.
All Chinese, including those in Taiwan, should join hands to jointly oppose Taiwan independence "separatist" forces and strive for peaceful development of cross-Strait ties, Mr. Xi added.
Taiwan's government did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
Despite Mr. Xi not mentioning the use of force, which China has never renounced for Taiwan, its military operates around the island daily, and is extending its reach further into the Pacific.
Taiwan tells people to stay away
Mr. Xi's speech comes a week ahead of the APEC summit in China's southern tech hub of Shenzhen, where he is due to meet U.S. President Donald Trump again, and also a little more than two weeks before important local elections in Taiwan.
Last month, Taiwan called on its people to steer clear of any of China's events to mark the Sun anniversary, saying China had a distorted view of him and his legacy.
Beijing calls Mr. Sun a great national hero and patriot, as well as the forerunner of the 1949 communist revolution that forced the defeated Republic of China government to flee to Taiwan, where it remains to this day.
Mr. Xi said Mr. Sun was a great national hero, a great patriot and a "great pioneer of China's democratic revolution".
"Mr. Sun Yat-sen consistently upheld the unity of the country and the solidarity of the nation, and he firmly opposed all words and actions that split the country or the nation," he added.
‘Father of the nation’
In Taiwan, Mr. Sun is officially considered the "father of the nation", and his portrait hangs in the presidential office, though his political philosophy, "Three Principles of the People", is no longer a required text for schoolchildren.
While Taiwan President Lai Ching-te and his ruling Democratic Progressive Party champion Taiwan's separate identity from China, they have been striking a tougher tone with Beijing on the role of the Republic of China.
Taiwan has been particularly upset by Beijing's effort to claim credit for the victory in World War Two, saying it was the Republic of China government that fought alongside the Allies, as the People's Republic of China did not exist at the time.
China says Mr. Lai is a "separatist" and has refused to speak with him. Mr. Lai says only Taiwan's people can decide their future.
