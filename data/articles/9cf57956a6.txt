A record number of children were living in a conflict zone in 2025, Save the Children findings revealed on Thursday.
In the latest report in the "Stop the War on Children" series, the humantarian group said 589 million children worldwide lived in conflict zones. That's the highest number since records began in 1990, and more than double the total in the mid-1990s.
"For millions of children, fear, violence and displacement are part of every day life," said Save the Children Germany CEO Florian Westphal.
He said this needed to be a "wake-up call" for the international community.
Where children are living in conflict zones
Around two in five children in both Africa and the Middle East were living in conflict zones in 2025, the report found. Africa, alone, accounted for 44% of all children living in conflict zones worldwide.
In terms of regional proportions, 40.2% of children in the Middle East were living in conflict zones in 2025. That represented roughly two in five children across the region, and a particularly steep rise in the number of children affected. The figure jumped by nearly 29% from 52 million in 2024 to 67 million last year.
Children's exposure to conflict in Africa has more than tripled over the past 15 years, with the share of children living in conflict zones rising from 12% in 2010 to 39% in 2025.
Grave violations against children in armed conflict zones increase
The report highlighted the dramatic increase in the denial of humanitarian access.
A total of 8,322 verified incidents of denying access to humanitarian aid to children were recorded in 2025, a 16-fold rise over the past decade. The figure was 509 in 2015, and only eight incidents were reported when the United Nations (UN) established the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) in 2005.
The Palestinian Territories accounted for the vast majority of cases, with 5,946 incidents, or more than 70% of all verified cases globally.
"No grave violation has increased as dramatically or changed the overall pattern of verified violations as much as the denial of humanitarian access," the study noted.
According to the report, denial of humanitarian access rose from around 2% of all verified grave violations against children in 2015 to more than 21% in 2025, becoming the second-most frequently recorded violation after the killing and maiming of children.
Grave violations as laid out in the MRM also include the recruitment of children by armed forces, sexual violence, abduction and attacks on schools and hospitals.
Where violations against children were concentrated
The study found that, overall, 38,967 grave violations against children were verified by the UN.
This was the second-highest annual total since the MRM was established, surpassed only by the record 41,763 violations documented in 2024.
More than half of these took place in just three areas: the Palestinian Territories, the Democratic Republic of Congo and Nigeria. The Palestinian Territories alone accounted for 12,449 verified violations, nearly one-third of all cases.
As well as the denial of humanitarian access, violations in 2025 included:
- 14,224 children killed or maimed in conflicts in 2025
- A record 5,128 child abductions
- 1,787 cases of sexual violence, a 71% increase in the past decade
- A fifth straight year of increased attacks on schools and hospitals, at 2,930 cases
The Palestinian Territories recorded the highest number of attacks on schools or hospitals, with 837 incidents, followed by Ukraine with 643. Together, the two accounted for more than half of all reported cases worldwide.
The sharpest rises in sexual violence incidents were recorded in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), where cases jumped from 358 in 2024 to 592 in 2025, and Sudan, which saw an increase from 74 to 193. Around 86% of all cases were documented in African conflict zones.
"Children have the right to live free from violence. Changing the odds requires political choices that put children’s safety first, and a sustained commitment to stop the war on children," the NGO said.
