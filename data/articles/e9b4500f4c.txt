This winter, fashion’s most adventurous heads are getting a little more imaginative with their cold-weather accessories—and Hunter Schafer has made her statement headgear a part of her excellent press tour fashions.
The actor, currently promoting her forthcoming sci-fi series Blade Runner 2099, has been having plenty of fun with the futuristic, otherworldly aesthetic of the franchise. For a recent press junket, she and stylist Dara dipped into the archives for a look from Undercover’s fall 2007 collection: a ribbed, patchwork knit with tiny cable-knit shorts, gray thigh-high socks, and a pair of fuzzy blonde-brown earmuffs that matched her hair.
Back in 2007, Undercover’s Jun Takahashi was thinking about the unpredictability of the climate crisis, and how to dress for a world of increasingly extreme and erratic weather. “You can’t tell whether a winter’s going to be warm or freezing,” he told Vogue’s Sarah Mower at the time. His answer was NASA-derived technology incorporated into temperature-responsive coat linings and waterproofing techniques applied to knitwear and wool. The collection was full of deceptively practical innovations hidden within the designs, from abbreviated shorts worn over woolly tights to elaborate knitwear and those off-beat earmuffs. Nearly two decades later, the concept feels even more prescient. And for Schafer and Allen, currently exploring the dystopian, environmentally ravaged world of Blade Runner, it adds to their expansive sartorial storytelling.
It was Hunter’s hair that really compounded the look. Hairstylist Rena Calhoun brushed her long, honey-blonde lengths back from her face and divided them into two low ponytails, secured just beneath the oversized, circular earmuffs. The warm brown faux-fur effect blended into her hair and the earthy tones of her knitwear, giving the impression of two enormous, furry ears growing out from either side of her head. Makeup artist Sandy Ganzer kept the beauty minimal, with softly defined eyes, rosy cheeks, and a muted pink lip.
Already, Schafer and her team have cultivated a trail of cyberpunk glam looks. Earlier this week at the Los Angeles premiere, she paid tribute to Daryl Hannah’s Pris in Ridley Scott’s original sci-fi classic with a dramatic, mask-like and metallic strip of eye shadow.
Schafer is already in excellent company when it comes to loving the earmuff. Margaret Qualley stared in Chanel’s new holiday beauty campaign sporting an oversized black-and-white fluffy pair shaped like cats. With her glossy pink lips and pink rosy cheeks, it was giving après-ski ingénue. Ariana Grande, meanwhile, is something of an authority on adorable head accessories. The singer has long favored an Alice band, but cutesy fuzzy earmuffs of all colors and proportions dominate her wardrobe in colder weather months.
For a cooler interpretation, I think back to a pap shot of Chloë Sevigny in 2018, ahead of the curve when she wore a quilted, shearling-lined Chanel trapper hat with large logo-covered ear flaps for a downtown take on the look.
It all feels like a natural progression from the hair kerchiefs that dominated the summer, and 2026 has been a particularly good year for those willing to experiment with their headwear. Dua Lipa made the case for jaunty chapeaux on the red carpet courtesy of Chanel, while Elle Fanning’s eccentric, Effie-coded Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping press tour has already delivered a miniature ballerina perched atop a pink Robert Wun pillbox hat, alongside a red satin creation by British milliner Rachel Trevor-Morgan. And then there are Taylor Russell’s fuzzy hat collaboration Ilana Blumberg, and John Galliano’s recently coveted baker boy-esque hats for Zara.
Vintage earmuffs are well worth hunting down, with some particularly fab Chanel and Max Mara versions circulating on resale platforms such as Vestiaire Collective. For something new, look to luxury skiwear label Goldbergh for fluffy ear warmers in playful colors, cozy footwear favorite Ugg for its sheepskin iterations, or fuzz specialists Yves Salomon for sumptuous shearling versions.
Whether you prefer Hunter Schafer’s sci-fi cool style or Qualley’s sweet take, opting for the off-beat head accessory doesn’t have to mean compromising on keeping toasty. And for the right vintage Chanel earmuffs, I might just reach for the hair dye…This is how a fashionista avoids frostbite!
