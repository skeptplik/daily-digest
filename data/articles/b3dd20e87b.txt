In short:
Wallabies coach Les Kiss has acknowledged recent losses are taking a toll on the team in the Nations Championship.
A 15–12 defeat to Scotland put Australia fourth among Southern Hemisphere countries in the tournament.
What's next?
The Wallabies will face Wales in Cardiff starting at 7:10am AEDT Sunday morning.
Coach Les Kiss is determined to ensure Australia finishes among the top three Southern Hemisphere teams in the Nations Championship after a rare weekend blowout for the Wallabies, All Blacks and Springboks.
For only the second time in rugby history, all of the "big three" were turned over on the same weekend by Northern Hemisphere opposition as South Africa lost 21-19 in France, New Zealand were sensationally beaten 35-28 in Wales and Australia lost narrowly 15-12 to Scotland at Murrayfield.
That had only previously occurred in July 2022, when all three suffered home defeats: New Zealand losing 23-12 to Ireland in Dunedin, South Africa going down 13-12 to Wales in Bloemfontein and Australia falling 25-17 to England in Brisbane.
Now, Australia has fallen to fourth in the Southern Hemisphere table of the Nations Championship, leapfrogged by Argentina, and should the Wallabies succumb to a fifth-straight Test defeat against Wales on Saturday, it will finish in that position.
Should Argentina upset France, even a Wallabies win would not be enough to claw them back to that third spot.
But Kiss made it clear this would represent failure for a team that had come on its British sojourn seeking a top-three finish.
"We were aiming for the top-three when we came here, New Zealand and South Africa already being up there and going to be hard to reel in," Kiss said.
"So we were aiming for the top-three for sure, and that's what we'll measure ourselves on."
It is still only halfway through the tour, but the grim scenario of a second-straight European wipe-out looms following defeats in England, Italy, Ireland and France last year, the first time in 67 years that such a whitewash had occurred.
Asked if the Wallabies had lost their confidence, Kiss mused: "Yeah, you do want a team with a bit of swagger. There's been losses throughout the last few years that have seemed to hang on, that's for sure.
"I wouldn't say it's confidence, I wouldn't say it's just belief. I think it's a mixture of those things coming together and it's just down to them believing that they can now win those big moments."
He referred to that last-second burst down the right touchline on Sunday, when Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii, Max Jorgensen and Tom Wright could not quite turn the break into a match-winning moment.
"The losses obviously takes its toll but, mate, they're hardy and they're going to continue to work hard," Kiss said.
It all gives Saturday's Test against a Welsh side currently residing on cloud nine even more of a "must-win" feel.
Captain Harry Wilson counters: "Every Test match is must-win. We're going to go into the Wales game with the same attitude we do every time. We need a win. We're going to do everything in our power to make sure we can."
But Kiss admitted the incredible Welsh win over the All Blacks, their first in 73 years, had "changed everyone's minds" about Steve Tandy's reinvented team.
"Honestly, it was compelling. It was phenomenal what they did. So we're going to need to be good to go there on a short turnaround."
But could Australia be facing a side with energy levels zapped from their legs after throwing themselves into a record-breaking 346 tackles against New Zealand? Kiss just smiled: "Nah, they'll be energised. That's inspiring what they did, isn't it?
"We've just got to make sure we've got our reason to be up for it too, which is that it's a bloody important Test match to win."
