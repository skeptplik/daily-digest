‘Grand Theft Auto VI’ hacker sentenced to seven years, released the same day
A British cybercriminal who hacked Rockstar Games and leaked footage of Grand Theft Auto VI was sentenced Monday to seven years in prison, and then released on license because of time served.
Arion Kurtaj, now 21, had previously been deemed unfit to stand trial by psychiatrists due to his severe autism.
In September 2022, while 17, Kurtaj broke into the systems of ride-hailing business Uber, fintech firm Revolut, and the developer of Grand Theft Auto in a spate of hacks committed while on bail for earlier offenses.
Kurtaj had been placed in a hotel for his own protection after being threatened by rival hackers. Although police had confiscated his computer, he managed to hack the companies using an Amazon Fire Stick plugged into the hotel room’s television.
He pleaded guilty last week at Woolwich Crown Court in London to six offenses, four under the Computer Misuse Act, one of fraud and one of possessing a mobile phone in prison.
Seven other charges against Kurtaj will “lie on file,” meaning prosecutors will not pursue them without a court’s permission. The charges do not count as convictions.
The judge initially set a sentence of nine years, reducing it by two years to account for the time Kurtaj had spent detained in a psychiatric hospital.
Kurtaj will serve the remainder of his sentence under supervision in the community and can be returned to prison if he breaches the conditions of his release.
“You are obviously a very talented young man,” Judge Mark Turner told him. “Don't waste those talents by spending the rest of your life in and out of prison.”
Police said they would apply for a Serious Crime Prevention Order, which allows courts to restrict a convicted offender’s activities to prevent further crimes. BBC News reported that a five-year order had already been imposed, prohibiting Kurtaj from using the messaging apps Telegram and Discord.
“Kurtaj knew what he was doing when he carried out these crimes and has finally admitted his involvement,” said Detective Inspector Mike O'Sullivan of the City of London Police.
Grand Theft Auto VI was released last Thursday.
