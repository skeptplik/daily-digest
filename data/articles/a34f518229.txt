ZucZug, the Shanghai fashion label built on “su,” the Chinese ideal of simplicity, has opened Agora, or “Datian,” a working farm on the city’s outskirts that pairs permaculture with hospitality, community and garment research.
One of China’s first B Corp-certified fashion companies, the 22-year-old ZucZug has grown to six spin-off brands, all shaped by its founder Wang Yiyang’s devotion to the natural world and, in a distinctly Socratic way, to the examined life.
Life at Agora revolves around 20 acres of farmland and harvest cycles that began with newly harvested rice in October. As leaves turn golden, visitors and artists-in-residence can stay in eight tatami rooms inside a renovated village house, cook together in the communal kitchen, and share quiet moments and camaraderie with local villagers in the tea house. Evenings continue at Nine Agora, the on-site brewery.
This harvest season, ZucZug devotees can look forward to the return of Che-mi-bo, the artisan bakery workshop, and the debut of a fermentation library. Local makers will be invited to a traditional town-hall meeting to help potentially rename the project “Agora Bay” and chart its future beginning in late November.
