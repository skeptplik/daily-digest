Nvidia board rules that co-founder Jensen Huang will not return as CEO
Nvidia's board chairman ruled out bringing back co-founder Jensen Huang as CEO on Tuesday, as the chipmaker forecast stronger-than-expected quarterly sales but faced growing questions over its leadership, national security exposure and long-term strategy following his abrupt departure.
Nvidia Board Chairman Harvey Jones opened a conference call with investors on Tuesday by saying the AI chip company would not rehire co-founder Jensen Huang as CEO and would not take questions on the matter.
Jones' start to a call about quarterly results was a forceful response to a movement to bring back Huang, who stepped down last week. With Huang gone, investors are eager for clues about Nvidia's future and strategy, from its commitments of billions of dollars to cloud providers, suppliers and AI startups to who will ultimately lead the world's most valuable chipmaker.
Shares of the company rose 2% in extended trading, after Nvidia forecast sales ahead of Wall Street targets.
"It's no surprise that they're doing very well, and there'll be people who will breathe a sigh of relief about it," said Rick Meckler, partner at Cherry Lane Investments. "But the story here is very complicated, and it's hard not to factor that in as an additional risk for people buying stock."
Jones said the board had hired executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to help find a new CEO.

Interim CEO Colette Kress, who is also finance chief, kept her focus on financials and products in the statement accompanying results. "Our momentum is real," she said, without addressing the leadership issue. "Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track," she added, referring to the company's latest flagship AI chip.
Huang, the AI boom's most prolific salesman, resigned on Thursday after revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang and two with Nvidia employees.
US intelligence agencies are trying to determine the extent of Alice Huang's ties to China's Ministry of State Security and what information she may have passed to Beijing, according to two people familiar with the matter.
Jensen Huang's exit ended three decades of founder-led control over Nvidia's chip roadmap, customer deals and Washington ties, making execution a bigger risk than near-term AI demand, analysts say.

Fourth-quarter outlook tops targets
The company expects fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, above analysts' average estimate of $125.25 billion, according to LSEG data. Nvidia said the outlook assumes no data-centre chip sales to China.
Adjusted gross margin is expected at 71% to 72%, compared with analysts' estimate of 71.7%.
Anshel Sag, principal analyst at Moor Insights & Strategy, said Nvidia's outlook was "quite positive, albeit not as ambitious as in previous quarters, which many have already anticipated." He added, "I don't think anything near-term will be affected by Jensen's departure. His executive leadership team is capable and has followed his leadership and executed effectively for years, reaching this point."
The board is considering internal and external candidates for CEO, with Kress seen offering steady leadership during the selection process. But a growing "Stand With Jensen" campaign has complicated the search, and Huang said on Tuesday he would be "honoured" to return if the board deemed it best.
National security scrutiny intensifies
Earlier on Tuesday, Nvidia's board created a committee on national security and export controls, its first major governance move since Huang's departure, as scrutiny grows over the chipmaker's national security role.
Washington has also tightened licensing rules for chip shipments to Southeast Asia and overseas data centres serving Chinese firms, while lawmakers prepare to question Huang, who had pushed for greater chip exports to China.
Investors were already uneasy about AI spending after the Democrats' sweep of the US midterm elections fuelled fears of tougher scrutiny and slower data-centre expansion. They also want to know whether Nvidia's next CEO will continue financing customers, a practice critics call circular.
Tuesday's results offer a first read on Vera Rubin, Nvidia's newest generation of AI processors and successor to Grace Blackwell. Nvidia said in August that Vera Rubin would account for about a fifth of data-centre revenue in the October quarter and forecast 70% revenue growth in the next fiscal year.
Nvidia's third-quarter revenue more than doubled to $115.84 billion, beating analysts' estimate of $109.11 billion. The company has topped Wall Street's revenue expectations for 17 straight quarters.
Adjusted gross margin rose to 74.9% from 73.5% a year earlier, also beating analysts' estimate of 74.1%, despite soaring memory prices and production ramps.
