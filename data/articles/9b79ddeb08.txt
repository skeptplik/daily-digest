For the first time in more than a year, the Food and Drug Administration has sent notices to companies for failing to report required clinical trial information.
The agency sends such notices when a trial sponsor fails to comply with a federal law that requires studies to be registered on ClinicalTrials.gov, a federal government database, within 21 days after the first human subject is enrolled. Summary results must also be posted to the database within 12 months after the trial’s primary completion date.
The FDA, however, has rarely sent noncompliance letters, despite years of criticism over lax enforcement. In recent years, calls for greater transparency have sparked a raft of studies showing a failure by drug companies, universities, and academic medical centers to comply with disclosure requirements.
