Chinese President Xi Jinping on Wednesday hailed relations with Russia “as a source of stability” in a “turbulent” world, even as he welcomed U.S. President Donald Trump with a rare airport reception a day ahead of a trilateral summit in Shenzhen.
On Thursday (November 19, 2026), leaders of the three countries will hold a summit, which Beijing has described as having “groundbreaking significance”. U.S. officials said the three leaders will likely discuss, among other issues, the war in Ukraine, the situation in Iran, global energy stability, and nuclear arms control.
Officials have described the rare trilateral leaders’ summit as more of an exploratory and symbolic meeting, playing down the likelihood of substantive outcomes, either with regard to a possible Russia-Ukraine deal or on the West Asian crisis. Whether they can see eye-to-eye on nuclear disarmament also remains to be seen.
At the same time, Beijing has highlighted the symbolism of the summit happening on its soil, describing it as a landmark moment for what China calls “major country diplomacy”.
In talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Wednesday (November 18, 2026), Mr. Xi described China-Russia relations “as a source of stability and certainty in a turbulent world and as a model of a new type of major-power relationship characterised by equality, mutual respect, trust, and win-win cooperation.”
The U.S. President landed in Shenzhen on Wednesday (November 18, 2026), and Mr. Xi welcomed him at the airport with a hug — a rare greeting from the Chinese President, who was reciprocating Mr. Trump similarly receiving him in September at Joint Base Andrews outside Washington.
The Chinese President opened the two-day APEC gathering with an address to a CEO Summit, where he made a pitch for openness and stable supply chains.
“The world is facing mounting instability, insufficient momentum for economic growth, and a resurgence of unilateralism and protectionism. At the same time, global trade is still growing despite headwinds and emerging technologies are advancing rapidly, reshaping global development,” he said, adding that “the key questions of the age were whether the world would choose openness or closure, cooperation or division, reform or stagnation.”
Mr. Xi called for barriers to trade and investment to be reduced and proposed establishing a World Bay Area Economic Cooperation Alliance. Beijing has been developing the Greater Bay Area, including Shenzhen, Hong Kong and Macau, as an innovation hub.
His remarks came amid continuing China-U.S. trade tensions over a number of issues, including semiconductors and rare earths. Both sides have so far only agreed to extend their trade truce until January, while they continue working on other unresolved issues.
On Tuesday (November 17, 2026), Beijing accused Washington of “abusing export control measures” after the announcement of new rules aimed at curbing Chinese firms’ remote access to advanced AI chips through data centres in third party countries. This followed renewed scrutiny over chipmaker Nvidia and its relationships with companies in the region, including those with links to China.
