The London maker of Milan design week’s much-discussed inflatable coffee table has unveiled his debut furniture collection, which includes veiny wax chairs and a rug adorned with cattle tags.
Jabez Bartlett is a British production-turned-furniture designer who caused a stir during April’s Milan design week, where he showed a pillowy PVC coffee table topped with an opalescent resin surface.
A circular version followed, and both pieces are part of The Freight Proportioned to the Groove, an exhibition of his debut furniture collection that opens today in Hackney, London.
Bartlett told Dezeen that he wanted to start creating furniture to experiment with materials that he has used to bring film sets to life on screen.
“With set design, things don’t necessarily have to be practical,” said the Devon-born designer. “It’s all about how they look on camera. I wanted to play with this in a more material and physical world.”
“There’s a lot of industrial materials we’ve always used that I think are incredibly beautiful,” he continued. “It’s interesting to use something heavy and practical in a luxury piece.”
Bartlett’s collection includes a triptych of marbled dining chairs made from a mixture of microcrystalline and crude oil wax cast in plywood frames.
The pieces were informed by a Japanese games table owned by the designer, characterised by a gridded top.
“I had the idea to remake it as a giant candle, with a wick in each square,” he explained. The idea became three chunky chairs that resemble large candles, embellished with playful wicks.
Bartlett’s collection also features a trio of lighting pieces clad in stretchy latex skins.
The smallest is a ceramic table lamp with a bloated shade, while the other two are angular floor lamps supported by metal structures, one of which was on display at the southwest London gallery Saatchi Yates earlier this year.
Despite their industrial materials, Bartlett also considers his pieces as nods to the wilderness.
“I started off wanting to make brutalist-folk furniture, and now I’m seeing it, I feel like I got there,” he said. “Memory and illusion play a big part, and of course, growing up in Devon leaves an imprint of the natural and the ancient world on you.”
Another of the collection’s pieces is a large diamond-patterned textile rug, overdyed and bleached, and embellished with metal tags normally used to identify cattle.
The tags hem the rug and were also embroidered into its fabric in decorative circles.
Also on display in the exhibition are a monolithic cast acrylic mirror, a glossy black plinth and column with protruding thorns and another floor lamp with a similarly spiky, elongated black body and a sandy leather shade.
The title of Bartlett’s exhibition refers to the final line of poet Emily Dickinson’s 19th-century poem, That Love is All There Is.
The Freight Proportioned to the Groove is on display from 26 to 29 November at Tuscany Wharf, 4B Orsman Road, London N1 5QJ. See Dezeen Events Guide for an up-to-date list of architecture and design events taking place around the world.
