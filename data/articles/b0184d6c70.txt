England score seven tries to hammer sorry Wallabies 45-22 at Twickenham
The Wallabies have been handed another brutal reality check, subject to a seven-try hammering by England at Twickenham.
England started fast and never let up, subduing a woefully off colour Wallabies team 45-22, their biggest ever total against the Wallabies, to retain the Ella-Mobbs trophy.
"England put us under the pump," Harry Wilson said after the match.
"We weren't physical enough, we didn't slow their ball down, we were just playing from behind."
England have now beaten the Wallabies in 12 of their last 14 meetings.
The defeat is the Wallabies' third in a row, and means they are mathematically eliminated from final contention in the Nations Championship.
England thought they had scored inside the opening two minutes, cutting through a painfully porous Wallabies defence only for the TMO to rule it out for a clear forward pass.
That didn't hold them up for long though, as Jamie George powered over from a rolling maul to open the scoring.
Ryan Lonergan kicked a penalty to keep Australia in touch, but the Wallabies were stung twice in the 15 minutes before half time, Ollie Chessum finishing a move on the left and Joe Heyes powering over from another catch and drive.
The Wallabies by that stage had been reduced to 14 men, when Angus Bell was sin binned for a hip drop tackle on the lively Immanuel Feyi-Waboso.
But against the run of play, the Wallabies managed to score before the break, Max Jorgensen with an intercept score that reduced the gap to just nine.
The more optimistic Wallabies supporters may have felt that slice of fortune could have been the turning point.
How wrong they were.
Jack van Poortvliet scythed through to score just two minutes after the restart, before Henry Pollock, the firebrand flanker, scored two quick tries after coming on as a winger.
Some quick hands from the Wallabies saw replacement flyhalf Ben Donaldson assist in sending Filipo Daugunu over in a rare passage of pleasing play from the Wallabies.
Then, Jeremy Williams dived on a Maro Itoje drop to further reduce the deficit.
But as the Wallabies pushed for a bonus point try, Ben Spencer intercepted a Daugunu offload to race away and seal a spectacular seven-try performance from England.
The Wallabies head to Murrayfield next week, searching for answers from another desperate performance.
"We've got seven days to just take some feedback from this game and make sure we're a lot better," Wilson said.
"Scotland are a really good team, we've got to be ready for it and take these harsh lessons."
