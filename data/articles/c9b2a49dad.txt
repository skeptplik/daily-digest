France has issued an arrest warrant for a German businessman as part of an investigation regarding the migrant deaths in the English Channel.
Joint investigation by several media outlets including French daily Le Monde and the BBC showed that Marvin R.* of MaRe Boote, managing director of a small firm in western German town Werne, is suspected of being one of the main suppliers of inflatable boats smugglers use to journey across the Channel.
According to the media reports, the 35-year-old businessman is accused of supplying smuggling gangs with inflatable boats, engines and life jackets worth hundreds of thousands of euros.
UK authorities estimated that boats or engines linked to MaRe Boote were responsible for three in every 10 detected crossings during the first four months of this year, the BBC and Le Monde reported.
Arrested and released in Germany
The prosecutor in the northern French city of Lille, Samuel Finielz, confirmed late on Thursday that "five people had been arrested and a German national was briefly detained in his country."
According to Finielz, this is part of the investigation about Channel crossings and sinkings since May last year.
The five others are now held in pre-trial detention and were suspected of offenses including "assisting illegal entry and residence as part of an organized criminal group, involuntary manslaughter, involuntary injury and endangering the lives of others," Finielz told the media.
Lille authorities in May issued an arrest warrant against "the German national implicated in this case," he said, on charges of manslaughter and endangering the lives of others.
Marvin R. was arrested and released by German authorities and now required to appear before a French court before January, the prosecutor added.
The BBC wrote that Marvin R. is contesting extradition to France. He can not be formally charged until he is extradited to France, it noted.
How many migrants cross the English Channel?
Migrants frequently cross the English Channel from France to the United Kingdom in small rubber dinghy boats. It is a dangerous journey as the Channel is one of the world's busiest shipping lanes.
More than 41,000 people crossed the Channel in small boats last year and there have been many deaths.
*Editor's note: DW follows the German press code, which stresses the importance of protecting the privacy of suspects, victims and those who are associated with a criminal case, and urges us to refrain from revealing the full names.
