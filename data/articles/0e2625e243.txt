Jess Wilson claims historic victory for the Liberal party
Jess Wilson, Victoria’s first elected female premier and, at 36, one of the youngest premiers in Victorian and Australian history, is speaking to party faithful at Melbourne’s Park Hyatt, taking the stage to chants of “Jess we can”.
She entered the room to huge cheers and resounding applause, swamped by ecstatic supporters as she made her way to the stage, while the upbeat dance classic Sunchyme by the late British artist Dario G played loudly.
She says:
Thank you, my friends, and thank you, Victoria. I first want to address the people of Victoria. Thank you for the trust that you have placed in me and my team.
I know that for many of you this is the first time that you have ever voted Liberal. And for others it was the first time you have ever given us your preference. And I realise that you have lent us your vote. We don’t own it. And we will work every single day to demonstrate that you were right to place your trust in us.
I know that you have elected me to bring back hope to this state. And that is what I pledge to do, starting from tomorrow.
One Nation's Warren Pickering fails to win Pakenham – ABC
Henry Belot
The ABC says One Nation Victoria’s leader, Warren Pickering, has failed to win the seat of Pakenham.
Here’s the national broadcaster’s election analyst Casey Briggs:
One Nation is getting a strong primary, but they’re not going to make it into parliament in many, or most of those seats, because preferences are not going to be their friends.
Warren Pickering will not be in parliament, the member for Pakenham will be a Liberal.
Loyal Victorian Labor staffers are devastated by tonight’s result. Former senior staffers have unloaded on the party’s campaign and its strategy.
One Labor source says there is “genuine dismay that the Government’s many achievements, including the construction of 121 schools, were not featured more prominently in Labor’s campaign”.
Former staffers are also dismayed by how diabolically bad Labor’s election campaign has been, with crime and the Big Build Royal Commission featuring prominently, strongly reinforcing the Coalition’s messages.
The Premier has been portrayed as basically a decent bloke who inherited a mess. This overlooks that his communication throughout the campaign has been poor, he was a minister for around 10 years – including deputy premier – and is also a creature of the factions and backroom deals.
Another former Labor staffer said:
This is what happens when you forget that government is a privilege, not a right.

Friends, I have said many times that we can’t fix Victoria overnight. It will take years of hard work and responsible governing to get Victoria back to the place it used to be. A place where people felt hopeful and optimistic, where they felt safe and content.

Wilson: Liberal party’s ‘last rites were premature’
Jess Wilson, still addressing the party faithful, alludes to the fact that for a long time and until not very long ago, the Liberal Party’s prospects of winning back government in Victoria seemed out of the question.
She says:
A lot has been said about the future of the Liberal Party. Tonight, we showed that those last rites were premature.
We showed that when you put in the work, you listen to the Victorian people, and you put forward a plan that will deliver hope for this state, you can win, and we did win.
Wilson thanks ‘decent man’ Ben Carroll after outgoing premier called to concede
Jess Wilson says she received a call from outgoing premier Ben Carroll earlier this evening.
He graciously congratulated me and the Liberal and Nationals team. He has served Victoria at the highest levels – as a minister, as deputy premier and as premier. Taking on the leadership of the state in difficult circumstances. Ben is a decent man, and I wish his family well for what lies next.
But today, Victorians have drawn a line in the sand after 12 long years. Victorians have sent a clear message that they cannot allow the state to continue on the direction it has been going in under Labor. Victorians want a fresh start.
I’m incredibly proud of the positive plan we have put to the Victorian people at this election. And now we have the mandate that we need to get started.
Earlier this year I travelled the length and breadth of this great state. Everywhere I went people told me that they needed a fresh start. And as I stand here tonight, I am deeply grateful to have earned the trust of the Victorian people to deliver that fresh start.
I am very conscious of the great privilege pledge and the weight of responsibility that has been placed upon me and my entire team tonight. To those who placed their faith in the Liberal and National parties tonight, I will not let you down. We will not let you down.
To those who did not, rest assured, I will govern for all Victorians. I will govern for all Victorians and in the best interests of Victoria every day.

The Coalition’s primary vote is actually down compared with 2022, so how are they doing so well in winning seats?
The Liberal party’s primary vote is down by about 1 percentage point, but they’ve managed to secure enough seats to form government.
Meanwhile, Labor’s primary vote is down by 19 percentage points, and One Nation has gained 21 percentage points, albeit off a base of almost zero votes in 2022.
The Coalition has produced an incredibly efficient geographic distribution of votes, managing to stay just ahead of enough candidates to be the beneficiary of the preferential system.
Greens and Victorian Socialist voters prefer the Liberals over One Nation, and with Labor being knocked out quite early in the count, the Liberal party is mopping up preferences from the left of politics.
We think that there is a possibility that Bendigo West could end up as a competition between the Greens and One Nation, making this an interesting one to watch.
The reason is that after the Animal Justice party and Victorian Socialists are excluded in the fifth and sixth positions, that allows the Greens to leapfrog Labor. One Nation may benefit from Family First voter preferences, allowing them to get ahead of the Nationals.
However, it’s also possible that when early votes are counted, the Nationals stay ahead. All of this makes for a complicated race to call.
