At the Liberal event at the Park Hyatt, the volume has been turned up very loudly on the giant TV screen on the wall which is showing News 24 (formerly Sky). The screen showing the ABC is hidden behind the TV news camera operators, who are set up on a podium to film the stage where Jess Wilson is expected to speak later this evening.
More people are here now; the room is slowly starting to fill out. The hors d’oeuvres on offer include chicken rice paper rolls and tartlets of confit cherry tomato, black olive and straciatella, or smoked salmon, roe and chives.
There are a few children here wearing VIP lanyards. A small boy in a blue shirt ran joyfully across the room earlier, seemingly oblivious to the flashes of the newspaper photographers following him, hoping to get a cute photo.
One Nation’s Warren Pickering says results so far “heartwarming”
At the same time the ABC called the election for the Coalition, One Nation’s state leader, Waren Pickering, was speaking in Pakenham. He says the numbers so far have been “heartwarming” to see:
After all these weeks, months and years that have been put into developing this party here in Victoria … this truly warms my heart. Whatever the outcome tonight, One Nation Victoria is part of the permanent political landscape here in Victoria. And this is another step on the road to New South Wales and the orange wave that’s going to sweep federal [parliament].
He says he will be back to speak again later in the evening but “whatever this position in the parliament or the numbers are after tonight, the real work starts tomorrow”. Pickering continued:
So let’s watch with interest these numbers come in and let’s watch this state get taken back into the hands of the conservatives and – whatever the disposition of the parliament, let’s take this state back!
It’s still very early days, but sources in the Nationals concede it’s “looking rough for us” in the regional seat of Murray Plains. This is a seat One Nation has targeted.
While the number of votes are still very small, senior One Nation sources are feeling much more optimistic than they were earlier this evening.
Greens candidates pay tribute to late Brunswick MP
At the Greens’ event tonight, upper house candidate Anasina Gray-Barberio opened the proceedings with a dedication to the late MP for Brunswick, Dr Tim Read. She said:
His legacy to our democracy in getting rid of corrupt group voting tickets will be honoured this evening.
Greens leader, Ellen Sandell, said she is hopeful the Greens will retain their three seats and pick up at least Northcote and Preston:
It’s been really positive for the Greens, particularly in the last few weeks, when we’ve seen the polls polling very well for the Greens. That’s reflecting what we’ve been feeling on the ground with our volunteers for some time now.
Touching on One Nation and Victoria’s values, she said:
Victorians value our multiculturalism, they value our diversity. They value a fair go. And I think that One Nation stands for a monoculture, and they stand for giving special treatment to the big end of town, like Gina Rinehart.
I don’t think it will be an endorsement of the Liberals. And, in fact, if there is a conservative government, we need the Greens in there, more than ever before, to fight for our progressive values of caring for people and planet.
To keep the punters fed, the Greens are serving up Sri Lankan hoppers, a type of rice and coconut crepe often served with an egg. They’re serving it with a daal and chicken curry.

The Greens party is heating up at the Mission to Seafarers in Docklands. The room has filled up quickly since Ellen Sandell arrived.

Seats such as Eureka, Gippsland East, Gippsland South, and Mildura are all indicating early in the night – with the usual caveats that there is more data to come – that Labor is not the only party in a world of pain tonight.
In what might be a shock to the Australian party system, the Nationals are doing poorly in regional Victoria while there is an elevated One Nation vote in the very polling places that the Nationals should be doing well in. There may not be a coalition in Victoria if this trend does not change with a little more data.

I am at the Buckley Park Bowls Club in Ben Carroll’s electorate of Niddrie, where Labor faithful started to gather after voting booths closed.
The room is wall-to-wall red, and they’ve served up bowls of chips and pizza (with pineapple) for media.
There’s a slow trickle of supporters starting to come in, and despite the possible reckoning coming for the party tonight, the vibes are high here. The music is pumping, beers are flowing, and the crowd, mostly in red T-shirts, are having a good time.
As the television stations live-cross, spelling out the issues facing Labor tonight; supporters seem hopeful it may not be as devastating as the polls suggest.
This is the point of the count where leaders are still downplaying expectations.
The leader of the Victorian Greens, Ellen Sandell, has told the ABC that when the campaign started, they thought success would be retaining its three seats in the lower house.
She says if the party wins two more seats – perhaps Pascoe Vale and Footscray – it would be an “incredible success”.
The party is also fighting to win back the seat of Prahran, which it won at the last state election but lost to the Liberals at a byelection. The vote count in that seat, according to Greens sources, will be complicated by a number of independents, open ballot preferences and a potentially surging Liberal vote.
Sandell says that whoever wins, it won’t be an endorsement of the Liberal policies.
I think it’s a rejection of the status quo and how politics hasn’t really worked for people.
Even premier Ben Carroll’s own seat of Niddrie, once considered safe, is at risk. Labor’s traditional heartland is culturally diverse and working class and so the campaign here has been fought on cost of living and local infrastructure.
In previous elections, the opposition hasn’t benefited from swings against Labor but this year One Nation hopes to be the beneficiary of the government’s unpopularity and the sense the area has been “ignored”.
Kos Samaras, a pollster at Redbridge, warned this block of seats would swing away from the government after the Covid lockdowns and he’s surprised how little Labor has done since to win these voters back.
The Victorian Electoral Commission has explained that while counting officially begins at 6pm, sometimes it can have slight delays – including to allow anyone who was in the voting queue by 6pm the chance to actually fill out their ballot.
We’re expecting the first results from smaller voting centres to start to land from about 6.30pm. However, the VEC advises that many larger voting centres aren’t expected to deliver their first preference results until about 9pm.

There is a different story entirely in Melbourne’s inner city. Here, the Greens have high hopes in the inner north, especially in Northcote, which has the smallest margin in the state at just 184 votes.
Labor believes it can regain ground here after the Greens lost its federal leader, Adam Bandt, at the last national election.
