Jensen Huang made his return to Nvidia’s headquarters with a hero’s welcome on Monday. Hours earlier he’d been reinstated as the CEO of the world’s most valuable chip company, following a whiplash series of events that involved a romantic affair with a suspected Chinese spy, a drastic plummet in the stock market, and an intervention from Donald Trump.
As Huang entered the company’s Silicon Valley office, wearing his signature dark-colored uniform, crowds of employees rushed to greet him clapping, hollering and chanting: “Jensen, Jensen, Jensen!”
“I’m going to be a better CEO the second time around,” Huang told employees, according to several verified videos posted to social media. The crowd went wild – it was Huang’s second coming.
“Our Jesus @JensenHuang is back,” Fuxiao Liu, a research scientist at Nvidia, posted on X.
Jim Fan, a Nvidia AI researcher, quoted Charles Dickens’s A Tale of Two Cities, saying: “It was the Winter of despair, it is the Spring of hope.”
As Nvidia employees swooned, the White House took a victory lap. Trump had reportedly dialed into Nvidia’s Sunday board meeting to advocate for Huang’s return, according to Semafor. Nvidia said it reached its decision with “input from senior levels of the United States government”.
Upon the CEO’s reinstatement, Trump posted an AI image on social media recreating his own assassination attempt, with Huang at the center instead. The CEO had blood on his face and his fist raised. In Huang’s hand: a bright red bra.
“President Trump was totally involved, and this is a great decision by the Nvidia board,” said Steven Cheung, White House director of communications. “We have to protect our geniuses.”
Tech leaders, like OpenAI’s Sam Altman and SpaceX’s Elon Musk, cheered the news and the stock market boomed on Monday. Nvidia shares surged after weeks of a downward spiral, jumping 32% and adding more than $1tn to the company’s market value.
The celebration came despite Huang being subject to several government and internal investigations, shareholder lawsuits and accusations that he’s a national security threat. His reinstatement indicates he’s untouchable, for now. It also cements his role at the helm of the burgeoning artificial intelligence boom.
Nvidia, a company Huang co-founded, is one of the most valuable businesses in the world, worth about $4tn, and the entire AI ecosphere relies on it. The company engineers advanced chips used to train AI models and power tech firms such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google.
“The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” said Harvey Jones, the chair of Nvidia’s board.
Still, Huang’s return has many lawmakers calling for accountability and industry watchers scratching their heads.
“I understand that he is ‘the face’ of the company, but that shouldn’t matter. It’s hard to argue that he is the only one who could handle the CEO role,” said Peter Ricchiuti, a business professor at Tulane University. “The graveyards are filled with irreplaceable people.”
Jack Reed, a Democratic senator of Rhode Island and member of the Senate banking committee, called for Huang to disclose all communications between himself and Trump, and Nvidia’s board to release a transcript of its call with the president.
“Mr Huang’s abrupt reinstatement is troubling on many levels,” Reed said. “This case raises serious issues about ethics, misconduct, corruption, national security, the integrity of our technology and military supply chains under the Trump administration’s watch.”
Reed also questioned “whether Nvidia itself is ‘too big to fail’ – or was Mr Huang too powerful to fire?”
