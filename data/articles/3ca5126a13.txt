India and Canada could be announcing a defence agreement during Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s official visit in December, according to the country’s High Commissioner to Ottawa, Dinesh K. Patnaik.
Both countries are also in the final stages of negotiations of a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement. “We are on the cusp of finalising it,” Mr. Patnaik said.
Addressing a gathering in the Canadian capital organised by the Centre for Strategic Statecraft and the Canadian International Council, he said that both countries cannot afford to let fringe issues derail their extensive mutual interests.
Mr. Patnaik took over as India’s High Commissioner to Ottawa in September 2025. Both countries had earlier expelled each other’s High Commissioners and reduced their diplomatic presence following the murder of Sikh Khalistani activist Hardeep Singh Nijjar in June 2023. Then Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau accused India of orchestrating the murder, throwing bilateral relations into a tailspin. Subsequent reports by Canadian agencies also listed India, alongside China and Russia, as an interferer in domestic politics. The turnaround in ties, which began with the mutual reinstatement of High Commissioners, is set to reach a milestone with Mr. Modi’s visit on December 11 and 12.
Mr. Patnaik said India and Canada are no longer talking about a reset of their derailed relations, but rather an overarching boost in ties spanning defence, space, nuclear energy, deep tech, trade, and the mobility of people. The two nations have moved from a complete lack of trust over a year ago to an unprecedented degree of mutual trust, he added.
“The fact is that in the core strategic concerns of Canada and India, there is no conflict. Still, all it took for the entire bilateral relationship to be derailed was the fringe diaspora issue,” he said, referring to the murder of Nijjar.
“The reason was the lack of trust. India believed that Canada was actively promoting separatism in India, and Canada believed India was organising violence in Canada. We had a series of meetings between senior Ministers, NSAs of both countries, and agencies at all levels as we set about rebuilding trust. Today, we have reached the highest levels of trust, where the most sensitive areas of cooperation, such as deep tech, defence, and space, are subjects of mutual cooperation,” Mr. Patnaik said.
Cooperation between Canadian and Indian agencies remains at a high level, the High Commissioner added.
