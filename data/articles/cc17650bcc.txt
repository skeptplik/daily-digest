A year ago, just as the U.S. was winding down its Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), Japan launched its own bureau of government efficiency, officially named the “Office for Administrative Reform and Promotion of Efficiency.” As Japanese Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama explained in a news conference at the time, “The public requires the government to show a constant commitment to maintain fiscal sustainability. It is crucial that this commitment is always present in Kasumigaseki and Nagatacho.”
As reported at the time, “Katayama seemed to dismiss concerns the new bureau would cause widespread havoc similar to that seen in the United States, where DOGE swept over the federal government with mass layoffs and funding cuts. … The bureau will, instead, narrow its focus to reviewing existing measures that grant special tax treatments to companies fulfilling certain conditions — such as raising salaries or investing in research and development — as well as government subsidies and reserve funds.”
In its first year, the office has stayed within those limits, scrutinizing tax breaks and subsidies. Last month, however, Katayama said she would deepen the country’s spending review and pressed ministries to return idle money held in more than 200 government funds, which are still projected to hold about ¥7 trillion by the end of fiscal 2027. The review, which began in December 2025, now covers funds, including pools earmarked for tech startups, as well as subsidies and special tax measures. Its aim is to find about ¥5 trillion a year to pay for a cut in the consumption tax on food.
