MILAN — “Home Is Where the Heart Is,” sang Elvis Presley in 1962, but for Ralph Lauren home is, yes, about emotional connection, but also a physical, welcoming space that is central to the designer’s lifestyle concept and reflects his holistic vision.
“For me, it’s always been about telling a story. I imagine not just how we dress, but how we live — it’s a complete world,” said Lauren, commenting on the opening of the brand’s first Home store in the world, to be unveiled Wednesday in Milan. “Home is at the heart of that story, a reflection of the life we create and the warmth and beauty we desire.”
Spanning over 6,500 square feet, the store is a few steps away from the Ralph Lauren flagship, Ralph’s Bar and children’s store on the luxury, pedestrian shopping street Via Spiga, near Via Montenapoleone.
“When you walk in, we want you to feel welcomed and experience the warmth and comfort that is a signature of the Ralph Lauren retail experience, of his family values,” said Charles Fagan, chief of staff to Ralph Lauren and head of Home & Hospitality, during an exclusive preview and walk-through of the store, which he described as “an important milestone” for the company.
“Milan is one of our strategically key cities in our ecosystem, as Patrice [Louvet, president and chief executive officer of Ralph Lauren Corp.] has been talking about for a long time [as part of the company’s Next Great Chapter: Drive strategy],” said Fagan. “Milan is clearly the center of design and of all things creative relative to home, and it’s always been important for us. Ralph Lauren has a long-standing history with the city and the Italians have always loved the brand, so having a home store here completes our lifestyle [message].”
The Ralph Lauren made-to-order furniture is crafted in Italy, and the brand has been unveiling its new home collections in Milan at its palazzo each April during Salone del Mobile, “when we experience firsthand the city’s extraordinary appreciation for design and home furnishings,” Fagan remarked.
He said the brand’s flagship, Ralph’s Bar and children’s store “have been so warmly embraced by the Milanese and visitors from all over the world, so it feels particularly meaningful to bring Ralph Lauren Home to Via della Spiga and offer the full expression of the brand on this charming street.”
Asked about the possible reasons for the success of the brand in Italy, Fagan believes “there is a sense of heritage here; Italians understand passing things on across family and generations, wearing things that you’ve had your whole life and that have lasted, and they admire quality and craftsmanship. There’s an enthusiasm for our kind of taste. I think we are sort of a little bit American, a little bit English, a little bit international, in a sort of fusion. Ralph has always kind of considered himself an ambassador for America to the world, but with very international values and look.”
Also, Fagan touted the Italians’ “special enthusiasm for the details. There’s a real intuitiveness about the nuances of each of the things that Ralph loves, the workwear, the Western, the sort of polished Savile Row, and then in terms of home, it’s such a family culture.”
To be sure, the store is minutely fine-tuned, with a razor-sharp focus on styling and trimming every nook and cranny.
Behind a traditional Milanese facade, the entrance is reminiscent of the Ralph Lauren Palazzo in the city, with its equestrian paintings and ornate crystal and gold Lantern chandelier. Inside, a warm palette of natural limestone and reclaimed oak is complemented by bronze, aged brass and wrought iron.
Beyond the arched entryway, the store was decked with holiday decorations, displaying a selection of gifting items, from picture frames and card games to traditional tartan porcelain and fun polished pewter ornaments shaped as cowboy boots, martini glasses, a top hat and slippers with the designer’s initials.
Across two levels, a series of immersive galleries unfolds, evoking Ralph Lauren’s signature cinematic vision, moving from a sophisticated city penthouse to the relaxed elegance of country and coastal life.
“Ralph inspires people to dream with the worlds he creates, and home is perhaps the ultimate expression, truly bringing his cinematic vision to life,” said Fagan. “The store invites you to step into his home, experience it in a complete way, and make it your own. The store brings together the breadth of our collections in one destination and reflects Ralph’s point of view on living, through layered spaces that feel personal with furniture and objects that have been collected over time.”
The store, which also further advances Ralph Lauren’s strategic collaboration with Haworth Lifestyle’s Luxury Living Group, will display seasonal collections. Some of the key fall pieces stand out across the spacious rooms that show the breadth of the division, such as the Cushing Étagère bookcase, in ebonized oak with a darker mahogany finish; the Lloyd sofa with a slipcover-inspired construction and the Beckett chairs, or the Hammond desk in bleached mahogany. But there are also some updated heritage items such as the Duke dining table or bar cart and the Hudson St. lounge chair.
There is the Kipton mixologist box in the classic Wexford plaid pattern and the Isabella dining collection crafted in France from fine Limoges porcelain, and pottery in a collaboration with British ceramics maker Burleigh that started in 2019, introducing here a marbleized pattern.
A bedding space with linens made in Italy, such as the Alistair collection in Giza cotton with a high thread count, was shown with a cashmere throw blanket also made in Italy, together with throw blankets with iconic equestrian motifs, and bath towels with a monogramming program, all newly developed for this store.
The furniture is made-to-order in Italy, “another exciting reason for us to be able to open the store here in Milan,” said Fagan.
Fagan said “it was hard to find the right location” in Milan for the home collection, which was first launched in 1983. “We’ve always had home in our flagship stores and Ralph always felt like it completed the whole world within the flagships, but we’re at a point now in terms of the scope of the line and our partnerships that we felt like it was the right time and this location became available, allowing us to keep the momentum going.”
Asked if the new upcoming home collection could potentially be presented in the new store, Fagan demurred, saying, “We are still toying with the idea, but we are still strategizing and feeling creative.”
On the lower level, comprehensive design studios and an extensive lighting gallery offer an array of customization services to create bespoke interiors. The store is meant to service the interior designer and the architect as well as the consumer, “playing with the fabrics and the finishes. We want people to come in, be inspired and be able to furnish their home, a room, or come in for gifts. All the items can be collected and mixed together, so it’s not so singular and one-noted, and it feels more authentic,” Fagan observed.
“I think that’s what makes us so unique as a brand, not separated or forced. People put the items together themselves, and so no room will be alike. We don’t really do suites.”
