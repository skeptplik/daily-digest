The biotech PepGen said Tuesday that its experimental drug had shown, by one key measure, the most promising early data yet in the race to treat a muscle disorder that affects over 100,000 people in the U.S. and Europe.
PepGen is a latecomer to the fight to treat myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1), a condition that typically robs patients of muscle control and strength beginning around adolescence. Tuesday’s data come from six patients, who were treated for 12 weeks.
Dyne Therapeutics is close to completing a pivotal trial for a DM1 drug. Novartis just announced that a DM1 drug it acquired in its $12 billion buyout of Avidity Biosciences failed to improve a key measure of muscle control in a Phase 3 trial, casting a pall over the field.
