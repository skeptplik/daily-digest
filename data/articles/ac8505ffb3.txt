Secretary of State Marco Rubio on Tuesday (November 17, 2026) sought to assure Americans that there's 'zero' information that a series of outages and technical failures across the U.S. are related to each other or to a malign actor.
Mr. Rubio looked to calm Americans as he was travelling with President Donald Trump as they prepared to leave Manila for key meetings with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, China.
“We have zero evidence or any reason to believe that any of these isolated or individual instances have anything to do with nefarious actors, with cyber hackers, with criminals, with another nation state, with a malign actor of any kind,” Mr. Rubio said.
President Donald Trump faced a groundswell of criticism as friends and foes alike said the White House must answer for a cascade of service outages that snarled travel, cut off work, limited bank access and disrupted classified communications.
Even staunch Trump allies accused the administration of lying to Americans or hiding information about the true cause of the disruptions. In recent days, Microsoft, Chase Bank, air traffic control and the National Security Agency have all been affected.
Each outage has been blamed on internal technical problems, with the companies and government agencies involved denying that they were the result of cyberattacks or rogue artificial intelligence systems. Mr. Trump was nonchalant after arriving in Asia on Tuesday, declaring that "things like that happen," a response that did little to calm the country.
Karoline Leavitt, Mr. Trump's former press secretary, offered advice for her old colleagues in the administration.
"What I would say to the White House is transparency is key," Ms. Leavitt said on Fox News. She said the president or someone else at the White House should explain what they know after Trump returns from Asia "to really calm the temperature."
She later praised the White House for having Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton address the outages in an interview on Fox Business Network. “Looking very diligently across the landscape, we are not seeing this as a cyber attack incident,” Mr. Clayton said in a clip that Leavitt shared on X. He said the U.S. government believes the incidents are caused by organisations making security upgrades that are taking longer than planned and encountering unexpected hiccups.
Tricia McLaughlin, a former spokesperson for Trump's Department of Homeland Security, was sceptical about the lack of information coming out of the federal government.
“Americans aren't stupid," she posted on social media. “We want and deserve to know what's really going on.”
Meghan McCain, who is sometimes at odds with Mr. Trump and his allies, had similar advice.
“Someone high in the administration needs to get on TVv and tell the American people NOW what is going on,” McCain wrote on X. “No one who lived through Covid is believing this is all a coincidence.”
The White House did not respond to a request for comment.
Some prominent figures in Mr. Trump's Make America Great Again movement expressed alarm.
“Our leaders are lying to us. This is very clearly a coordinated cyber attack,” Laura Loomer wrote on social media. “President Trump should come back to the United States.” She did not present any evidence of an attack.
The series of outages began on Thursday (November 12, 2026), when Microsoft's email and other tools went down, paralysing millions of workers who rely on them. Microsoft traced the issue to "an earlier network capacity upgrade."
Flights across the country were grounded the next day in the longest so-called ground stop since 9/11. The Federal Aviation Administration blamed a router upgrade.
By Monday (November 16, 2026), the banks were in the spotlight, as customers reported difficulty accessing online accounts at several major financial institutions and the money transfer service Zelle after a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase caused ripple effects throughout the financial system. Chase said it was caused by "a software bug" and was "not cyber-related."
The same day, United Airlines said that it had resolved an internal system outage that delayed departing flights.
And on Tuesday (November 17, 2026), an internal network that the National Security Agency uses to communicate classified information was offline after what the agency described as "routine maintenance."
Mr. Trump was dismissive when asked whether he was concerned about the Chase outage.
“It's life. When you're big and powerful and you have all the money, things like that happen. It's fine,” Mr. Trump said in Manila, Philippines, where he began a three-day Asian trip on Tuesday (November 16, 2026).
Mr. Trump's nonchalance was not shared by those already distrustful of the government and big institutions. And for Americans across the political spectrum, the drip-drip-drip of alarming and frustrating outages is an unsettling reminder of perils posed by interconnected tech networks.
“Where is the transparency? Where is the accountability?” conservative commentator Kylie Jane Kremer wrote on X. “And why does it feel like we're being told everything is fine while the very systems our country depends on are experiencing serious failures?”
