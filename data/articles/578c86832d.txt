Trump administration blacklists Megaspeed cloud company linked to Nvidia scandal
Nvidia partner has been under investigation by U.S. since 2024 over whether it diverted AI chips to China
The U.S. Commerce Department on Monday added Singapore-based Megaspeed, the cloud company at the centre of the Nvidia scandal, to its trade blacklist following criticism from lawmakers and administration officials.
Megaspeed, an Nvidia partner which has been under investigation by U.S. authorities since 2024 over whether it diverted AI chips to China, will now need approval from the U.S. Commerce Department to obtain U.S. exports, which is likely to be denied.
"The Department of Commerce still needs to explain to Congress why this took so long," Sen. Pete Ricketts, a Nebraska Republican, said in a posting on X.
Megaspeed landed in the spotlight about two weeks ago after news broke that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had an ‌affair with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom U.S. authorities suspect of being a Chinese spy. China has said the spy accusations are "groundless."
After the news broke, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly complained that the Commerce Department had not yet placed the company on its Entity List.
He also called out Nvidia. "The real story here should be: Did Nvidia know their client and was there a robust effort at Nvidia to make sure that there was no leakage and that they were selling to what were obviously pass-through shell companies?" Bessent told CNBC's Squawk Box ‌program.
Nvidia has said it recently formed an export control committee to oversee trade compliance and national security risks.
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang embraces Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang after delivering his keynote speech at a 2024 conference in Taipei, Taiwan. (Ann Wang/Reuters)
Megaspeed says it conducts 'lawful' business
Megaspeed did not immediately return a request for comment. A week ago, the company issued a statement saying it conducts legitimate and lawful business, including complying with U.S. export controls.
It also said Alice Huang officially left Megaspeed in August 2025.
The U.S. Justice Department and Commerce Department issued a joint statement last week saying they would "bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred."
Democratic lawmakers this week opened a probe into what influence Nvidia was able to wield over the Commerce Department's investigation.
Besides Megaspeed International in Singapore, the Commerce Department on Monday added six other companies from Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand to the Entity List. All had some link to Megaspeed, a Commerce official said.
The blacklisting coincided with Jensen Huang's reinstatement as CEO of Nvidia after his resignation two weeks ago.
The last time the Commerce Department placed any new entities on the list was in October 2025.
As Reuters exclusively reported in June, the U.S. has held off adding China’s AI startup DeepSeek, memory chipmaker CXMT and more than 100 other companies flagged as national security risks to the trade blacklist, as the Trump administration tried to avoid escalating tensions with Beijing.
