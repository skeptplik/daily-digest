Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz said on Tuesday (November 24, 2026) that Iran is developing chemical weapons and vowed to stop Tehran from obtaining them.
"Iran is in the process of developing chemical weapons but has not yet reached the capability to attack Israel with them," said Mr. Katz in a statement published by his Likud party on Telegram.
"We must not allow this to proceed," he added.
Last week CNN, citing U.S. intelligence, reported that Iran has been developing plans to target senior Trump administration officials by detonating a so-called dirty bomb in Washington.
The report quoted anonymous sources characterising Iran's plans as "an effort to give itself options rather than an indicator of an imminent attack".
A dirty bomb is a conventional explosive laced with chemical or radioactive materials.
Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi dismissed the report, calling it "Iraq War-style propaganda" with "Israeli fingerprints all over it".
"Nobody is buying it," Mr. Araghchi wrote on X.
In a briefing to Israeli reporters on Tuesday (November 24), Mr. Katz floated the possibility of a preemptive strike against Tehran if necessary.
"If we recognise that the Iranians intend to attack Israel, can't we tell the army to attack earlier and thwart the threat?" he said, according to a transcript from Israel's Channel 12. "If the Iranians attack us first, it will be a failure."
Israel held a general election last month in which neither caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu nor his main opponent Gadi Eisenkot obtained a clear majority.
Mr. Eisenkot has been tasked by President Isaac Herzog with attempting to form a government, and Mr. Netanyahu has talked up the threat from Tehran as he insists on the need for a unity government that includes his Likud party.
He has also reportedly urged U.S. President Donald Trump to resume war on Iran, following two rounds of fighting last year and earlier this year.
But Mr. Katz said Mr. Netanyahu had dissuaded Mr. Trump from an attack before the Israeli vote last month.
"Trump was the one who considered attacking before the (Israeli) elections. The prime minister told him: I don't want to attack beforehand," he wrote in his statement on Tuesday (Novmber 24).
On Monday (November 23), Mr. Eisenkot said he saw no immediate reason to attack Iran.
During a press briefing at Israel's parliament in Jerusalem on Monday (November 23), Mr. Eisenkot said the "Iranian threat is the most serious threat to the State of Israel".
"But at this moment I do not see a direct and immediate threat to the State of Israel that would require action before a government is formed," he added.
