Nintendo's Shigeru Miyamoto will be the first recipient of the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences' new Legend Award.
The AIAS said Miyamoto will be honored at the 30th annual DICE Awards, which take place at the Cosmopolitan in Las Vegas on February 18.
The Legend Award is designed to recognize "an industry icon whose enduring vision continues to redefine interactive entertainment," the organization said, confirming it will be presented "no more than once per decade."
"Shigeru Miyamoto is widely recognized as one of the most respected and impactful figures in the games industry and beyond," said AIAS president Meggan Scavio.
"We believe he perfectly embodies our inaugural Legend Award through his impact and reach, and we are so humbled and excited to have Mr. Miyamoto accept this award at the 30th anniversary of the DICE Awards."
Miyamoto joined Nintendo in 1977 after studying industrial design at Kanazawa College of Art. He created Donkey Kong in 1981, and went on to develop Super Mario Bros. and The Legend of Zelda, as well as the Pikmin series, Wii Sports, and Wii Fit. He has been Nintendo's representative director and fellow since 2015, and is currently focused on bringing Nintendo's characters to theme parks and film.
It isn't Miyamoto's first honor. Last month, he was announced as the first game developer to receive Japan's Order of Culture award.
