Iran on Tuesday said French accusations that it was responsible for cyberattacks on supermarket fridges last month were "baseless".
"Iran's foreign ministry categorically rejects the French government's baseless and one-sided accusations against Iran regarding alleged cyber activities in France," the ministry said in a statement.
It added that it "strongly condemns this hackneyed policy of levelling accusations against the Islamic republic".
The response follows a statement by French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot on Monday that said Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps was behind the series of attacks targeting French supermarket fridges on the night of 11-12 October.
Hundreds of French supermarkets were hit by cyber-attacks, which raised the temperatures in their refrigerators and freezers, forcing many to throw away entire inventories of perishable food.
Stores belonging to Carrefour, Monoprix, Intermarché and Coopérative U were among those hit when attackers manually overrode fridge temperatures.
Similar incidents were then reported in at least 10 other European countries in early November.
Along with summoning Iran's ambassador and seeking European-level sanctions, Barrot said Tuesday that Paris would "rule nothing out and will take all measures — including those we may not disclose publicly — that we deem proportionate and necessary to deter any further attacks".
French authorities have added that this was not the first time Iran had targeted France with "destabilisation acts", citing a hack on satirical newspaper Charlie Hebdo in 2023 and hostile actions during the 2024 Olympic Games.
Tehran meanwhile said it would "not hesitate to take necessary and appropriate measures to safeguard its national interests and security in the face of unlawful foreign activities".
It added that France "is merely projecting its own governance failures" with these accusations.
Later in the day, Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi blamed the French government for the incident.
"Unable to manage fridges let alone addressing grievances of its people, French government is shifting blame for its failures onto others," the top diplomat said on X.
Also on Tuesday, the European Union said it would look to provide a "swift and united response" pending discussion with member states after France's announcement.
