Major mobile-phone carrier KDDI plans to release a dumbphone limited to calls, text messages and other basic features in its iconic Infobar design, which became a fad when it was first unveiled 25 years ago.
Dumbphone models, named in contrast with smartphones, have already made a resurgence in the United States and Europe.
“In this era, as a company that delivers cutting-edge AI and communication, au proposes a future where you can choose how you connect, and the comfortable space of ‘deliberately not doing something,’” KDDI said in a news release Wednesday, referencing artificial intelligence and its mobile brand au.
Infobar Zero, which opened for preorders Wednesday and will be released on Dec. 25, cannot connect to the internet. The phone has a built-in music player and a 2-megapixel camera, allowing users to recreate the retro photography style of the early 2000s.
According to a KDDI blog post, the idea to bring back the Infobar — which was first conceived in 2001 and released in 2003 — grew out of the company’s existing “second number” function, which currently allows customers to register two phone numbers to a single device, and desire to create a phone handset with limited functionality.
Users may register a second phone number to their Infobar Zero phones for use alongside their primary number on a smartphone. The company foresees some customers using the device exclusively for its nonphone features — as an alarm clock, camera or music player — or as a landline phone.
With smartphones now practically essential for daily life, KDDI presents Infobar Zero more as a supplementary device than a replacement for a smartphone.
Japan was relatively slow to adopt smartphones when they were first introduced. A 2015 Pew Research study found that 39% of Japanese respondents reported owning a smartphone compared with 88% in neighboring South Korea, 58% in China and 72% in the U.S. In the survey, 50% of the respondents in Japan reported owning a nonsmartphone cellphone and 11% did not own a phone. An NTT Docomo study of mobile phone owners in 2025 found that 98% were smartphone users, up from 51.5% in 2015.
Flip-phone users who held on to their older models were gradually forced out of their contracts as mobile providers shut down supported network services.
Remaining holdouts would have needed to switch over earlier this year, when NTT Docomo — the final provider of 3G service — discontinued its FOMA and i-mode networks in March. Rival carriers KDDI au and Softbank ended their 3G services in 2022 and 2024 respectively.
