I have fine hair and an oily scalp, which means a fresh wash looks great for about a day before my roots start to fall flat and my bangs give up on me. For years, I’d wash my hair every morning, until I realized all that shampooing and blow-drying could be swapped for something much easier—dry shampoo. A little at the roots on day two buys me another day or two between washes, and it’s saved more than one blowout I wasn’t ready to let go of. These days, a can lives on my vanity, another in my bag, and one permanently in my suitcase.

The reason it works so well comes down to what’s inside. Most formulas rely on ingredients like rice starch to soak up the oil sitting at your roots, which is what makes hair look fresh again, but getting rid of that oil also takes away the weight that makes roots fall flat in the first place. That’s why a good dry shampoo gives you a little lift almost instantly, and the powder leaves behind just enough texture to help a style hold without feeling stiff. If you’ve ever noticed that second-day hair is easier to style than freshly washed hair, that’s a big part of the reason.

Everything You Need to Know
How to Pick the Right Dry Shampoo
Picking the perfect formula for your hair type makes all the difference. “All textures can use dry shampoo, but it may not be necessary or as beneficial for someone with coily hair,” says celebrity hairstylist Erinn Courtney. “Straight, wavy, or loosely curled hair benefits from dry shampoo most. Some dry shampoos are made for more oily hair, so they should be picked according to what kind of help your hair needs to feel fresh again.” Brooklyn-based hairstylist Mickiela Smith—also a trichologist and founder of hair-care platform—adds that for those with allergies or sensitive skin, it may be worth having a conversation with your doctor as they can provide a medicated, waterless shampoo to ease scalp woes.
How often should I use dry shampoo?
Trichologist and colorist Bridgette Hill says that depending on hair type and texture, a dry shampoo can be used up to twice a day. “The important factor is ensuring there is a healthy hair care routine that balances the use of dry shampoo,” she notes. “If someone is using a dry shampoo daily, a proper shampoo with water should be performed at least every two to three days.” Board-certified dermatology nurse practitioner Catie Boucher reminds us that if we’re using dry shampoos regularly, it’s also essential to ensure we’re properly removing product buildup from our scalps during hair wash regimens. “I’ll be honest, I only wash my hair 1-2 times a week. I used to wash my hair daily, and not only did it not help with oiliness, it was a lot of work with long hair,” Boucher tells Vogue. “If you’re a dry shampoo loyalist like myself, or you use a lot of styling products, addressing product build-up with a scalp detox is never a bad idea.”
Is dry shampoo good for your hair?
Dry shampoo can absolutely be part of a healthy haircare routine, as long as it’s used correctly and in moderation. “I see it as a styling and refreshing product rather than a replacement for washing your hair,” says Macias. “One of its biggest advantages is that it allows you to extend the life of a blowout or hairstyle, which can sometimes mean less frequent heat styling. From my professional perspective, that’s particularly useful when working with colored, fragile or heavily styled hair.” In any circumstance, the scalp always needs proper cleansing. “Using too much product or layering it repeatedly without washing can leave the scalp feeling uncomfortable and the hair looking dull,” warns Macias, who advises to only use dry shampoo where it’s needed, avoid overapplying and maintain a regular cleansing routine that suits your hair and scalp.
