NAIROBI – More than half the field workers at the U.S. government’s famine monitor will lose their jobs by the end of the year due to a lack of funding, six people said, leaving it unable to help fend off famines made more likely by El Nino and aid cuts.
Dec. 1 30-day termination notices to dozens of employees of the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET) mean it will be unable to produce its next food security update, three of the people — all with direct knowledge of the issue — said.
An email from the monitor to aid officials sent on Tuesday said the December update would not be published because the U.S. State Department had not confirmed funding for the fiscal year that began in October, forcing staff to “initiate contingency closeout actions.”
