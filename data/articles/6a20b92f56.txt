Palo Alto Networks on Tuesday announced PAN-OS updates that patch the Fragnesia Linux kernel vulnerability exploited to root its firewalls.
Tracked as CVE-2026-46300, Fragnesia was disclosed in May 2026 as a privilege escalation bug in the kernel’s XFRM ESP-in-TCP subsystem. It allows unprivileged attackers to overwrite sensitive system files to gain root permissions.
On October 26, cybersecurity firm Dream warned that a Chinese-speaking threat actor successfully exploited the critical PHP2Shell bugs and the Linux kernel weakness to hack two servers of a US government-affiliated organization.
As part of the AI-assisted campaign, the attackers compromised 25 servers (24 firewalls and one internal firewall management server), exploiting PHP2Shell to gain remote code execution (RCE), and executing a Fragnesia exploit to deploy webshells with root privileges.
On October 29, Palo Alto Networks confirmed that the threat actor hit PAN-OS firewalls with management interfaces exposed to the internet in attacks chaining PHP2Shell with Fragnesia.
By October 30, the company had released patches for PHP2Shell across all supported PAN-OS versions, closing the known initial-access path.
“If you are running a fixed PAN-OS listed in the CVE-2026-109618 [PHP2Shell] advisory, you are protected,” Palo Alto Networks notes in a Tuesday advisory.
Regardless, the company released new PAN-OS versions that address CVE-2026-46300: 12.2.4-h3, 12.1.10-h6, 12.1.7-h15, 12.1.4-h15, 11.2.16-h3, 11.2.13-h6, 11.2.10-h19, 11.2.7-h25, 11.2.4-h25, 11.1.16-h6, 11.1.13-h18, 11.1.10-h38, 11.1.7-h14, 11.1.6-h42, 11.1.4-h40, 10.2.18-h15, 10.2.16-h14, 10.2.13-h29, 10.2.10-h45, and 10.2.7-h41.
Palo Alto Networks recommends applying the fixes for Fragnesia, although the only known way to exploit it against PAN-OS is to first exploit PHP2Shell via an internet-exposed management interface.
The company also recommends not allowing direct access to the management interface from the web. Instead, users should only allow access from the management network or route the access via a VPN.
“Exploitation of CVE-2026-46300 has been observed targeting the Palo Alto Networks management interface exposed to the internet,” Palo Alto Networks notes.
