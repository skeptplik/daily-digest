The situation in the India-China border areas remain “generally stable”, China’s Defence Ministry spokesperson and PLA Senior Colonel Chen Xi said on Thursday (November 26, 2026) adding that China “does not recognize the so-called Arunachal Pradesh illegally set up by India.”
“China and India maintain communication through diplomatic and military channels, including general-level talks, to jointly implement the consensus reached by the two sides and safeguard peace and tranquility in the border areas”, he said in response to a question at a press briefing on recent tensions in the Eastern sector of the border, Chinese state media reported.
On November 24, 2026, India and China concluded a second round of Corps Commander-level talks aimed at easing tensions along the Line of Actual Control (LAC) in Arunachal Pradesh and putting in place measures to prevent friction between troops deployed along the frontier ahead of the onset of harsh winter conditions.
Sources in the Ministry of Defence confirmed that the closely guarded discussions, which began on November 8, 2026, a day after Deepavali, continued for nearly 10 days, with a short break in between. The talks were described as cordial, though it remains unclear whether the measures discussed will be implemented on the ground before winter sets in fully.
The latest round of talks primarily focused on establishing engagement protocols between the two sides, preventing face-offs and misunderstandings between troops deployed along the frontier, maintaining open communication channels and reducing tensions in the eastern sector. Both countries have deployed substantial numbers of troops in the region.
