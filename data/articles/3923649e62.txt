In short:
Football Australia is looking for an alternative venue with Socceroos players unwilling to travel to Jordan to play a friendly in Amman.
FA CEO Martin Kugeler as well as other big hitters have flown to Doha in order to try to find solutions.
What's next?
The Socceroos are scheduled to take on Qatar in Doha on Saturday before the scheduled Jordan clash in Amman on November 18.
Socceroos players remain unwilling to travel to Jordan as Football Australia (FA) scrambles for alternatives to playing an upcoming friendly in Amman.
But US Soccer's decision to withdraw its team from the under-17 World Cup in Qatar over safety concerns is not expected to affect either the Socceroos or their Joeys counterparts.
The Socceroos and Joeys are both in camp in Doha ahead of their respective friendlies and World Cup campaigns.
The senior team is due to face Qatar on Saturday (AEDT) before travelling to Amman to face Jordan on November 18.
The latter is the issue, with PFA boss Beau Busch saying last week that Socceroos players had "overwhelmingly expressed their view that the Jordan match should be rescheduled or relocated".
The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) advises Australians to reconsider their need to travel to Jordan "due to the unpredictable security situation" and the "threat of terrorism".
FA had been keen for the game in Jordan to proceed, but has said it was monitoring the security situation in the area.
The friendlies against Qatar and Jordan were arranged as preparations for January's Asian Cup, but the postponement of the tournament to December 2027 due to conflict in Saudi Arabia has decreased their significance.
FA heavy hitters including chief executive Martin Kugeler and football director Heather Garriock flew to Doha this week, as did Busch and men's head of player relations Jon McKain.
Players are not expected to back down and agree to play in Jordan, likely leaving cancellation or relocation as the options.
One option is Australia remaining in Qatar and playing Jordan in Doha.
But that proposal is understood to have been met with some reluctance from both the Qatari and Jordanian football associations.
Jordan has already had to deal with Indonesia pulling out of a friendly this window.
Playing the Jordan game somewhere in southern Europe is another option, along with cancelling or postponing the game altogether.
Coach Tony Popovic, due to speak to media in his pre-match press conference on Thursday night, has indicated no players would be blacklisted from future selection if they withdrew from his squad for security reasons.
Elsewhere, in a letter sent to players, US Soccer said it was cancelling its team's pre-tournament camp and its participation in the under-17 World Cup "in light of current circumstances".
US Soccer cited US State Department's Level 3 travel advisory for Qatar and related directives for embassy staff and families.
But the Joeys are not expected to follow the US in withdrawing from the under-17 tournament in Qatar, while the Socceroos are understood not to have issues with being in Qatar.
