Nigeria's President Bola Tinubu says the presidential villa suffers from cuts in electricity and water - failures that plague ordinary citizens.
He said he was shaving last week when the water supply at the villa, known as Aso Rock, in Abuja cut.
He is seeking re-election next January and vowed to address these issues..
Tinubu made the disclosure on Sunday at a special prayer session organised in his honour by members of his family in Lagos.
He explained: "On the Wednesday of last week, I was shaving, and the water went off. In the villa, you won't know punishment dey dia too [thereâs punishment there too in Nigerian pidgin].
"The water went off. So we will correct all this. No light, no water, no electricity, no development. We have just started. We can do it, and we will do it for Nigeria,â he said.
Aso Villa serves as both the office and official residence of the Nigerian president. It is situated at the base of Aso Rock in Abuja.
Nigeria has long endured electricity shortages, but recent severe disruptions have been caused primarily by insufficient gas supplies and declining hydropower output, rather than the usual nationwide grid collapse.
Persistent blackouts in Lagos, Abuja and elsewhere have forced distribution companies to implement rotating power cuts, severely impacting households and businesses.
The country also suffers from chronic water shortages, the result of a structural crisis rooted in the gap between rapid urban population growth and decaying, inadequate public water infrastructure.
Tinubu made a high-profile electricity promise during the 2023 election campaign, but the exact wording and how binding it is has become a major point of contention ahead of the 16 January election.
