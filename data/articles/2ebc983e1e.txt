King Charles opens new HQ for National Cyber Force, UK’s offensive hacking unit
King Charles III on Monday opened the new headquarters of Britain’s National Cyber Force, the secretive unit that runs the country’s offensive cyber operations against hostile states, terrorists and criminals.
Staff are already working in the building near the Lancashire village of Samlesbury in northwest England, named Ribble House after the nearby river and Ribble Valley. The government chose the village as the force’s permanent home five years ago and committed £5 billion ($6.5 billion) to the NCF through 2030.
The NCF brings together staff from the cyber and signals intelligence agency GCHQ, the Ministry of Defence, the Secret Intelligence Service (MI6) and the Defence Science and Technology Laboratory. Officials say it carries out operations every day.
“In an age of radical uncertainty, contested geopolitics, and rapidly changing technology, the NCF keeps the country safe by delivering high-impact cyber operations every single day against those who threaten our interests,” said GCHQ Director Anne Keast-Butler who showed the king around the site.
As first reported by Recorded Future News, a new commander is currently in charge at the NCF, replacing Air Vice-Marshal Tim Neal-Hopes in September, although officials have yet to name them publicly. Neal-Hopes took over in October 2023 from James Babbage, a former GCHQ officer who subsequently moved to the National Crime Agency.
To date, the NCF has rarely discussed its work in public, saying disclosures would undermine the effects of its covert operations. In a 2023 doctrine paper, it described its operations as “accountable, precise, and calibrated,” in a way that was fundamentally different from those practiced by Britain’s adversaries.
Gen. Sir Rob Magowan, commander of Cyber and Specialist Operations Command, said: “The opening of the National Cyber Force’s new headquarters marks a significant milestone in strengthening the UK’s ability to combat threats in an increasingly contested world.
“We must be ready to confront, deter and prevail in the cyber domain, taking the initiative against those who threaten our national security. The National Cyber Force plays a vital role in giving the UK that advantage,” he added.
British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting called the headquarters “a major investment in the North West, in defence innovation, and in keeping our country safe.”
Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said: “As hostile actors use cyberattacks to disrupt our way of life and cause us harm, our world class intelligence agencies ensure that we can tackle those threats head-on.”
Ribble House’s ceremonial opening comes on the same day a House of Lords committee warned the country is “underprepared” for “potentially catastrophic risks” including cyberattacks from Russia, China and Iran.
The committee said the government “appears complacent” and stressed the “need to move fast to turn things around.”
