The Food and Drug Administration said Friday that it had rejected a cell therapy intended for patients with Duchenne muscular dystrophy developed by Capricor Therapeutics.
The decision to block the treatment, called deramiocel, from reaching the market should have been an easy one for regulators to make. The targeted patient population — teenagers and young men with Duchenne who had already lost the ability to walk — have few treatment options today. Deramiocel didn’t help them either. Capricor conducted two randomized clinical trials. Both failed to show deramiocel could stave off heart failure or preserve muscle function in their arms.
But nothing is straightforward when it comes to regulating drugs for Duchenne, a progressive, muscle-wasting disease.
