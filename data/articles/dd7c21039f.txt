In short:
Australia beats France 4-0 in its Billie Jean King Cup play-off tie.
Maya Joint beat Elsa Jacquemot 6-1, 6-3 in the final match.
What's next?
Australia will compete in the qualifiers for the 2027 Billie Jean King Cup finals in April.
Sam Stosur's Australian team will be back for a crack at the Billie Jean King Cup crown next year after "unreal" Maya Joint joined the party with two victories to seal a crushing 4-0 win over France in their play-off tie at Le Portel.
Joint had watched her teammates Storm Hunter and national number one Talia Gibson give the visitors a convincing lead in the French seaside town after the first day on Saturday, but was thrust into the action alongside Hunter to try to seal victory in the key doubles rubber on Sunday, local time.
Even on the Le Chaudron clay court chosen by the hosts, the Aussie duo were in a different league to the home pairing of world number 38 Diane Parry and world No.8 doubles player Kristina Mladenovic, racing to a tie-sealing 6-2 6-4 victory.
Joint, who produced some spectacular tennis in that win, then got in on the singles action, swiftly outplaying Elsa Jacquemot 6-1 6-3 in under an hour. Victory in that "dead" rubber underlined the triumph before organisers scrapped the last scheduled match between Hunter and Clara Burel.
"Maya was unreal today. It was a really tough match against quality opponents," said Hunter, whose own game always seems to go up a notch when she wears the green and gold.
"We're really happy with the result."
Joint, delighted to be returning to her best at the end of a season when her Wimbledon triumph over Serena Williams proved the absolute highlight, added: "The vibes were high all week, we were just really happy and excited to all be here."
The three Australians, between them, had blasted through their four matches without dropping a set, and conceding just 20 games over the whole weekend of one-sided fare.
It was also a moment of revenge for captain Stosur, who played in the 2019 final, which she lost 3-2 on home soil in Perth against a French team that included Mladenovic.
If they had lost the tie, she knew Australia would have been condemned to relegation to its regional group next year, thus ruling it out of a chance to shoot for the 2027 title.
Instead, they will be back in April's qualifiers amid what looks like a renaissance in the Australian women's game, looking to seal a place in the week-long finals in Shenzhen where they could pursue a first title since 1974, when the World Cup of women's tennis was known as the Federation Cup.
"It's a fantastic feeling for us right now; we put in a lot of hard work to try and make this result happen," Stosur said.
"We put on these jackets in the green-and-gold, and it really does bring out the best in a lot of our players. If we can keep building the way we are, for sure we want to try and lift that trophy one day again."
The seven-time champions are looking to bounce back from a disappointing early exit in 2026 when they lost 3-1 in a home qualifier against Great Britain in Melbourne in April.
It's now four years since Australia last reached the final, losing 2-0 to Switzerland in Glasgow.
