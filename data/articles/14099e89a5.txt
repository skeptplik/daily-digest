Awards season is officially underway. On Sunday night, Hollywood’s biggest names gathered at the Ray Dolby Ballroom at Ovation Hollywood for the 17th annual Governors Awards, hosted by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences. The star-studded evening celebrated extraordinary achievements and contributions to the film industry, honoring Floyd Norman, Glenn Close, and Ridley Scott, as well as Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler, who received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award.
After posing for photos on the step-and-repeat, celebrities made their way to the reception just outside the Ray Dolby Ballroom, where the crowd quickly grew shoulder-to-shoulder. Nathan Fielder was among the first to arrive, chatting with fellow guests about his outfit for the evening: a midnight-blue Armani tuxedo. Soon after, a beaming Parker Posey made her entrance in a black lace-and-mesh ensemble, accompanied by Martin McDonagh and Sarah Finn, with whom she recently worked on the new comedy-thriller Wild Horse Nine.
Servers weaved through the sea of guests, offering trays of savory bites, including mini burgers, pierogies, tuna cones, and dates stuffed with goat cheese and pistachios. Close paused to examine the latter, asking one server, “What is that tiny little thing?” Though the legendary actress ultimately passed on the appetizer, the interaction appeared to make the staff member’s day, as the server couldn’t help but smile.
Around 5:45 p.m., an announcement over the loudspeaker signaled for guests to make their way into the ballroom, where dinner would soon be served. Charles Melton lingered at the reception a little longer, chatting with a group that included actress Piper Curda. Inde Navarrette, meanwhile, settled in early, as a waiter poured her a glass of white wine.
Emmy Rossum made her way inside just as the five-minute warning was announced, squeezing past Elizabeth Debicki and Carla Gugino, who were deep in conversation. Among the last to enter the ballroom were husband-and-wife duo Zendaya and Tom Holland, who walked hand in hand.
Upon taking their seats, attendees were greeted with an assortment of appetizers, including smoked salmon oscar matzah, fennel-crusted seared tuna on confit potato, spicy carrot hummus on pumpernickel, and a wagyu carpaccio roll with winter vegetables.
Lynette Howell Taylor, president of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, was the first to address the room. “Take a look at the five artists we’re honoring here tonight,” she began, sparking a round of applause. “When we think about their extraordinary careers, it’s easy to see the great films, the iconic performances, and the groundbreaking achievements. What we don’t always see are the years of determination behind them, the risks that didn’t pay off, the doors that didn’t open, and the moments when it would have been easier to take a different path. And yet, they kept going, and that is something that should be awarded.”
Following her remarks, dinner was served: pan-roasted chicken with truffle velouté, roasted heirloom carrots, and potato purée. Guests sipped on a selection of wines throughout the evening, including La Clarté de Haut-Brion, Le Dragon de Quintus, and Piper-Heidsieck Champagne.
Norman was the first honoree of the evening, with Mahershala Ali presenting him with an honorary award. “I am so deeply honored to present the first award tonight to a pioneering animator, story artist, and writer who has been creating exceptional work for more than 70 years, the great Floyd Norman,” the two-time Oscar-winning actor said.
During his acceptance speech, Norman reflected on his childhood and the pivotal role his parents played in nurturing his passion for film. “I was a lucky kid because my mom and dad often took me to the movie theater, and those times gave me the education I needed,” he recalled. “In that darkened theatrical classroom, I was just learning the language of film. Somehow, even at that young age, I absorbed the excellent storytelling techniques of lighting, composition, the effective use of sound and music from the Hollywood masters, and seeing on screen what they created in the Hollywood Dream Factory gave me my own dream to become a filmmaker.”
John Malkovich then took the stage to present Close with her honorary award. The actress appeared visibly emotional as she approached the podium, though the mood quickly turned lighthearted when she stumbled over her words while reading from the teleprompter, drawing laughter from the room. “What is that? Wait, put it down the other way. Oh, okay, I got it now!” she exclaimed.
Like Norman, Close looked back on her upbringing, revealing that she was “under the thumb of a restrictive cult” from ages seven to 22. “Don’t look at yourself in the mirror. No flirting. Dress this way. Talk this way,” she recalled of the rules she was expected to follow.
After leaving the cult, Close enrolled at the College of William & Mary as a 22-year-old freshman, and she soon found her calling in the theater department. “It was like water on the desert, the revelation and the deep sense of purpose and fulfillment that started to bud when I stepped onto the stage of Phi Beta Kappa Hall has led me directly to this very moment in this room.”
Next to take the stage was Matt Damon, playfully poking fun at Scott who accepted his award remotely. “Gladiator, Black Hawk Down, Alien: Covenant—these are just three extraordinary Ridley Scott films that he did not cast me in, and I was totally available,” the actor joked, drawing laughter from the room. “All is forgiven though, as I did get to make The Martian with him.”
While accepting his first Oscar, Scott paid a heartfelt tribute to his late brother, Tony. “Now past 80, I’ve thought about those days and moments and it does resonate, it seems like yesterday, both of us sharing our first film together,” he recalled. “I’m flattered and proud to accept this award, and I also accept it for Tony.”
Last but certainly not least, producers Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler were honored with the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award, presented by Julianne Moore—dressed in Bottega Veneta—and Todd Haynes. Before presenting the award, however, the duo had a surprise in store for the audience: a performance by Neil Patrick Harris, who sang “The Origin of Love” from Hedwig and the Angry Inch, a film produced by Vachon and Koffler.
“Tonight’s honorees, your speeches were so incredible,” Vachon began, kicking off their acceptance speech. “We were sitting there going, ‘Oh no! We’re just producers, guys.’” She then turned her attention to her decades-long collaboration with Koffler. “Pam and I have been making movies together for over 30 years. Our longevity and endurance are owed to a few factors: very hard work, plenty of really good luck, and working with the absolute best people, and of course our partnership.”
Koffler then took a moment to look back on their early days working together. “We decided to call ourselves Killer Films. An ethos emerged in those early years, and it has guided the company from day one to champion voices we believe in, stories we love, with a pragmatism that enables them to actually exist.” And just like that, the ceremony came to an end. If the evening was any indication, though, Hollywood is in for a memorable awards season.
