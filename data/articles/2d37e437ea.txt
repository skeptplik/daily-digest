Hongkongers to draft end-of-life treatment refusals online from next year
Authorities say the digital version will carry the same legal effect as paper, while do-not-attempt cardiopulmonary resuscitation orders remain in hard copy
Hongkongers will be able to create advance medical directives electronically, setting out which life-sustaining treatments they do not wish to receive when terminally ill on the city’s eHealth record platform as early as next year, authorities have said.
In a written reply to lawmakers on Wednesday, Secretary for Labour and Welfare Chris Sun Yuk-han said the electronic service for advance medical directives, which currently exists only in paper form, would be rolled out “in phases”.
After consulting government units including the Health Bureau, he said eHealth would serve as the designated electronic system for residents to make, store and revoke a directive.
