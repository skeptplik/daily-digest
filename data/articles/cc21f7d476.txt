PARIS — Sephora Collection, the beauty retailer’s eponymous line, turns 30 this year and enters a new decade with a four-pronged strategy.
“Sephora is on a winning trajectory for the past few years, in terms of being the leading and the only global beauty retailer,” said Guillaume Motte, chief executive officer of Sephora, adding it now has almost 3,500 stores, 55,000 employees and 80 million consumers. “We are winning share in all our markets.”
Sephora Collection is a crucial component, with industry sources estimating its annual sales alone run northward of 1 billion euros.
“It is a brand which has constantly innovated, made beauty accessible to all and has played a very important dynamic in our trajectory — and that’s still the case,” Motte said. “It’s at the heart of our success.”
Dominique Mandonnaud, who founded Sephora — which is now owned by LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton — in 1969, kicked off Sephora Collection with 365 shades of lipstick.
Another milestone for Sephora Collection came with its introduction of facial masks in the 2010s, before they became the ubiquitous products of today.
Key to Sephora Collection’s success, according to Motte, is the retailer’s 49,000 beauty advisers, who give constant feedback on the product line, based on their own and consumer insights.
“That’s also why the brand is so relevant,” Motte said. “We are talking about a very big brand, global scale, thanks to the Sephora network, the reach that we have and the accessibility of the brand.”
The executive said the brand has a lot of agility, which enables it to be highly locally relevant. “It can be adapted both product-wise or marketing-wise in China, then adapted product-wise or marketing-wise in the U.S., while staying always true to the Sephora Collection promise.”
Sylvia Tournery, a beauty industry veteran, stepped into the role of president of Sephora Collection in June. She had worked for international brands, mainly at L’Oréal, at times when they had to simultaneously preserve their DNA and enter a new growth phase, she said.
“These kinds of moments, turning points, are what I love to do,” Tournery said. “This is why I joined Sephora Collection.”
She explained when a brand turns 30 it is because of its savoir-faire, expertise and high standards of quality.
“At the same time, it has this accessibility,” Tournery said of Sephora Collection, adding nine out of 10 of the brand’s products are priced below 20 euros, or $22.30. “This is a unique model. The magic of it is the Sephora touch — the fact that we have always felt the pulse of beauty, making sure that we always capture what’s happening within society, within beauty culture, and making it accessible to women.”
She said this creates an emotional link and connection with women, that many of them discover beauty through the Sephora Collection and are still buying the brand.
“Now looking ahead, the ambition is as high as Sephora’s ambition,” Tournery said. “Because the brand is at the heart of Sephora. If Sephora accelerates, then its signature brand needs to accelerate.”
That informs her and the Sephora Collection team’s roadmap. “Meaning concretely: making the brand even more desirable and even more a must-have brand within the global beauty scenery,” she said.
The strategic focus will be on four dimensions. “The first one is the brand itself,” Tournery said. “We want to make sure that people know the personality of the brand even more. Concretely, it means working on the brand platform, the way the brand expresses itself and the role we want to play in the influence on the culture of beauty.”
Being more visible on social media is part of that.
“Not only to be visible, but also to give our own point of view on beauty,” she said.
The second dimension is the offer strategy.
“Today, we have an amazing offer — very rich — it’s part of the DNA of the brand,” she said. Sephora Collection has more than 2,000 products with icons and products it is known for.
“For example, we have a strong leadership in the eye category with our mascara Size Up, which is a volume mascara that is the number-one mascara at Sephora,” Tournery said. “We sell one every 10 seconds. It’s really an iconic product that women keep buying and we are very proud of.”
Sephora Collection masks also keep whipping up a lather of excitement. “We are still selling 60 masks every minute since the beginning of the year,” she said. “It’s our number-one volume product.”
In accessories, the brand has a strong leadership position with its makeup brushes.
“One out of two brushes sold at Sephora are Sephora Collection brushes,” Tournery said. “But also in the makeup artistry universe, a lot of makeup artists know them and use them. So we have a very strong credibility that was built over time.”
As the beauty market and pace of innovation evolve rapidly, “our objective now is to make sure that we are selective enough in the innovation we bring to the market on one side,” she said. On the other side, the aim is to be certain that Sephora Collection icons are scaled in all geographic markets.
“We still have potential to make them even bigger,” Tournery said.
Vis-à-vis innovation and segments, makeup is Sephora Collection’s main business driver today and the category for which it is known.
“It’s more than half of our business, and we will continue innovating,” she said, referring to Sephora Collection’s new mascara, It’s a Stretch, in the tubing category, as an example. “It’s already the number-three mascara at Sephora. It shows the appetite for the brand in makeup is super strong, and we will continue fueling this fire.
“[Still], we want to make sure we grow other categories, such as skin care, which is a big growth driver for the retailer Sephora overall,” Tournery continued. “We definitely have a card to play on the category.”
Global expansion is the third dimension of Sephora Collection’s strategy.
“We are historically very strong in Europe,” she said. “It’s our historical market, and most of our growth comes from Europe today. We are very strong globally in all regions — don’t get me wrong — but we see a bigger potential to grow, especially in the U.S. first and in China.
“It means being globally consistent, but also locally relevant,” Tournery explained. “It’s something that is very important for us and is really in the DNA of Sephora — to make sure that even if we can recognize the brand globally, we are locally adapted in terms of offer.”
That means having specific shades and specific products according to consumer needs across regions, as well as how the Sephora Collection brands are activated.
In China, for instance, Sephora Collection recently collaborated with the popular domestic fashion brand Goodbai, founded by Chinese actor and singer Bai Jingting, on specific products.
“It was a big success,” Tournery said. “And beyond the business, it shows the importance for us to be culturally relevant, local and [certain] that we understand locally the consumers’ needs.”
The fourth prong of Sephora Collection’s strategy is having an organization to meet the brand’s ambition.
“It’s about agility,” Tournery said. “It’s always been in the DNA of Sephora and Sephora Collection to have this ability to be very fast, agile in the way we work. We want to continue to lead in that game and make sure that we work hard keeping that step ahead, because the market is becoming so fast, and overall competitivity has become less about innovation and more about speed of innovation.”
When a trend is emerging on TikTok, coming from the field or any worldwide city, the goal is for Sephora Collection to capture it quickly.
“We decide very fast if we want to go for it and make it a product,” Tournery said. “Then we work very closely with our partners, our suppliers, to bring it as fast as possible to the market, while keeping our quality very high — because we never compromise on quality.
“These are the four dimensions of the strategy to make us grow even stronger and be even a stronger driver of growth for Sephora overall,” Tournery said.
“Sephora Collection is at the heart of our success,” Motte said. “It’s at the heart of who we are. It bears our name. When we talk about our purpose, our values, it is there also.”
