Smart glasses company Xreal has released its magnum opus—the Xreal Aura, a $1,279 pair of extended-reality glasses that runs on Google’s Android XR platform. The Aura glasses can project virtual screens into the wearer’s field of vision and include augmented-reality elements for use in the real world.
The Aura glasses arrive just before the first wave of Android XR glasses from Google and Samsung, in partnership with glasses manufacturers Warby Parker and Gentle Monster. Those devices aim squarely at Meta’s smart-glasses empire. But the Aura glasses are more like virtual reality headsets, now in a more comfortable, glasses-like design. They go head-to-head with Meta’s VR Glasses, slated for release in early 2027.
The first big caveat about the Aura glasses is that they’re wired. A long cord hooks onto the end of one of the glasses’ arms via a USB-C cable. That cord connects to an attached compute puck, about the size of a smartphone and weighing a little more than a half-pound. It comes in 12 or 16 GB storage options and has a battery that powers the glasses for roughly four to five hours with constant use.
When the Aura glasses work, they’re wonderful. The glasses are pretty lightweight, at 95 grams. A four-mic array sits in the arms with Bose EQ tuning that sounds great. (You can also connect wireless headphones.) The display screen has a 1920 x 1200-pixel-per-eye resolution that is noticeably better than other Xreal glasses I’ve worn. A 70-degree field of view means the screens and virtual elements pop up in a wide frame of the wearer’s vision. That means the outer edges of your view will still be the real-life room around you. That can help if you’re trying not to fall over, but it can also be noticeable when trying to feel immersed in a movie.
The big thing about Aura is that you can, in theory, run every app that already exists on Android. Xreal has a few primary categories for how it expects people to use its Aura glasses. The big one is entertainment. Like other Xreal glasses, the Aura can project a screen right in your field of vision. You can watch movies, TV shows, or YouTube videos, right in your field of view. The virtual screen stays fixed, or it can follow you around as you turn your head. You can also unplug the glasses from the compute puck and use them as display glasses to extend your Steam Deck’s screen, for example, though I had issues with that when playing more graphics-intensive games like Cyberpunk 2077.
