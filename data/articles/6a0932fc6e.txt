In short:
Kurtley Beale, 37, has been named on the bench for the Wallabies ahead of their clash against Ireland.
Allan Alaalatoa will make his 100th appearance, with Teddy Wilson set to make his debut.
What's next?
The Wallabies take on Ireland at Twickenham at 6:10am AEDT on Saturday morning.
Kurtley Beale's return to the gold jumper is no sentimental, one-off reward for the veteran playing a selfless backroom role and being a mentor to young Wallabies this past month.
Coach Les Kiss says Australia's great rugby maverick has won his place back at the age of 37 absolutely on merit.
There's not a soul in the Wallabies' camp who has not been enthusing about the 37-year-old's return to the starting 23 in Friday's (Saturday AEDT) Nations Championship finale against Ireland at Twickenham.
Players such as Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii have been gushing about how he has been an inspirational presence in the team room and on the training park following his shock call-up to the squad for the spring tour to the UK.
But Kiss was quick to make it clear on Wednesday that Beale's selection on the Test team's bench for the first time since he won cap number 95 for the Wallabies against Wales in 2021 was no sentimental end-of-tour present.
"This isn't a gift to Kurtley," Kiss said.
"I couldn't speak highly enough of him. He has been unbelievable and he's been selected on merit. He's earned it; that's the bottom line."
With Australia going for a six-two split of forwards and backs on the bench, Kiss felt Beale's remarkable versatility — he has played in five different positions in the Wallabies' back line over his 17-year international career — could be crucial on Friday.
It means he has jettisoned both Ben Donaldson and Filipo Daugunu, two of the rare Australian success stories off the bench on a trying trip this past month, with Kiss admitting it was tough on the pair.
"But if you saw Kurtley's Super Aus games [for the Western Force], he played at 10, then he went to 12. He helped engineer those wins,'' the coach said.
"He was kicking goals from everywhere, kicking corners. And that type of influence has come through in every session, every video session, every computer session on this trip.
"He's earned his call. He's been nothing short of exceptional, really, how he's worked with the group, brought his wisdom, been patient, and he's clearly earned his spot, not only in my eyes but in his peers' eyes."
Suaalii was among those singing Beale's praises.
"I grew up watching Kurtley. I've always admired him from afar,'' he said.
"I've enjoyed working with him, just sitting at computers, picking his brain off the field, and just seeing what he's like as a person and how much he loves the game.
"How he sees the game is different from a lot of people, and I've just been asking him different questions and just trying to grow my game as a player. He's a great man, and the crew love him."
Up front, Allan Alaalatoa will earn a 100th cap while, at the other end of the spectrum, winger James Ramm will make his debut.
Prop Alaalatoa is the 14th Wallabies centurion, having made his debut in 2016. He will line up with Isaac Kailea and hooker Brandon Paenga-Amosa in the front row, with Harry Wilson dropping to the bench.
"We've taken some steps forward, grown our game and tested our squad depth this year and I know the team is highly motivated to put in a performance that reflects the hard work put in by everyone in our final test," Kiss said.
Ireland, meanwhile, has made two changes from the side that beat the Springboks last week in Dublin, with Australian-born winger Mack Hansen out of the squad.
