The financial regulator has admitted that it has miscalculated part of the compensation scheme for victims of the car finance scandal, and told Barclays to stop making settlement offers while it works through the data problems.
The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) revealed it had identified “some errors in how we categorised a small group of loan agreements’, which will affect the average compensation payout to drivers.
The errors were discovered as the FCA prepared its response to a legal challenge against its £9.1bn compensation scheme for drivers who were overcharged for loans as a result of commission payments between lenders and car dealers.
That scheme, announced in March, prompted a legal battle that has forced the regulator to partly suspend it. A hearing to decide what happens is due to go ahead in February.
“While the potential impact on average compensation is small – less than £10 per agreement, on average – we recognise this affects ongoing litigation,” the FCA said on Thursday. “We are sorry for the further uncertainty this creates for consumers and firms. We will provide an update on any further material developments.”
Earlier this month Barclays, which has set aside £430m in connection with alleged motor finance mis-selling, started offering compensation settlements.
Barclays is the first big UK lender to begin offering payouts to some customers who have complained, with hopes the move might mean some receive payments by Christmas.
“Barclays has been offering full and final settlement offers to their customers,” the FCA said. “We have asked that they pause making these offers while we work through the data issues identified and implications they might have, if any.”
The FCA said two of the legal challengers are due to serve their expert evidence on Friday.
“We will discuss with the challengers and the court the impact on this deadline, and for the timeline of the litigation,” the FCA said.
Lenders have set aside about £7.5bn to pay compensation owed, and are preparing to handle large volumes of complaints.
