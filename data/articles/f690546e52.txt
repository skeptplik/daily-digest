Australian politics live: Government lacks ‘moral authority’ on decarbonisation, Monique Ryan says; eight teens arrested over alleged schoolies assault
Australia lacks ‘moral authority’ to advocate for global decarbonisation, Ryan says
Over to the cross-bench, Monique Ryan points out that the Cop31 statement led by Australia did not mention the words fossil fuels.
She asks:
Prime minister, did you accept that a government which still approves new fossil fuel projects and expansions lacks the moral authority to effectively advocate for global decarbonisation?
Anthony Albanese again says he accepts the science of climate change, but that you can’t change from one type of fuel to another overnight.
(That doesn’t explain why the statement completely omitted mentioning fossil fuels though.)
Albanese says:
What I also absolutely support is that if action on climate change – which I believe does have support of the Australian public – is to maintain that support, is that when people go and flick on a switch to turn on the light, if the light doesn’t go on, then that support will go off. That is what I support. And that’s why we support a sensible orderly transition to renewables.
Tennis Australia condemns abuse after graphic threats towards teen player
Tennis Australia has thrown its support behind up-and-coming player Taylah Lessue after a slew of “abhorrent” online abuse and death threats were directed at the 18-year-old at a tournament in Adelaide.
Lessue won the first set 6-4 before dropping the next two to love as fatigue, chest tightness and muscle spasms set in. The teenager had been suffering from glandular fever.
Within minutes of Sunday’s match in Adelaide concluding, graphic threats of sexual violence and physical threats against her person appeared on Lessue’s social media.
Tennis Australia told Guardian Australian it condemns abuse of all types and that abuse has no place in sport. A spokesperson said:
The safety and wellbeing of our players is the highest priority, and we have a member of the TA Integrity team onsite supporting the player and family.
Labor moves to expedite vote on digital duty of care in Senate
With the government having secured the support of the Greens for its digital duty of care bill, its eager to get it passed ASAP.
The leader of the government in the Senate, Penny Wong, has moved to suspend standing orders to bring forward debate on the bill by 6:30pm for one hour, and then vote on the bill after about 10am tomorrow.
The Greens are supportive (as you would expect) but others on the cross-bench, including David Pocock, Lidia Thorpe and Fatima Payman, vote against it because it crunches down the time to debate the bill.

Eight arrested over alleged assault in the Gold Coast that left 15-year-old fighting for life
Zeina Khochaiche
Eight teenagers have been arrested over an alleged assault in Surfers Paradise on the Gold Coast that left a 15-year-old fighting for his life.
Queensland police said the incident took place at about 3.20am today.
Eight males were located and arrested, including four 17-year-olds, three 18-year-olds and one 19-year-old. The four 17-year-olds were registered as schoolies attenders with the Queensland government, police said in a press conference.
The 15-year-old boy sustained significant head injuries in what police described as a “prolonged” attack. He was transported to the Gold Coast university hospital in a critical condition.
In a separate incident, a 42-year-old Runcorn man was found with serious injuries to his head and body in Broadbeach, on the Gold Coast, just after midnight.
Three teenagers, two who were registered as schoolies attenders, had charged over the Broadbeach assault, police said. They were scheduled to appear at Southport children’s court today.
Police said the man was assisting a girl who allegedly had her phone stolen by the three teenagers when the attack took place. The man was transported to Robina hospital.
Police are investigating the connection between the incidents and schoolies.

Nationals MP quotes ‘Jim from Logan’ in question sidestepped by PM
The Nationals MP Alison Penfold is up next and quotes “Jim from Logan” (ie the treasurer, Jim Chalmers), saying “interest rates are biting, unemployment is rising, prices are going up, wages aren’t keeping pace, and families are hurting.” She asks, when will the prime minister end his government’s “assault” on aspirational Australians.
Anthony Albanese hears just one word from that question, aspiration, and then talks about that.
I’m asked about aspiration. I’ve been many times to Logan. A fantastic place.
He then defends other government decisions, such as changes to stage three tax cuts. We don’t really learn anything new.
‘Our job is to lead’ Albanese deflects questions calling for frontbench resignations
The Liberal MP Mary Aldred takes a slightly different approach, targeting both Jim Chalmers and Chris Bowen in the one question, asking when the prime minister will sack these “failed ministers”.
She has to ask the question twice after Milton Dick tells Labor MPs, who are shouting, to be quiet. That was just moments after he kicked out another Labor MP Meryl Swanson and implored the chamber to please behave.
Albanese repeats familiar lines about how Labor believes in climate change and a strong economy.
But what our job is to do, isn’t to follow – it’s to lead. It’s to lead. To lead Australia to where Australia needs to go.
I’ll just note here that every time Albanese is asked one of these questions, he doesn’t actually defend either frontbencher.
‘Never any light from those opposite’: Coalition go for treasurer’s scalp
The shadow treasurer, Tim Wilson, is at the despatch box now, and asks when the prime minister will get rid of his treasurer, with his colleagues sick of “his daily diet of doom, denial, and deflection”.
Anthony Albanese takes a personal shot at Wilson:
I thank the shadow treasurer, who has an appropriate title, given there’s never any light, never any light from those opposite.
He then spends the rest of the answer talking about how things aren’t really that bad and that the economy is better than that of other nations.
The treasurer’s statement [to the house] set out that path forward: the government playing our part to get inflation down, making sensible savings, making sure at the same time we don’t leave people behind.
