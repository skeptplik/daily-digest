The ransomware attack is over. Your team has restored the client’s data, brought systems back online and put business operations back on track. From a technical standpoint, the recovery was a success.
But the client still has questions. Why did updates go quiet for two days? What drove the recovery costs up? And where did the 31 hours of emergency labor on the invoice come from?
Recovering from ransomware is only part of the job. Clients also need to know what happened, what recovery involved and what it cost them. When communication falls short or unexpected charges appear, even a technically successful recovery can leave clients questioning the value of their MSP relationship.
The disruption may be behind them, but their doubts can carry into the next renewal conversation.
The trend: Slow, fragmented recovery erodes trust and renewals
Ransomware recovery can take days, even when teams have backups and a response plan in place. According to industry reports, only 16% of affected organizations fully recovered within a day. Over half (55%) recovered within a week. For businesses that depend on their systems to serve customers and generate revenue, every additional day of disruption carries a cost.
The stakes extend to the MSP, too. Kaseya’s 2026 Cybersecurity Outlook report found that 12% of MSPs lost clients following security incidents, with ransomware, account takeover and business email compromise among the main reasons.
When recovery involves delays, fragmented efforts or gaps in communication, clients struggle to understand what is happening and when they can expect normal operations to resume. They may also question unexpected costs or wonder whether their provider has the situation under control.
Clients expect cyberattacks to happen, but they also expect their MSP to respond quickly, coordinate recovery and keep them informed. When those expectations go unmet, a technically successful recovery may still leave clients questioning whether they should renew their contracts.
A successful recovery depends on both the technical response and the client experience. MSPs that set expectations early, communicate consistently and explain costs before they become surprises have a stronger chance of preserving client trust long after the systems come back online.
Slow, disconnected recovery puts renewals at risk, even when the restore itself goes well.

Where your recovery process breaks down
Most MSPs have skilled people. The trouble starts when those people work across disconnected tools in the middle of a crisis.
Tool sprawl slows everyone down
Backup, monitoring, ticketing and billing live in separate consoles. Your techs spend incident hours piecing together context instead of restoring systems.
Handoffs break
An alert fires in one tool, a ticket opens in another and a restore job runs in a third. Nothing links them, so no one can say what the attack touched or what’s still down.
Billing falls through the cracks
Emergency labor, restore time and after-hours work often go untracked. Someone reconstructs those hours from memory days later. You either lose margin or send an invoice the client doesn’t recognize, and you do it at the worst possible time.
Client updates depend on who’s free
Without a single ticket trail, communication comes down to whoever has a spare minute. Some clients get a call every few hours. Others hear nothing until the work is done.
Each of these problems looks small by itself. Together, they turn a recoverable incident into a trust problem.
5 signs your recovery process is costing you client trust
- Clients chase you for updates. They have to ask what’s happening instead of hearing from your team.
- Your team scrambles across tools. Technicians waste valuable recovery time piecing together information from disconnected systems.
- No one owns the full picture. Handoffs leave gaps in who knows what’s restored, what’s still down and what needs attention.
- Recovery bills come as a surprise. Emergency labor and after-hours work appear on invoices without clear explanations.
- Recovery ends without clear answers. Clients are left unsure what happened, what it cost or what you’ll do to prevent a repeat.
What good recovery looks like for an MSP
Strong recovery runs on one timeline and one source of truth. Everyone on your team sees the same facts, so your client hears the same story no matter who calls. Updates go out on a schedule you set, before the client asks.
When the incident closes, the invoice matches the work, because your team logged the work as it happened.
Track three numbers to see how you’re doing:
- Time to first client update: How long after detection does the client hear from you?
- Time to restore: How long until their critical systems run again?
- Time to invoice: How fast can you send an accurate bill after closing the incident?
If any of these takes too long, you have a process gap that clients will notice. Closing those gaps takes more than working harder during an incident. It takes tools that share data from the start.
