In short:
Carlos Alcaraz comes back to beat Ben Shelton in three sets at the ATP Finals.
Alcaraz stretched his overall winning streak to 13 matches with a 3-6, 6-3, 7-6 (7-1) victory to reach the final in Turin.
What's next?
Alcaraz will face Alexander Zverev in the final after the German beat Daniil Medvedev.
Seven-times grand slam champion Carlos Alcaraz battled back from a set down to beat American Ben Shelton 3-6, 6-3, 7-6 (7-1) in their ATP Finals semifinal to charge into Sunday's showcase for the second consecutive year.
Alcaraz, beaten by Shelton in the US Open quarterfinals on the Spaniard's return from a four-month injury break, stretched his winning run to 13 matches after back-to-back titles in Vienna and Paris.
He will face Alexander Zverev, who beat Daniil Medvedev of Russia in the other semifinal.
"I was a little bit frustrated at the beginning because I didn't start the match the way I wanted to," said Alcaraz, unbeaten on indoor courts this season.
"Just happy that I came back in the second set playing such great tennis, step by step, focused on all my things and ended up playing good tennis. The third set was even closer."
Shelton, making his second consecutive appearance in the season finale, grabbed an early break to race to a 3-0 lead.
Alcaraz, whose fitness had come under scrutiny after he left the court with heavy strapping on his left leg following Wednesday's win over Medvedev, had his thigh wrapped heavily against Shelton.
He upped his game and started moving the American around more, engaging him in longer rallies. He earned two break points at 4-2 but could not convert either, with Shelton's booming serve getting him out of trouble.
The world number four fired a second-serve ace on his first set point to move ahead but Alcaraz went 5-3 up in the second before serving out the second set.
The pair traded blows in the third set before Alcaraz eased through the tie-break, sealing the win with a return winner.
Zverev beat Daniil Medvedev 7-6 (0), 6-4 in the second semi-final at the elite season-ending event for the year’s top eight players.
In their only meeting this year, Alcaraz beat Zverev in the Australian Open semifinals. The Spaniard also holds a 7-6 edge in career meetings.
"We had a practice before the tournament started. It was unbelievable. We played one set for one and a half hours. So I hope it’s going to be similar tomorrow," Zverev said.
"I hope both of us are going to be at our best and hopefully it’s a really entertaining match for everybody."
