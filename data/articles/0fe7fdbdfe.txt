The Bundeswehr is moving into retail: with its own online shop, the German armed forces aim to improve their public image and establish themselves as a brand. The launch range includes T-shirts, mugs and a rubber duck armed with an assault rifle – which proved so popular that it quickly sold out.
Anyone who likes a bit of military company in the bath might soon find just what they are looking for at the Bundeswehr. Germany's armed forces have launched their own online shop, and one of its most eye-catching products is, of all things, a camouflage-clad rubber duck armed with a miniature assault rifle.
What might initially sound like a joke is, in fact, part of a new marketing strategy. Through the shop, the Bundeswehr aims to reshape its public image, raise its profile and, above all, appeal to younger audiences. Alongside the unusual rubber duck, the range also features other branded merchandise and clothing.
Bundeswehr duck makes a splash
The duck is attracting particular attention. The small rubber toy, dressed in military gear, has quickly become a bestseller and was temporarily sold out shortly after the shop launched.
Demand for the new online shop has exceeded expectations, reportedly causing technical issues soon after launch. Clearly, military-themed marketing can make quite a splash, even when it ends up in the bath.
Bundeswehr seeks to boost its public image
Behind the unusual merchandise is a serious objective: the Bundeswehr wants to raise its public profile and build its brand beyond barracks, trade fairs and recruitment campaigns.
Recruitment is a key priority as the armed forces seek to expand their ranks. The merchandise is designed to make the Bundeswehr more visible in everyday life and strengthen its connection with the public.
Criticism of military marketing
Not everyone is convinced by the new strategy. Critics warn that merchandise and light-hearted promotional items risk trivialising the realities of military service. The marketing of weapons and military equipment as lifestyle products has drawn particular criticism.
The strategy has also drawn criticism from politicians. Representatives of the Left Party warn that such marketing risks making the military an increasingly normal part of everyday life.
From the parade ground to the bathtub
Whether the rubber duck will actually help attract new recruits remains to be seen. But as a promotional tool, it has already completed its first mission: generating attention, sparking conversation and, apparently, selling out in record time.
And who knows what might come next? Perhaps a lifebuoy in Bundeswehr camouflage for emergencies in the bubble bath, or a mini drone to keep an eye on rising foam levels.
A camp bed for exhausted bath ducks could also be on the cards. After all, the troops need to be prepared for every mission – even in the bubble bath.
