Russian strike on Zelenskyy's hometown in central Ukraine kills 2, wounds 60
Missile attack in Kryvyi Rih came hours after 2 Russian missiles came down in Poland
A Russian strike on the centre of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's hometown, Kryvyi Rih, killed two people and wounded 60 others, local officials said Saturday.
A seven-year-old boy and a 20-year-old woman were killed in the attack Friday night on the city in central Ukraine, the head of the city administration, Oleksandr Vilkul, wrote on the Telegram messaging service.
Oleksandr Hanzha, head of the regional military administration, said 60 people were wounded, including six children.
The strike caused four fires and damaged about 25 apartment buildings, a hospital, schools and an industrial facility, as well as businesses and vehicles, Vilkul said.
The attack fits the pattern of Moscow's heavy responses to eye-catching Ukrainian attacks, such as the ones this week on the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to illegally annexed Crimea.
The site of a Russian drone and missile attack on Friday night in central Kryvyi Rih, Zelenskyy's hometown. (Press service of the National Police of Ukraine/Reuters)
The missile attack came hours after two Russian missiles and a suspected drone came down in Poland, in what its prime minister said was likely a provocation timed to coincide with European military exercises in his country for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine.
European troops were taking part on Friday in an unprecedented two-week military exercise that began Nov. 9 in southeastern Poland.
Forces are preparing for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine aimed at helping to stabilize the country and rebuild its military.
European countries previously have expressed alarm at what they deem to be Russia's attempts to destabilize the continent.
The aftermath of Friday night's Russian drone and missile attack in central Kryvyi Rih. The attack killed two people, including a child. (Press service of the National Police of Ukraine/Reuters)
NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said in Warsaw on Saturday that the alliance is commited to defending "every inch of Polish territory."
The development stoked tensions between Europe and Moscow as the U.S. tried to build momentum for talks on ending the war, which began with Russia's invasion on Feb. 24, 2022.
U.S. President Donald Trump discussed the war with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping at a summit in China on Thursday, according to his envoy, Steve Witkoff. Trump called Putin his "friend" during the meeting and continued to tout their relationship at a White House news conference on Friday.
David McGuinty, Canada's defence minister, said in an interview with CBC's Adrienne Arsenault that Ottawa and leaders in Europe are anticipating the Trump administration will become "less forthcoming" in its support for Ukraine, but noted Canada is staying the course.
"The United States is going to do what the United States is going to do as a sovereign country," McGuinty said.
"But we're … working hand-in-glove with our European colleagues to make sure that the Ukrainians get the support they need."
Zelenskyy calls Putin 'a killer'
Asked about Trump's remarks in an interview with CNN, Zelenskyy called Putin "a killer."
"For us, he is an enemy. And of course, for us, he is a killer," Zelenskyy said Friday. He warned that describing the Russian leader in friendly terms could mean "he doesn't feel enough pressure."
Elsewhere in Ukraine, 11 people, including two children, were wounded when a Russian drone attacked a five-storey apartment building in the Kharkiv region, Ukraine's State Emergency Service said Saturday. One person was wounded in a Russian attack on the capital, Kyiv, according to the service.
In Russia, local officials said Saturday that Ukrainian drone attacks wounded three people in the Bryansk and Belgorod border regions.
A firefighter works at the site of a Russian missile strike in Kyiv, during Russia's attack on Ukraine on Friday night into Saturday. (Press service of the State Emergency Service of Ukraine/Reuters)
Russian cargo vessel on fire in Finnish waters
Meanwhile, Russian officials said on Saturday that a cargo vessel bound for Kaliningrad was attacked by two drones in neutral waters.
The crew was being evacuated and the extent of the damage was being assessed, said Aleksandr Drozdenko, the Leningrad regional governor, shortly after the attack was first reported.
The ship caught fire after being hit by drones in the Baltic Sea and later drifted into Finnish waters, prompting Helsinki to impose temporary air and maritime restrictions. It said Ukraine was probably behind the attack.
The Russian cargo ship Sparta II is seen damaged in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday. (Finnish Border Guard/Reuters)
Finland's Border Guard said there was no danger to people or maritime traffic, that rescue units were at the scene, and that it was co-ordinating operations with neighbouring countries.
The Sparta II vessel is known to have links to Russia's armed forces, Finnish Border Guard Cmdr. Ilja Iljin told Reuters.
"At the moment, there is no confirmed information about what cargo is on board, but it cannot be ruled out that there is military material on board," he said.
"The war is spilling over into our neighbouring areas. Finnish authorities are handling the situation in the Gulf of Finland, where a drone strike has hit a Russian merchant vessel. Ukraine has the right to fight against the aggressor," Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said on X.
Ukraine's foreign ministry did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
Russia's embassy in Helsinki did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
The vessel is subject to U.S. sanctions and is linked to Russian shipping company Oboronlogistika, according to the U.S. Treasury Department.
Oboronlogistika provides logistics services for the Russian defence ministry, according to the company's website.
Kaliningrad Gov. Alexei Besprozvannykh said the incident posed no threat to supplies to the region. Kaliningrad, a Russian exclave on the Baltic Sea that borders Poland and Lithuania, has occasionally been a flashpoint between Russia, the European Union and NATO.
