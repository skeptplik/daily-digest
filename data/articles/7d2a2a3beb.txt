In short:
Cameron Smith and Lucas Herbert have agreed to a deal with the DP World Tour that allows them to play "a specific number of tournaments".
Fellow former LIV Golf player Bryson DeChambeau has received unconditional membership to the tour.
What's next?
Smith will tee up at the Australian PGA Championship and Australian Open in the coming weeks.
Cameron Smith has found a new home after the demise of LIV Golf, joining the European Tour, albeit on a limited basis.
Australian superstar Smith, along with several other former LIV players, including compatriot Lucas Herbert, has agreed to a deal that involves invites to a "specific number of tournaments".
These will not include the tour's highest-profile Rolex Series events, but smaller ones at which the presence of the former LIV players would "add value", the European Tour said on Tuesday.
The 2022 British Open winner joins a raft of former players on the now-defunct rebel circuit to have joined the European Tour, known as the DP World Tour.
They are headlined by big-hitting Bryson DeChambeau, who signed just before the deadline, and former world number one Jon Rahm. DeChambeau joins in "category one" status, meaning the American can play in whichever tournaments he chooses.
However, it looks as though he will not feature in the two Australian events that open the tour: The Australian PGA Championship at The Lakes, Sydney, starting on November 26, followed by the Australian Open at Melbourne's Kingston Heath, opening on December 3.
The latter clashes with the Golf Challenge in Sun City, South Africa, and DeChambeau has said he will play that. Smith was already on the entry list for both Australian events.
Former major winners Dustin Johnson and Bubba Watson have joined on a similar basis to Smith and Herbert, as have Abraham Ancer, Michael La Sasso, Joaquin Niemann, Carlos Ortiz and Thomas Pieters.
It is not yet clear which tournaments the newcomers will play in.
"The inventory of invites across the season has been carefully managed by the tour's Player Relations Department, following extensive modelling to ensure and preserve playing opportunities for existing members," said the Tour, in a reference to concern among existing members that opportunities could be curtailed by an influx of LIV Golf players.
Eighteen players competing on LIV in 2026 are now on the European Tour, including one-time stalwarts of the circuit such as former Ryder Cup stars Lee Westwood, Sergio Garcia, Henrik Stenson and Ian Poulter.
Rahm and Tyrrell Hatton — winners of the last two events of the 2026 European season — had already announced they would be returning to the tour after the collapse of LIV.
LIV Golf, which shook the sport to its foundations when it launched its breakaway tour in 2022, announced this month it would begin winding down operations after losing key players and the funding needed to continue operating.
Smith is one of several players owed significant amounts of money by the collapsed Tour.
