Juniper Networks and the US cybersecurity agency CISA this week warned that threat actors have been exploiting a critical vulnerability in Session Smart Conductor.
Tracked as CVE-2026-103406 (CVSS score of 9.8), the security defect is described as a remotely exploitable OS command injection bug.
It affects the Session Smart Conductor’s AutomatedProvisioner service and could allow attackers to execute arbitrary commands with root privileges via a specially crafted Salt minion ID.
A centralized management and policy solution, Session Smart Conductor is used for the orchestration and management of Session Smart Routers across networks. The Salt minion ID is a router’s unique ID when registering with the Salt master (Session Smart Conductor).
CVE-2026-103406 was patched on October 28 along with 17 other flaws in Session Smart Router and Session Smart Conductor. On Tuesday, Juniper updated its advisory to flag the security defect as exploited.
“At least one instance of malicious exploitation of CVE-2026-103406 has been reported to the Juniper SIRT. Customers are encouraged to upgrade to a fixed release as soon as possible and in the meantime take steps to mitigate this vulnerability,” reads Juniper’s update.
On Wednesday, CISA added the bug to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog, urging federal agencies to patch it within three days, as mandated by BOD 26-04.
Although BOD 26-04 only applies to federal civilian executive branch agencies, all organizations are advised to review CISA’s KEV list and address the relevant security weaknesses as soon as possible.
Juniper patched CVE-2026-103406 in Session Smart Router and Session Smart Conductor versions 7.2.3, 7.1.7, and 7.0.5.
CISA also added five Mitel MiCollab flaws, a Vates Xen Orchestra issue, and three Danfoss AK-SM 800A series vulnerabilities to the KEV list.
