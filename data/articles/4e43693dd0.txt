A Boston man won $1M US in the lottery. Days later, ICE arrested him
The ticket 'ultimately led to his arrest,' ICE said, without elaborating how
Ariel Antonio Velasquez-Morales held an oversized cheque for $1 million US and smiled in the photo posted Saturday by the Massachusetts State Lottery.
Local media described the Boston man as "all smiles," reporting that he planned to save and invest his winnings after matching 11 words on a $10 US scratch ticket. Lottery officials said it was the first puzzle he'd ever scratched.
A few days later, his photo was posted online again — but this time, by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), which said it had arrested the "illegal alien from El Salvador" on Tuesday. Now, Velasquez-Morales will remain in ICE custody until he's removed from the U.S., the agency said in a news release.
The ticket "ultimately led to his arrest," ICE said, without elaborating how. CBC News has reached out to ICE and not yet heard back. Velasquez-Morales does not yet appear on ICE's Online Detainee Locator System, where members of the public can search for people held in the agency's custody.
“Looks like his luck ran out,” said ICE public affairs assistant director Micah Bock in the news release.
"Welcome to the ERO [enforcement and removal operations] Boston lottery! Where illegal aliens and sanctuary politicians are out of luck, and the law-abiding citizens of New England are always the winners!" ICE Boston posted on X.
It's unclear if Velasquez-Morales has legal representation. CBC News has reached out to several Boston-area immigration lawyers and not yet heard back.
A woman identifying herself as Velasquez-Morales's sister-in-law wrote on Facebook Wednesday asking for respect and empathy for him, his wife and children.
"There is a family waiting for him," she wrote in Spanish.
Another woman identifying herself as Velasquez-Morales's older sister commented on the same post, writing her wishes that he would soon be back home with his family.
CBC News has reached out to both women for comment and not yet heard back.
A photo of Ariel Velasquez-Morales posted by ICE in a new release on Nov. 25, announcing that ICE Boston had arrested the man a day prior. (U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement)
Can you win the lottery if you're not a citizen?
Velasquez-Morales illegally entered the U.S. in 2013 through the Texas border, according to ICE. An immigration judge ordered him removed in 2014, the press release noted, but "he illegally remained in the country."
While ICE posted on social media that "the rights and privileges reserved for American citizens are for American citizens," state lotteries typically do not require citizenship or residency — if you meet the age requirement and are physically present in a participating jurisdiction when buying the ticket, you can play.

CBC News has reached out to the Massachusetts State Lottery and has not yet heard back. But its website help desk notes that anyone age 18 and up who is physically present in the state can buy lottery tickets at retail locations and win prizes. The Boston Globe reports that the lottery also doesn't require winners to have social security numbers, but that non-resident winners often face higher federal taxes on their winnings.
Some U.S. states allow lottery winners to remain anonymous no matter the amount, others depend on how much a person win, but many — including Massachusetts — do not let winners claim prizes anonymously.
"The Massachusetts State Lottery treats a winner’s name, town and prize as public information and releases them," explains the Massachusetts Legal Resources website.
"The lottery’s position, and the state’s, is that publicity is part of the game: It demonstrates that real people win, and it deters fraud in the claiming process."
Michael Baker, a Massachusetts attorney who works with lottery winners, told the Boston Globe that immigrants may not realize how much exposure winning the lottery can bring them.
"I’m sure people, when they play, they’re not expecting being at risk of being arrested," he told the media outlet.
