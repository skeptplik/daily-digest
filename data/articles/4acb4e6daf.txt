Nearly half of consumers say they will do most of their holiday shopping in the five days between Thanksgiving and Cyber Monday. However, last-minute shoppers may give fashion sales a substantial boost, according to two new studies.
A new study from data analytics firm Circana found that 43 percent of consumers expect to do most of their holiday shopping during the five days.
Black Friday will likely be the busiest, with 24 percent of consumers saying they expect to do the largest share of their holiday shopping on that day, according to Circana’s annual Holiday Purchase Intentions study.
Meanwhile, more than half of consumers (56 percent) expect to complete their holiday shopping in December, including 17 percent of shoppers who say they typically shop right up until the last minute.
“Consumers have entered the holiday season with a plan, and they appear to be sticking to it,” said Kiara Barrett, head of global thought leadership at Circana.
A second wind for fashion sales
Don’t count out last-minute shoppers, though.
Apparel and beauty purchases typically increase in the final two weeks before Christmas, Deluxe Corp. said, based on an analysis of credit card transactions processed across thousands of small and medium-sized retailers over the past few years.
While sporting-goods retailers that carry hunting equipment bag the biggest haul around Thanksgiving, fashion sales enjoy a second wind ahead of Christmas, the report showed.
Last year, Deluxe said apparel and accessories posted the largest increase among the categories, with average transactions per merchant increasing 20 percent during Christmas week compared with Thanksgiving week.
Beauty services, on the other hand, saw an 18 percent increase in average interactions per merchant during the same comparative periods, while sporting goods merchants saw 18.9 percent more payment volume per merchant.
To understand these patterns, the financial technology firm analyzed credit card transactions processed across approximately 50,000 small and medium-sized retailers from 2022 to 2025.
“Understanding how and when consumers spend empowers businesses to make better decisions about staffing, inventory, and marketing, and we’re in a great position to help small- and medium-sized businesses interpret the data Deluxe has accumulated,” said Kevin Jones, president of Deluxe Merchant Services.
Transactions increased across all retail categories analyzed. However, growth slowed substantially over the four years, as consumers tighten their wallets to cope with rising prices.
In 2022, the average transactions per merchant were 17 percent higher during the peak Christmas shopping week than Thanksgiving week. The gap narrowed to 16 percent in 2023 and just one percent in 2024, before widening to four percent in 2025.
