U.S. President Donald Trump said on Thursday (November 26, 2026) he had asked Vladimir Putin to "pull back all military aggression" against Europe, in a fresh shift in tone towards the Russian leader he called his friend just last week.
Mr. Trump said he had requested Mr. Putin when they met in China last week, adding that he thought it would help lead to a deal to end Moscow's grinding war in Ukraine.
Mr. Trump's comments come a day after he hosted NATO chief Mark Rutte in Florida, after which the two leaders issued a joint statement calling on Russia not to expand the Ukraine war.
"During my conversations in China with President Vladimir Putin, of Russia, I spent a lot of time talking about the fact that I would like to see President Putin pull back all Military aggression against territories throughout Europe," Mr. Trump said on his Truth Social network.
Mr. Trump said he had done so "in the hopes that a Deal can be made between Russia and Ukraine, and made quickly."
"I really believe this is a strong possibility," he added.
The statement did not specify which territories Mr. Trump meant -- and did not specifically include Ukraine, which Russia invaded in February 2022, kicking off the largest war in Europe since World War II.
European nations have been increasingly concerned by what they regard as Russian provocations, including three Russian cruise missiles landing in NATO-member Poland last week.
In their joint statement on Wednesday (November 25), Mr. Trump and Mr. Rutte said they "discussed the importance of Russia avoiding any actions that intentionally, or unintentionally, expand the war."
In a notable contrast with Mr. Trump's recent criticisms of Ukraine for striking targets deep inside Russia, the U.S. leader made no call for Kyiv to show similar restraint.
Speaking to reporters at his Mar-a-Lago estate in Florida on Thursday (November 26), Mr. Trump said he was unsure if Mr. Putin would be coming to the G20 summit next month in Miami, but "but if he wanted to come, he certainly can."
'Deadlock'
Mr. Trump's tone appeared markedly different to when Mr. Trump met Mr. Putin in the Chinese city of Shenzhen last week, both face-to-face and together with Chinese President Xi Jinping.
Mr. Trump warmly greeted Mr. Putin and called him his "friend" at the summit, renewing concerns in Kyiv and European capitals that he was once again pivoting towards the Russian leader.
Asked on Thursday (November 26) about the apparent firmer tone this week, he said: "I don't know if I was tougher. I mean, I think I'm the one that gave them the anti-tank busters, right?"
Mr. Trump often cites his approval of Javelin missile sales to Ukraine during his first term as saving the country from Russia's initial assault.
The U.S. envoys on the Ukraine war, Mr. Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner and business friend Steve Witkoff, have been accused of a similar willingness to take Moscow's side.
In an interview with the German MD Meets Podcast on Wednesday (November 25), Mr. Kushner said that Mr. Putin had "showed restraint" because he had "agreed not to use nuclear weapons."
Mr. Kushner also described the Russian leader, whom he has met several times, as a "grandmaster of politics" -- while calling the Ukraine conflict "a proxy war where Europe is funding and arming Ukraine against Russia."
But he acknowledged that the issue of territory in eastern Ukraine claimed by Moscow remained a sticking point with Mr. Putin.
"What Steve and I have deciphered from having many, many hours with President Putin and from the Russian team, is that you know the territorial issue is right now a little bit of a deadlock," he said.
And he warned against any downward spiral in the Ukraine war, saying "it won't take a lot of miscalculation for this thing to escalate massively."
