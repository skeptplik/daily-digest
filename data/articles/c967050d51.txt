On Monday, the Supreme Court announced it would not hear an appeal of a lower court decision that blocked the government from canceling the grants of a handful of researchers whose funding had been revoked during the Trump administration’s purge of DEI-related work. While the decision applies to only seven researchers in California, the lower court had ruled that the government’s actions violated the First Amendment and so may have broad implications.
The Supreme Court provided no discussion of its rejection, and no justices filed a dissent regarding the decision.
The suit was initially filed by a large group of researchers at the University of California, Berkeley and UC San Francisco, contesting grants originally issued by many federal agencies, from the National Institutes of Health to the Department of Transportation. At issue were grants that had been terminated because the agencies decided they violated a Trump executive order blocking the use of DEI. This occurred despite the fact that the government hadn’t bothered to define DEI, and targeted grants were often identified by little more than a keyword search.
In the wake of a Supreme Court decision that held that most grant termination cases had to be heard by a different court, the case was narrowed considerably to just seven UC researchers and a handful of agencies: The Environmental Protection Agency, the National Science Foundation, and the National Endowment for the Humanities.
