S&P revises France's outlook from 'stable' to 'negative'
Rating agency S&P revised France’s outlook to "negative" from "stable" on Friday, citing growing budget pressures as weak economic growth and higher interest costs make it harder to reduce the deficit.
S&P Global Ratings on Friday confirmed France’s sovereign rating at A+/A-1, but assigned it a negative outlook, raising the threat of a further downgrade in the medium term.
The US agency attributed its decision to “the increase in public debt against a backdrop of moderate economic growth prospects”, as the French economy entered a technical recession, according to figures released Friday by France's official statistics bureau INSEE.
Emphasising the “need for fiscal consolidation”, S&P also pointed to “the high risk that political fragmentation will persist after the 2027 presidential election,” which will weigh on economic growth.
The agency said it expects France’s public debt to continue to rise next year and for the interest paid by Paris to its creditors to increase gradually between now and 2029.
