Prosecutors on Wednesday sought the death penalty for a 79-year-old man accused of murdering a couple he knew and setting fire to nearby homes, destroying a total of eight buildings in Kashiwa, Chiba Prefecture, in December 2024.
The demand was made during the lay judge trial of Kaoru Sakamaki at the Chiba District Court. The verdict is set for Dec. 3.
According to the indictment, Sakamaki stabbed Toshiaki Watarai and his wife, Reiko Watarai, both 59, to death with a butcher’s knife at a house in the Takayanagi district of Kashiwa on Dec. 18, 2024. He is also accused of dousing three buildings, including his own home, with gasoline and setting them on fire, causing a total of eight buildings to be completely destroyed.
At the opening hearing on Nov. 10, Sakamaki admitted to killing the husband and setting fire to his own home but denied the other charges.
The defense argued that he had no intention of killing the wife and therefore should not be found guilty of murder in her case. It also maintained that he was responsible for setting fire to only three buildings.
In its opening statement, prosecutors said Sakamaki had grown increasingly resentful over the husband’s failure to repay a loan of approximately ¥7 million ($44,000). They also argued that he had become despondent following the breakdown of his own marriage.
