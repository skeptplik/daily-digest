The Economist's deputy editor Tom Standage joins us for a futuristic retrospective, taking us back to the future as we spotlight "The World Ahead 2027". We begin with the "leader of the free world", the FIFA Peace Prize recipient presiding over an America under the "Donroe Doctrine": "Its enemies no longer fear it and its allies no longer trust it in the way they did before."
Standage reflects on the devastating fallout of the Iran war, which he acknowledges was the magazine's biggest forecasting blunder for 2026. He expects the conflict's effects on energy supplies, fertilizer and food security to continue well into 2027, warning that another 50 million people could face acute food insecurity.
He also deconstructs Donald Trump's reluctance to name his successor, turning the race to succeed him into a real-life version of The Apprentice, in an edge-of-your-seat, hurry up and wait thriller. We dive into the growing risks surrounding Artificial (Super) Intelligence, contemplate the theme song for the next James Bond film, and fret over a very different kind of bond in the lead-up to the French presidential election.
After all eyes were on the US for the historic midterms, which left many feeling blue, "all eyes will be on France" in the spring of 2027, perhaps with a very different shade of blue.
