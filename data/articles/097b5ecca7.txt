Parts of England could see snow in the coming days as cold and unsettled weather continues into the weekend, forecasters have said.
Heavy rain sweeping eastwards on Friday could turn to snow over higher ground in northern England and Scotland.
The Met Office has issued a yellow ice warning for parts of northern England and the Midlands from 4am to 2pm on Friday.
Freezing rain could leave roads and pavements icy, particularly on higher routes across the Pennines, with up to 3cm of snow also possible, the warning states.
Forecasters also said there is a small chance of road closures, public transport disruption and injuries from slips and falls.
The Met Office meteorologist Honor Criswick said: “The cold is here to stay over the coming days.
“Not only this but it’s set to be rather unsettled at times, with heavy outbreaks of rain, possibly even some hill snow.”
Criswick added: “Also, a chance we could see some freezing rain towards high ground in northern England.
“This can lead to some slippery surfaces, so do take care if you’re out and about early first thing on Friday.”
Temperatures could plummet to -10C in parts of Scotland on Friday night, while widespread frost is also expected across Northern Ireland.
Heavy rain and hill snow are possible in northern England and Scotland on Saturday, with temperatures largely in single figures, although parts of the south-west could reach unseasonable highs of 14C.
The Met Office said that Saturday is expected to be wet and breezy for most of the UK with a brighter showery interlude on Sunday before more rain bands sweep through on Monday.
