The 3-way meeting reminds some of the 1945 Yalta conference
Some in the West are comparing the three-way meeting to the 1945 Yalta conference, where the wartime leaders of the U.S., Britain and the Soviet Union divided Germany and Berlin, carved out zones of influence in Europe, and agreed to establish the United Nations.
The Kremlin, which long has courted the concept of a “multipolar world” offering an equal say to all nations, plays down the Yalta comparisons, which are also unwelcome to China for similar reasons.
Fyodor Lukyanov, a pro-Kremlin foreign policy expert, noted that even as Moscow publicly rejects the Yalta analogy, it seems to believe the leaders could “reach agreements and make decisions that will determine global developments”.
Of the three nations, “it seems that only Russia really believes in the possibility of global control”, he told a recent roundtable.
Chatham House director Bronwen Maddox said in a commentary that “standing with Xi and Trump, Putin will look like one of the three main leaders of the world”, even though “Russia is no superpower” and its economy, overloaded with military spending, is dwindling.
She warned that “the danger for Ukraine is that Trump uses this summit to try to advance his notion of peace, including giving Russia territory it has not managed to seize by force”.
The meeting follows US-mediated peace talks between Russia and Ukraine held in Abu Dhabi last week for the first time in nine months. The negotiations produced few visible results as the parties stuck to their sharply divergent demands. -- AP
