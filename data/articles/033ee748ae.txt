Water companies will use government data to identify as many as 250,000 vulnerable households in England eligible for discounts, after criticism that not enough people are aware of the £428 support for their annual bills.
The Department for Work and Pensions will provide information to identify and contact a quarter of a million households who are missing out on support to which they are entitled under the WaterSure scheme.
The scheme is open to people on benefits who need to use a lot of water either for medical reasons or because their household has three or more children under 19.
The government said that 300,000 customers are signed up to WaterSure, but fewer than half of eligible households claim it because either they are unaware or they may have difficulty proving they qualify.
The Labour government has sought to portray itself as tough on the water industry. However, Andy Burnham has yet to outline his plan for the sector, despite saying he would bring in “more public control” of companies.
Angela Eagle, the environment secretary, said it was “common sense” to share information about eligible households with companies.
“Hundreds of thousands of the most vulnerable people aren’t getting help with their water bills because they don’t know how to access it,” she said. “That’s not right. By sharing data across government and water companies, we’re fixing that problem – meaning getting that help will be far easier for those who need it.”
The changes received the backing of Martin Lewis, founder of the MoneySavingExpert advice website.
He said: “Too many people miss out on help they desperately need and are entitled to. Water companies shoulder some blame for confusing applications and underpublicising support. Yet it is important the government joins the dots too. If one part of the state knows someone needs help, another shouldn’t leave them in the dark. Help is only effective if it actually reaches those who need it.”
Eagle also announced that the Consumer Council for Water, which helps households with complaints, will be given new powers to investigate water companies’ customer service and work with regulators to rectify problems. It will be named the Water Watchdog.
A spokesperson for Water UK, a lobby group for the industry, said: “Water companies have long called for government to automatically provide us with the information needed to give people heavily discounted water bills. As a result, hundreds of thousands more people will now benefit from WaterSure.”
