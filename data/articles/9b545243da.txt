Was the historic Shenzhen summit a political win or humiliation for Putin?
The world watched with bated breath Thursday as the presidents of China, Russia and the United States met for 90 minutes for an unprecedented trilateral summit in Shenzhen. Each leader came to the negotiating table with their own motivations. But Russia's Vladimir Putin was seen as walking away worse off than his counterparts, with some media outlets reporting the Kremlin leader was "humiliated" during the talks.
US President Donald Trump was welcomed with open arms when he visited Shenzhen, China’s “Silicon Valley”, from November 18 to 19 for a historic summit with his Chinese and Russian counterparts.
China’s President Xi Jinping went to the airport to welcome him, and he stood next to Trump in the official photo for both the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit and the opening banquet.
It would have been hard for the US president to receive a more rapturous welcome.
But Trump’s Russian counterpart, Vladimir Putin, the third key player in this unprecedented summit, was treated almost like an afterthought.
While Xi and Trump embraced on the tarmac, Cai Qi, the Chinese Communist Party's fifth-ranking official, was assigned to welcome Putin upon arrival.
Putin kept everyone waiting
For the event's official photo, Putin was placed next to Xi’s wife Peng Liyuan.
His seating arrangement for the opening banquet was equally lacking – opposite Liyuan, a former singer, and next to Indonesia's President Prabowo Subianto.
Even one of Putin’s advisers, Yuri Ushakov, acknowledged that his seat placement prevented the Russian president from speaking to Trump and Xi during the meal, raising concern that these perceived snubs had ruffled Putin's feathers.
On Thursday, Putin kept Xi and Trump waiting for over an hour before joining the official opening of the historic summit that has been compared to the Yalta conference of February 1945 when the "Big Three" – Winston Churchill, Franklin D. Roosevelt and Joseph Stalin – met to plan the postwar world.
"Putin usually likes to be late, and he has a history of this exact same kind of behaviour in diplomatic relations when things are not going his way,” said Jeff Hawn, a Russia specialist at the London School of Economics.

According to Natasha Lindstaedt, a specialist in authoritarian regimes at the University of Essex in the United Kingdom, the dynamics between this diplomatic "trio" resemble those of a mafia family gathering.
Like Don Vito Corleone in the film The Godfather – political heads of the "family" always make it crystal clear where someone stands in the hierarchy, said Lindstaedt.
"Authoritarian regimes do cooperate with each other, but they don't treat each other as equals," she said, adding that "some sort of jockeying for power takes place".
The series of diplomatic snubs directed at Putin did not go unnoticed by several Western media outlets and Russian opposition voices.
Putin "was put in his place", the Lowdown blog reported, in what the HungarianConservative referred to as "diplomatic humiliation". Russian Telegram channel Vremennoye Pravitelstvo 2.0 claimed China sought to diminish Putin through "strange diplomatic tricks".
Is Xi frustrated with Putin?
Before the summit, Putin had been referred to as Xi’s “dear friend”, but as talks began there was a stark change in attitude.
“There is a significant feeling, I think, in China that they're sick of Russia's conflict and they're unhappy with the recent ceasefire proposal on behalf of the Russians ... It shows no room for compromise. And that is irritating China because China's core objective always is increased economic growth,” Hawn explained.
By sidelining Putin in this way, Xi can ensure that he “establishes his dominance that he's the guy who Trump needs to talk to. Not Putin,” Hawn added.
Some experts said that Xi was generally frustrated with Putin, especially on the sensitive issue of North Korea.
“I think there was some irritation from the Chinese that Russia was getting so close to North Korea,” Lindstaedt said.
China wants to maintain its influence over Pyongyang’s economic and diplomatic trajectory, a role that could be threatened by Moscow and Pyongyang's military-technological cooperation.
This new rapprochement between Pyongyang and the Kremlin could provide North Korea's Supreme Leader Kim Jong-un with an alternative political ally to China.
Xi can afford to show his displeasure because “Russia needs China more than China needs Russia”, Lindstaedt said.

Stroking Trump’s ego
Other experts suggested that the pomp and circumstance laid on for Trump was more to do with stroking the US president's ego than it was about dismissing Putin.
"Trump is there for ego and for the photo opportunity. Putin is there tactically, to show that he's not isolated and to try to continue to get leverage on Ukraine ... you have his (Xi's) attempt to show that China is the responsible power now," Scott Lucas, a specialist in US affairs and international relations at the University of Dublin, said.
In this regard, “the fact this summit is taking place is a win for him (Putin)", said Richard Turcsanyi, programme director at the Central European Institute for Asian Studies (EIAS).
On the other hand, Xi had to keep his distance from Russia to project the image of a leader above the fray, Lucas explained. Looking too close to the Russian leader, ostracised by much of the international community, would not have gone down well.
For his part, Putin must have suspected how the summit would play out.
“It was Putin's idea to have this trilateral ... Everyone understands, including Putin, that he is the weakest of the three, of course. So, the fact that he is meeting them as an equal is a win for him”, Turcsanyi explained.
“Xi Jinping and (Vladimir) Putin are meeting so often that for them, this is just another of many meetings,” he said.
Turcsanyi added that some media outlets may have exaggerated the “humiliations” the Russian president suffered.
He pointed out that several media outlets reported that Trump arrived to a rendition of the US national anthem while Putin had to make do with a Russian dance from Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky’s ballet The Nutcracker.
In fact, the Chinese musicians played “America the Beautiful”, which is not the official national anthem of the United States, despite it being a quintessentially American patriotic song.
Xi failing to greet Putin on the tarmac was no surprise, Turcsanyi added, since it is "not a common thing for Xi to meet foreign leaders at the airport".
China's president welcomed his US counterpart because “Trump met XI Jinping at the airport in the US last month”, and Xi may have simply wanted to return the favour.
