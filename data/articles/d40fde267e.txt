NEW YORK – U.S. President Donald Trump and his cronies are eager to normalize relations with Russia in exchange for lucrative oil deals. In doing so, they are advancing Russian President Vladimir Putin’s strategy of systematically isolating Ukraine by making its defense so costly that popular support for the country in Europe evaporates.
After more than four years of full-scale war, it is abundantly clear that Russia cannot win on the battlefield. Nor can it force Ukraine to surrender through economic or political means. The Kremlin’s next step was to secure Trump’s assistance. Putin’s goal at the August 2025 summit in Anchorage, Alaska — as evidenced by statements from multiple Russian officials — was to persuade the Trump administration to pressure Ukraine to surrender. When the Ukrainian government stood up to American pressure, the Kremlin concluded that it needed the Trump administration’s active involvement on Russia’s behalf.
The recent discussions about a return of U.S. companies to Russia, led by Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner and Trump’s special envoy (and fellow New York real-estate baron) Steve Witkoff, reflect that effort. Using nebulous promises of fossil-fuel profits “after the end of the war” as a carrot, Putin is seeking to align Trump’s pecuniary interests with Russia’s strategic objectives, giving Trump a reason to declare the war over or, better yet, to force Ukraine to capitulate. Never mind the years of horrific conflict, untold war crimes, millions of people killed or displaced and the generational trauma; Trump wants his cut.
