MILAN — In Stone Island’s lexicon, Milan is one of its 11 “lighthouse cities” alongside New York, Paris and Shanghai, to name a few.
These are destinations central to the brand’s growth, storytelling, and community. Milan is also home to the Moncler-owned cult sportswear brand, which splits its operations between the city and Ravarino, Italy, where it was founded.
That makes the nine-month renovation of its flagship in town especially resonant.
The store bows this week in the same 1930s building as its former location, on central Corso Matteotti.
Expanded in surface and spanning 6,458 square feet over two floors, it’s Stone Island’s biggest store yet, and boasts a revamped design concept. Its previous incarnation opened in 2019.
“Because of the location, it was important to arrive in the right way — the Stone Island way,” said chief executive officer and president Robert Triefus in an interview at the brand’s headquarters ahead of the opening.
“When you enter the store, you should immediately have a sense of place and of Stone Island’s values,” he added.
The CEO also touted the location at the intersection with Via Montenapoleone.
“We took a location that is a little ‘off-Broadway,’ but almost ‘on-Broadway’ because of where it is,” Triefus said, drawing a parallel with the Manhattan thoroughfare. “In a way, I think it says a lot about Stone Island: we play in different parts of the sandpit. I like that we are there, but not quite there, because it allows us to be truly distinct.”
Stone Island again conscripted OMA/AMO, the design and architectural firm that has overseen the brand’s retail blueprint of industrial tactility for the past five years, and developed units in New York, Paris and Hangzhou, among others.
“I think this is our best iteration to date. Milan is, in a sense, our hometown, alongside Ravarino and I feel we have put our best foot forward,” Triefus remarked.
The store, designed by Samir Bantal, partner at OMA and director of AMO, is anchored by a sculptural centerpiece crafted from carved cork and dubbed “Hyperobject,” which connects the unit’s spaces, integrates a recessed display system and continues into the staircase, the space’s focal point.
Built from a powder-coated metal grid filled with resin, the orange stairs — a feature already present at the brand’s Rue Saint-Honoré store in Paris, as well as stores in Rome and New York — become the ideal setting to display objects and host cultural initiatives.
In sync with Stone Island’s research-intensive approach to materials, the OMA/AMO concept embeds burnt, sandblasted and coated cork, fused glass that is wrapped around a column at the rear of the store and pressed paper combined with recycled resin for furniture and display modules scattered throughout the space for enhanced flexibility.
“In these times of submission to the digital, Stone Island is one of the few companies dedicated to all that makes the physical and tangible beautiful.…Building on the brand’s decades-long research of shaping these recipes for textile treatment and color dyeing, I wanted to apply that same approach to the materials used in the stores: pressed paper, cooked cork, green glass.…Outside the capital of design, in the Milanese countryside, many fabricators, manufacturers, craftspeople, maintain an important heritage of the industry of making things,” Bantal said.
Highlighting its collaborative mindset, Stone Island and OMA/AMO invited young design talents to develop some of the store’s elements and decor. These include lighting by Tim Hooijmans; the “Supersedia” lounge chair by Markus Töll; speakers by Neuf Voix; furniture by Tim Teven; a glass table by 6AM Glass; display objects by Elvis Wesley, and audio systems developed by Ledongil Workshop.
“They are all part of a collaborative design community that can express itself in our stores,” Triefus said. “I certainly think the work with this community of designers could evolve into product collaborations. We are still relatively young in this concept; it has developed over the last couple of years. What is exciting is that the designers’ work is very true to Stone Island, so it could find a future expression within the brand,” he added.
The project reflects Stone Island’s ambition to measure retail success by more metrics than just sales.
“I’ll be straightforward: we do not open stores unless we believe they will be successful. Success means being commercially successful [but also] authentic to who we are,” the CEO said.
“Today, retail is defined by a sensorial experience: people should feel immersed in what they can discover. At Stone Island, that means seeing the value of the product while also experiencing our strong sense of community,” he said.
The ground floor will host the main retail area, with the upper level intended for activations, experiences and events.
“We focus on creating a calendar of occasions: DJ sets and talks about the subcultures Stone Island is connected with, whether [it’s] music, sport or art, as well as our product. We also hold panel discussions about materiality.…We do not put on random events; they are purposeful and connected to the ‘life’ or the ‘lab’ of Stone Island,” Triefus said, referencing the two communications pillars of the brand.
As an example, he mentioned Stone Island’s recently wrapped takeover of The Corner Shop at Selfridges, which focused on its community, and specifically collectors from around the world, who sold personally curated vintage pieces in the department store’s concept space.
The Milan store’s revamp falls within Stone Island’s retail optimization strategy, Triefus said.
“We build steadily. We do not take shortcuts or try to fast-track growth. Our intent has not been to suddenly open lots of stores. We are focused on optimizing an already important retail footprint of 94 stores…[and] we have to be strategically smart about maintaining momentum across regions,” he explained.
“That optimization strategy is beginning to deliver results. We judge the health of a brand partly by comparable-store growth, and we are seeing healthy comp growth globally and, in every region,” he added.
“In all our regions, [retail] projects allow us to…create a sense of elevation. By elevation, I mean delivering the best possible experience across all touchpoints. A retail environment is part of an elevated customer experience, but it is not the only part,” Triefus said.
Since taking office in 2023, Stone Island’s direct-to-consumer channel has grown 11 percent under Triefus’ lead.
In the first nine months of 2026, direct-to-consumer grew by 7 percent to 155.9 million euros, or almost 51 percent of sales. In the third quarter, revenues in this channel remained on a positive growth trajectory, up 2 percent at constant exchange.
“When I arrived, wholesale represented a greater share of the business than DTC. That has changed, but it does not mean we do not value wholesale. We absolutely do. Stone Island would not be where it is today without wholesale; the channel made the brand well-known, particularly in Europe,” Triefus said.
The executive added that over the past couple of years the brand has zeroed in on like-minded partners, which include bigger players such as Selfridges, Harrods, Breuninger, Galeries Lafayette and Printemps, as well Nordstrom and Saks Stateside.
Stone Island has also strengthened its relationship with independent stores, which he described as “authenticators of the brand.”
“They may not represent a huge share of total wholesale business, but their role is very important.…The way they curate is valuable: they allow us to be present in secondary or tertiary cities in a representative way,” Triefus explained.
Particularly in North America, wholesale has helped the brand deepen its footprint beyond its directly operated stores in New York’s SoHo, which was recently relocated, Costa Mesa South Plaza, Miami, Los Angeles and Toronto.
Earlier this year the brand relocated its store in Rome on Via del Babuino, and opened new outposts in Naples, Italy and Changsha, China.
Stone Island revenues were up 6 percent to 306.2 million euros in the nine months to Sept. 30, compared with 288.1 million euros in the same period last year. At constant exchange rates, they rose 8 percent. Third-quarter revenues were up 5 percent at constant exchange rates compared with the same period last year.
