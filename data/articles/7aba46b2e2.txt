NVIDIA is investigating claims of a security breach after the Gentlemen ransomware group has added the company to its dark web data leak site.
Although the ransomware operation now lists the American multinational tech giant, it has not yet shared any proof of the breach.
On their leak site, the cybercrime gang threatens to release data and has added a countdown that ends in nine days, on November 28.
While the company told BleepingComputer it hasn't found evidence that its systems were compromised, it said it's now looking into a separate incident that may have affected one of its service providers.
"NVIDIA is investigating an incident that may have affected a third-party partner," an NVIDIA spokesperson told BleepingComputer when asked to confirm the cybercrime gang's claims.
"We have found no indication that NVIDIA systems or networks were affected. We continue to monitor the situation."
NVIDIA confirmed a data breach earlier this year, in May, when it told BleepingComputer that some Armenian users' information (including their names, email addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, and usernames) had been exposed after a regional GeForce NOW Alliance partner was hacked.
However, the company added that the May breach did not affect its network or NVIDIA-operated services.
The tech giant also disclosed another data breach in March 2022 (claimed by the Lapsus$ extortion gang) that affected more than 71,000 employees after attackers accessed its network a month earlier.
The Gentlemen surfaced last year as a closed ransomware group and then switched to a Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) model, offering affiliates up to a 90% cut of ransom proceeds (compared to the typical 70%–80% other ransomware groups offer).
It operates as a double-extortion group, exfiltrating sensitive data from victims' networks before encrypting their systems and pressuring them with the threat of leaking the stolen information online.
Since it emerged, The Gentlemen has claimed more than 1,100 victims from various sectors, spanning manufacturing, healthcare, transportation, energy, education, financial services, and more.
