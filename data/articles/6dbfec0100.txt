We get it—Ordering from Amazon is easy. But aside from being community anchors in their respective towns and neighborhoods, small, independent businesses offer some critical things the big guys don’t: curation and specialization. If you don't know anything about stationery, it helps to buy it from someone who lives and breathes fountain pens and Japanese notebooks. You may not want to sort through the millions of toys on Walmart's website, but a mom who really knows toys can help you find just the right gift for just the right age.
For Small Business Saturday, November 28, and throughout the holiday shopping season, consider shopping at an independent business, whether in your own city or via one of the links below. Each store is one that someone on our Reviews team has visited in person or bought something from and loved. Some are in our hometowns, some we happened upon on vacation, and others are places we regularly shop. Here’s what we’d buy.
This locally owned Greenpoint shop is recommended by Gear editor Julian Chokkattu as being a mecca for writers and sketchers. Its brick-lined walls hold shelves’ worth of every type of pen, paper, and notebook imaginable, with an emphasis on Asian stationery like Hobonichi's popular Techo paper planners (which we highly recommend). Yoseka’s Shop store allows customers to buy pens individually, so you can put together a sampler or select only the colors you know your recipient will want.
Founded in 1948 by Edith and Brian Heath, Heath Ceramics still makes the same ceramic dinnerware, vases, and tiles the founders made back then, which are fully back in style now due to their midcentury modern flair. As a fifth-generation San Francisco Bay Area native, I still have a set of green Heath cereal bowls my grandmother bought when the Sausalito showroom—which is still in operation and is still attached to the factory—was relatively new.
WIRED operations manager Scott Gilbertson loves this locally owned toy store and community space known for its quirky and original toys, from vintage-inspired matchbox stuffies to keychain-sized digital cameras and retro candy. (There’s also a sister location, Clubhouse Kid and Craft, just outside of Atlanta, though that one has different owners.) As a side note, I knew few kids—including my own—who haven't loved Magna-Tiles.
Part of the growing “slow fashion” movement, this vintage-inspired boutique sells shirts, pants, jackets, and even underwear made painstakingly by hand right in Brooklyn and meant to last a lifetime. It's mostly men's garments online and accessories like the waxed-cotton pouch above, though there are some women's cashmere coats in the physical store.
Though I live in San Diego, one of my best friends lives in the Dallas area near McKinney. Walking around the adorable historic downtown is one of our favorite pastimes when I visit, and a shop we always find ourselves back at is Fair and Square Imports. The shop is focused on fair trade goods, working with artisan cooperatives and Fair Trade partners from around the world. As you peruse the shop, you’ll find all kinds of home goods, ranging from ornaments for the holidays, beautiful home pieces, bath goods, kids’ books, and so much more. On my last visit, I bought a massage stone and bath salts, while my friend bought flavored honey and a fantastically scented lotion. You won’t find all the fun goods online that they have in store, but you can shop an impressive range of their well-priced goods online. —Nena Farrell
Owned by a local color designer, this downtown Portland gift shop is hard to miss with its cheery window displays and decidedly mod vibe. It features all manner of colorful art and gifts by independent artists; Woonwinkel's website lets you sort selections by Black-owned, POC-owned, USA-made, and eco-friendly, though the Shop site does not. In any case, if you or a giftee likes bright, stylish things with lots of color, this is the place.
When you choose made-to-order footwear from Crown Northampton, it feels like stepping backward in time, pun intended. You’re supporting a family business dating back to 1908, now in its fifth generation of stewardship; managing director Chris Woodford is the great-great-grandson of founder Earnest Woodford. And you’re investing in stellar craftsmanship—true buy-it-for-life goods. These are premium boots made by hand with the highest-quality materials (like Horween Chromexcel leather) and techniques (stitchdown construction), with zero outsourcing. Most things simply aren’t made this way nowadays. Most other shoes aren’t initialed in pen to note that they passed inspection. But the Woodfords are proud to keep the tradition alive. —Cooper Fleishman
Hamilton’s iconic field watch is perfect for nearly any setting, hardy enough to endure toddler mealtime or outdoor adventures but dashing enough to pair well with office attire. Watch nerds call it GADA—a “go anywhere, do anything” style. On one hand, it’s the last timepiece you’ll ever need to buy. But on the other hand, what’s the fun in that? A great field watch is a gateway to all sorts of fun and distinctive wristwear at any price, much of which can be found at Windup Watch Shop in Brooklyn. My friend Nick Coe, who runs the menswear brand network Kutoku, calls it a true enthusiast-first shop. While other retailers can feel intimidating and exclusive, Windup welcomes all. There’s a terrific range of brands, products, and prices—check out the wall clocks!—with approachable service, a high taste level, and an even higher bar for quality. If you love watches—or if you want to love watches but you’re afraid you can’t afford to—Windup is the perfect place to start your collection. —Cooper Fleishman
This hip, brick-lined design shop in Melrose Market draws you (OK, maybe just me) in with its display of houseplants, but it's also a hot spot for housewares, stationery, interesting soaps, and cool, high-end casualwear like vintage-cut plain tees and Japanese selvedge denim. The physical store also offers a host of creative DIY workshops on topics like working with natural dyes or making kokedamas (moss ball planters).
Founded in 2006 by James Beard Award-winning cookbook author Mark Bitterman, the Meadow features some gourmet foods and gifts here and there, but the focus is on one thing: salt. Exotic flavored salt, finishing salt, Himalayan salt cooking blocks, and chocolate bars with fleur de sel are all featured both in-store (three locations in Portland, one in SoHo) and online. I have shopped in-store many times, and my favorite purchases are always the smoked salts, like the Halen Môn smoked salt from Wales, or the sea salt cold-smoked over cherry wood.
My wife’s go-to bag is this buttery-soft full-grain leather ring bag that can be worn in several ways: crossbody, shoulder, or handheld. It’s made by our local Hyer Goods, whose founder, Dana Cohen, shows a deep commitment to sustainable luxury by adapting surplus leather from well-known fashion houses’ Italian tanneries. We love the classic Ring Bag, and we fight over the wool whenever it’s chilly. —Cooper Fleishman
