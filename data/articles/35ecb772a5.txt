While I personally noticed an improvement in my hair density, experts say to temper your expectations. “It is important to recognize that not all oils have been clinically proven to stimulate new hair growth,” says Marisa Garshick, MD, a board-certified dermatologist at MDCS Dermatology. “Rosemary oil and pumpkin seed oil have some preliminary research supporting their potential benefits, while others—such as castor, argan, and jojoba oils—are more beneficial for moisturizing the scalp, strengthening the hair shaft, and reducing breakage.”
Corinne Erickson, MD, a board-certified dermatologist and founder of Georgia Skin Specialists, considers them “adjuncts to medical management for hair loss, not stand-alone solutions. While topical minoxidil remains the gold standard for thinning hair, there are several oils with early data showing help for hair thinning that are generally safe when used as directed.”
Generally speaking, many hair oils are safe when used as directed, though some can cause irritation or allergic reactions. You may want to use them alongside other nourishing hair products for the best results. With all that in mind, these are the best oils to consider for thinning hair.
Rosemary Oil
Of all the hair oils, rosemary is the most promising for thinning hair. Dr. Garshick explains that rosemary oil’s anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties may support scalp health, and she cites studies suggesting it may help with pattern hair loss. Although evidence remains limited, she points to one small study in which rosemary oil produced similar improvements in hair count to 2% minoxidil after six months.
She and Dr. Erickson recommend Mielle’s Rosemary Mint Scalp & Hair Strengthening Oil, which mixes rosemary oil with castor oil, jojoba oil, and other botanical ingredients to treat breakage and nourish the scalp. “[It] is an easy, affordable option,” says Dr. Erickson. If you’re looking for something lighter, you can also try Dr. Groot’s Scalp Hair Thickening Serum, a serum made with rosemary, biotin, and caffeine designed to support scalp health and fuller-looking hair.
Pumpkin Seed Oil
Dr. Garshick says that pumpkin seed oil contains phytosterols and fatty acids, which preliminary research suggests may inhibit DHT, a hormone implicated in pattern hair loss. “This may help explain [pumpkin seed oil’s] potential benefits for hair growth,” she says. However, much of the clinical evidence involves oral pumpkin seed oil supplements rather than topical applications, so it remains unclear whether applying the oil directly to the scalp provides the same benefits.
Dr. Erickson likes the Leven Rose Organic Pumpkin Seed Oil, which is pure and cold-pressed, with no fragrance or additives. “Promising, though not yet proven,” she says. You can also consider something like SVA Organic Pumpkin Seed Oil, another cold-pressed option you can easily massage into your hair as a pre-shampoo treatment.
Castor Oil
Castor oil is another popular oil used in hair-growth treatments. Dr. Garshick says that although there isn’t strong scientific evidence that castor oil affects hair growth, it can help nourish your hair and scalp. She says that it is rich in a fatty acid called ricinoleic acid, which is known to add moisture and condition to the skin and your strands. “It may help reduce dryness and breakage, which can make the hair appear thicker and healthier over time,” she says.
Try something like the Fable & Mane HoliRoots Hair Oil, a pre-shampoo treatment that mixes castor oil with ashwagandha, an antioxidant-rich berry called amla, and a plant-root blend designed to strengthen strands and soothe the scalp. You can also try the Carol’s Daughter Goddess Strength 7 Oil Blend Hair & Scalp Oil, which blends castor oil with black cumin seed and six other nourishing oils to hydrate the scalp and treat breakage.
Avocado Oil
Avocado oil contains fatty acids and vitamin E, which can help moisturize and condition the hair. While there aren’t studies showing avocado oil directly stimulates hair growth, it may help create a healthy environment for hair to thrive. “By helping support a healthy scalp environment and strengthen the hair shaft, it may help hair appear fuller and healthier while minimizing breakage that can contribute to the appearance of thinning,” Dr. Garshick agrees.
She likes Kiehl’s Magic Elixir, a pre-shampoo treatment that combines avocado oil with rosemary leaf and safflower oil to moisturize the scalp and alleviate dryness. “It is especially useful for those looking to incorporate scalp care into their routine,” she adds, “while improving hair manageability, smoothness, and shine without adding another leave-in product.” You can also try the Grow Good Everything Serum, which has a nourishing mix of oils (avocado, coconut, sunflower seed, and more) to seal the cuticle and prevent split ends and breakage.
Argan Oil
Argan oil is extremely nourishing for hair; it contains vitamin E, fatty acids, and antioxidants that protect hair from environmental aggressors that cause damage, keeping hair feeling smooth and hydrated. Dr. Garshick says that although it hasn’t been proven to stimulate hair growth, it can improve hair softness, reduce frizz, and minimize breakage, which helps hair retain length and appear fuller.
She recommends something like the Augustinus Bader The Hair Oil, which combines argan oil with vitamin E, baobab and pomegranate oils, and a special complex that smooths out frizz and treats breakage so hair looks—and feels—healthier and fuller. You can also try SheaMoisture’s 100% Argan Oil, which, as the name suggests, is made of pure argan oil to nourish and restore dry and damaged hair.
Everything You Need to Know
Are There Any Downsides to Using Oils for Thinning Hair?
Dr. Garshick says that while hair oils can help with dryness, breakage, or scalp issues, they might not be the best solution for hair thinning. She explains that some oils can cause irritation or an allergic reaction, while heavier oils can cause buildup or worsen scalp conditions.
“It is also important to remember that hair loss can have many causes,” she adds. Genetics, hormonal changes, nutritional deficiencies, and medical conditions could all be reasons your hair is thinning, so you may need a more effective treatment. For those noticing persistent shedding, a widening part, or thinning that is progressively getting worse, she recommends seeing a board-certified dermatologist to identify the cause and get the right treatment.
How Do You Use Hair Oils for Thinning Hair?
Dr. Erickson says you must always dilute essential oils in a carrier oil (like jojoba) to reduce the risk of scalp irritation. If you’re prone to flaking or scalp conditions like seborrheic dermatitis, she recommends skipping oils altogether.
You’ll generally just massage in your oil of choice to stimulate circulation on your scalp. Start slowly to see how your hair and scalp tolerate it, then increase frequency up to a few times a week. Dr. Garshick says dermarolling may help by creating micro-injuries that activate wound-healing pathways and growth factors, promoting healthy hair growth. But you’ll want to be careful, as there’s a risk of irritation, allergic reactions, and inflammation. So when in doubt, ask your doctor.
