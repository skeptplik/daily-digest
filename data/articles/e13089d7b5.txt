Someone claiming to represent ShinyHunters has announced the notorious cybercrime group’s alleged return. Separately, the operators of a new extortion campaign named Inception have denied having ties to ShinyHunters.
According to DataBreaches, the return of ShinyHunters was announced in a message posted on the Exploit cybercrime forum. The post came from an account linked to ShinyHunters since 2020.
The message included the address of the new leak site and a new PGP key to authenticate future communications. Its authors also noted that they will not be active on other cybercrime forums and disavowed several hackers who claimed to be members of ShinyHunters.
The post’s authors attempted to establish their identity using a crypto key known to be associated with ShinyHunters, but DataBreaches noted that the original PGP key had reportedly been leaked.
Other threat actors have sought to capitalize on the ShinyHunters name and reputation, and the possibility that the comeback message is the work of impostors cannot be ruled out.
The announcement follows the arrest of several individuals suspected of being involved in ShinyHunters attacks, including a Canada-based cybersecurity expert, a Jordanian national believed to be group leader Rey, as well as a suspect in the Netherlands.
New group Inception
The cybersecurity community has started tracking a new cybercrime group called Inception. Its leak site emerged earlier this month and lists several major US companies, including AMD, PepsiCo, Lucid Motors, Henry Schein, and Cigna Healthcare. The group has leaked hundreds of gigabytes of data allegedly stolen from these companies, but the data’s authenticity has not been independently verified.
According to research conducted by Okta, Inception may involve hackers previously associated with ShinyHunters. Okta has been tracking these hackers since March as O-UNC-053.
Okta says the cybercriminals’ phishing domain registrations dropped in September and October (around the time of the arrests), but activity picked up in November.
The company’s researchers previously saw organizations targeted by O-UNC-053 being listed on the ShinyHunters data leak site. After the ShinyHunters site went offline, they did not see O-UNC-053 victims being listed on any other website until November 16, when companies targeted by the group were named on the Inception website.
“We cannot currently make a high confidence assessment as to whether O-UNC-053 is operating the [data leak site] themselves or is partnering with another extortion actor,” Okta said.
Security researcher Dominic Alvieri has also suggested a possible connection between Inception and ShinyHunters. “ShinyHunters are not all in jail,” Alvieri noted.
In a lengthy statement posted on its website, Inception denied having any connection to ShinyHunters.
“[Okta’s] evidence amounts to shared use of commodity services — domain registrars and CDN providers available to anyone — and overlapping targets. Neither constitutes operational linkage. We acknowledge that privately available tooling circulates among independent operators. Shared access to a service does not equate to shared identity,” the hackers wrote, adding, “We are not ShinyHunters. We were never ShinyHunters. We are Inception.”
ReversingLabs has also monitored Inception’s activities and reported this week that it has uncovered phishing domains suggesting that the cybercriminals may have also targeted companies such as Anthropic, Amazon, Adobe, Nintendo, Netflix, OpenAI, and Tesla. There is no indication that any of them were successfully compromised.
The security firm identified 21 confirmed phishing domains and another 41 associated with the campaign with high confidence.
