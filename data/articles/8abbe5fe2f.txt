Taiwan headed to the polls Saturday for local elections that could reshape the political field ahead of the 2028 presidential contest, with voters galvanized by an unexpectedly close race in the capital Taipei.
Some 19.6 million people are eligible to vote for mayors and county chiefs across 22 cities and counties, with more than 11,000 smaller roles up for grabs. Polls run from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., with initial results expected in the evening.
The vote will test whether President Lai Ching-te’s Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) can translate its record run of three straight presidential victories into gains at the local level, where the opposition Kuomintang (KMT) has long been stronger. For the KMT, which advocates friendlier ties with China, the results could reorder the field of politicians vying to challenge Lai in 2028 — a contest Beijing is closely watching.
