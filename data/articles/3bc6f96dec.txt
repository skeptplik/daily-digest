Unknown attackers have reportedly breached an open-source intelligence (OSINT) analysis system at the US Central Intelligence Agency (CIA), according to a source familiar with the matter.
The source told The New York Times, which first reported the incident, that the cyberattack was carried out using the ARTEX AI-powered penetration testing framework and also affected the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) and the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency.
While the CIA and the ODNI have not yet replied to a request for more details regarding this incident, no classified data or systems were exposed in the breach.
"The hack did not involve our classified networks or classified information, and no other systems are currently at risk," the source said.
Hours after the CIA security breach was reported, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) also disclosed that it's investigating a cybersecurity incident involving unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems. After detecting the attack, the agency took some systems offline as a precaution and told banks to use the Financial Institutions Hotline to file reports, since some services may be unavailable.
FinCEN is a US Department of the Treasury bureau that acts as the country's official Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) and protects the nation's financial system from illicit activities by sharing financial intelligence with law enforcement agencies both domestically and internationally.
BleepingComputer reached out to a FinCEN spokesperson with questions about the incident, but a response was not immediately available.
One week ago, the US National Security Agency's classified network also suffered a days-long outage, but the agency blamed scheduled routine maintenance and said its mission-critical capabilities remained fully operational.
US agencies targeted by cyberattacks
These incidents follow a series of other cyberattacks that have led to security breaches at other US federal agencies in recent years.
For instance, two years ago, the Silk Typhoon Chinese state-backed hacking group hacked the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC), another Treasury Department office that administers economic and trade sanctions programs.
The same threat group also breached the Treasury's Office of Financial Research, and it reportedly infiltrated the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (a Treasury Department office that reviews foreign investments for national security risks).
More recently, as BleepingComputer reported over the weekend, hackers reportedly breached the FBI's National Crime Information Center (NCIC). However, it is unclear how the attackers gained access, when the breach occurred, or whether they stole any information from the compromised systems.
This follows a claim by the ShinyHunters extortion group that it breached the FBI's recruitment website, FBIJobs.gov, prompting an FBI investigation that led to the arrests of multiple suspected ShinyHunters members, including a Canadian cybersecurity executive reportedly linked to the attack.
