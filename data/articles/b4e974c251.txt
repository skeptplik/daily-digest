President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has urged the United States and Elon Musk to lift restrictions on Ukraine's access to the Starlink network, saying "It is important to be on the right side of history."
"Our interceptor drones lose connection with their operators at a speed of 161 kilometers per hour and simply cannot catch up with Russian jet-powered 'shaheds,'" Zelenskyy wrote in a post on X.
"We are counting on these restrictions being lifted."
Kyiv relies on the network for its frontline communications and operating drones, including interceptors that shoot down incoming Russian drones.
Last week, Zelenskyy said that Ukraine's military was facing "systematic" restrictions on the service, operated by Elon Musk's SpaceX, with connections only working when devices were moving slower than 161 kilometres per hour.
"We still have no clear explanations," Zelenskyy said on Friday, confirming the restrictions were still in place "along the entire front line" and over territory occupied by Russia.

Wave of drones
Meanwhile, Ukraine struck the bridge linking annexed Crimea to Russia, disrupting traffic on the key passageway, a regional official appointed by Moscow said on Monday.
"The enemy has hit the Crimea bridge," Sergey Aksyonov wrote on Telegram. "As a result, traffic will be restricted, likely until the afternoon."
He did not say whether anyone was killed or wounded in the strike.
"All necessary services have been mobilised to manage the situation," he said.
Those comments come after Russia said on Monday it had intercepted more than 1,300 Ukrainian drones overnight, including hundreds targeting Moscow, one of Kyiv's largest attacks of the nearly five-year war.
Air defences intercepted around 600 drones flying towards the Moscow region overnight and into Monday morning, Moscow Mayor Sergey Sobyanin said on Telegram.
"Most were destroyed by the defence ministry's air defence system on distant approaches," he said, adding that 191 drones were "neutralised on their approach to Moscow".
There was no immediate information on damage and casualties.
Russia's defence ministry later said air defences had intercepted and destroyed 1,336 Ukrainian fixed-wing drones overnight across multiple regions, including the Moscow area, as well as over the Black Sea and Sea of Azov.
The interceptions came hours after Ukrainian forces struck a major Russian petrochemical plant, an oil refinery and a military production site as part of Kyiv's campaign of attacks deep inside Russia.
The barrage came days before a summit in Shenzhen between President Vladimir Putin, US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping.
Beijing confirmed on Monday that Xi would hold talks with Trump and Putin in Shenzhen on November 19 on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit.
The Kremlin and White House had previously confirmed the meeting.
Beijing provided no details about the agenda, while Trump has said he intends to discuss denuclearisation and the Iran war.
He is not expected to discuss Russia's war against Ukraine in the trilateral talks, according to US officials.
Washington is pursuing separate negotiations involving Kyiv and Moscow aimed at ending the conflict, with US negotiators meeting both Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy last week.
German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier said Xi recently showed "irritation" at suggestions that Ukraine could be discussed in the trilateral format.
"He explicitly pointed out that this is a European conflict, in which the Europeans must also be at the table," Steinmeier told the public broadcaster ARD.
Zelenskyy has warned that the Shenzhen meeting risks becoming a 21st-century version of the 1945 Yalta conference, where major powers could attempt to redraw spheres of influence without smaller countries at the table.
