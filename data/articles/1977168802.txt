Transport for London (TfL) has announced that around 265,000 Oyster photocard customers may have had their account details exposed in a data breach.
TfL is London's public transport authority, operating the Underground, Overground, buses, trams, and the Elizabeth line. It recorded 3.53 billion passenger journeys in 2025/26, including 1.78 billion bus journeys and 1.20 billion Tube journeys.
Oyster photocards are travel smartcards with the holder's photo that give eligible passengers free or discounted travel.
The organization discovered the security incident on November 16, 2026, after receiving customer reports about emails requesting them to create an account, which came directly from TfL's mail systems as a result of a compromise.
The Oyster photocard website has now been taken offline and the organization is sending impacted individuals data breach notifications.
Oyster photocards remain valid for travel, and customers who need to renew their cards or check their journey history can still do so by contacting TfL's Customer Services.
These notifications, with one shared on Reddit, state that the following customer data may have been exposed:
- Full names
- Phone numbers
- Email addresses
- Home addresses
- Dates of birth
- Security questions and answers
TfL's current assessment is that passwords, bank and payment card details, journey information, and the names and dates of birth of children with Zip Oyster cards were not compromised.
The transportation organization also said it has found no evidence so far that the affected data has been misused.
TfL estimates that around 265,000 photocard customers may have been impacted, up from an estimated 250k previously, but the investigation is not yet complete and the figure may increase further.
TfL says it has now contained the incident and contacted affected customers. It has also notified the UK's Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).
Those who received the previous email urging them to create an account on the platform should ignore it.
The leak of security questions and answers is particularly concerning, as those may be used in account access restoration, so customers should remain vigilant for suspicious activity and report it to the organization as soon as possible.
Once the Oyster photocard portal is restored, all photocard customers will be prompted to change their password, security question, and answer.
However, if those same security questions are used at other sites, they should be rotated to new questions and answers.
It is also recommended to change passwords on other services where the same information was used.
In 2024, Scattered Spider members breached TfL's network and stole customer data and disrupted online services, including Oyster refunds.
The attack cost the agency £29 million in losses and recovery costs, while two of the hackers pleaded guilty in June 2026 and were sentenced to five years and six months in prison.
