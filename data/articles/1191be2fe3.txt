Bahrain has arrested dozens of Shia clerics since the Iran war began in late February. Authorities accuse them of plotting with Tehran, while rights groups have raised concerns about torture and forced confessions. Gulf states have backed Bahrain’s judiciary.
A Bahraini court sentenced 64 people, most of whom are Shia clerics, to life in prison on Monday over an alleged Iran-backed plot to overthrow the government.
Five others received 15-year sentences, according to the UK-based Bahrain Institute for Rights and Democracy (BIRD). The organisation said all 69 defendants were convicted, including 60 Shia clerics.
Among those sentenced to life was Sheikh Isa Qassim, a leading religious figure in Bahrain’s Shia community who lives in Iran, BIRD told Reuters. He was among 13 defendants tried in absentia.
Prosecutors said the defendants had formed and run a terrorist group linked to Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). They accused members of raising money without authorisation and using mosques, sermons and charitable organisations to promote allegiance to Iran’s leadership.
The court also fined some defendants 100,000 Bahraini dinars (€240,000) and ordered the confiscation of more than 8 million dinars (€19.2 million) for charitable purposes, prosecutors said.
Before the verdict, 20 human rights organisations, including Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch, had called for an independent investigation into torture allegations and urged the court to exclude any confessions obtained through abuse.
The groups said defendants had described prolonged blindfolding, sleep deprivation and threats against them and their families. Some said they had been questioned without lawyers and pressured into signing statements they were not allowed to read.
Defence lawyers were also prevented from obtaining copies of case files and had to rely on handwritten notes taken under tight time limits, the groups said.
Bahrain’s public prosecution said it respected defendants’ legal rights throughout the investigation and trial. It said lawyers representing those in custody had attended hearings and were able to examine evidence, question witnesses and present their arguments.
BIRD said the court brought the verdict forward from 22 November to Monday at prosecutors’ request. Defence lawyers were notified the evening before it was delivered.
The UK government said last Tuesday that it was aware of concerns about the trial and regularly discussed human rights, including fair trial guarantees, with Bahrain.
Iran condemns trial as Gulf states back Bahrain
Iran’s foreign ministry condemned the court's ruling on Tuesday as a human rights violation and rejected allegations linking the defendants to Tehran.
Bahrain rejected the criticism, saying its courts judged criminal acts rather than religious beliefs and that the defendants had received the safeguards required by law.
On Wednesday, Gulf Cooperation Council Secretary-General Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi expressed the bloc’s confidence in Bahrain’s judiciary and accused Iran of interfering in the country’s internal affairs.
Bahrain, which hosts the US Navy’s Fifth Fleet, has faced Tehran's missile and drone attacks during the Iran war.
Authorities arrested dozens of Shia clerics and religious speakers in May and June, accusing them of seeking to overthrow the government with Iranian support.
Sunni and Shia are the two main branches of Islam, split in 632 over who should lead the Muslim community after the death of Prophet Muhammad.
Bahrain has a Shia-majority population, but it is ruled by a Sunni royal family.
