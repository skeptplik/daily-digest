Ponds are to be restored and freshwater habitats created for amphibians and reptiles at two former colliery sites in South Yorkshire.
The Froglife charity has been awarded nearly Â£890,000 of Lottery cash to carry out the work in Rother Valley, as well as at sites in North and West Yorkshire.
Kiveton and Dinnington Community Woodlands were originally regenerated through the National Coalfields Programme after the pits there closed in the 1990s.
Sheila Grundry, from Froglife, said staff were "delighted" to work in the area because "freshwater will make a lasting difference for frogs and other wildlife and it's a great way for local people to learn new skills".
The Â£889,761 investment in Froglife's Coalface to Wildspace project will also help local communities connect with nature, according to the charity.
The aim of the project across Yorkshire is to create 48 new ponds, improve 93 existing freshwater habitats and establish new "neighbourhood wildlife corridors" like small ponds and bug hotels.
These "corridors" will act as stepping stones for local wildlife from insects to birds, providing food, shelter and water to improve biodiversity across former industrial sites and help wildlife move between one habitat and another.
Conservationist Andy Goff, project manager in Yorkshire, said the transformation of the sites would be for everyone.
"We want people to engage with the nature that is on their doorstep and wildlife in general," he explained.
"I'm really excited about getting people involved from all over Yorkshire."
The Coalface to Wildspace project is funded by The National Lottery Heritage Fund for the next four years, with Froglife having already run similar projects in the Midlands and Scotland.
Rother Valley MP Jake Richards said the project was important for the area.
"These former industrial sites are an important part of our area's history," he said.
"This project will help transform them into thriving habitats while creating opportunities for local people to get involved."
