The past year has been... a lot. Record-breaking temperatures, devastating floods and catastrophic wildfires have brought the realities of climate change into sharp focus for people around the world. At the same time, many governments and organisations have scaled back on their climate action.
If you’re anything like me you might be feeling it’s all a bit ‘doom and gloom’. So in this series I decided to face my climate anxiety head on and set out in search of reasons for optimism.
In this first episode of Cooked? I talk to experts around the world about what the data really show and why there are still reasons to hope.

Figuring out how much data centres may be using fossil fuels, though, is not easy. Ketan explained to me that when companies report their power consumption it’s not quite as simple as them just stating what proportion of renewable energy versus fossil fuels they’ve used. Similarly to carbon offsets, companies can purchase certificates from renewable energy providers and then use them to show that their emissions are lower than they are. Microsoft recently stopped using this method, which has been widely criticised, and in their most recent report there was a large rise in their emissions.
The companies can also have positive effects on renewable energy. With their vast resources they can fund new wind and solar farms. Microsoft, for example, has announced a $6 billion agreement to build AI infrastructure powered by renewables in Norway. Deals such as these can also then be included in companies’ reports on what kinds of energy they use.
