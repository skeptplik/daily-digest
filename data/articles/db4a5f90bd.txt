SecurityWeek’s weekly cybersecurity news roundup offers a concise overview of important developments that may not receive full standalone coverage yet remain relevant to the broader threat landscape.
This curated summary highlights key stories across vulnerability disclosures, emerging attack methods, policy updates, industry reports, and other noteworthy events to help readers stay well-informed about the evolving cybersecurity environment.
Here are this week’s highlights:
ChatGPT Sites exposure
Pluto researchers combed web archives, public code repositories, search engines and other sources and found 16,666 sites created with ChatGPT Sites (which builds and hosts web apps for ChatGPT and Codex users), 12,830 of which were accessible to anyone. Manual checks confirmed sensitive data exposure at 27 organizations, including a Fortune 500 company’s sales dashboard, a freight forwarder’s customer quotes, and a vendor’s sales pitch. Sites are visible only to their owners and admins unless shared, but owners can open them to the entire internet.
RansomwareHelp co-founder accused of profiting from ransom payments
US authorities have charged Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, a Colombian national and co-founder of the ransomware recovery firm RansomwareHelp, with three counts of wire fraud. Prosecutors allege that instead of using its own decryption technology as advertised, his company purchased decryptors from cybercriminals at prices significantly lower than what it charged customers. In one case involving undercover FBI agents, Palacio allegedly paid a $6,000 ransom and billed the purported victim $13,260 for decrypting their files.
USDA payment systems breach
A system used by the US Department of Agriculture (USDA) to make payments to farmers has been breached, according to NBC News. Two former department officials said the intrusion could let hackers access databases containing sensitive information on food safety and diseases carried by animals.
SolarWinds and Adobe patch vulnerabilities
SolarWinds has patched several critical vulnerabilities in its Serv-U managed file transfer product, including flaws that can lead to privilege escalation and arbitrary code execution. Adobe has addressed critical security holes in Campaign Classic and Experience Manager, including ones that allow arbitrary code execution, and a medium-severity DoS vulnerability in InDesign.
Container Store restores systems after cyberattack
US retailer The Container Store has restored its systems following a cyberattack that prevented customers from placing orders online. There is currently no evidence that customers’ financial information was stolen. The n0n ransomware group listed the company on its leak site earlier this month, but The Container Store has not addressed the claim.
VirusTotal adds new ransomware detection capabilities
VirusTotal has introduced new ransomware detection capabilities that rely on evidence of user files being encrypted during sandbox analysis. The improvements include a new behavior tag and stricter requirements for assigning the MITRE ATT&CK technique associated with data encryption for impact. VirusTotal demonstrated the effectiveness of the changes against ransomware that evaded detection by all 75 static antivirus engines at the time of analysis.
$17 billion targeted in US government anti-scam operation
The US Justice Department’s crackdown on fraud has targeted more than 800 defendants across over 400 cases since March. Authorities have also helped disrupt scam centers in more than 10 countries. According to the FBI, its Operation Blackout has led to the seizure or restraint of nearly $17 billion.
McDonald’s Japan hacked
McDonald’s Japan has disclosed a breach involving a database storing information associated with its mobile application. Hackers gained access to customer purchase histories, device location information, internal customer identifiers and IP addresses, but not names, contact details or credit card data. The number of impacted customers has not been disclosed.
Bithumb uncovers wider breach
South Korean cryptocurrency exchange Bithumb says more individuals and more types of personal information were compromised in an October cyberattack than initially reported. Bithumb also said the hackers had access for more than two weeks, not one hour as initially believed. The hackers stole data from an internal collaboration system, including identity documents, bank account numbers, transaction histories, and crypto wallet addresses. South Korean media reported that the initial victim count exceeded 7,000, including customers and staff.
Artex gains traction among state-sponsored hackers
Microsoft has observed several state-sponsored hacking groups, including China’s Salt Typhoon, as well as cybercriminals using the AI-powered penetration testing framework Artex. Hackers have leveraged the tool to identify exposed secrets in public code repositories, conduct supply chain attacks and find vulnerable systems. Microsoft says the framework can significantly accelerate intrusions, with attackers achieving privileged access and establishing persistence within minutes.
Quantum Act targets Europe’s cryptographic readiness
The European Commission’s proposed Quantum Act includes measures to accelerate the development and adoption of post-quantum cryptography and prepare the EU for the security risks posed by quantum computers. The legislation is part of a broader effort to strengthen Europe’s quantum technology industry, reduce reliance on foreign suppliers and establish global leadership in the field by 2030.
Tanker cyberattacks
Up to 50 ships carrying oil, liquefied petroleum, chemicals and other potentially dangerous cargo are believed to have been targeted in a wave of cyberattacks, according to The Wall Street Journal. US authorities are investigating whether Iran is responsible for the attacks.
Radware hacked
An internal report obtained by BleepingComputer shows that hackers breached cybersecurity firm Radware through an unprotected Cloud WAF management API exposed to the internet. Customer contact information and credentials associated with AWS, Azure and SFTP storage used by some customers may have been compromised. The company has not responded to SecurityWeek’s request for comment.
