Doctors and public health leaders expressed grief and alarm at the loss of US measles-free status, a hard-won public health victory that once made the US a leader in infectious disease elimination.
Measles was declared resurgent in the United States, Bolivia and Mexico by the Pan American Health Organization (Paho) on Tuesday, meaning the virus has spread continuously for 12 months.
The announcement formally ends a 26-year record of measles elimination in the US, and comes amid the worst measles outbreak since 1991, making the loss of measles elimination status widely predicted. The Americas were once the only measles-free World Health Organization (WHO) region on the globe.
“There’s no question it was emotional, because we’ve been working on this initiative for decades,” Dr Jon Andrus, the chair of the Paho advisory committee that recommended the US lose its measles elimination status, told the Guardian.
“To see the US, once a global leader, lose its status is of course sad,” Andrus continued, citing the work of friends and colleagues, “and it was also sad to see our Mexican colleagues lose their status, as well as Bolivia.”
That grief was in sharp contrast to the reaction of the Trump administration. The health secretary, Robert F Kennedy Jr, who has spent years spreading misinformation about the measles, mumps and rubella combination vaccine (MMR) and downplaying the disease, had his agency promote an appearance with a Texas-based chef and posted a picture of himself as James Bond this week. After devastating midterm losses, Trump appointed the Maha advocate Stephen Miller as White House chief of staff.
Measles elimination status is a public health designation. It does not come with penalties or travel restrictions. Instead, it is a project of public health workers, from localities to international officials, who rank vaccination among the most cost-effective ways to improve human life, alongside public sanitation and clean drinking water.
“There’s a huge return on investment that is pretty amazing,” said Andrus.
The loss of elimination status has already heightened calls for Kennedy to be fired – including from critical swing vote senators such as Republican Bill Cassidy of Louisiana. On Sunday, Cassidy called Kennedy’s tenure as health secretary “a series of failures”. Raphael Warnock, Democratic senator of Georgia, criticized Kennedy for posting “AI slop” when the US lost its measles-free status.
For his part, Kennedy said in a statement posted on X late on Tuesday afternoon that the US “will stop measles outbreaks, protect American families, and work to regain US measles elimination status”.
He added that he would “continue to recommend MMR vaccination as the most effective way to prevent measles” and that officials with the Department of Health and Human Services “are working to close vaccination gaps and rebuild public trust. We will answer families’ questions honestly about vaccine benefits and risks and work with trusted health care providers and community leaders to prevent measles, limit its spread, and support timely treatment.”
The US is in the midst of the worst measles outbreak in decades, with more than 5,400 cases and 10 measles-associated deaths reported by state health authorities. Measles is considered one of the most infectious diseases known to medicine. The infection kills one to three unvaccinated people per 1,000 and hospitalizes up to one in five.
“The measles virus is not harmless,” said the American Academy of Pediatrics president, Dr Andrew D Racine. “It can cause pneumonia, hospitalization, brain damage and death. We have an extraordinary tool to prevent it: a safe, effective, inexpensive and readily available vaccine.”
Notably, the worst of this year’s outbreak would not have even been considered by Andrus and colleagues at Paho, since the group only examined data from the first half of 2026. The announcement was nevertheless widely predicted and even leaked. It comes amid a global resurgence in measles that saw Canada lose its measles-free status in 2025, and the UK this year.
“As a pediatrician and a parent, I am heartbroken that we have reached this point,” Dr Richard Besser, president and CEO of the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation, and a former acting director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), said in a statement.
“Medical providers and state, local and tribal health officials across America are doing what they can to stop current measles outbreaks and prevent new ones,” said Besser. “Yet for the past two years, the federal government has largely been missing in action. That needs to change. It is no surprise that the resurgence of measles has coincided with an administration that has inflicted significant damage to our healthcare system while spreading false claims about vaccines.”
The Democratic senator-elect Dr Annie Andrews of South Carolina, a critic of Kennedy and one of several newly elected doctors and scientists, said: “We need to restore trust in public health, make sure every family has access to life-saving vaccines, and put the health and safety of our children ahead of our politics.”
Health experts also called on the US to begin work on an action plan for Paho, one that could help the US stop transmission and make gains in vaccination. Considered a multi-year project, Brazil lost measles-free status in 2019 and regained it five years later. On Tuesday, Paho announced that Canada had stopped transmission for 60 days, a step toward regaining its measles-free status.
As measles spreads in the US, Americans face a new reality where pockets of low vaccination rates can combust into outbreaks. In places such as Cleveland, Ohio, a nearby outbreak has spurred the local public health director to tell residents to find decades-old vaccination certificates, lest they be exposed and require a blood draw or quarantine.
“Because measles is so contagious, it’s considered a bellwether, a signal of problems in efforts to control disease,” epidemiologist Gregg Gonsalves, a Yale researcher who serves on the coordinating committee for advocacy group Defend Public Health, said in a statement.
“RFK Jr has spent over a decade trying to destroy disease prevention, the last two as HHS secretary, and his public health malpractice is coming home to roost.
“Congress can no longer sit back and twiddle its thumbs.”
