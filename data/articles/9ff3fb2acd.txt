Barristers could be barred from practice if they perpetuate rape myths to defend clients, the justice secretary has said after Russell Brand’s trial.
Alex Norris has written to the body that regulates barristers amid concern over the tactics deployed by the comedian’s counsel. In his letter to the Bar Standards Board (BSB), Norris said it was “unacceptable” for the profession to “perpetuate myths” about rape victims in court, and warned that those who broke the rules could face disciplinary action.
A series of rape myths were allegedly deployed in the defence of Brand, 51, during his trial at Southwark crown court, including the argument that his accusers should not be believed because they had not screamed, kicked or fled.
Brand was convicted of attacks against four of the six women who gave evidence against him, and is serving a 10-and-a-half-year prison term for one count of rape and three sexual assaults.
In his closing speech to the jury, Brand’s defence barrister, Ian Winter KC, argued that the comedian could not be a rapist because his accusers had not “resisted or screamed or even tried to leave”.
Winter asked the jury whether they could be sure that of the “many, many women” Brand had slept with over a decade, he had “raped just three of them”.
Winter has reported himself to the BSB after criticisms over an exchange with a Sunday Times journalist outside court as well as his conduct within it.
Norris wrote in his letter to the BSB: “[The board] must send a strong signal to the profession on the harm arising from rape myths, and that the perpetuation of such myths within a court room is unacceptable, just as they are unacceptable in our society at large.”
A spokesperson for the BSB told the Times it agreed that rape and sexual assault cases “must be decided on the basis of the evidence and not prejudice”. They added: “That is one of the reasons why we set up an expert reference group to advise on strengthening standards for barristers acting in rape and serious sexual offence cases.”
A spokesperson for the Bar Council, which represents barristers, told the paper that a defendant denying a rape charge had a right to have evidence tested in court, and stressed the importance of the BSB remaining independent of the government.
In a separate development, Brand’s wife, Laura Brand, shared a message from her jailed husband in which he claimed he was “not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening”.
Ed Miliband, the foreign secretary, said Brand’s message was “disgusting” and apologised for appearing on the disgraced comedian’s online show as Labour leader in 2015.
Miliband told Sky News: “His crimes are heinous and awful. I think it’s revolting, by the way, that he shows no remorse today in his comments. No remorse, no sympathy for the victims. Just disgusting comments.
“I’ve said that if I’d known any of these allegations against him, I would never have done that interview, and I regret doing it.”
Meanwhile, the culture secretary, Lisa Nandy, urged the entertainment industry to “get its house in order” or face a government crackdown.
She is due to meet senior media, arts and sports figures on Monday and will call on them to introduce systems to protect young employees and freelancers from harassment and abuse by older, more powerful men.
Over the weekend, Brand was transferred from the category B Wandsworth prison in London to HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire, which houses men convicted of sexual offences.
