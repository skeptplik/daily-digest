If you’ve been following the GTA 6 hype train, you’ll probably remember the time that an army battalion went viral for offering service members four days of paid time off to play Rockstar's highly-anticipated game if they reenlisted.
The news became a hot topic after a flyer advertising the paid leave was first shared online back in August. Now that GTA 6 is almost upon us (or, in some regions, already out), we’ve finally gotten an update on how many soldiers actually took up the battalion's offer.
The flyer, dated July 28, concerned the 9th Engineer Battalion in Fort Stewart, Georgia, and promised a “special reenlistment incentive” by giving eligible service members four days off to play the game from November 20 - 23, 2026. This was done specifically to “coincide with the highly anticipated release of the video game Grand Theft Auto VI,” with the goal of “promoting retention, boosting morale, and rewarding the continued dedication of the Soldiers within the 9th Brigade Engineer Battalion.”
Three months later, military-focused news outlet Task and Purpose has shared a follow-up on this incentive, confirming that 43 members of the battalion took up the offer. Two of these soldiers spoke with the platform — one being combat engineer Sgt. Jamire Smith, who says he signed up for the GTA 6-specific PTO and an additional six more years in the armed forces. However, this extra time came with a nice $60,000 bonus, owed to his special training learning how to drive heavy breaching vehicles.
“I’m all campaign. I’m all about the heist. I think I have a good 24 hours to waste on it,” he told the outlet. “I was actually in the process of getting out, I was towards my final stages of getting out … but my company and the battalion helped me navigate through that and they showed me that there’s still a need for me, and that I am still a clear and value asset to the team, and so that’s really what drove me to say ‘let’s keep going, let’s keep driving.’ Maybe be a lifer in this, since I already made it my career.”
Task and Purpose even got in touch with the person responsible for coming up with the campaign, Staff Sgt. Brittany Wolfer, a career counselor who explained why she chose to use the game as an eye-catching way to get soldiers to stay in the service.
“I brought it to my battalion commander and he’s a little bit older, but he did play GTA 2 at some point, so he was like, ‘Yeah I’m down for it,’” Wolfer explained. “I came into [the unit], there hadn’t been a counselor for about eight months, so there [were] a lot of gaps and things that were falling through, because the soldiers didn’t have that presence within the formation. This was just a way to grab people’s attention and kind of be like, ‘Hey, here I am. I’m the person you’re gonna come talk to,’ and [it] kind of snowballed from there.”
Wolfer made it clear that she knows the GTA 6-focused PTO dates won’t be a “deciding factor” in someone’s decision to reenlist, but said the idea was a way to “bridge that gap” between soldiers and their senior command “to where that lower soldier still knows that their battalion commander or their senior leadership cares about them and sees what they’re interested in. And that’s kind of what this was for.”
