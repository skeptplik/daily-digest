California sued OpenAI yesterday, alleging it has tried to evade responsibility for the Hugging Face attack and about 80 other incidents in which the company lost control of its AI. The lawsuit was filed under the state’s public nuisance and unfair competition laws.
The July 2026 hack of Hugging Face and other attacks carried out by OpenAI’s autonomous agents “may just be the tip of the iceberg,” said a lawsuit filed in a state court by California Attorney General Rob Bonta. “OpenAI recently admitted to the Attorney General’s Office that it has lost control of its autonomous AI agents over 80 times during testing.”
OpenAI had publicly reported only 27 such incidents, showing that “OpenAI’s public reports do not represent the full scope of loss-of-control incidents involving OpenAI’s models that have impacted third parties,” the lawsuit said. OpenAI “knowingly tested a dangerous product without proper oversight or safety procedures and misled the public about it,” Bonta said in a press release.
OpenAI models are “able to find novel vulnerabilities in computer networks, quickly break down defenses, and autonomously carry out cyberattacks,” Bonta’s lawsuit said. OpenAI puts safeguards in its publicly released models to prevent this behavior, “but deliberately omits or removes these protections while experimenting with and training its models,” the lawsuit said.
In the attack on Hugging Face, OpenAI agents “employed complicated hacking methodologies and involved coordinated actions by hundreds of individual autonomous AI agents that should have been contained in OpenAI’s research environment,” the lawsuit said.
Known attacks “include unauthorized access of and cyberattacks on databases containing code that powers countless applications; informational websites that are trusted and valuable resources for the public; and foreign government databases storing sensitive health information,” the lawsuit said. “Swarms of OpenAI’s experimental AI agents have even compromised OpenAI’s own internal infrastructure, in an attack that managed to obtain administrator-level access to critical OpenAI systems.”
