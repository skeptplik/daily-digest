2 missing police officers found dead in India-administered Kashmir's Kishtwar
The bodies of two special police officers were recovered in Jammu and Kashmir's Kishtwar district on Thursday, Indian media reported.
Indian media said the pair were deployed with the Special Operations Group (SOG) of the local police in the remote Kiyar village.
They had reportedly gone missing on Wednesday while traveling from their camp to an army camp in the Dharna-Marwah area.
The officers' bodies were found riddled with bullets and their service weapons were missing. Police sources suggested this could indicate a possible militant angle.
"One body was found around 8 a.m., and the second was recovered around 9 a.m. It is deeply tragic," Bharatiya Janata Party's (BJP) Shagun Parihar, who represents the Kishtwar Assembly constituency, told news agency ANI.
In a separate incident on Wednesday, three personnel of the paramilitary Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) were injured in South Kashmir's Kulgam when suspected militants opened fire on a security checkpoint.
Kashmir, a disputed territory between India and Pakistan, has witnessed a decades-long insurgency.
Mallikarjun Kharge, the president of India's main opposition Congress party, on Thursday addressed the Kishtwar and Kulgam incidents and said that India stood "united and resolute against terrorism."
Man dies in paragliding accident in Bir Billing
A tourist has reportedly died after falling from a paraglider in Bir Billing, known as India's paragliding capital, Indian media has reported.
The incident has raised concerns over the safety of commercial skydiving ventures in the popular adventure tourism spot located in the Kangra district of the Himalayan state of Himachal Pradesh.
Reports said the accident took place on Tuesday evening and identified the deceased tourist as a 26-year-old man from Uttar Pradesh's Noida.
News agency PTI cited police as saying the man had been on a tandem paragliding flight with a local pilot. He reportedly fell just six minutes after take-off.
The police have registered a case of death due to negligence against the pilot adding that a further probe into the incident was underway.
This is the second paragliding death to be reported in the densely forested and mountainous district in less than a month.
On October 25, a 47-year-old Bulgarian national was killed in a paraglider crash near Rising Star Peak in Kangra district's Dharamshala.
Mega museum on Indian civilization gets cabinet nod
Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government has approved the development of a massive museum in the heart of India's capital, New Delhi, estimated to cost around $340 million (around €306 million).
The 'Yuge Yugeen Bharat Museum' — which was first announced by Modi in 2023 — is being billed as the museum "with the largest display area in the world."
The Sanskrit name of the museum translates to 'India through the ages'. The project has been envisaged as the country's landmark museum, the government said, and will showcase elements of Indian history and civilization, which spans thousands of years.
Modi hails 'timeless richness' of Indian civilization
The century-old North and South Blocks in New Delhi's Raisina Hills — the seat of the Indian government — will be reused for the museum.
The colonial-era twin blocks once housed government offices, including Modi's own, before they were vacated over the last year as part of the Modi government's redesigning of India's hub of political power.
The Hindu nationalist government has long sought to emphasize India's pre-colonial cultural identity.
"Buildings that have been centres of governance will now become a place where millions can discover, experience and celebrate the timeless richness of our civilisation," Modi wrote on X on Wednesday night.
Calling the museum a "magnificent celebration" of India's "civilizational journey", Modi said the museum's galleries will bring to life aspects including ancient knowledge systems, mathematics, astronomy to architecture, art, languages and spirituality, through "immersive experiences."
Inaugural gallery to open in 2027
According to a press release from the Indian cabinet, the museum will encompass an area of nearly 178,509 square meters with the ability to welcome some 10 million visitors a year.
The finalization of the project isn't planned until 2030/31, but the first gallery on the theme of "adornment" will open its doors to the public next year, with more galleries gradually opening after that.
The content and collection analysis for the museum is currently underway, with the government having identified some 1.8 million objects for possible display from national and state museums, alongside archaeological and private collections.
