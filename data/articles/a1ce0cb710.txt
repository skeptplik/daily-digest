A new public art trail will mark the arrival of Asiatic black bears, Jersey Zoo has said.
Sculptures of bears and bees would be put up across the island's wild landscapes, coastal spots and urban spaces to celebrate the arrival of the new species in their new enclosure next year, said bosses.
They said the installation, which launches in July next year, aimed to shine a light on "connections that sustain the natural world, from its largest inhabitants to its smallest".
Artists will be invited to submit designs to turn the sculptures into "distinctive works of art" while schools and community groups will also be encouraged to participate.
It is hoped the sculptures will then be sold a fundraising auction at the end of the summer.
Rebecca Brewer, chief executive of Durrell Wildlife Conservation Trust, said: "We want everyone who connects with our work, whether at Jersey Zoo or through our conservation projects around the world, to feel they can make a difference for nature.
"Our public art trails are a wonderful way to bring that belief to life."
She said previous Go Wild Gorillas and Tortoise Takeover trails "brought so much joy to Jersey, encouraging people to explore parts of the island they might never have visited, walk new paths, meet new people and create lasting memories".
