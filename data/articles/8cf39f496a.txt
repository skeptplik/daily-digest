Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan said Thursday that his government is committed to pursuing EU membership despite tensions over the issue with Moscow, comments that come just days ahead of his planned visit to Russia.
The Kremlin has increasingly warned the South Caucasus nation against joining the European Union while it remains a member of the Moscow-led Eurasian Economic Union.
“I do not consider the repeal of this law possible,” Pashinyan told reporters, referring to legislation adopted last year launching Armenia’s EU accession bid.
Earlier this month, he said Yerevan plans to formally apply for EU membership by 2028.
The Eurasian Economic Union is expected to discuss the possible suspension of Armenia’s membership in December.
Russia has issued sweeping bans on a range of Armenian products this year, widely viewed as retaliation for its pivot toward Europe. It has also threatened to suspend or terminate a natural gas supply agreement with Armenia
Speaking ahead of his Dec. 3 visit to Moscow, Pashinyan said he hopes his talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin will help ease tensions.
“We have never sought to harm Russia and never will. We expect the same from Russia,” the prime minister said.
Pashinyan also repeated plans to gradually end Armenia’s decades-long reliance on Russian border guards, who patrol its border with Turkey and Iran.
“We are grateful to Russian border guards,” he said. “But I think the time has come for us to assume full responsibility for our borders in the coming years.”
Armenia, once a close ally of Russia, has slowly fallen out with Moscow since Azerbaijan’s military offensive in September 2023. During that offensive, Azerbaijan took control of the breakaway region of Nagorno-Karabakh.
