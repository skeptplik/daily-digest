Next has warned that hackers have gained access to data relating to the clothing and homewares retailer through a technology supplier.
The firm said on Monday that it had been informed that one of its third-party suppliers had “experienced a cyber incident” resulting in unauthorised access to the data.
The high street chain said it believed that customers’ personal data had not been compromised and the information affected was “limited in nature and, based on our current assessment, cannot be used to facilitate fraud against our customers or Next”.
It is understood the data accessed does not include information such as contact details, addresses, passwords or bank details, which could be used to commit fraud.
It said Next’s systems had not been compromised, though it was “affecting the supplier’s environment” in its business.
Investigations into the hack are in progress. Next said in a statement to investors: “Despite the limited and benign nature of the affected data, we are treating the matter with the utmost seriousness and are working closely with the supplier, law enforcement and relevant regulatory authorities.”
The latest incident comes amid a spate of cyber-attacks against high-profile retailers around the world.
Last week it emerged that a secret UK government cybersecurity operation had warded off the risk of a cyber-attack on fridges and freezers in UK supermarkets, warehouses, restaurants, petrol stations and stadiums.
That came after hackers caused many millions of euros of damage in continental Europe, with attacks on fridge and freezer control systems in recent months.
Fashion retailers including the owner of Hobbs and Whistles, TFG London, and the online fashion seller Asos have faced cyber-attacks in which customer data was accessed.
Last year, several other British retailers, including Marks & Spencer, the Co-op and Harrods, were hit by damaging cyber incidents which affected operations for months.
