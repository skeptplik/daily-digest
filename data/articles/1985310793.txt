'Miracle on the Mississippi': All aboard river cruise boat survive crash with tanker in New Orleans
New Orleans mayor says 32 people were taken to the hospital after a massive rescue effort
A tanker in New Orleans that apparently lost its steering slammed into a docked river cruise boat with 180 people aboard on Friday, the mayor said.
All aboard survived after the crash prompted a massive rescue operation for passengers and crew who plunged into the Mississippi River as the cruise boat took on water and began to topple over.
Video from the scene showed the much larger Atlantic Asphalt tanker hit the cruise boat while it was docked as metal and glass crashed and crunched.
Tugboat operators, nearby workers, police and several other rescuers rushed to help. New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno called it "a miracle on the Mississippi."
The mayor said 32 people were taken to the hospital, but none of them had critical injuries.
The American Heritage, a paddlewheel boat, started listing within minutes and flipped while emergency workers were still trying to get everyone off it.
Dozens of people on board and at least two police officers were rescued from the river and taken onto nearby ships.
'It was just chaos'
Devon Flanagan was at his job nearby at the Port of New Orleans when workers rushed down the dock. He got out of his truck and saw the American Heritage on its side and passengers in the water.
"It was just chaos," he said. "You had all types of ships, like other barges and other companies down there trying to help out, which also was a little convoluted because you had people still in the water."
It appeared the tanker had "some type of loss of steering," but the exact cause of the crash was unknown, U.S. Coast Guard Capt. Melanie Burnham said.
The cruise ship was built in 2015 and operated by American Cruise Lines. It is five storeys and can hold up to 150 passengers.
The American Heritage was finishing an eight-day cruise to several Mississippi River destinations in Louisiana and Mississippi.
The cruise line said it was working with the U.S. Coast Guard and would share more information when it could.
The tanker involved in the crash specializes in carrying large quantities of asphalt and bitumen, a semi-solid substance often used for road construction and roofing, according to the live shipping tracker MarineTraffic.
