Kenyan cult leader Paul Mackenzie was on Friday (November 27, 2026) found guilty of the manslaughter of 123 of his followers, the latest in a series of convictions against him for the deaths of hundreds of his followers.
The founder and pastor of the Good News International Church was found guilty of asking his followers to fast to death in order to meet Jesus. He was convicted together with 29 of his followers, including his wife Rhoda Mumbua, at a trial in the coastal city of Mombasa.
Authorities in 2023 uncovered more than 400 bodies in mass graves scattered across a vast ranch near Malindi town, where Mackenzie’s followers had relocated to evade scrutiny. Children were reportedly the first to succumb to the starvation.
Mackenzie was sentenced to 200 years in prison on November 7, 2026, for the murder of 191 people, including 190 children and one adult. He is facing a series of trials on various charges reflecting different ways members of his organisation died.
He was also convicted on Wednesday (November 25, 2026) of child cruelty and radicalisation in a separate case, and is due to be sentenced on those charges in December 2026.
