France has linked October cyberattacks that disrupted refrigeration systems at more than 700 supermarkets and other retail locations to Iranian state-backed hackers, saying the attacks caused food losses and sparked at least 15 fires.
In a statement issued Monday, France's Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs blamed the attacks on Shahid Kaveh, a cyber-offensive unit affiliated with the electronic and cyber center of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC).
"France condemns in the strongest possible terms the unacceptable cyberattacks carried out on its territory by the Islamic Republic of Iran against its interests," reads the statement.
The attacks occurred overnight on October 11–12, when hackers compromised industrial refrigeration controllers and remotely changed defrost settings to high temperatures, disrupting systems used to preserve food.
As BleepingComputer reported, the attacks affected an estimated 250 to 350 French supermarkets, forcing stores to discard fresh and frozen products.
French authorities have now revealed that approximately 700 retail locations were targeted, with the cyberattacks causing at least 15 fires.
A technical report published by France's CERT-FR says Casino, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Metro, Carrefour, and Intermarché, along with smaller butcher shops, a slaughterhouse, and cooperative food-processing facilities, were affected by the attacks.
French authorities say the attacks caused tens of millions of euros in food losses and significant material damage.
French investigators also detected attacks against Schneider Electric industrial equipment at small hydroelectric installations during the same period, which they linked to the same campaign.
These incidents reportedly had moderate effects on the targeted equipment.
The October attacks were followed by a second wave of refrigeration system attacks reported in November, affecting more than 115 stores across six European countries.
France's national blood service also reported a similar incident that altered temperature and defrost settings at three facilities used to store blood products.
Last week, the FBI and CISA warned of ongoing cyberattacks targeting industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems worldwide.
The French government says it will summon Iran's ambassador to condemn the attacks and propose new European Union sanctions against Iranian individuals and organizations involved in malicious cyber operations.
However, France has attributed only the October attacks against French supermarkets to Iranian hackers, not the later attacks.
Iran's Shahid Kaveh blamed for attacks
France's Cyber Crisis Coordination Center (C4), which includes the country's cybersecurity agency ANSSI, military cyber command, and intelligence agencies, attributed the attacks to the Iranian Shahid Kaveh IRGC unit.
According to CERT-FR, Shahid Kaveh operates through several online hacktivist identities, including CyberAveng3rs, Soldiers of Solomon, Mr. Soul, and Cyber Isnaad Front.
"The cyberattack targeting refrigeration controllers was relayed on the propaganda outlet CyberbanNews, associated with the Shahid Kaveh unit, as early as October 13, 2026," the C4 says.
French investigators linked the refrigeration attacks to Shahid Kaveh through the following observations:
- The attacker's infrastructure was also used to conduct reconnaissance against Schneider Electric industrial systems, using techniques previously associated with the Iranian unit.
- The attackers used the FOFA internet-connected device search platform, which CERT-FR says the group commonly uses in its operations.
- CyberbanNews, which France says is linked to Shahid Kaveh, publicized the refrigeration attacks on October 13.
- Investigators discovered traces of Farsi-language used in the attacker's infrastructure.
CERT-FR says Shahid Kaveh claimed responsibility for a large-scale sabotage campaign targeting Israeli and U.S. interests in March and April 2026, including attacks on Israeli refrigeration equipment and other industrial control systems.
In April 2026, the unit was observed conducting large-scale reconnaissance of Schneider Electric industrial control systems, including systems in France.
