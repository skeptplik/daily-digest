The employment tribunal between Rockstar and members of the Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain (IWGB) has concluded, with both parties issuing their closing arguments.
The tribunal concerned the dismissal of 34 employees last year. Rockstar alleged they "distributed and discussed confidential information in a public forum, breaching company policy and their legal obligations."
The claimants, including 23 of the affected employees, argued they were dismissed for union activities. Richard O'Keeffe and Lord John Hendy KC represented them at the tribunal.
As reported by BBC News, O'Keeffe stated that Rockstar management was aware of the Discord server for three years but took no action until union membership neared 10%.
"As grim as it is to see and to have to recognise, these posts simply were not the respondents' reason, far less their principal reason for dismissal," said O'Keeffe.
"Those respondent witnesses I've mentioned simply did not regard IWGB as a good fit for them, by which we can infer they meant too confrontational, too effective, and wished to prevent them gaining any leverage or power over the respondent."
Additional reporting from Sky News included further comment from O'Keeffe about the nature of the Discord server. "Those union members and organising committee members made extensive use of a private online forum for their activities, the server."
"Despite those individuals' extensive knowledge of the server, the nature of the discussions of working conditions, the criticism that management received and the presence of IWGB officials and, inadvertently, a number of ex-employees, no action was taken to investigate or raise any disciplinary concern about the server over three years until October 7 [2025], when one of their covert informants relayed that IWGB was approaching 10% member density within the workforce."
O'Keeffe referred to comments by HR executive Charlie Kinloch, noting that by October 2025, the server had grown to include 10 ex-employees, compared to three when it started. "If disparagement and sharing of HR communications in a server of 350 people and 10 ex-employees was genuinely viewed as gross misconduct, it would be similarly concerning in a server of 250 people and three ex-employees."
"That disparagement and discussion of HR communications was not viewed as gross misconduct; it wasn't concerning."
Rockstar was represented by Thomas Cordrey. In his closing statement, he said the server's moderators did not want to "over police" the platform and "let the taps of disparagement and confidential information flow," which he alleged included release timings for GTA 6 and unreleased features.
"This was a case of 'we know the policies, we know our employer would be opposed to the use of a semi-public Discord to disparage and breach confidentiality, but we did it anyway and we would do it again," said Cordrey.
"The reason why these individuals were dismissed was for that reason – flouting of contractual policies and obligations."
A spokesperson for Rockstar added: "Rockstar Games' position has been clear and consistent throughout. Individuals across the UK and Canada were dismissed for gross misconduct following the sharing of confidential company information, not because of alleged trade union membership or activity. The tribunal has heard our evidence and seen the facts. We remain confident of a positive outcome."
Judge Lucy Wiseman, who oversaw the tribunal, stated that while a timeframe could not yet be given for the judgement, she would "use [her] best endeavours to get that judgement out as quickly as possible."
