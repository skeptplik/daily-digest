Defense firms fall as Democratic victory stokes fears of tighter Pentagon budget
US defense shares fell on Wednesday as the Democrats' resounding midterm election victory stoked
concerns about increased pressure on the Pentagon's spending plans.
The sector has benefited from President Donald Trump's call for a $1.5 trillion US military budget for 2027, well above the currently approved $901 billion level, and the Iran war, which has fueled demand for missiles, air-defense systems and other weapons.
With Democrats set to control Congress, investors bet future defense funding may not be as robust as previously expected. Shares of Lockheed Martin, which oversees the biggest US weapons program, the F-35 fighter jet, fell 6.9%. Missile and radar maker RTX declined 3.1% and L3Harris Technologies sank 6.9%. General Dynamics and Northrop Grumman also dropped.

Once drawn to Trump, Latinos snub him big time in midterms
Latinos who helped re-elect Donald Trump turned on him in the midterms, irked by his unfulfilled pledges of lower prices and his aggressive crackdown on immigrants -- often other Latinos.
From Texas to California and elsewhere, Latinos voted en masse for Democrats on Tuesday as the party came in from the political wilderness to seize control of the House and the Senate, in a stinging rebuke of Trump and his Republican Party.
Tuesday's Latino turnout will probably be the largest ever in a US midterm election, said Janet Murguia, president and CEO of UnidosUS, a Latino advocacy group.
Many Latinos voted for Trump in 2024 because he pledged to bring down prices, but he has failed to do so, she said.
The 2028 election season begins as both parties navigate midterm fallout
The final midterm votes are still being counted, but make no mistake: The next election season has already begun.
Presidential prospects in both parties jockeyed for position on Wednesday, even as the political world was digesting the impact of the Democratic wave that crashed over American politics the night before. Democrats celebrated their party's stunning success — and began promoting themselves — while dejected Republicans scrambled to distance themselves from President Donald Trump's wreckage.
The Republican with the most at stake was likely Vice President JD Vance, who entered Tuesday night as the GOP's leading presidential prospect and woke up on Wednesday far more vulnerable — like any Republican who is close to Trump.
“The reason we lost is the war,” Vance said in a morning postmortem post on X. “If you’d like to argue it was worth it, that losing the Senate for the next 6 years was worth it, then do so. Argue it was a worthwhile cost to pay. Agree or disagree, that’s at least honest.”
He added, “And any honest conversation about where we go from here must start there.”
Alaskans wait for more votes in US Senate, governor's races
Alaskans settled in Wednesday to await more results from key races on the general election ballot, including a bitterly fought US Senate campaign and an open seat for governor.
A ballot measure seeking to repeal Alaska's unique system of open primaries and ranked vote general elections also was too early to call.
If no candidate wins more than 50% of first-choice votes, rankings will come into play. Alaska accepts ballots from overseas voters for up to 15 days after the election, making Nov. 18 the date targeted by state elections officials to tabulate under the ranked vote system.
The major candidates in the Senate race are two-term Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan and Democratic former Rep. Mary Peltola.
Trump says Hegseth doing 'a very good job' as questions swirl about his future
US President Donald Trump backed Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Wednesday, a day after Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in midterm elections that reflected voter anger over the Iran war.

Republicans call for Trump to change course
Republican allies and strategists called for Trump to shake up his White House personnel, restore discipline and scrap policies they blamed for the losses, including the Iran war and tariffs, as his presidency enters its final years.
Veteran Republican strategist John Feehery called the results a "huge disaster" for Trump.
"He needs to clean house," Feehery said. "Every day from this point forward, he's becoming more and more lame."
But Trump showed little indication that he viewed the election outcome as a reason to change direction.

El-Sayed tells AP Michigan rejected ‘hateful, spiteful campaign’ after months of Republican attacks
Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed told The Associated Press on Wednesday that Michigan voters had rejected “a hateful, spiteful campaign” after months of Republican attacks on his Muslim identity in one of the nation’s most closely watched Senate races.
El-Sayed said he was “running on adrenaline and caffeine” after his race with Republican Mike Rogers was called early Wednesday, giving Democrats another significant victory in a premier swing state as they won a substantial majority in the Senate in Tuesday’s midterms.
Throughout the cycle, Republicans sought to demonize El-Sayed, who was born in the U.S. after his parents immigrated from Egypt. President Donald Trump repeatedly called him a “jihadist” who was “on the side of the terrorists.”
“Michigan proved just how big we are. Rejected a hateful, spiteful campaign that had nothing to say about the issues and only wanted to point to my name and my face,” El-Sayed said. “Michiganders rejected that. Every single county, every single one, pushed against that.”
Democrats win full control of more state governments and the Texas House
Democrats in Texas took control of the state House after historic wins for governor and U.S. Senate, part of a series of sweeping gains nationwide in Tuesday’s elections that shifted the balance of power across several battleground and Republican-leaning states.
It will be the first time Democrats control the House in Texas since Republicans took over after the 2002 election. Republicans are keeping control of the Senate in the state — which also elected Gina Hinojosa its first Democratic governor since George W. Bush ousted Ann Richards in 1994.
In this year's midterm elections, the party took full control — both statehouse chambers and governors' offices — in at least five states where they previously shared power.

Authors of Democrat-led permitting bill 'not going to wait' for new US Congress
The Democratic architects of proposed bipartisan energy permitting legislation said they would not delay the bill in hopes of securing a better deal next year when Democrats take control of both chambers of Congress.
The measure, known as the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, would overhaul federal permitting processes for energy and infrastructure projects and is expected to be a major focus of Congress now that the midterm elections are over. The bill is the culmination of a years-long effort to pass legislation aimed at speeding construction of transmission lines, mines and both renewable and fossil fuel energy projects.
"This is the closest we have come to actually finding something that can get signed into law, and I'm not going to wait," Senator Martin Heinrich of New Mexico said in a statement to Reuters.
In a separate statement, Senator Sheldon Whitehouse of Rhode Island said: "A good permitting bill comes together over years of complex negotiations... It's a bird in the hand. We'd be fools not to take it."
