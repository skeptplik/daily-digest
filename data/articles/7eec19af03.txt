Democrat Roy Cooper flips Republican-held US Senate seat in North Carolina
Democrat Roy Cooper won North Carolina’s US Senate race on Tuesday, according to media projections, defeating Trump-backed Republican Michael Whatley and giving Democrats a crucial pickup in their battle for Senate control.
Cooper, a well-known former two-term governor, defeated a hand-picked candidate of President Donald Trump after campaigning on the high cost of food and energy as Trump’s approval ratings fell.
Trump coalition appears to crumble
The coalition of voters that powered Trump’s 2024 victory showed early signs of coming apart on Tuesday.
Republicans saw sharp declines among Black men, Latinos, young voters, evangelical Christians and voters without college degrees, according to the early exit poll data.
Support for Republicans among voters aged 18-29 fell from more than 40% in 2024 to 25% on Tuesday. Among Hispanics, it dropped from nearly half to about a third, while backing among Black men slipped from 21% to 13%.

Democrat Chris Pappas defeats Republican John Sununu in race for US Senate seat in New Hampshire
Democrat Chris Pappas won New Hampshire’s open US Senate seat on Tuesday, according to media projections, defeating Republican John Sununu and preserving a crucial Democratic seat as the party fights to take control of
the chamber.
Pappas’ victory denied Republicans a pickup in the cold-weather state, where high energy prices had become a major issue amid President Donald Trump’s war with Iran. 
He will succeed Democrat Jeanne Shaheen, who is retiring after 18 years. Pappas, a moderate US House member, blamed Trump and fellow Republicans for the Iran war, which has disrupted oil shipments and driven up gasoline and heating oil prices.

Rising Democratic star Jon Ossoff retains US Senate seat in Georgia
Georgia Sen. Jon Ossoff was reelected Tuesday to a second term, holding a key seat for Democrats by defeating Republican Rep. Mike Collins in a race that catapulted the Senate’s youngest member to the forefront of his party’s national stars as it looks to stymie President Donald Trump in his final White House years.
Ossoff, 39, began the cycle as a top Republican target and the only Senate Democrat running for reelection in a state Trump carried in 2024. But from the start, the senator ran as an aggressive Trump critic, with blistering critiques of “a failed president” that became a staple of voters’ social media feeds across the country.
He became a fundraising juggernaut, pulling in more than $100 million and sparking speculation about a potential 2028 presidential bid to succeed the man he said has used the Oval Office to enrich himself and damage the US political and economic system.
“Corruption in American politics runs a lot deeper than Donald Trump,” Ossoff said during the campaign. “It’s not just the money that’s corrupted our politics. It is a moral corruption, the cynical indifference of powerful people.”
Democrats Wasserman Schultz, Frankel and Gilbert win Florida House seats
Democrats Debbie Wasserman Schultz, Oliver Gilbert and Lois Frankel won election to the US House of Representatives in Florida on Tuesday.
Wasserman Schultz defeated Republican Brent Andersen in the state’s 20th Congressional District, while Gilbert won in the 24th Congressional District.
Democratic incumbent Lois Frankel has defeated Republican Deborah Adeimy in the race for Florida's 23rd Congressional District, according to the Associated Press.
They will represent their respective Florida districts in the House.
Democrat Mark Warner wins reelection to US Senate from Virginia
Democratic incumbent Mark Warner won election Tuesday to the US Senate representing Virginia, defeating Republican Bert Mizusawa.
Warner won a fourth term to the Senate, where he serves as vice chairman of the Senate Intelligence Committee. Mizusawa is a retired Army major general and Afghanistan war veteran.
Warner’s victory comes in a state with some of the highest numbers of federal workers, who bore the brunt of President Donald Trump’s efforts to slash the government workforce early in his second term. The Associated Press declared Warner the winner.

First polls close in US midterm elections
The first polling stations closed Tuesday in the US midterm elections, with Democrats hoping to secure control of Congress and the future of President Donald Trump's agenda in the balance.
Parts of Indiana and Kentucky were the first to shut polling places at 6:00 pm (2300 GMT). At stake in the midterms are all 435 seats in the House, one-third of the Senate, and a slew of state and local positions.
Cost of living and government corruption are top issues
About two-thirds of US midterm voters say Trump’s policies have worsened economic conditions in the US while only about 3 in 10 say they’ve improved things. Similarly, about two-thirds of voters said Trump’s tariff policies have hurt the national economy.
Views of Trump’s priorities more broadly — and his capabilities as a leader — are also mostly negative. More than half of voters say Trump is not a strong leader, does not fight for people like them, does not have the right priorities and does not have the mental capability to serve effectively as president.
About 6 in 10 voters said Trump places a higher priority on his own personal interests than the country’s interests, while about 4 in 10 said he puts more weight on the country’s interests.

Pennsylvania’s Chester County extends voting hours in three precincts
Officials in Pennsylvania’s Chester County say they’re extending voting hours in three precincts after “machine set-up issues” delayed the opening of polling sites just west of Philadelphia.
The Chester County Court of Common Pleas granted the three precincts a 30-minute extension, meaning voters will be able to cast ballots until 8:30pm EST, according to the county.
Voters casting ballots at these three sites after 8pm EST — when polling stations close statewide — will use provisional ballots.
Election officials extended voting hours at each location after county election supervisors reported delays caused by technical problems with computers.
Michigan election officials object to poll monitor questions about workers' training
Michigan election officials objected after hearing that federal poll monitors in Dearborn and Flint were asking workers about their training or requesting to see training materials, a spokesperson said.
“This is not permitted under the law,” said Angela Benander of the Department of State.
The US Justice Department agreed and pledged to inform the election monitors, Benander said.
Separately, she said federal monitors in Lansing were trying to talk to voters, which is OK if the exchange happens after a ballot is cast and is outside the polling place.
“We don’t have any concerns,” Benander said.
