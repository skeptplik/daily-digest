Bangladesh’s main Opposition party, Jamaat-e-Islami, has sent a legal notice to a major Australian newspaper for publishing a report against its party chief, who has returned from the country, cutting short his overseas tour amid a controversy over his visit.
In a statement, the party late on Thursday (November 28, 2026) said the legal notice sent to The Australian newspaper pointed out “seven defamatory imputations and demands corrections, retraction of the report, an apology and the preservation of relevant documents” involving Jamaat Ameer Shafiqur Rahman’s tour.
“The publisher has been asked to provide a written response within 48 hours,” the statement said but did not explain what measure the party would take if the news outlet did not comply with the demand.
The report by The Australian described Mr. Rahman as a "Hamas-praising Bangladesh Islamist leader" and criticised the Australian government for allowing him into the country without stricter visa screening.
The Jamaat firmly rejected the report and said Mr. Rahman's early return was due to pressing organisational commitments in Bangladesh.
The Jamaat chief reached Sydney on November 20 as part of a weeklong overseas tour to Australia and Malaysia.
In Australia, he was scheduled to hold meetings with senior Australian government officials in Canberra and join receptions and community gatherings.
But a major media and diplomatic controversy erupted on November 23 when The Australian published a report titled “Sinwar was a hero': Fury as Hamas-praising Bangladesh Islamist leader arrives. Other Australian Jewish and Hindu advocacy groups alleged that he has in the past made statements comparing working women to prostitutes and eulogising former Hamas leader Yahya Sinwar.
Jamaat secretary general Mia Golam Porwar called the report "false, misleading and defamatory". His foreign affairs advisor and lawmaker Barrister Mir Ahmad Bin Quasem sent the formal legal notice to the publisher of The Australian.
Mr. Porwar said the entire report was based on the application seeking the cancellation of Mr. Rahman’s visa by an immigration representative and “it is understood that the individual in question is the writer and director of Baba, a film glorifying (Bangladesh’s founder) Sheikh Mujibur Rahman and his family”.
Sydney-based migration adviser Raymond Solomon lodged an official application with Australian Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke, urging him to cancel the Jamaat chief's visa under Australia's Migration Act and remove him from the country.
Two other Australian media organisations -- Australia Today and the Daily Mail -- also carried reports covering the backlash from local community groups over Rahman’s visit and the controversy surrounding Canberra’s decision to grant him a visa.
According to the Jamaat, their chief had "successfully and peacefully” completed his scheduled programmes" in Australia but cancelled his planned Malaysia trip.
Several Bangladesh newspapers reported his early return. Bangladeshi daily Kaler Kantha carried a report with the headline -- “Jamaat ameer cuts tour short, leaves Australia amid visa cancellation fears” while The Daily Star wrote “Jamaat chief cuts short Australia-Malaysia trip for possible talks with PM Tarique”.
The party’s publicity wing chief Ahsanul Mahboob Zubair earlier told The Daily Star that Mr. Rahman has "cancelled the programme in Malaysia (and) is returning home after completing his engagements in Australia".
Asked if Jamaat chief was returning early because of possible talks with Prime Minister Tarique Rahman, Mr. Zubair said, "Yes, that is also one of the reasons why we feel the need for him to return" but declined to elaborate.
The Prime Minister had a private discussion with Mr. Rahman on November 18 at the parliament complex amid heightened political tensions over deposed premier Sheikh Hasina’s stated plan to return home despite a pending death sentence.
PMO officials said no formal schedule or date was fixed until Thursday (November 28) for a meeting between Tarique Rahman and Shafiqur Rahman.
