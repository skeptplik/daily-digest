I recruited the biggest Star Trek fan I know to help me road test the new Magic: The Gathering set. Right away he was impressed by cards that referenced the Star Trek: Prodigy cartoon and Star Trek Beyond—a third movie in the J. J. Abrams-verse I completely forgot was even made—but if that kind of trainspotting was all it took to make a successful crossover then people would have liked the Spider-Man cards a lot more than they did.
What makes the better Universes Beyond crossovers work is how they bring out the themes of their source material, turning motifs into mechanics that make a set feel like a genuine expression of the thing being adapted rather than just another property being fed into the content machine. Having played some games with the cards, yeah, I think Star Trek works.
I built a blue-green deck around Strange New Worlds, a mechanic that rewards you for having different lands rather than just stacking Islands and Forests. When I played Deep Space Scan I drew a card for each differently named land I had, and those lands also lowered the cost of powering up my Astute Terraformer.
Meanwhile, the Federation mechanic gives bonuses for having different creature types on the table like Vulcan and Klingon and Romulan (though not Borg). With just those two mechanics, the set emphasizes exploration and diversity. You really do want to put together a crew representing multiple species and send them into the final frontier. (There is of course a card called Final Frontier.)
Star Trek also makes use of the spacecraft mechanics introduced in Edge of Eternities, where a ship starts with limited capabilities but has to be stationed by tapping creatures to add charge counters to access its full abilities—like flying and fighting. Lt. Cmdr. Geordi La Forge is a solid combo, doubling the amount of charge counters you add each time and letting you get the Enterprise into the air twice as fast.
There are cards for various different iterations of the Enterprise, but I'm partial to U.S.S. Enterprise-A, Free Spirit, because when you play it you get three red 1/1 officer tokens for free. That makes it faster to station, and after that you've got three disposable officers to chump block with. Which is why they're red.
There's also a Beam Me Up mechanic to bring a card back from the discard pile at the cost of returning a card from the table to your hand, and a cute assimilate mechanic that lets you wipe creatures' existing types then turn them into artifacts under your control for your Borg deck. But what makes the Star Trek cards feel like Star Trek is how much they emphasize teamwork.

The Common Goal card lets two creatures deal damage together, and there's a whole suite of cards that get better when you've got creatures with a total power of 8 attacking as one. Worf, Honor-Bound Warrior, who doubles his own power when he's part of that attack, proved a gamewinner for my red/white deck more than once. The thing that really comes across is Star Trek's utopian ideal of everyone pulling together in a crisis and being better for our differences. It's optimistic, with stars in its eyes, and even a casual Star Trek watcher like me can appreciate that.
Magic: The Gathering's Star Trek set is available now, for both tabletop Magic and Arena.
