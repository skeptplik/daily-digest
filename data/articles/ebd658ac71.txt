Three teenagers have been arrested in Poland on suspicion of planning a terror attack, Tomasz Siemoniak, the minister in charge of Poland's special services, said in a post on social media on Wednesday.
The suspects, a 16-year-old and two 17-year-olds, allegedly had links to an international neo-Nazi movement.
The arrest comes days after a gunman with reported far-right leanings opened fire in Krakow, killing six people.
What do we know about the suspected neo-Nazi terror plot?
Siemoniak said in his post on X that the three teenagers had been arrested by the officials of the Internal Security Agency (ABW).
"The teenagers not only promoted Nazi and fascist ideology but had already begun taking steps toward committing terrorist offenses," he said.
During a search, ABW agents discovered chemicals used to make explosives, according to the minister.
The suspects were also found to have exchanged information over messaging platforms on how to make explosives, firearms and pyrotechnics at home, with the intended use of carrying out a terror attack.
The two 17-year-olds have been taken into pre-trial detention, with the 16-year-old being sent to a juvenile detention facility, Siemoniak said.
Poland is on high alert after 24-year-old Michal Oksinski killed six people and injured 15 more when he started shooting on Krakow's Main Market Square on November 7.
Justice Minister Waldemar Zurek said Oksinski had been fascinated with Norwegian neo-Nazi mass killer Anders Breivik and had amassed a large amount of ammunition before his rampage.
