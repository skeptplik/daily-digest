Marvel has no shortage of superheroes. But when Magic: The Gathering designers started assembling the cast of Marvel: Darkhold Destiny, they discovered that even decades of comic book history couldn’t quite give them everything they needed. And what they needed was more green. The solution was to create an entirely new Marvel character.
In an interview with Polygon at MagicCon: Atlanta, Magic vice president of design Aaron Forsythe talked about the unique approach that Marvel and Wizards of the Coast are taking for the game’s third crossover set, specifically about the development of a unique character that will feature prominently in Darkhold Destiny and the accompanying Marvel Comics.
“The idea for a unique character was Marvel’s,” Forsythe says when asked outright about the new character, who is named Fraxi. “I will say that she’s a green card.”
That might sound surprising in a universe that includes the Incredible Hulk, but Forsythe meant something deeper than gamma-irradiated skin. Magic divides its characters, spells, and gameplay philosophies across five colors with distinct identities that define much of the game. Finding recognizable characters who fit green’s combination of nature, instinct, and growth can be a challenge when adapting another franchise.
“It’s a tough color in a lot of Universes Beyond sets because there are not many bestial or druidic characters,” Forsythe says. “But there are plenty of red, blue, white, and black characters throughout all sorts of IPs. Green’s always a little bit more of a stretch, the way Magic defines the ethos and power suite of green.”
In developing Darkhold Destiny, Marvel pitched the idea for the Magic team to design a unique character for the set that would join Marvel canon. In the set's unique storyline, Dormammu and Hela combine a page from the Darkhold to rip open the walls between realities, unleashing arcane nightmares across the Multiverse. Thor and Doctor Strange are positioned as the main heroes, but a third brand-new hero will seemingly join them. According to Forsythe, making that character aligned with green in the game emerged as a focus.
“That way, it would really help us flesh out our roster in a way that feels authentically Magic,” Forsythe says.
In recent weeks, listings for some Magic accessories, including a deck box and playmat, identify a character named "Fraxi, Scion of Yggdrasil." When Polygon brought up the name and suggested a connection to the World Tree, Forsythe declined to elaborate. “I will not contradict what you’re saying,” he admits.
According to a blog post from principal designer Mark Rosewater, “Scion” as part of a creature’s name essentially just means they are a “descendant” or heir of something. For someone associated with Yggdrasil, that means that Fraxi could be the World Tree’s offspring, an embodiment of its power, or someone chosen to inherit that power. None of this is confirmed by any means, but it raises interesting possibilities about the nature of Fraxi and her power. Is she the physical embodiment of Yggdrasil, created to defend the various worlds it is a part of? Is she literally Yggdrasil’s avatar, or merely a character empowered by the World Tree in the same way that plenty of other Marvel characters are chosen to wield a cosmic entity's power?
In Marvel’s cosmology, Yggdrasil is the World Tree associated with Asgard and the other realms of Thor’s mythological corner of the universe. That makes it a particularly meaningful connection for Darkhold Destiny, whose story involves an invasion across dimensions.
At an October press event attended by Polygon, narrative set lead Dillon Deveney explained that after Hela and Dormammu use the Darkhold to launch their conquest, the ensuing conflict spans more than two dozen dimensions, including the 10 realms of Marvel’s Norse mythology. A character tied to the tree connecting those realms would have an obvious stake in their survival — and potentially a useful role in defending them.
That probably feels familiar to longtime Magic players. The game’s Norse-inspired setting, Kaldheim, has its own World Tree connecting its realms. That’s distinct from Marvel’s Yggdrasil, but the shared imagery makes Fraxi’s apparent premise an especially natural fit for the crossover. Deveney’s account of her creation also suggests that her role goes beyond filling a gap in the color pie. While developing the story, he kept encountering a role that existing Marvel characters didn’t quite fit.
“There was an obvious hole,” Deveney told Polygon in October. “I found myself forcing other characters into this role.”
The team developed roughly five different character concepts and sent them to Marvel. One, Fraxi, prompted a particularly enthusiastic response.
“They came back really fast, and they were like, ‘I don’t know how you guys knew, but this one, we’ve been looking for a character like this for a while,’” Deveney recalled.
Her origin story will be told in a Darkhold Destiny comic written by Jackson Lanzing and Collin Kelly. The first chapter will be released digitally on Feb. 26, 2027, with a physical release planned to coincide with the launch of Magic's Darkhold Destiny set on April 9. For now, Fraxi looks poised to give Marvel a new connection to its World Tree — and give Magic the kind of nature-powered hero its designers were struggling to find for Universes Beyond.
