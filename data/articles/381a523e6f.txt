Sauron is one of the most famous villains of all time, but The Lord of the Rings doesn’t really explore why the Dark Lord is so obsessed with conquering all of Middle-earth. The prequel series The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power has been working to make Sauron (Charlie Vickers) more complex — which isn’t hard since he gets to have conversations with other characters rather than solely appear as an ominous eye. He’s a master of deception who has appeared in multiple guises to further his schemes, but in one of the latest episodes released Wednesday, Sauron gets honest and reveals his true motivations.
Sauron is sometimes called the Great Deceiver, and his morality has seemingly shifted along with his face. He is a Maia, a cosmic being created to serve Aulë, the divine smith revered by the dwarves. But Sauron began working for the fallen Valar Morgoth and followed him to Middle-earth to serve as his chief lieutenant. When Morgoth was defeated, Sauron tried to take control of his Orc armies. Instead, Adar (Joseph Mawle), an Elf tortured and twisted by Morgoth and Sauron into becoming the progenitor of the Orcs, killed Sauron with Morgoth’s crown. It took Sauron a thousand years to put himself back together.
The humiliation not only changed his appearance but seemingly made Sauron rethink his outlook and regret some of his past misdeeds. He traveled with a group of refugees fleeing Orcs and was convinced that choosing to do good every day might make up for the evil he had done in the past. But when the refugees were attacked at sea, Sauron didn’t help. He betrayed a man that had been kind to him and stole an heirloom to create a cover story that positioned him as the lost king of the Southlands.
That deception allows him to act on his true motivations, which he reveals in season 3, episode 6, “From Valinor.” Sauron is obsessed with discovering how the Elven smith Celebrimbor (Charles Edwards) created the Elven rings, and believes he can learn the secret from Celeborn (Jamie Campbell Bower), who was imprisoned next to the last of the Elven smiths. Threatened with torture, Celeborn asks Sauron why he’s trying to master the rings and remake the world.
“It will bring me bliss,” Sauron responds. “I have lived a thousand lifetimes, and existence has but one true joy that is god to all others: To know, to feel the moment, the precise instant another being submits his or her will to yours. There is no greater pleasure, and I shall have it with all, for always.”
Celeborn is disgusted that Sauron has unleashed so much suffering because it makes him feel good, but the Dark Lord responds with a smile, “That is the only reason any free being has ever done anything.”
Sauron savors the sweet taste of submission
Sauron repeatedly demonstrates the sadistic pleasure he takes from getting people to give in to his wishes. He gives Celeborn a little smirk when he says “or her,” clearly thinking of how he seduced Celeborn’s wife Galadriel (Morfydd Clark) and persuaded her to proclaim him a king and introduce him to Celebrimbor. While disguised as a servant of the Valar called Annatar, Sauron deceived Celebrimbor into helping him make more rings, a mistake that eventually led to the smith’s death and the destruction of the Elven city Eregion.
Celebrimbor’s body hangs in Sauron’s workshop and an apparition of the dead elf has appeared to Sauron throughout this season, taunting the would-be Dark Lord that the knowledge needed to master the Elven rings and Galadriel will forever elude his grasp. Sauron is infuriated that Celebrimbor would keep secrets from him, but Celebrimbor explains that they were never truly partners. “The machinery of your soul is missing a piece, the one that allows for a true bond, a true union with another being,” he says. “So in place of connection you seek control.”
Whether or not this version of Celebrimbor is just a figment of Sauron’s imagination, he’s certainly right. Sauron is unable to gain bliss from love or friendship, the forces that fuel the heroes of Middle-earth such as Frodo, Aragorn, and Sam. Instead, Sauron seeks to crush the wills of those around him. He forces his future Ring-servants to fight to the death for a chance at power. He poisons an Elf prisoner to convince her husband to give him information about the rogue Orc Marnûkh (Adam Young), then denies him the antidote he promised. He persuades Marnûkh to give him information to spare his children, and then reveals that they’ve already been killed.
“From Valinor” ends with the culmination of Sauron’s plans for Celeborn. After being brutally tortured and returned to his cell, Celeborn is seemingly rescued by Galadriel. Giddy with relief, he spills the secret he has been keeping — that he knows Celebrimbor used gold and silver from Valinor to make the Elven rings. Galadriel leans in to kiss her husband, eyes tearing with relief, but as the two embrace, Sauron reveals his deception. “And I thought it was bliss when Galadriel submitted to my will,” he says, caressing Celeborn. Not only did he get the pleasure of breaking Celeborn, Sauron is also one step closer to his master plan of crafting the One Ring that will allow him to finally control all of Middle-earth.
