One of the first rules I learned when I entered the job market was: never work for free. Adam Driver and Steven Soderbergh didn't care much about that rule, because they had been given such strong assurances that Disney would approve their Star Wars film, The Hunt for Ben Solo.
In a new interview with Sam Fragoso on the “Talk Easy” podcast, Driver (who played Ben Solo/Kylo Ren in the Star Wars sequel trilogy from 2015 to 2019) told Fragoso that then-Lucasfilm president Kathleen Kennedy had reassured both Driver and Soderbergh that the movie would happen, and that Disney was on board.
With that validation in mind, Driver and Soderbergh continued to work on The Hunt for Ben Solo for free, until the project was canceled.
The Hunt for Ben Solo had one guiding principle — but it wasn't enough to save it
“We were gonna do a Ben Solo movie and that didn’t happen," Driver told Fragoso. "And we worked on it for a long time. Soderbergh did that for nothing, because we were given a lot of confidence. Like Kathy [Kennedy] said, we have a 100% approval rate [from Disney]. We worked on it. No one’s paying us to work on it. The only reason we were working on it was we fucking loved the idea, how we wanted to do it, and I loved that character.”
Driver said their love for the project and the character was their “guiding principle,” and that they were even figuring out how to make it on a low budget so they could realize their vision for the movie.
Because Kennedy was confident Disney would approve the project, Driver and Soderbergh believed the money would come later, once it was greenlit.
That turned out not to be the case. Kennedy, vice president Cary Beck, and former chief creative officer (and current Lucasfilm co-president) Dave Filoni were interested, but Disney studio executives Bob Iger and Alan Bergman shut the project down. Their reason? They couldn’t see how Ben Solo could still be alive. That’s a bit rich coming from a franchise where Darth Vader and Anakin Skywalker haunt the narrative around the clock — but we digress.
Earlier this month, Driver also confirmed on The Rich Eisen Show that his character wouldn't be returning and that the dream was over. “No more Star Wars,” Driver said. “We tried to do one more, but they’re like, ‘No, he’s dead.’”
It’s a sad ending for a character people clearly wanted to see more of. But the worst part isn’t that we’ll never see the project come to fruition; it’s that Driver and Soderbergh weren’t paid a dime for it. Never work for free, folks.
