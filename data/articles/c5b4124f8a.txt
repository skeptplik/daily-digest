That GTA 6 was going to be a hit was never in doubt, and the glowing reviews that arrived as the copies finally unlocked on the 18th ensured it. The scale of its success still managed to surprise, though: 32.5 million copies and revenue of $3.79b on the first day, surpassing all expectations. Analysts talking to GamesIndustry.biz have commended the game's unprecedented attach rate, but say that with the console install base limited, the currently-unconfirmed PC release is the chief scale opportunity.
Joost van Dreunen - who says the release was "unlike anything else we've seen before" - says his initial projection of $2b after the first weekend "put more weight on the relative sluggishness in overall consumer spending and the recent price increases for both hardware and software," conceding that "clearly GTA 6 didn't suffer any gravitational drag during its launch." Sensor Tower's Karl Kontus, meanwhile, points out that the firm's widely-reported figure of 24.5 million pre-orders only referred to digital pre-orders, while Rockstar's number included day-one sales and physical sell-in; he says the digital pre-order figure ended up at 26M, and holds the firm's estimate was "pretty much spot on".
Sensor Tower now projects 47m units sold by the end of the launch weekend, while Ampere Analysis suggests 40.2m in the same period (albeit on the GMT timezone). All firms have high expectations for year-end: Ampere's Piers Harding-Rolls says that "50 million+ is achievable by the end of the year," van Dreunen expects 50m, and Gareth Sutcliffe of Enders Analysis points to the firm's previously-stated "bull case" range of 50-60m. "I don't think it has hit its entire addressable market yet, especially with the major holidays still ahead of us," says van Dreunen.
"Franchises build momentum over time. Take-Two initially focused on its premium console fanbase, but has clearly reached a much broader audience. Their marketing campaign has focused as much on core gamers as on a more mainstream audience. Take-Two positioned GTA 6 less as a game and more as a blockbuster entertainment event."
For the next level of sales growth, the limit is the number of consoles in the market. "There are about 137 million current-gen consoles in the world, and a quarter of them bought a copy before its release," says van Dreunen. "The rest of this year is about the other three-quarters, and next year is about PC." Kontus calls the attach rate "unprecedented" and Sutcliffe agrees. "It’s clear that GTA 6 will have an attach rate to install base that exceeds any other title ever," he says.

Gareth Sutcliffe, Enders Analysis
"The key question is if Sony and Xbox have any capability to provide increased and meaningful console supply into the market this quarter, and in Q1. Indications are that they can’t, otherwise they would and definitely should be saying so. The title is clearly going to run up against console install base limitations sooner at this level of run rate."
All eyes are on the PC release, then, and van Dreunen predicts that ultimately the sales will end up at the same level as its predecessor: "It will become a staple for both console and PC gamers, so we can expect a similar number of units sold over its lifetime as GTA 5, translating roughly to 200-250 million for GTA 6."
Sutcliffe suggests an additional wild card: mobile. "If Take Two were to do something ingenious with Apple, such as much broader Apple silicon support beyond Mac, reaching into late generation iPhone and iPad SKUs for example, you would expand the total addressable market into the hundreds of millions of unit range," he says. "We would be in new territory at that point, and also very radical, but given so few were predicting this level of success, the question is how does Take Two get to 100m units otherwise, especially in year 1? It needs more platforms to get there, and getting there quickly will hold its retail price up."
"What I do anticipate is that Take Two will want to expand addressable market faster than the previous release, particularly given this console generation has a reduced net install."
All are united in expecting the PC release to deliver that expansion. While that blowout launch figure exceeded even the highest estimates for the development cost of the game, Take-Two's multi-year investment will have been predicated on a still-larger return, and the current console market has no way of delivering it.
"A decade ago Take Two would have been receiving sunny projections from both Sony and Microsoft on console install growth that have completely fizzled, meanwhile they’re committing to $1b plus in dev spend," says Sutcliffe. "They need to go where the market is as a result, and console has a defined ceiling."
