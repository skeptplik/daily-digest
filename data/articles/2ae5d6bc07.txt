France holds nationwide protests against violence towards women
Demonstrations condemning violence against women are set to draw thousands across France on Saturday, with feminist organisations, labour unions, students and Yellow Vest activists expected to take part. The marches come amid a broader wave of social unrest, with cost-of-living protests entering their seventh consecutive week.

Calls to protest gender-based violence across France this Saturday are expected to draw thousands amid the ongoing cost-of-living demonstrations that have continued for almost two months.
“We are calling for a feminist tidal wave to be organised in all our cities, to acknowledge that sexist and sexual violence must stop,” Sophie Binet, general secretary of the France's CGT labour union, told reporters.
The feminist demonstrations come amid a wave of protests across French cities, with students demanding more resources for schools and universities alongside cost-of-living demonstrations entering their seventh consecutive Saturday.
The protests, which have caused multiple clashes with police, appear to be losing momentum. Several hundred thousand people joined marches on the first Saturday of protests on October 17 compared with tens of thousands last week.

Two marches in Paris
Two separate marches against gender-based discrimination are expected to take place in Paris from 2pm, with the police prefecture estimating a turnout of around 40,000 people.
One of the demonstrations, organised by the feminist collective #NousToutes, will march from Place de la République to Place de la Nation to denounce “gender, social, and state violence.” The other, organised by associations and unions brought together as part of the Feminist Strike collective, will mobilise from Place du Panthéon to Place d’Italie to demand “the elimination of violence against women”.

The Yellow Vest movement, mobilised against the cost of living and for more direct democracy, has also called for demonstrations on Saturday against sexist and sexual violence and for gender equality. In Paris, it has arranged to meet at République with #NousToutes.
Sexism and sexual violence
At the heart of the feminist demands is the adoption of a bill against sexism and sexual violence. Born out of recent feminist protests and placed on the parliamentary agenda after the shockwave caused by the death of schoolgirl Lyhanna in June, the text aims to address the chain of violence against women and children, from prevention to reparation.
The law was adopted by the National Assembly on October 13 and is due to be examined by the Senate in January.
“The goal is for the text to be voted on in time [before the parliamentary session scheduled for February 28, 2027], that it be as ambitious as possible, and that it be given a comprehensive budget,” said Floriane Volt, from the Women's Foundation. The organisation estimates that the bill's implementation will cost €4 billion per year.
Last year, demonstrations organised around the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women brought together 17,000 people in Paris and as many across the rest of the country, according to data cited by French police.
More than one hundred femicides took place in France last year, while 272,000 women were victims of rape in 2024, and 1.3 million victims of sexual harassment, according to an official report published on Wednesday.
