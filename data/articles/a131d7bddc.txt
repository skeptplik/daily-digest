Australia has signed a new treaty with the Solomon Islands aimed at rolling back China’s access to the Pacific, partly reversing Beijing’s gains in the region three years ago that alarmed the U.S. and its allies.
The agreement, signed Wednesday in Canberra, is the culmination of Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese’s efforts to counter a 2022 deal that offered China security access to the Pacific island nation, signed just before he took office.
“Solomon Islands has been for a number of years a source of uncertainty and doubt in the region,” Matthew Wale, the archipelago’s prime minister, said in a joint appearance with Albanese, without specifically mentioning the China deal. “Those days are done.”
