In short:
Dan Sheehan will miss Ireland's clash against the Wallabies after receiving a two-week ban.
Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii says he relished playing at outside centre alongside Len Ikitau.
What's next?
The Wallabies face Ireland at Twickenham on Saturday morning (AEDT).
Ireland stand-in skipper Dan Sheehan will miss his side's final Nations Championship match against the Wallabies after being given a two-week suspension following his red card for a croc-roll against South Africa at the weekend.
Sheehan's initial yellow card eight minutes from the end of Saturday's 20-17 win was upgraded to a red after the match.
The 28-year-old has captained Ireland in the absence of injured Caelan Doris.
Sheehan, usually a hooker, played at number eight for the first time in his international career against the Springboks as the side were grappling with an injury crisis at the back of the scrum.
Ireland are already without at least three number eights through injury, with Jack Conan and Sean Jansen missing, as well as Doris.
Given the Wallabies' strength at the back of the scrum, the absentees will be cause for concern for the Irish.
Ulster's 23-year-old giant, James McNabney, should be fit to return after missing the Springboks clash after he was knocked out on debut against Fiji, although his Ulster teammate, backrow utility forward Nick Timoney, will likely start.
Ireland, which finished third in the Northern Hemisphere section, will face Australia at Twickenham in a play-off to decide fifth and sixth place.
Meanwhile, Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii said he enjoyed the combination at centre with the experienced Len Ikitau as the debate over his best position for the Wallabies continued.
Suaalii played at centre in his first 22 starts for Australia, following his high-profile defection from rugby league, but new Wallabies coach Les Kiss moved him to wing for the 42-38 victory over South Africa in Perth in September and the first Bledisloe Cup clash against New Zealand last month.
The absent Hunter Paisami may be expected to challenge him for that outside centre spot, while Ikitau could benefit from a move to 13, with Carter Gordon at 12.
In the Nations Championship this month, he was picked on the wing against England but returned to the midfield against Scotland and last weekend's 29-26 win over Wales.
"I just love playing rugby and, being able to play 13, you get to be both sides of the ball. You can defend and attack and you're in the front line," he said in London.
"Playing alongside Lenny, he's great with his comms. He's quiet off the field but once he goes on the field, he helps you quite a bit."
The 23-year-old Suaalii has also been a regular fullback for the New South Wales Waratahs in Super Rugby but has not been picked at number 15 for Australia.
With the hosting of the World Cup now less than a year away, and where home hopes will rest heavily on his shoulders, where best to use Suaalii's powerful presence has stirred plenty of debate among Aussie pundits.
"I think my biggest thing is just, I'm still learning the game. I'm still trying to get better each week," he added.
"It doesn't matter what number I have on my back; I'm just trying to use the skill sets I have … defence, aerial attacking, ball in hand, breakdown.
"I see myself just growing my game in all that sense: the attack, defence, breakdown, transition. I'm really just focusing on putting my energy towards trying to get better in that sense and then position-wise, it's just up to the coach.
"I've always said that. I'm happy to play anywhere, just always just trusting what the coach is going to do and, at the end of the day, I'm playing for my country, so I'm always grateful for that."
