PARIS — For its second pop-up in London, Nomasei has invited a local friend to join its temporary Notting Hill home, running from Nov. 12 to 22.
British sustainable label E.L.V. Denim, launched by former fashion editor Anna Foster, will have a three-day residency kicking off on Nov. 19 that will include an evening of styling of its pieces with the French brand’s footwear.
Once more located at 2 Blenheim Crescent, a 500-square-foot space located opposite the bookstore made famous by the 1999 romantic comedy “Notting Hill” starring Julia Roberts and Hugh Grant, the Nomasei pop-up will offer a range of styles, from its bestselling Nono loafers to the relaunched lace-up Slalom winter booties, its first shoe made in Portugal.
“It’s always this idea of meeting people, particularly those with whom we share values and a common approach to product,” cofounder Marine Braquet told WWD.

But she sees such occasions also as a form of self care. “It’s a moment between women where discussions flow, there’s empathy,” she added. “It goes beyond a desire to team up with another brand; it’s about being with others and taking care of yourself.”
The London pop-up also shows the growing importance in Nomasei’s business of the U.K. market, which has leaped 40 percent year-on-year, double the company’s overall 20 percent growth in sales. Braquet described the U.K. as a dynamic market with strong purchasing power, akin to the American one.
Meanwhile, the company has recently finalized the creation of a subsidiary in the U.S., which remains its largest market, although the territory’s proportion decreased as sales grew in the U.K. and France.
In addition to alleviating the impact of U.S. tariffs on its goods, the move is part of a wider ambition to be more present in the American market, Braquet said. “Now that we have this, we could offer the experience we have in Paris with a showroom, somewhere clients can come and try the product without us committing immediately to a store,” she continued.
“It’s a really dynamic market with a capacity to spend that is different from other markets and a physical presence is indispensable given it accounts for half of our business,” Braquet added, highlighting a difference in purchasing behavior between American consumers and their French counterparts.
Where the former sees shopping as “a way of life” and a social occasion, “in France, whenever the context turns a little subdued, you feel it immediately,” she noted. French consumers also tend to stick closer to weather conditions, for example, buying winter shoes when temperatures drop rather than when they arrive in stores early in the fall.
The 10-day duration of the London pop-up, as well as the recently concluded third turn in New York City, is part of a broader move toward physical retail that will see the brand land at Paris’ Le Bon Marché Rive Gauche from March to May next year, with its first brick-and-mortar retail on the horizon in the French capital in the second or third quarter of 2027.
Although the entrepreneurial duo was “happy not to have physical retail” in recent years, marred by a succession of protest years and the COVID-19 pandemic, they are coming around to the idea. Sales at the Paris showroom have leaped 50 percent this year and the founders are seeing “a real demand to try the product,” according to Braquet. After Paris, why not “two small outposts in London and New York,” she added. “But our ambition is not to have some giant flagship store on Fifth Avenue.”
With 40 percent of its revenue generated by returning customers, Braquet said this focus was the logical next step and the motivation in their ongoing first funding round. Last year, Braquet and cofounder Paule Tenaillon told WWD they were looking to raise a “one-plus-10” amount.
Ahead of the London pop-up, Braquet said they had adjusted their goal to a 5 million-euro target, having raised 500,000 euros from friends-and-family investors. Behind the scenes, the brand has been solidifying its fundamentals to improve its margins and “prove the pertinence and scalability of Nomasei’s model,” Braquet said. Among the changes are the arrival of one of its business partners, who owns a factory, as full-time technical director.
Now they’re “looking for a partner who knows this industry and has already done this because the challenge is Nomasei is something of a hybrid,” Braquet said. “We are a brand built on exceptional craftsmanship; we create luxury handcrafted goods but we don’t aspire to present ourselves or communicate as the major luxury houses do — we want to write something new.”
