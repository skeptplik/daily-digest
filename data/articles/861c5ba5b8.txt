Warsaw Modlin airport closed after bomb threat reported on London flight
Meanwhile, I am also following reports from Poland after a Polish LOT airline flight from London Heathrow made an unplanned landing at Warsaw Modlin airport following a bomb alert.
The airport – the Polish capital’s secondary port, located just north of Warsaw – has just confirmed that L0286 flight from Heathrow to Warsaw Okęcie, with 181 people on board of a Boeing 737 MAX aircraft, landed safely in Modlin.
The plane will be inspected by security services, and the airport remains closed until further notice.
Back to Poland briefly, Danish media are reporting that a separate threat was also reported in relation to an SAS flight from Copenhagen to Warsaw Okęcie, which also landed in the Polish capital this morning.
The Guardian understands the airport received a report about a potentially dangerous item on board of the SAS aircraft, and it is currently being assessed by security services.
Kremlin confirms Abu Dhabi talks with US, Ukraine next week
Back to the Russian invasion of Ukraine, the Kremlin confirmed that US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks to end more than four years of war will take place in Abu Dhabi next week.
“The Russian delegation will arrive on 10-11 November in Abu Dhabi to take part in trilateral talks,” Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said, quoted by AFP.
He added that presidential aide and former culture minister Vladimir Medinsky will represent Moscow at the meeting.
AFP noted that Medinsky, who is 56 and previously served as culture minister, is known for his nationalistic views on Russian history and took part in previous failed talks.
In the latest update from Warsaw Modlin airport, passengers are now allowed to disembark from the plane, and will be asked to stay in the main terminal.
The airport said in a press statement:
“Further actions are being carried out in accordance with safety procedures. At this time, we are not providing any additional information regarding the operations of the emergency services.”
The bomb alert comes just a day after a WizzAir flight from London Luton to Bulgaria’s Sofia was redirected to Budapest after a reported mid-air bomb threat.
After careful inspection, it proved to be a false alarm.

Russian attacks on Ukraine on US election day 'telling,' Zelenskyy says
Before arriving in Berlin, Ukraine’s Zelenskyy condemned Russia’s overnight attacks on critical infrastructure across the country, which he said were causing “emergency and unscheduled power outages”.
He said the attacks – which involved 289 drones, 59 cruise missiles and 17 ballistic missiles – also hit civilian buildings, including a city hospital in Kharkiv, where 73 people were injured.
“A rescue operation has also been underway since yesterday in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region. Tragically, the death toll has risen. Russia has killed 29 people.”
He added:
“It is very telling that all of this is happening on the day of the US congressional elections. The Russians believe that America will not notice this signal from them. But such terror must be noticed and stopped. Ukraine must be helped to defend its skies, otherwise Russia will never stop its missile strikes. It is essential to help build peace honestly and reliably, and America can do that.”
Morning opening: Zelenskyy to make unexpected visit to Berlin for talks with Merz
Jakub Krupa
Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy is set to make an unexpected visit to Berlin for talks with German chancellor Friedrich Merz ahead of next week’s trilateral talks with Russia and the US in Abu Dhabi.
The rumours about Zelenskyy’s potential visit started swirling last night, with Merz confirming the visit during an industry speech this morning. The Ukrainian president’s trips are usually kept secret until the last moment for security reasons.
Recalling the infamous showdown between Zelenskyy and Trump at the White House last year, Merz said:
“He’ll be here with me just after midday, and things will be a bit different then.”
In his speech, Merz said the German push to rapidly increase its defence spending last year was triggered by the scenes in the Oval Office.
“What we experienced last year … led me to conclude that we must ensure, on our own, that we are capable of defending ourselves in Europe,” he said.
He added that this was not meant to question Nato, but strengthen it. “We are making Nato more European so it can remain transatlantic,” he said.
Elsewhere, we are expecting updates from Sweden, with a second parliamentary vote on the new government, which is expected to fail once again (51 days on from elections).
We should also get some lines from Spain, where transport minister Óscar Puente is will present new ideas on reforming high-speed rail after last week’s tragic crash in Toledo before facing a parliamentary committee.
