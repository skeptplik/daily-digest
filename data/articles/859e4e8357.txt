Finland announced Thursday that it will keep its border with Russia closed for another two years, with Finnish President Alexander Stubb expected to formally approve the extension later this week.
The Nordic country shut its 1,340-kilometer (830-mile) eastern border in December 2023 amid an uptick in asylum seekers from the Middle East and Africa. Finnish authorities accuse the Kremlin of encouraging the asylum seekers to cross their shared frontier in a destabilization ploy — a “hybrid attack” Helsinki sees as Moscow’s retribution for having joined NATO.
The Finnish interior ministry said the widely anticipated extension, which takes effect Dec. 1 and runs through Dec. 31, 2028, was necessary to “combat attempts to put pressure on Finland, strengthen border security and help Finland prepare for the most serious incidents of instrumentalized migration.”
The measure extends the existing Border Security Act without altering its provisions, the ministry said, adding that the risk of renewed migrant surges “remains high.”
In 2024, Finland adopted a controversial “deportation law” that allows border guards to refuse entry to asylum seekers crossing from Russia. The law, to be enforced at the government’s discretion, has drawn criticism from human rights advocates who say it may violate Finland’s international legal obligations.
Finland became a NATO member in April 2023, abandoning decades of military non-alignment and drawing condemnation from Moscow, which warned of “countermeasures.”
In July, Russia closed its railway border checkpoints with Finland. Russian officials at the time did not disclose the reasons for the closures or when the checkpoints might reopen.
