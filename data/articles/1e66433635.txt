Nine years after selling his first nutritional bar company, RXBar, to Kellogg’s for $600 million, Peter Rahal is yet again one of the buzziest brand builders in the category.
That’s thanks to the smash success of David Protein, which in just two years has managed to disrupt the protein space with its fashion-forward approach, culturally attuned marketing and, of course, its macros: 28 grams of protein in every 150-calorie bar.
“The main actual objective in designing the product was [to offer] the most protein with the least amount of calories,” said Rahal at the 2026 Beauty Inc Catalysts conference in conversation with editor in chief Jenny B. Fine.
To help a great product break through, the founder sought inspiration beyond the food category.
“In my study, it’s luxury, beauty and fashion that have the strongest brands; you can measure that through pricing power, how people wear them or how they signal status,” said Rahal. “So the simple strategy was just, all right, everyone in the protein bar food space is super boring visually — let’s just imitate the best.”
The brand’s efforts in that vein have included tapping Julia Fox for its viral “Men Disappoint. David Satisfies” campaign and most recently, a photo shoot with Stella Maxwell to ring in David’s new milkshakes, which feature 30 grams of protein per bottle and are priced at $49 for 12 shakes.
Leaning into the unexpected has been key to David’s momentum, said Rahal, who is also building more nascent healthy-snack brands HallPass and Epogee via his holding company, Medici Brands, and just teased an upcoming apparel line, called Diet and Exercise, via Instagram.
“[I look for] ideas that are not obvious, but are true,” said Rahal of his approach to expansion. “If you survey 100 people and an idea emerges in 80 percent of people, it’s not a good idea. That’s generally how I think about it — is, what’s a secret or a mystery that no one’s really thinking about, that is actually true.”
Case in point, Epogee will develop snacks that utilize a modified triglyceride called esterified propoxylated glycerols (EPG). “We found an ingredient that no one can pronounce…[which] in the food business, that’s viewed as ‘bad,’” said Rahal. “[But] that ingredient allows us to get all the benefits of fat without the caloric impact, so our current strategy is to expand that and apply it to large categories like ice cream, protein, salty snacks.”
In terms of getting each of his brands off the ground, an early focus on community building has been integral.
“You have to participate in the community in an authentic way and be generous with it…give product away; don’t make it feel transactional — just give, give, give,” said Rahal, adding that his mother is an art history teacher, which fostered in him an appreciation for 15th- and 16th-century artworks including the brand’s namesake, David by Michelangelo.
“I love beautiful things,” he said, adding that more broadly, the question he keeps top of mind is, “What do our customers want?
“They want better taste and texture…they want better prices, and they want better nutrition,” he said, adding of the convergence between beauty, health and wellness: “If there was a pyramid of health and beauty…it really starts with sleep and nutrition and the physical part. At the bottom of the pyramid, getting your nutrition right, your sleep right, is foundational. It’s the same consumer, different categories.”
