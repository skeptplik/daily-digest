The leaders of Japan and South Korea are heading to China this week with mounting angst: Their closest security ally is set to sit down with Beijing and Moscow for talks that could tilt the diplomatic balance in Northeast Asia. Tokyo and Seoul, however, will be watching from outside.
U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin are due to hold an unprecedented three-way meeting Thursday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen. Trump has floated nuclear arms control as a possible topic.
The looming prospect hung over Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s meeting with South Korean President Lee Jae Myung in Manila on Tuesday. In their 20 minutes on the sidelines of Association of Southeast Asian Nations meetings, the leaders reaffirmed the importance of trilateral cooperation with Washington and agreed to strengthen strategic communication.
