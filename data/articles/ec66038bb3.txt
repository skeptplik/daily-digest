Sir Gregor Smith, who has resigned as Scotland's chief medical officer, was appointed to the role when his predecessor quit for breaking Covid rules.
He leaves the position having become caught up in a very different controversy.
Smith said he had taken a "personal decision" to stand down because he did not have the "good mental health" necessary to continue.
One government source suggested to me that Smith had taken the right decision and that it was better than anyone having to ask him to go.
So did he actually do anything wrong?
He came under pressure because he knew about the 2022 report into liver and pancreas surgery at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary which identified 19 potentially avoidable deaths.
That report was never published by the Scottish government or NHS Grampian.
Politicians and journalists have not unreasonably wanted to know what he did with the information and why.
By his own account, it was almost accidental that he had any involvement in responding to the report.
He told a Holyrood committee that it was "unusual" for this kind of issue to be referred to him.
He said he was contacted by a senior official at NHS Grampian who also proposed an action plan that included temporarily suspending this type of surgery.
Could the crisis have been averted?
Smith seems to have considered the plan "appropriate". He called a wider meeting to discuss and ensured the then health secretary Humza Yousaf was briefed.
He was kept informed until surgery restarted on the advice of an external expert in May 2023 and according to what Smith told MSPs on Wednesday he was "not directly involved in any further work".
His disengagement became problematic because we now know this type of surgery was suspended on two further occasions. Cases are currently being referred to NHS Lothian.
Strictly speaking, it is not the chief medical officer's job to supervise the performance of any surgical department.
But had he chosen to keep an eye on things, or to ensure someone in the Scottish government did, some of the current crisis might have been averted.
The 2022 review certainly did not end concerns with how liver and pancreas surgery was being performed in Aberdeen.
These concerns have now been laid bare by one of the surgeons in that team, Dr Mohammed Habib, who went public last week.
That has prompted the Scottish government to order an independent review, led by an experienced KC, to find out who knew what and when, what they did and why.
Ministers are not convinced that somehow the report - or at least its key findings - could not have been made public by NHS Grampian.
They are not satisfied with some of the answers they have been given from the health board including the idea that patient confidentiality prevented them going public.
Big questions for officials
The review will have big questions to ask of officials at both NHS Grampian and in the Scottish government. It will also be able to question the role of ministers too.
Labour wants the Scottish Parliament to commission the review so that the Scottish government is not seen to be marking its own homework.
Both they and the Conservatives seem to want to expand the terms of the review to take account of other distressing stories emerging from NHS Grampian.
Dr Habib is worried about the outcomes in 62 liver and pancreatic cases where he believes there may have been either avoidable death or other avoidable harms.
There will be a full debate about what form the inquiry should take at Holyrood on Tuesday.
It's not clear how long the review process will take, what further investigations may be required or indeed if anyone else may leave their jobs over what's gone wrong in NHS Grampian and how problems have been handled.
