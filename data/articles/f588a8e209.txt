Ten countries, including the United States, Japan and the United Kingdom, have condemned Hong Kong’s decision to sentence pro-democracy activist Joshua Wong to 10 years in prison, while the European Union has deplored a separate 12½-year sentence imposed on former Macao pro-democracy lawmaker Au Kam-san. The unusually strong international response reflects growing alarm over the erosion of civil liberties and the rule of law in the former British and Portuguese colonies, which increasingly resemble mainland China in their repression of dissent.
Wong, who turned 30 last month, was sentenced Nov. 5 on a charge of “conspiracy to collude with foreign forces.” He had already spent six years in detention and was originally expected to be released in January 2027 after his conviction in the case involving 47 pro-democracy figures arrested over their participation in a 2020 primary election. Wong pleaded guilty, apparently expecting a more lenient sentence.
The following day, Au, 69, received a 12½-year sentence for “subversion of state power” in Macao. His sentencing took place in a closed-door hearing. Wong will be 40 when released; Au will be over 80.
