Ubisoft is clearly serious about implementing generative-AI deeply into the game development process. The French publisher now expects teams pitching new project ideas to be prepared to explain how gen-AI will help with developing the game.Teams who don't want to use gen-AI will need to clearly justify their reasoning to the higher-ups.
That's according to a new Vertical Slice report, citing multiple anonymous sources currently working at Ubisoft.
Several employees said that pitching a game idea without gen-AI or without explaining why it does not involve the use of the controversial tech will not receive the greenlight. This is especially relevant for smaller games with tight teams who now reportedly must deal with Ubisoft leaders' desire to implement gen-AI if they want to get their pitches reviewed.
"We were told that using AI will increase the speed at which pitches can be made by 'small lean agile teams,'" one source said. "We were told that as the goal was to move fast, iterate and discard projects to sift out the good stuff. AI was essential in maintaining a rapid pace."
While some said Ubisoft is not openly mandating gen-AI use to staff, others feel "cornered" by gen-AI. One source said Ubisoft views the tech as "unavoidable", and that only a minority of the company's audience reject it.
Ubisoft recently gave name to an internal initiative to expand the use of generative-AI and research how its developers can make use of the technology. It's called Motherbrain, and it has its own division within Ubisoft.
Over a year ago, Ubisoft embarked on a major restructuring effort that has seen the formation of new subsidiaries, the consolidation of its biggest brands into 'creative houses', the closure of studios and, of course, multiple rounds of layoffs.
"Ubisoft is in a top-down nightmare of AI psychosis," one source added, pointing to the already low morale within the company. "Yves [Guillemot] has hitched his wagon to the tech, demanding the rest of the company follow on."
