Why raising prices in Japan amid ‘insanely high’ inflation backfires on firms
Uniqlo’s socks U-turn and Saizeriya’s Doria apology show it’s not easy to change consumers’ entrenched deflationary mindset
That came after Italian-style family restaurant chain Saizeriya issued an almost grovelling apology as it decided to add 20 yen (US$0.13) to the price of its trademark Milanese Doria, lamenting its “lack of effort”.
The very public angst, including in one of the world’s most recognisable clothing brands, highlights the struggles Japanese firms face as they battle rising costs in a country where decades of deflation have nurtured a consumer class averse to higher prices.
