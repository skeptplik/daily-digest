In life, only death and taxes are certain. But in Japan, there is another certainty after life: cremation. With 99.97% of the deceased cremated in the country, crematoria are a vital element of civic infrastructure. And because of the public importance of these facilities, almost all crematoria in Japan are run by local governments.
But Tokyo’s 23 wards are an exception, where six out of nine crematoria are operated by a private company whose parent is under partial Chinese ownership.
There are two key points at issue: whether crematoria should be operated by a company in which foreign capital is involved, and whether a private crematorium will be able to offer stable service in the long run without government oversight.
