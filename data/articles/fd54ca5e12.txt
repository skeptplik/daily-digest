The Oscars might be four months away, but potential nominees already have entered the red carpet style race.
Presented by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, the Governors Awards, which were held this past Sunday, have become an unofficial opening act for awards-season campaigning, drawing Oscar hopefuls, industry insiders and Academy members into the same room.
And while many of the stars have had months of fashion red carpet parades at film premieres and other events, the stakes are getting higher the closer it gets to awards season.
At Sunday’s 17th edition in Los Angeles, the red carpet delivered its own share of drama, from archival discoveries to sculptural couture and classic Hollywood glamour.
Zendaya made a statement in an archival Giles Deacon fall 2007 gown; its sculptural hips and tonal bronze fringe gave the vintage design a decidedly of-the-moment perspective. Tessa Thompson embraced couture originality in Schiaparelli, with a sculptural cutaway bodice and intricate lace and tulle detailing. Léa Seydoux brought old-school glamour in a dramatic red Louis Vuitton gown finished with an oversize bow at the back and sweeping train.
Penélope Cruz delivered predictable elegance in Chanel, while Charlize Theron, whose Dior fashion ambassadorship has come to an end as she has become the face of Dior Beauty, made a revealing appearance in a red Tom Ford look by Haider Ackermann. Julianne Moore offered a more unexpected interpretation of glamour in a spotted Bottega Veneta design with asymmetric cape detailing. Gemma Chan demonstrated the appeal of minimalism in Calvin Klein Collection, while Carrie Coon made a strong case for architectural dressing in a metallic Balenciaga gown.
Together, the looks offered a reminder that, long before the envelopes are opened, the awards-season fashion conversation is already underway.
