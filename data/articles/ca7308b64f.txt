Ukraine submits Black Sea ceasefire offer, awaits Russian response
Ukraine made a Black Sea ceasefire offer to enable food exports and passed the details on to international allies, a senior Ukrainian official said Friday, adding that Kyiv was waiting for a response from Moscow. The announcement came a day after Russia attacked two foreign grain carriers near Ukraine's Black Sea port of Odesa, according to Ukrainian authorities.
Ukraine has offered a Black Sea ceasefire to enable food exports and passed this on to international allies, Kyiv's ‌foreign minister Andrii Sybiha said on Friday.
"We are ‌ready for a ceasefire. We are grateful to India, Turkey, and Egypt, which are playing an active ‌and important role in de-escalating tensions in the Black ‌Sea, as well as in mediation efforts. We are now awaiting a response from Russia," Sybiha was cited by Suspilne public broadcaster.
He added that Russia had proposed Ukraine suspend attacks on the Russian oil sector under the truce, but Kyiv would only consider this as part of a bigger energy ceasefire deal.
Speaking at the 5th International Food From Ukraine Conference, Sybiha said that while Kyiv had presented its partners with clear Black Sea ceasefire proposals, Russia was trying to include oil supplies among the conditions related to agricultural products, Ukrainian news agency RBC-Ukraine reported.
The announcement came a day after Russia attacked two foreign cargo ships carrying more than 100,000 metric tons of grain overnight near Ukraine's Black Sea port of Odesa, according to Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi.
He did not say where the grain originated nor where the ‌vessels had loaded their cargo.
Ukraine has said grain shipments from its key Black Sea ports around Odesa have all but stopped ‌since late July after Russia sharply intensified attacks on grain terminals and merchant vessels.
Russia says it does not attack civilian targets.
Before the escalation, the three ports in the Odesa hub handled about 90 percent of Ukraine's agricultural and metallurgical exports.
Kyiv ‌has not formally banned port operations, but shipowners have avoided the ports because of security risks, while soaring insurance costs have deterred traders.
At an APEC summit in Shenzhen, China, last week, Russian President Vladimir Putin said Moscow would no longer consider a moratorium on strikes in the Black Sea or on energy infrastructure following Ukraine's ‌attacks on the bridge linking Russia and Crimea.
Putin's threat came shortly after Kyiv struck the Kerch Strait Bridge for ‌the third time in a week, damaging a route long used by Moscow to move men and materiel into Crimea and the southern front.
Frustration over Trump's 'strange' friendship
Meanwhile Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky on Friday expressed frustration with the US stance toward Putin, in the latest sign of deepening tension between Kyiv and Washington, as new questions emerged about Europe’s future financial commitments to Ukraine.
Zelensky said in an interview with the BBC broadcast Friday that he wants the US to apply more pressure on Putin to stop his invasion of Ukraine, which began almost five years ago and grinds on despite President Donald Trump’s efforts to broker a peace deal.
The Ukrainian president described Trump calling Putin “my friend” at a recent summit in China as “strange” because the Russian leader is “an aggressor and a killer”.
“He can’t be a friend,” Zelensky told the British broadcaster in Kyiv, likening the statement to someone calling Adolf Hitler a friend during World War II.

The White House did not respond to a request for comment on Zelensky’s remarks. But Trump on Thursday defended his reference to Putin. “We want to make a deal. We want to stop the killing,” he said.
“I could walk in and say, ‘I don’t like you!’ and then we have the same problem that we have with Zelensky and Putin,” he said. Speaking to reporters at Mar-a-Lago, he added: “The biggest problem with that settlement is the fact that you have two leaders that really hate each other.”
Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Zelensky was “insulting his counterpart”, showing that the Ukrainian leader “is not willing to follow the path of (a) peace settlement”.
The International Criminal Court has issued an arrest warrant for Putin for war crimes, accusing him of personal responsibility for the abductions of children from Ukraine. The Trump administration has imposed sweeping sanctions on the court as part of its campaign to dismantle the tribunal over investigations into the US and its allies, including Israel.
The Kremlin has said that Russia also does not recognise the ICC and considers its decisions “legally void”. Peskov in 2023 called the court’s move against Putin “outrageous and unacceptable”.
Kids return from Russia and Russian-occupied territories
Nine Ukrainian children were returned Friday from Russia and Russian-occupied territories of Ukraine, including an 8-year-old boy who ran out of a bus as it pulled up at Ukraine’s northern border in the Chernihiv region.
“Mom!” he shouted into the crowd. His mother, Alina Sydorenko, was there to reunite with him after nearly five years apart.
She hugged him tight as he kept saying, “Mother, mother.”
“I want to show him everything that he missed and was taken away from him,” Sydorenko said.
Some of the children had lost their parents, who died in occupied territory. Relatives met them at the bus.
Zelensky announced the latest Bring Kids Back UA operation on social media, thanking US first lady Melania Trump for her role in mediating recent wartime reunifications, which so far have brought 43 children home, the White House said Wednesday.
