Twenty-seven years have passed since The Matrix was released in cinemas, changing the entertainment landscape in perhaps more ways than its creators, Lana and Lilly Wachowski, could have envisioned. The Matrix was part of a three-movie deal that the Wachowskis landed with Warner Bros., and the rights to the franchise remained firmly in the hands of the studio, recently absorbed by Skydance in a much-discussed merger. But things may change in the future.
As revealed by Eriq Gardner in an article for Puck, the Wachowskis have formally notified Warner Bros. that they are exercising their copyright termination rights. This means that, as early as February 2029, Warner Bros. could lose the rights to produce and distribute new Matrix movies, which would go back to the creators. With a fifth movie currently in development, helmed by Drew Goddard, a legal battle could have a profound impact on the franchise.
A battle over The Matrix rights could have an impact on the fifth movie
The Wachowskis are not involved with The Matrix 5, but, according to Gardner's undisclosed sources, they "have been encouraging of the newest sequel and have even increased their involvement lately." This means that, despite Lilly's statement in September 2026 that she was "kinda done" with The Matrix, the sisters are still involved in some way, and their relationship with Warner Bros. isn't necessarily bad.
In fact, the motion to terminate the copyright doesn't mean that Warner Bros. and the Wachowskis will engage in a legal battle over The Matrix. The studio could simply negotiate a fresh grant of rights with the Wachowskis and keep making The Matrix movies. If the issue goes to court, the key will be proving which party can truly be considered the "creator" of the franchise. The commonly reported origin story of the franchise, also mentioned by Gardner in his article, is that a Warner Bros. executive, Lorenzo di Bonaventura, commissioned the Wachowskis to write and direct the movie, after being impressed by their earlier work on Assassins and Bound. Commissioned work is ineligible for copyright termination, but proving that in court will be another matter.
Overall, the most interesting takeaway from this story is that, in 2029, the Wachowskis could start working on a reboot of the Matrix franchise, freed from the shackles of corporate oversight. Meanwhile, there have been no updates about The Matrix 5 in a long time, but speculation points to a 2029 release. As Morpheus said, "I do not see coincidence, I see providence."
