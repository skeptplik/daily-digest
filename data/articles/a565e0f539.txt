The Best Planners
If you need a single planner that can do it all and won't burn through your back-to-school budget, this is the one. The Roterunner Purpose Planner is an undated planner that has monthly spreads, weekly spreads, and two full open pages for each week, so there's plenty of room to write longer lists or a project brainstorm. Each monthly and weekly spread has a ton of premade checklist sections for you to use, too. There's even space to track your meals, water intake, and gratitude. It's a planner that actually helps me stay organized and gives me space to track and take notes without running out of room.
The undated nature makes it easy to pick up anytime and use for whichever months you need. Fell off the boat for a few months? No problem. No pages will go to waste when you can fill them in as you go. It only covers six months, but I actually like that; it once again feels like a feasible commitment, especially if you're waffling on how much paper planner you want to promise to use. I love the massive B5 size ($35) since it's finally enough space for me and all my various to-do lists and brainstorms, but there's also a smaller (and cheaper!) A5 ($28) size. It's truly my go-to planner since it has so much space for me to both use the pre-made lists and the open space to actually stay organized and put all my ideas, plans, and notes into one planner. Most others make me feel like I need a companion notebook, but not the Roterunner.
The covers come in a variety of simple colors, and in both hard cover or soft, but the butter-soft faux leather soft cover is my favorite of the bunch. Get it in B5 size if you're always looking for more room in your journals and planners—you won't regret it.
If you're getting into planners, there's no way you won't hear about Hobonichi at some point. There are a few different planners the brand makes, with the Techo being the most popular model. I tested the similar Hobonichi Cousin, which adds weekly spreads to the front of the planner after the monthly overviews. While I don't typically prefer a daily planner style, I was surprised by how much I liked using the Hobonichi. All of Hobonichi's planners use Tomoe River S paper, which is super thin but won't bleed through to the other side. It's also super smooth to the touch and to write on. I liked adding my own structure to the daily pages; I sketched a box to separate my to-do lists and habit trackers from my hourly agenda for the day, and then would usually freeform the rest of the page based on what was on my mind. The Cousin I tested and photographed is an A5 size, while the standard Techo is the smaller A6.
Hobonichi's 2027 editions are here, so you can stock up on your planner for next year right now, plus tons of colors, covers, and other accessories, some of which are unique to 2027. I recommend picking out a protective cover since I did ding up my soft cover quite a bit, and that was just from being used around the house with an occasional trip in a bag. There are little quotes in the corners, but I got a Japanese version instead of an English one, so I can't actually read mine. But honestly, I'm glad I did; I prefer the Japanese lettering to English words on the corners of each page.

It makes it easy to change the design, too, since you can just switch out a single notebook when it's full.

The Pocket Plus would be fun to bring on a trip; I wish I'd had it on my latest trip to store little mementos and important things like parking receipts, and could have also written out notes and the plan for the week-long trip. I've heard a lot of good things about Erin Condren. Founded in 2005 by Erin Condren, it's known for the LifePlanner that's full of various spreads and productivity tools that you can customize, but there are other variations of planners you can find from the brand. While you'll usually customize your Erin Condren planner if you shop directly on the site, there's a predesigned Target line you can shop that won't give you options, but will give you a well-made planner that's ready to go immediately.
I've been testing the Erin Condren Academic Planner you'll find at Target, but it's sold out as we head into the new year; Erin Condren's website has similar, but more expensive options with more cover and customization options. FAQs
Why Choose a Paper Planner?
Studies show that it's good for your brain to write things down. Sure, you could put your calendar in an online tool, but putting pen to paper activates more brain connectivity. We're more likely to remember and learn things if we write them down by hand, so using a paper planner for your to-do list can make it easier to remember. Plus, it's fun! You're given a creative space to let out everything you need to remember, from appointments to the day's tasks, and customize it in a way that best helps you stay organized.
What Style Paper Planner Is Best for You?
Paper planners mainly come in two styles: weekly and daily. Daily planners have an entire page dedicated to each day, while weekly planners have an entire week spread across just two pages. These aren't the only options (monthly is another popular one, for example, but not as popular as these two styles), but they're the most common you'll see. I like daily planners for folks who have a really busy schedule they want to plan out, and long daily to-do lists. Weekly planners are great if you want to be able to write down a few things per day but don't need an entire page.
What Sizes Do Planners Come In?
Here are a couple common planner sizes. My favorite are the larger sizes since I like lots of room to doodle, but the smaller A5 is a popular choice for a book-sized planner that won't take up too much space. Not all paper planners use the same sizing names—Moleskine, for example, calls its A5 size a large—so you'll want to check measurements before you purchase.
- A5 (5.8 by 8.3 inches)
- Regular/Medium / B5 (7 by 9 inches)
- Large/Letter (8.5 by 11 inches)
- Personal/Pocket (3.5 by 5.5 inches)
