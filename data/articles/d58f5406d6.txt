Ferrari Style has brought its “exclusive and inclusive” retail experience to New York City for the first time.
On Thursday, a two-level, 6,835-square-foot Ferrari Style flagship opens at 92 Prince Street on the corner of Mercer Street, in its own freestanding building in the heart of SoHo.
While the logo inscribed on the facade of the flagship reads Ferrari, inside the store it’s all about the Ferrari Style collection and its unique merchandising. There’s an atelier for exclusive, tailor-made clothes and customizing products, Ferrari collectibles are displayed as well, and there’s ready-to-wear for men and women, including bomber jackets and tracksuits, as well as leather goods, accessories, footwear and luggage.
At this point, Ferrari Style barely scratches the surface in the U.S., with just two stores located in north Miami’s Aventura Mall and American Dream in East Rutherford, N.J., as well as an outlet in Orlando Vineland Premium Outlets. There’s also a temporary store in the Bellagio in Las Vegas, which was supposed to stay open for only weeks after its November 2025 debut. But the Ferrari team was surprised by the response and how passionate Vegas tourists and locals were about racing, so the weeks turned into months, and the site continues to operate indefinitely.
A fifth Ferrari Style store in the U.S. is expected to open in the Miami Design District in 2028. But for now, New York City is the focus.
“We feel SoHo is at the right intersection of culture, design, art and architecture,” Carla Liuni, chief brand officer of Ferrari Style, told WWD. “It’s a true, tangible way for us to express what the Ferrari lifestyle is all about and to get in touch with the local community, to enter their everyday lives, and give space to individual expression. When we select a location, we always look at three things — cultural authority, luxury maturity and relevance for our clients.”
By establishing itself in SoHo, Ferrari, Liuni said, can engage in a direct dialogue with the United States’ “rapidly evolving society of aesthetic tastemakers, social innovators and technological pioneers.”
Runway Ahead
A Ferrari Style spring 2027 runway show will be staged at the Jack Shainman Gallery in Manhattan’s TriBeCa neighborhood on Thursday, in conjunction with the SoHo flagship opening that day.
“Racing is obviously our DNA. It’s the most inclusive side of the brand, considering Ferrari has over 400 million fans,” Liuni said. “The most exclusive side is our sports cars, which basically fulfill the dreams of the very few, which we call ‘Ferraristi,’ fundamentally Ferrari car owners and collectors. And then you have the Ferrari Style, the third component of the brand,” which the SoHo flagship captures.
Most people around the world are more familiar with Ferrari’s performance sports cars and Formula 1 racing team. But as Liuni pointed out, “Ferrari is much more than cars. It’s built over time a strong legacy, which, after all, is about culture. So the role of each of these components — racing, sports cars and lifestyle — are complementary and basically fulfill and nourish the power of the brand. The brand is our most precious asset.”
Ferrari’s physical retail presence in the U.S. began in New York in 1954, when Luigi Chinetti — driver of the first Ferrari ever to win Le Mans and a close friend of Enzo Ferrari, the late legendary race car driver and founder of the Ferrari business — opened its first North American showroom for cars on West 55th Street in Manhattan. There’s still a showroom for its cars in Manhattan, at 425 Park Avenue.
Last March, Ferrari Style launched a flagship on Bond Street in London, unveiling the brand’s new retail concept. It’s located in a 1905 Queen Anne-style building, mixing the architecture of an historic building with contemporary materials, underscoring the brand’s legacy for creative thinking and an innovative design language, which has also been applied to the SoHo store. Local touches bring relevance to both sites.
In Italy, there are Ferrari Style stores in Milan, Rome, and in the small town of Maranello, where Ferrari is based and has its factory. From time to time, Ferrari Style stages pop-up events, which have been held in such places as Le Mans, France; Pebble Beach, Calif., and Singapore.
Ferrari’s brick-and-mortar retail development has been gradual and careful. “This is not opportunistic growth. It’s just selective expansion,” Liuni said. “We are building this business and our retail strategy with a unique mindset because the relationship with our client is unique, and we have the responsibility to keep it that way.”
“This SoHo opening is a very big opportunity for us because here we can host both sides of our brand — it’s inclusivity and exclusivity,” said Rocco Iannone, creative director of Ferrari Style. “The luxury market is moving very fast and becoming multidimensional today, especially offering experiences as well as products. In a world where many players are losing their identity, this is the moment where we need to push our identity and expand this idea of experiences, offering a full vision of what a Ferrari lifestyle means.”
The Ferrari store is rooted in the credo of the Ferrari Officina, the workshop at the Maranello headquarters, and is conceived as a place where collections can be contextualized within the wider design language of Ferrari.
	To create the store, Iannone worked with Berlin-based architecture studio Gonzalez Haase AAS, and Milan-based design practice Formafantasma. Under the direction of Iannone, Gonzalez Haase AAS developed the architectural framework and spatial identity of the store, while Formafantasma shaped its display language and the presentation of products and objects throughout the space. A sleek Nespresso coffee bar previously occupied the flagship site.
“The Officina is the place where intuition is given form through discipline, and where the human touch works in harmony with mechanical precision,” said Iannone. “For New York, we wanted to translate this spirit into a space in which architecture, fashion and object design each retain their own identity while contributing to a single Ferrari language. Every material and detail has been chosen to express this balance between art and science, creating an environment that is open, evolving, and in direct dialogue with the energy of SoHo.”
He said that standing just outside the SoHo flagship, at the intersection of Mercer and Prince streets, one perceives a merging of the aesthetics of SoHo with those of Ferrari, creating a unique architectural language.
The facade is constructed in metal and large glass panes, enabling natural light to flow in. The steel, aged-effect metal and highly polished surfaces create a contrast between rawness and precision, while the transparency of the facade lends lightness to the flagship’s compact, block-like form.
“Our Offina workshop in Maranello is where ideas, human touch, and machines come together, creating something very special. For me, this was important to be injected into the store,” said Iannone. “So when you enter the store, you immediately find a very modern space, very different than traditional retail spaces. And you can immediately find the intentional clarity and the attention to the design, and a harmony of contrasts that are very sophisticated and elegant. You will find a vibration of the materials — glass, leather, aluminum, clay. They are together in a very harmonic way. There’s this warm yet contemporary idea of space. It’s a beautiful canvas that express the souls of our brand. There are some moments where we will celebrate the racing soul, and there are certain moments where we want to express luxury at its maximum through our atelier, where we do tailor-made, but it’s also where our clients can customize pieces.”
	The fashion categories are presented alongside Ferrari Collectibles, which include rare mechanical auto components, repurposed engines, bonnets (the front cover of a car protecting the engine) and hand-assembled scale model cars. A Formula 1 engine created by race car driver Michael Schumacher was once bought.
Throughout the space, display structures developed by Formafantasma draw on the functional apparatus of the workshop in Maranello. Metal and glass are shaped into frameworks, trays and vitrines that house the merchandise and showcase the character of each item.
A sculpted staircase connects the ground floor to the more intimate level above. The women’s ready-to-wear, footwear and accessories collections are presented in a sequence of spaces that gradually lead toward the Ferrari Style Atelier. Only Ferrari’s London and New York flagships house these ateliers, which are dedicated to made-to-measure, made-to-order, and one-of-a-kind, tailor-made fashion styles. Enclosed by floor-to-ceiling leather curtains, the SoHo atelier offers clients a private setting to personalize pieces from across the collection.
The Ferrari Style fashion collection has what Liuni referred to as a “pyramid” offering, with the atelier tailor-made exclusive pieces at the top of the pyramid, catering to the most important clients.
“Here you have the Maranello Clutch, which mimics the shape of our Daytona Ferrari car,” Iannone said. The clutch can be customized in different colors, materials, finishes and hardware, whatever the customer prefers. “There’s this beautiful round table where you can find all the different steps involved in personalizing the Maranello Clutch. You can choose every single detail,” Liuni added.
The SoHo opening will also be marked by a Ferrari Style capsule collection conceived just for the flagship and New York. “We like the idea of a store that must allow this multidimensional approach to discovering the Ferrari world,” Iannone said.
“What was important for us is the intentionality of design. When you enter the store, you feel this is unmistakably Ferrari, but not in the most literal way. Ferrari is about contrast. Ferrari is about volumes. Ferrari is about excellence of material. So you will see beautiful leather mixed with brushed aluminum cases and at the same time you feel you are entering a very cozy welcoming place. Every single detail expresses what the Ferrari brand expresses.”
“When we talk about design, Ferrari is about intentional clarity,” Iannone explained. “When you see a Ferrari car, you can immediately recognize a very clear shape made by very distinctive elements. What are those distinctive elements? The voluptuous volumes, the sensual lines, the vibrant colors, the material contrasts, the softness of the interiors, the handmade details. Ferrari is all about beautiful cars and beautiful sensual lines, and is very strongly inspired by the human body.”
But there is coherence in design across the Ferrari spectrum of products, be it fashion or a car. “Coherence is important for us,” said Liuni. “Coherence allows you to recognize where you’re coming from. Coherence, however, doesn’t mean uniformity. Coherence doesn’t mean be literal about it. Coherence is a different thing. It requires much more work in terms of research, and in terms of understanding the brand.”
