The Kremlin on Wednesday dismissed a series of new investigative reports using leaked Rosstat data on Russian troop losses in the Ukraine war, as it repeated calls to trust only official information provided by Russia’s Defense Ministry.
Earlier this month, two hacking groups designated as “extremist” organizations in Russia published data they said was stolen from the state statistics agency Rosstat.
Initial media analyses of that data placed Russia’s military death toll at more than 450,000, while the exiled news outlets Meduza and Mediazona more recently estimated the losses could exceed 700,000 when accounting for unrecorded deaths and missing troops.
“The only entity that can officially disclose casualty figures is the Russian Defense Ministry,” Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters in his first public comments on the Rosstat leaks. “I suggest not paying attention to any other data.”
Russia has not released an official military death toll since September 2022, when it acknowledged that nearly 6,000 soldiers had been killed during the first six months of the full-scale invasion.
The Rosstat leak supports earlier research showing that Russia’s ethnic republics have suffered disproportionately from mobilization and recruitment into the armed forces, with major cities like Moscow registering a proportionally smaller share of overall casualties.
Russia’s Defense Ministry has offered huge financial incentives to lure low-paid workers from poor regions to fight in the Ukraine war.
It has also recruited men from prisons by offering pardons in exchange for military service, drafted more than 300,000 reservists and enlisted thousands from abroad, drawing accusations of having coerced foreign citizens into fighting.
North Korea has also sent tens of thousands of its soldiers to support Russia’s army, according to Western intelligence officials.
Significant numbers of Ukrainian soldiers have also been killed in what has become Europe’s deadliest conflict since World War II. Kyiv has also been reluctant to reveal the scale of its troop losses.
