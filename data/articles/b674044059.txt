In short:
Dawn Fraser says the 70th anniversary of the Melbourne Olympics has motivated her to make it to Brisbane for the 2032 Olympics.
The Melbourne Games began with the opening ceremony at the MCG on November 22, 1956.
Fraser and others will gather at the MCG today for a lunch to celebrate the milestone.
Amid the reunions and celebrations, Dawn Fraser is drawing some inspiration from the 70th anniversary of the Melbourne Olympics.
Australia's most famous Olympian has come to Melbourne for a lunch on Sunday at the MCG to mark the November 22 opening ceremony date of the 1956 Games.
Fraser is 89 and had to miss a commemorative 1956 Olympics dinner in Melbourne a few weeks ago because of a health issue.
Fraser says she will go from table to table in her wheelchair to meet old friends and make new ones at the lunch, which coincides with the opening ceremony at the MCG 70 years ago.
Asked if she might be there for the 2032 Brisbane Olympics — Fraser was also a key figure at the Sydney 2000 Games — there is a reminder of what made her an all-time sporting great.
"I would love to be there at '32, to support our athletes," Fraser told AAP.
"I think of my age — I just turned 89 — and, 'Oh well, that's so many years off.' But then when I look at some of the athletes who are coming to this testimonial, some of them are 95, 99.
"Then I'm thinking — excuse the expression — 'Shit! I can do that. If they can do it, I can do it.'"
Likewise, the 19-year-old from a hardscrabble Balmain upbringing had her eyes opened wide at the 1956 Olympics.
Until then, Fraser had only swum against other Australians.
"I never thought about it. [To me, the Olympics] was just another swimming event. But when I won my gold medal, I realised how big it was," Fraser said.
"It wasn't until after I won … competing against so many nations.
"To win a gold medal was something out of this world. You didn't think about it until a month later, that you were the best swimmer in the world."
Fraser won the 100-metre freestyle in Melbourne and was a member of the 4x100 women's freestyle relay team that also claimed gold.
She went on to become the first of only four swimmers who have won gold in the same individual event at three-successive Olympics. Somewhere in there, she became "Our Dawn".
Two days after the reunion, the Sport Australia Hall Of Fame annual dinner will feature the Dawn Award. It goes to an individual, team or organisation that shows courage, breaks new ground and changes sport for the better.
But as far as Fraser is concerned, she is just one athlete at the reunion.
"I'm an athlete among athletes, and I'm very proud to be among the athletes who I met and competed against in 1956," she said.
Just as the Melbourne Games dramatically changed Fraser's life, it also was a landmark for Australian sport. Only nine years after World War Two, Australia was still an international backwater.
"Here we were, a small country, having an Olympic event that meant so much to a lot of sportsmen and women in our country … and throughout the world," Fraser said.
"We were holding the biggest sporting event ever in Olympic history.
"It was the start of not only my swimming career but for a lot of other sportsmen and women in Australia — the start of their international careers.
"It was a big stepping stone for all of us because we didn't realise we were competing against the best athletes in the world."
And so to 2032, where Fraser hopes Australia can raise the bar again.
"I want them to make it as successful as all the other Olympic Games — if not a little bit better. It's very hard to do that, but Sydney, I thought the same thing," she said.
"Sydney outshone Melbourne and I'm sure Brisbane will outshine Sydney."
