Hey, maybe don’t just immediately go to the strip club. Look, I know that’s what you want to do right away when you start Grand Theft Auto VI. This is a GTA game after all—that sort of outlet for titillation is a common theme in this franchise. But going straight to the good stuff isn’t the best way to play this game.
The biggest video game ever has turned out to be one of the very best. With near-universally perfect review scores, GTA VI has emerged as an absolute behemoth that has lived up to 13 years of hype.
I’ve spent a few hours in the game, and one thing becomes clear right away: This game is a slow burn. The GTA franchise has always been high-octane, arcade-style, prioritizing hyperviolence and exaggerated physics that let your character go wild in the digital sandbox. But the latest installation is much more reserved. Vehicles and player movements feel heavier. The characters have deeper, more emotional backstories and motivations. The world has more interactivity and reacts to your actions in much more varied ways.
It evokes a play style that feels very much in common with Rockstar’s previous title, Red Dead Redemption 2. It was a Western that made the player feel the friction of an often slow, plodding life that didn’t shy away from simulating all the chores of a horse-bound cowboy in the 1800s. GTA VI, set in the modern era, is faster-paced but still carries that intentionality to slow the player down.
That manifests in ways that might feel like busywork. Instead of having access to all your weapons and outfits, you have to make space for the things you want to bring with you and store everything else in the back of your car. You have to call someone to fix your car and wait for them to arrive. Yeah, you have to paint your house and buy your girlfriend flowers. Get used to it. This can sometimes feel like a chore, but it slows the player down to give them time to examine the world around them.
Once you start looking around and really take everything in, the level of detail is truly unique. Leonida—GTA VI’s mirror version of Miami, Florida—is so varied and splendid it is almost overwhelming. After the game’s on-the-rails introductory missions open up into the first opportunity for an open-world sojourn, the sheer scale of this world becomes apparent. Zooming out on the map and seeing the vast expanse of unexplored territory is dizzying.
There is just so much Leonida, and none of it feels sparse or unfinished. Every place, person, animal, and environmental asset feels fully fleshed out. Driving down the road, with other cars zooming past, is photorealistic enough to look nearly like real dash-cam footage. This game, as I have muttered to myself over and over again while playing, “is simply insane.”
If you’ve followed the GTA VI saga, this likely won’t surprise you. Developer Rockstar and its parent company Take-Two have spent around a billion dollars to develop the game. It’s taken 13 years. The company even delayed the game multiple times to make sure it was polished.
There is massive hype, and at this point I’d say what Rockstar has achieved here has surpassed that hype. The game is likely to be the pinnacle of this era of video games, the final grand blockbuster achieved without the use of generative AI.
I’ve been playing GTA games for a long time. I’ll probably be playing this one for the rest of my life. Like me, you’re probably very excited to dive into this gigantic world. The best way to do that is not to try to see it all at once. Pace yourself. Look out for the small moments, as they may lead to something more profound than you’re expecting. Slow down, and you can really feel how the world reacts.
Nothing else like this game exists. And there probably won’t be for a very long time. Might as well make it last.
