Eurogamer can confirm that at least 28 of the 34 employees fired by Rockstar roughly a year ago - in an action that directly led to the employment tribunal Rockstar is currently facing in Scotland - are named in the credits of Grand Theft Auto 6.
We cross-checked a list of 34 names supplied to us by the Independent Workers Union of Great Britain - the same organisation taking action against Rockstar on behalf of the fired employees. And of those names, we found 28 direct matches in the credits, along with a couple of discrepancies we've since verified, and some close-sounding names we're attempting to clarify. Some names may simply be missing. We won't publish the list for privacy reasons.
A number of those fired employees are seeking compensation for unfair dismissal amid allegations of "union busting", in an employment tribunal that comes to an end today. The delayed climax of the case couldn't be more fateful, aligned as it is with tomorrow's release of GTA 6, and the outcome will be historic either way.
Rockstar recently revealed that more than 4,711 people worked at the company making GTA 6, employed across 12 offices around the world - a staff-count that has more than doubled since Red Dead Redemption 2. It's a game that's wowed critics - Eurogamer awarded GTA 6 five stars in our review this afternoon - and which observers expect to generate $2bn in revenue in its opening weekend alone.
But perhaps Christian Donlan put it best when digesting the opulence on display in the Netflix 'extended look' GTA 6 trailer. "To spend all this money making something so beautifully rich and detailed, to pay billions for this game and sweat every little animation, to float it out to us on Netflix, amongst genuine discussion around whether Netflix can handle it? That's commitment," he wrote. "And if you're willing to do that, and willing to do that to tell an underdog story, you should let your workers who made everything form a union."
