Players who abused World of Warcraft: Forever's Blackmaw Hold unintended gold farm, which allowed a small minority of players to hoard thousands of gold in a few days, have drawn the ire of WoW publisher, Blizzard. Suspensions have been issued, trade locks have been put on accounts, and ill-gotten gold is being seized.
For those unaware, the World of Warcraft: Forever community was greatly alarmed by this gold farm, which had players clear the same non-elite enemies from the new late game dungeon, Blackmaw Hold. By quickly clearing select mobs with area-of-effect damage, players would loot upwards of 100 raw gold an hour. This exploit, clearly not intentional, was nonetheless farmed until Blizzard hotfixed it earlier in the week.
There was much speculation on what would happen to those who participated in this exploit. That speculation would come to an end late last night with an official statement from the World of Warcraft: Forever development team.
"As players continue their journey into WoW Forever’s higher-level content, we are closely monitoring the game economy to ensure that gold sources and costs are well balanced," the statement reads. "Finding efficient farming spots has always been part of the game, and is how most players have earned their epic mounts since 2004. When a single location proves so lucrative that it overshadows all other sources, we generally adjust it to bring it in line with the rest."
It goes on to explain the nature of the bug that made this gold farm possible, before reassuring those who may have accidentally benefited from some extra cash from their regular runs of Blackmaw Hold they have nothing to worry about. The same cannot be said for those who took advantage of the situation.
"Players who simply ran Blackmaw Hold a few times and benefited organically from the extra loot did nothing wrong, and no one should worry that they’re putting their account at risk if they accidentally benefit from a bug," it continues. "But there were a couple hundred players worldwide who recognized that this was unintended and spent significant time exclusively farming that group of enemies.
"Yesterday, we put an economy lock on their accounts to prevent broader impact to the game economy while we investigated the extent of the exploitative gains, including gold earned by vendoring bugged drops. Today, we are in the process of removing all such gold earned, as well as applying account suspensions to significant abusers."
The post concludes by thanking players for their feedback on the matter, and a final warning: "As always, we advise all players to refrain from abusing game mechanics that are clearly unintended. We will continue to take action against intentional abuse."
This decision certainly sets the precedent moving forward with World of Warcraft: Forever that such exploits won't be tolerated, and rolling back the wealth of those who made out of Blackmaw Hold like bandits is a good measure that should prevent continuous damage.
Inevitably the exploit would have had some impact, specifically in those days prior to trading blocks being put in place. While the gold on the exploiter's accounts may be removed, any purchases they made on the in-game auction house will have affected others through buying and selling goods with ample funds.
Those who were suspended temporarily will be able to regain access to the game by the time the first wave of raids come out in December, so it very much feels like a firm slap on the wrist in that regard. Even so, given the establishment of punishments for such behavior, one can only wonder if future events will come with harsher penalties.
World of Warcraft: Forever, despite this small economic roadbump, is proving exceptionally popular for Blizzard. It's already broken records for the series, and has impressed players so far with its revamping of the classic MMO.
