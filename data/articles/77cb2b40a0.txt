Starbucks Korea said it would return to profit in the fourth quarter, as the coffee chain shows signs of recovering from a public-relations crisis over the controversial “Tank Day” promotion that contributed to its first-ever losses earlier this year.
Rebuilding customer trust and preventing a repeat of the debacle have been top priorities, new Korea Chief Executive Offer Shin Dong Woo told reporters on Thursday at the opening of a sprawling new store at the landmark Namsan Seoul Tower. Since taking over, Shin has revamped internal reporting, created a team to vet marketing activities and continued employee training on social sensitivities.
“It would be presumptuous of me to declare that customer trust has been fully restored,” he said. “However, revenue has rebounded enough to absorb our cost structure, putting us on track for a bottom-line turnaround.”
Shin took the helm in June at SCK Company, which operates Starbucks in Korea, after his predecessor was sacked over the May “Tank Day” event that sparked a nationwide boycott, criticism from President Lee Jae Myung and a police raid on Starbucks Korea’s Seoul headquarters.
The campaign, designed to offer discounts on the company’s large Tank series tumblers, fueled public outrage as it coincided with the anniversary of a 1980 pro-democracy uprising that saw South Korea’s then-military junta deploy troops and tanks to crush protests, killing hundreds.
Shin previously held senior roles at Shinsegae Property, the real estate arm of retail conglomerate Shinsegae Group, which controls Starbucks Korea through its supermarket unit E-Mart.
South Korea is Starbucks’ largest market outside the U.S. and China, with more than 2,200 stores. E-Mart owns a 67.5% stake in Starbucks Korea, with the remainder held by Singapore’s sovereign wealth fund GIC.
In May, Shinsegae Group Chairman Chung Yong-jin bowed repeatedly during a televised apology and took responsibility for the blunder, while U.S.-based Starbucks Corp. issued a statement calling the promotion “unacceptable.” Starbucks Korea also took the unprecedented step of closing all stores early in June for employee training on modern Korean history and social sensitivity.
The company posted its first quarterly loss in August since beginning operations in 1999 as boycotts mounted. E-Mart this month reported an earnings shock as Starbucks Korea’s third-quarter loss widened further.
