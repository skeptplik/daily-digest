Space Verdict
The Lego Star Trek: U.S.S. Enterprise Bridge diorama is a wonderful bit of nostalgia and a stunning recreation of a sci-fi icon. The playset features arguably feel a little out of place, given that the audience for this set will be older fans who want to board the Starship Nostalgia. But maybe that's the whole point: Making old Trek more accessible to young 'uns.
Reasons to buy
- + Beautiful diorama recreating the iconic sci-fi set.
- + Full main cast in minifigure form.
- + A big bundle of nostalgia in a box.
Reasons to avoid
- - Not as many Easter eggs as I'd like
- - Playset features feel out of place
- - Lots of stickers

It's not quite a five-year mission, but as Star Trek celebrated its 60th anniversary in September of this year (we may have mentioned it once or twice), I decided it was high time I finally watched the original Star Trek.
Before this year, I hadn't seen a single episode of "Star Trek: The Original Series", as it's now known. I'm a millennial; I grew up on "The Next Generation", "Deep Space Nine" and "Voyager". By comparison, the old series (TOS) always looked so… old and naff whenever I saw clips of it.
But now I have seen the original series. Over the course of 2026, I've ploughed through all 79 episodes, spread across three seasons. And you know what? It's pretty damn good. Even with the shiny 2006 remasters, you'll have to prepare yourself for some bad special effects work, but the scripts are interesting, the sci-fi concepts are out there, and the acting — especially from Shatner and Nimoy — is top-notch.
And just as I finally discovered a love for this classic sci-fi series, what should drop into my lap? Why it's the new Star Trek: U.S.S. Enterprise NCC-1701 Bridge diorama set from Lego. Now I have more on my wishlist to add to the Best lego space sets.
Boldly building where no one has built before
Let's start with the Band-Aid rip: this set is $200. I know; it stings, especially when Star Trek promised us a future where money doesn't exist. But at 1701 pieces (nice, I see what you did there), it's much better value from a bricks-per-dollar perspective than the X-Files set we checked out recently.
Still, that's no small investment for a toy, even if it will make a lovely display piece when it's built.
As the name suggests, you get a full Lego-ified recreation of the bridge of the USS Enterprise (NCC-1701, no A, B, C, or D). This includes Captain Kirk's chair, the helm positions ahead of him (manned by Chekov and Sulu), and all of the surrounding computer displays where you usually find Spock, Uhura and Scotty.
There's even a viewscreen that can sit in front of the crew, displaying an adorable Klingon commander facing off against Kirk.
Board the legendary starship and meet the crew of the U.S.S. Enterprise NCC-1701 for an amazing building experience. Recreate the iconic bridge and transporter room that served as the backdrop for epic scenes from "Star Trek: The Original Series".
Alongside the main bridge, there are a few fun extras bolted on, including a rotating transporter pad (which can make minifigures "disappear") and a working turbolift door connected to the back of the main bridge diorama. You'll have to make the swoosh sound effect yourself, though.
It's a wonderfully faithful recreation of Kirk's command center, complete with computer panels and screens, swiveling chairs and that weird scope thing that Spock was always looking into.
That said, it's a little lacking in proper Easter eggs. There are some tribbles hiding around the build, and the monitors have custom stickers showing cool Trekkie things. The weapon display racks on the back hold an array of pointy Klingon blades, Starfleet phasers, Sulu's fencing rapier and even Spock's harp, but compared to, say, the X-Files set we built recently, it feels a bit lacking in those deep-cut references.
Helping Star Trek live long and prosper
All in all, it's a fantastic little diorama, but when it was announced, I'll admit I was a little puzzled as to who it was aimed at. "Star Trek fans, duh!" is the obvious response, but the Venn diagram crossover between "people who are nostalgic for the original series of Star Trek" and "people who want to buy $200 Lego sets" has to be pretty small, right?
Star Trek first aired way back in 1966, which means the first generation of fans are now 70 years old and upwards. Lord knows I hope I'm still building Lego sets when I'm 70, but is that a large enough crowd to make this set a success? Then again the show was repeated and syndicated throughout the 70s and 80s, which means hopefully there'll be some younger buyers, too.
The playset aspects of the set are even more puzzling. The working door, exploding monitor and spinning transporter pad are great additions to a kids' playset, but this isn't that — it's a display for nostalgic Star Trek fans. The design of the set just feels a bit confused.
But then it hit me. Maybe this isn't just for fans of the original series; maybe it's meant to create some new fans. I started watching the original Star Trek 60 years after its debut because… well, I'm the sci-fi editor for a space website, and it felt wrong that I hadn't seen it. But most people don't have that sort of professional impetus. The average person won't jump back to watch a 60-year-old TV show without a polite nudge, and this set does a great job of highlighting the charm and optimism of Star Trek's debut outing.
I don't know, maybe I'm just projecting here. Maybe Lego bought the rights to make Star Trek sets and needed something to do after they'd mined all the nostalgia they could from the excellent Enterprise D set they released in 2025. Maybe this is just a toy for big kids.
Or maybe… just maybe, it's meant to inspire the next generation of Star Trek fans to check out where the magic started.
