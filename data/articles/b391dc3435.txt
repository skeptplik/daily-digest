With Putin in the room, Carney condemns Russia's illegal invasion of Ukraine
Speaking at APEC summit, Carney tells Putin what he thinks of Ukraine war
Prime Minister Mark Carney condemned Russia's war with Ukraine while Russian President Vladimir Putin, the man prosecuting that war, was in the room for leaders' speeches at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in China.
Carney's speech principally focused on the government's efforts to boost the economy and make the country a more favourable place to invest for Asian partners.
However, toward the end of those remarks to the plenary session on Thursday, he veered into the yearslong conflict that has threatened Ukraine's territorial integrity, killed tens of thousands of soldiers and civilians and shaken the global economy.
"Sustained prosperity also depends on a stable international environment. Geopolitical crises, including Russia's illegal invasion of Ukraine, continue to have material economic consequences across the Asia-Pacific and beyond," Carney said, according to a copy of his prepared remarks shared by the Prime Minister's Office.
Wars like Putin's "underscore the importance of reliable partners who respect international law and territorial integrity while building resilience among like-minded countries," he said.
This is believed to be the first time these two leaders have ever been together in the same space since Carney became prime minister.
Before making those comments, Carney was more pointed in a news conference when asked about Putin's claim that sanctions on his country are ineffective and "a thing of the past."
"Mr. Putin can ask his financial institutions, he can ask his oligarch friends, he can ask his hockey players whether or not sanctions are a thing of the past. I think he'll find out that they're very real, they're biting and they're absolutely necessary because of his illegal and unjustified war. Canada is speaking with action," Carney said.
Carney and United States President Donald Trump leave the family photo at the summit, which also included Russian President Vladimir Putin. (Adrian Wyld/The Canadian Press)
When asked if he was willing to be blunt with Putin face-to-face, Carney praised former prime minister Stephen Harper's 2014 decision to personally chastise Putin for invading Crimea.
At the time, Harper said to Putin: "I guess I'll shake your hand, but I only have one thing to say to you: you need to get out of Ukraine."
According to an account of that moment in Harper's recently published memoir, Putin told him in response, "I'm not in Ukraine."
"Well, that's why it's a waste of my time to talk to you," Harper said he told the Russian leader.
Carney said he was willing to say something similar.
"If I have the occasion to be next to him, I will make that clear. But it is already clear, and not for the first time, Vladimir Putin is lying about the situation on the ground. Sanctions are very real and they're going to continue to increase as long as this war goes on," he said.
After his speech taking issue with Putin's war, Carney did pose for the usual leaders' family photo as the summit wrapped.
Putin was one row down and two leaders over from Carney, but it wasn't apparent if the two ever spoke.
The Russian Embassy in Ottawa did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
World leaders including Carney, Putin, Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping are seen posing for the family photo on Thursday. (Greg Baker/Getty Images)
Since Russia's 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine, Putin has largely avoided international summits where Kyiv's Western allies are present. He hasn't been to APEC, for example, in years.
But he was in Shenzhen, China, this year for a rare trilateral meeting with U.S. President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping where the war with Ukraine was a major focus.
Putin also had a one-on-one meeting with Trump, who greeted the Russian leader at the start of their bilateral discussion by saying: "He's my friend," a comment that has been roundly condemned as inappropriate given Putin's alleged war crimes.
Trump shakes hands with Putin as Xi looks on before the APEC summit welcome banquet. (Iori Sagisawa/AFP/Getty Images)
Trump hailed the visit as "Highly Consequential" on social media as he returned to Washington. But it wasn't clear what, if anything, had been accomplished beyond a vague commitment to avoid nuclear war.
Xi said the meeting focused on "promoting world peace."
Putin, for his part, said the countries "hold joint responsibility for maintaining international peace."
Leaders from the Coalition of the Willing, comprising some 34 Western nations supporting Ukraine in this war, met later Thursday to discuss the Putin-Trump-Xi meeting.
Carney was in the air on his way back to Canada but his defence minister, David McGuinty, attended in his place.
According to a readout from McGuinty's office, the minister "reaffirmed Canada's unwavering support for international efforts and emphasized that any peace agreement must preserve Ukraine's sovereignty, independence and territorial integrity, while strengthening rather than weakening Euro-Atlantic security."
Canada's position is that Ukraine "must be fully involved in decisions regarding its own future," the readout said.
