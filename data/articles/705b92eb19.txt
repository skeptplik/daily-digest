Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and Chinese President Xi Jinping held talks on Friday, following which Xi hailed a "comprehensive turnaround" in the ties between two nations and Albanese said his country was engaging "constructively" with Beijing.
The leaders met on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Forum (APEC) summit in China's Shenzhen.
What did Xi say to Albanese?
Xi pointed out to Albanese that they had met four times since the latter became Australia's Prime Minister in 2022.
"I think it is fair to say that together we have brought about a comprehensive turnaround in China-Australia relations," the Chinese President said.
Xi also called for enhancing cooperation in agriculture, energy, the green economy and artificial intelligence, Chinese state news agency Xinhua reported.
Why is China important for Australia?
China is Australia's top trading partner.
The relations between the two countries hit rock bottom under Canberra's former centre-right coalition government, which had called for an independent inquiry into the origins of the coronavirus.
An angry China retaliated by imposing import curbs worth $13 billion (€12.2 billion) on Australian timber, coal, barley, lobsters and wine.
Since Albanese's centre-left Labor party came to power, China has lifted most of its blocks on Australian exports.
But friction in ties has surfaced occasionally on issues such as China conducting a missile test in the South Pacific and Australia tightening a foreign investment screening in the critical minerals sector.
"It is important that Australia continue to engage in a constructive way with China, manage our differences, but not be defined by them," Albanese told the media on Friday.
"What happens in China does matter for Australia," he added.
Iron ore, a pressure point
Albanese is expected to travel to Beijing and meet China's Premier Li Qiang later on Friday.
On Monday, Australia, which is the world's biggest producer of iron ore, called out China's "manipulation" of trade in the commodity over Beijing's formation of a central buying body to purchase ore on behalf of its steelmakers.
A single buyer has increased bargaining power in negotiations with sellers.
Albanese and Li will attend a business roundtable comprising of senior executives from Australian mining giants Rio Tinto and BHP and Chinese company executives.
