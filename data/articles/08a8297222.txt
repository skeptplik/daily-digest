China to launch first space mission with a foreign astronaut
Pakistan's Muhammad Zeeshan Ali will become the first foreign astronaut to board China's Tiangong space station when the latest crew blasts off Wednesday. Ali said he would be carrying "the hopes of 250 million Pakistanis" with him.
The first foreign astronaut on a Chinese space mission said on Tuesday that he will be carrying "the hopes of 250 million Pakistanis" when the latest crew blasts off on Wednesday.
Beijing has poured billions into the Chinese space programme, whose prized Tiangong space station is stewarded by teams of three astronauts that are typically rotated every six months.
The Shenzhou-24 mission is set to lift off at 8:18am (0018 GMT) on Wednesday from the Jiuquan launch centre in China's northwestern Gobi Desert, China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) spokesman Zhang Jingbo told a news conference on Tuesday.
Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a Pakistani payload specialist, will become the first foreign astronaut to board China's Tiangong space station.
"I carry the name of Pakistan on my shoulder, the hopes of 250 million Pakistanis," Ali told a separate news conference held later to introduce the crew.
"To every child who is watching while I'm in orbit, just look up at the sky and remember that I am up there with your dreams," he said in English, following other remarks in fluent Mandarin and Urdu.
Ali will join a short orbital flight expected to last around five days before returning with the Shenzhou-23 spaceship, which docked at the Tiangong in May.
Pakistani officials welcomed Ali's selection as a significant step in relations between Islamabad and Beijing.
Information Minister Attaullah Tarar described the mission as a "source of joy and pride for the entire nation".

Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal said the mission underscored the trust and "iron brotherhood" between the two countries.
Ali is not the first Pakistani in space, with Monaco-based polar explorer Namira Salim having flown on Virgin Galactic's Galactic 04 mission in October 2023.
Beijing has invested heavily in its space programme in recent years, seeking to catch up with its principal rival, the United States.
Pakistan, which maintains close diplomatic and trade ties with Beijing, is a natural partner in the endeavour.
The crew currently aboard the Tiangong space station is headed by 40-year-old Zhu Yangzhu, Lai Ka-ying and Zhang Zhiyuan, also 40.
Lai is Hong Kong's first astronaut and the only woman currently aboard the space station.

After the new, all-male crew reaches the Tiangong, Pakistan's Ali will return to Earth alongside Lai and Zhang.
Zhu will remain on board to complete China's first full-year stay in space – a key test of endurance critical for future lunar and potentially deep-space ambitions.
Zhu will be accompanied for the next six-month Tiangong rotation by 51-year-old space veteran Tang Hongbo and Wu Yi, in his late thirties and making his first voyage to space.
Tang told reporters that learning to work together was more challenging in this mission, pointing to differences in language, culture and even psychology. But efforts from both sides have been paying off, Tang said.
“The labels and operating instructions aboard the spacecraft and space station are all in Chinese, and the Pakistani astronaut began training with virtually no foundation of the language," he said, adding that he and Wu also stepped up their English learning.
The Long March-2F rocket that will carry the crew into space will soon be filled with propellant, the CMSA's Zhang said.
The crew is in good condition and the space station is functioning normally, meeting all requirements for the mission, he said.
