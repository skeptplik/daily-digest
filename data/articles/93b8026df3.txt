The king is officially 78! Charles III, who was born in 1948—just four years before his mother, Queen Elizabeth II, took the throne—celebrated another trip around the sun today. And while the official birthday celebration of the monarch falls in June with the annual Trooping the Colour military parade, the royal family marked his real date of birth with a new portrait of him and Sir. Elton John.
The photo was taken during the taping of a special edition of John’s Apple Music 1 radio show, Rocket Hour, in which the “Tiny Dancer” musician was joined by Charles to mark 50 years of the King’s Trust charity. And in addition to discussing the nonprofit, the two also chatted about modern music, introduced over a dozen hand-selected tracks, and joked about aging.
“We and the Trust are definitely still standing,” the king teased, highlighting the Grammy-winning singer’s “I’m Still Standing” as one of his favorite songs. The musician, from his seat at the table, replied, “I’m still sitting now, though.”
At one point, the monarch gave a particular shoutout to Raye, who he said he “had the pleasure of being introduced to” during a “very memorable performance” at Apple.
A key focus of the Rocket Hour podcast has been to champion emerging artists—a mission that resonates with the King’s Trust’s focus on supporting the next generation. Formerly known as the Prince’s Trust, the organization was founded in 1976 to help young people facing unemployment, homelessness, academic troubles, and more. John has celebrated the charity’s milestones before, even performing at Wembley Arena for the 10th anniversary (1986). In 2001, he joined the royal family for a 25th anniversary dinner at St. James’s Palace in London.
“We are so richly blessed with the talents of our musicians, singers, and performers, particularly yourself, if I may say so,” the king continued, turning to his host. “You’ve become such a national treasure.”
