Thailand is asking China to help shut mines in northeastern Myanmar for at least six months as it tries to stop arsenic pollution from flowing across the border into rivers used by communities and farmers.
The request came during talks Tuesday involving Thailand, China and Myanmar, according to Thai officials. Bangkok is seeking Beijing’s involvement because Chinese companies operate some mines in Myanmar’s Shan State, and much of the mineral output is shipped to China, according to Foreign Minister Sihasak Phuangketkeow and Science and Research Minister Yodchanan Wongsawat.
China hasn’t issued its own readout of Tuesday’s talks. Its Foreign Ministry and embassy in Bangkok didn’t immediately respond to requests for comment on the Thai officials’ accounts or the proposed six-month shutdown. Beijing said earlier this month it wasn’t familiar with specific allegations involving Chinese companies but that it requires its firms to protect the environment and is willing to cooperate with Thailand on water pollution.
