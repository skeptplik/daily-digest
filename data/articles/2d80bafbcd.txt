SPOILER WARNING: This article features details on a GTA 6 Easter egg. Although it won't ruin the game's story or anything like that, we recognize some people want to organically discover and find interesting things on their own. You've been warned!
In 2021, Rockstar Games released the somewhat controversial remastered collection of the PS2 era GTA titles. It was... a mess, to say the least. However, one notable thing came from that collection, but fans weren't sure if it actually meant anything. There was a picture of a house in this new version of GTA: San Andreas that wasn't there in the original game, and it didn't look like a location from any other GTA game. Fans theorized this was actually our first look at GTA 6... and it turns out, they were right!
The house is real. It's located in Leonard County, in the suburb of Belville (shoutout Jared from the GTA mapping community for discovering this). However, that's just the tip of the iceberg. If you approach the house's garage, you can actually break in. Upon entry, it becomes clear this is a lair for some kind of conspiracy theorist. These aren't just ordinary in-universe ramblings — they're from someone who believes they are in a video game.

Various notes and writings on the wall point out that they can't leave Lenoida at all. They tried to purchase a ticket to Los Santos and couldn't, nor could they drive out of the state, and instead are planning to get a boat to try and escape. They also noted that there are an extraordinary amount of doppelgangers in reference to NPCs, that there is nothing behind closed doors, their own neighbors can't enter their homes, and that people phase through walls or "glitch." There's also a graph that shows a comically large spike in violent crime and a bunch of 6s written all over the place, likely poking fun at some of the people who drove themselves mad theorizing about this very game.
Perhaps one of the most notable bits is that this conspiracy theorist has determined two people, who go by "J" (Jason) and "L" (Lucia), are immune to whatever problems they are experiencing, suggesting they have freedom and autonomy. Ironically, they may have the least amount of autonomy, though, since they are controlled by the player, but they respawn, are largely free of longterm legal consequences, and can cause tons of mayhem, so they're less restricted than NPCs.
It's a very silly Easter egg, and one that leans into the absurdity of the GTA community in many ways. It's unclear whether this house was always meant to be like this, or if someone at the studio decided to have some fun with it after they saw how mad it drove fans. Either way, it gave me a good chuckle.
