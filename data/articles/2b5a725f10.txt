If there ever were a mountain top to reach in Australian women's domestic football, there is no doubt Michelle Heyman would stand at the top, alone with all the accolades one could dream of.
Today, she will add yet another accomplishment to her already glittering resume, becoming just the first A-League Women's player to reach 200 games for a single club, Canberra United.
It is a point Heyman herself did not think she would ever reach.
"It's a really proud moment," she said.
"I've been thinking about it a lot lately. Did I think this day was ever going to come? Definitely not."
Along with the goals, the national team call-ups and Olympics, in 2019 her football journey was put on hold, and she was forced to find her love of the game — and herself —again.
"I had to leave Canberra … play in Adelaide and have a year away, which was a terrible season for myself," Heyman said.
"I just wasn't feeling good. I didn't like who I was as a person. I wasn't getting the best out of my football, so I thought, 'This is it, this is time.'
"For me, having that time away from football, at the time I didn't think it was going to be so important, but it actually changed the way I viewed the game."
After rejoining the competition in the 2020/21 season, Heyman returned as if she never left — winning that season's Julie Dolan medal, the highest individual honour in the league.
The return to the league was not just about proving something to herself; for the star striker, it was also about "reclaiming the narrative".
"The last five, six years have been probably the most important journey that I've ever had within football," Heyman said.
"I think that amount of time is probably better than the first 10 years that I had playing the game.
"So, for me, I was lucky enough to get a second chance."
Having been a part of the competition since its inception, the 38-year-old has seen and done it all: a two-time premiership winner, multiple Julie Dolan medals and golden boots, and watching the league expand from 10 games to 20.
But the one thing she says has not changed is the league's professional status.
"I'm not going to lie, I don't think a lot of things have changed; we just play more games. I still think we still have the exact same problems as we did back then," Heyman said.
"People might judge me for saying that, but I still think we've got a long way to go to make it a more professional league.
"You look over what's happening in England, in America, and I'm like, 'Why are we so far behind?' So if we can copy them, I think we can improve and make it an incredible league."
Though her time in the league will eventually come — now being closer to the end of her chapter as a footballer than the start — her legacy will forever remain solidified not only at Canberra United but in A-League Women.
That legacy is something her coach Antoni Jagarinec believes cannot be overstated.
"You talk about people being part of the furniture, and I've heard that a lot this week," Jagarinec said.
"She's not [part of the furniture]. She's the whole house."
While Heyman has not set a date for when her time in football will end, she said she "won't be around in five years' time, that's for sure".
But one thing is certain — she will do it the way she has always done it: on her terms.
"I don't want them to tell me that I need to retire because it's their opinions on me. I want to do it because I want to do it," Heyman said.
"When I do decide to retire, I'm going to retire on my own goodwill and on my own abilities."
