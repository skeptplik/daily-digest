Shoe Station Group is pumping the brakes, and even reversing, on its ambitious store conversion plans.
On the company’s third quarter 2026 earnings call on Tuesday, Cliff Sifford, interim president and chief executive officer of Shoe Station Group, told analysts that the retailer plans to return rebannered stores to the original banner.
“We currently operate 131 rebannered stores,” Sifford said. “Of those, 27 are Shoe Station at Rogan’s locations, and they will return to operating solely under the Rogan’s name. We are evaluating the remaining 104 stores now and will determine the exact number over the next few weeks. We currently estimate that 75 percent or more of them will return to the Shoe Carnival banner.”
Because of the time needed for permits and new signs, the company said it expects these changes to officially take place in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. But conversions will start inside the store in the first two weeks of December for the holiday season.

“Many of these stores are in markets where decades of advertising and operating have built brand awareness and loyalty to both Shoe Carnival and Rogan’s,” the interim leader said. “In addition, we believe the Shoe Station name is the banner that offers us the best opportunity for long-term growth. We believe the clear delineation of the two banners allow us to grow both Shoe Station and Shoe Carnival.”
Sifford added that Shoe Station is the better branded shoe and accessory business, while Shoe Carnival is a moderate-priced footwear store for the entire family. Rogan’s “remains an important banner” that services the blue-collar worker through its stores and trucks.
In the future, Shoe Station growth is expected to come from new stores located in higher-income demographic areas of the cities we already serve under the Shoe Carnival banner and new cities where we have not yet opened a Shoe Carnival store, he explained.
“For that strategy to succeed, we cannot continue to operate Shoe Station stores in Shoe Carnival type centers or with Shoe Carnival product assortments,” Sifford said.
He added that the decision to upgrade the product in those rebannered stores alienated Shoe Carnival customers that was there for years.
“She came in, she looked, she couldn’t afford,” Sifford said when referring to the customer visiting Shoe Station stores converted from Shoe Carnival locations. “And basically, that was backed up through our focus groups.
The interim CEO said that customers within its focus groups told the company that when it rebannered Shoe Carnival stores to Shoe Stations, “we put a new name on the outside of the store and raised the prices.”
“If we change the product to the customer that shops those stores today, they essentially become Shoe Carnival stores with a Shoe Station banner on the outside,” Sifford added. “We can’t operate those stores that way and expect to get the growth that we’re looking to get out of Shoe Station. It’s a dramatic change.”
The company expects to incur approximately $4 million of expenses in the fourth quarter to return certain rebannered stores to the original layout. What’s more, returning stores to the original banners is expected to cost between $2 million and $3 million, which the company expects to incur as an expense primarily in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. The anticipated banner expense falls in fiscal 2027 and does not affect fiscal 2026 guidance, the company said.
This comes as the Fort Mills, S.C.-based company reported on Tuesday that it saw a net loss in the third quarter of fiscal 2026 of $12.3 million, or a loss of 46 cents per diluted share, compared to net income of $14.6 million, or 53 cents per diluted share, in the same time last year.
Net sales were $279.96 million compared to $297.16 million in the same time last year. Comparable store sales declined 5.1 percent.
The rebannering and even the change in the shoe retailer’s corporate name from Shoe Carnival to Shoe Station Group began a year ago under the helm of former CEO Mark Worden.
Worden “departed” abruptly on Feb. 24, 2026, and Sifford — he was Worden’s predecessor — returned as interim CEO. Sifford is the retailer’s vice chairman of the board, and continues as interim CEO while the company continues its search for a permanent CEO.
