PARIS — White Mountaineering has appointed veteran Italian menswear designer Alessandro Pungetti to succeed Yosuke Aizawa, following the founder’s decision earlier this year to step down after 20 years at the helm of the Japanese label.
Pungetti, longtime head designer of C.P. Company and cofounder of Ten C with Paul Harvey, will take over as head designer and creative director, starting with the fall 2027 collection to be presented in Paris next January, the brand said in a statement shared exclusively with WWD.
“Backed by a career spanning numerous brands, Pungetti brings expertise in textiles and processing techniques, as well as a deep understanding of diverse styles and cultures — including military, sports and workwear. He will drive the brand’s further development by opening up new possibilities while upholding the core values that form the foundation of White Mountaineering,” the brand and its parent company, Rhino Inc., said.
“We are confident that his creative approach — born of his keen aesthetic sensibility and extensive experience — makes him the ideal person to lead the brand into a new chapter,” it added, lauding his “unwavering commitment to design, his curiosity about materials and manufacturing techniques, and his boundless passion.”
Pungetti said he would juggle his new responsibilities with his existing design roles.
“I didn’t say ‘yes’ straight away, because it was quite different from what I’d done before. I spent a lot of time thinking about how I could bring my experience and perspective to this line,” he told WWD. “In the end, I thought in a very Italian way: ‘Why not?’”
Founded in Tokyo in 2006 by Aizawa, a former assistant to Junya Watanabe at Comme des Garçons, White Mountaineering is known for designs fusing high-performance technical fabrics with avant-garde urban tailoring, and for its collaborations with brands such as Uniqlo, Adidas, Reebok, Timberland and Umbro.
While Pungetti has always been interested in bringing military and workwear influences into the sportswear sphere, he was intrigued by the way White Mountaineering has injected fashion and streetwear sensibilities into functional outdoor gear.
“It completely broke the mold of how traditional outdoor apparel was perceived, which I thought was brilliant. Outdoor had always been seen as this clean, technical design sphere, with very few lines and a geometry that can sometimes be — no offense — a little boring and repetitive,” he noted.
“White Mountaineering offered an entirely different way of looking at it. It brought a fresh perspective that upended those conventions. In that sense, I’d call it revolutionary,” he added.
Pungetti, who also logged a stint designing knitwear for Stone Island, said he plans to bring some of his design signatures to the brand, starting with a presentation slated for the next edition of Paris Men’s Fashion Week.
“Clearly, there will be garment dyeing. The dyeing techniques will be closely connected to Japanese culture — more artisanal than industrial. We’ll also look at how to reinterpret some of the iconic imagery of the mountains. There will be references to the military world, too, but brought into a context closer to outdoor wear,” he teased.
Pungetti said he has long been curious about Japanese culture, noting his brother is a sixth dan in kendo. Early in his career he was profoundly marked by seeing a Comme des Garçon men’s collection in Paris, and he credited Japanese designers with introducing then-novelties like black denim.
Though he will be traveling regularly to Tokyo, Pungetti acknowledged the challenges of collaborating long-distance.
“While I find the fusion of an Italian perspective with a Japanese brand incredibly appealing, I also understand that it won’t be easy. However, I don’t view this as a reckless adventure. I intend to work as a united team to make this challenge a success,” he said in the statement.
He believes a strong design signature is key to standing out in a saturated market.
“The sector is undoubtedly experiencing fatigue. Perhaps it’s due to the challenges facing wholesale. Propositions aren’t as compelling, partly because consumers are facing real spending constraints, while players in the accessible fashion segment are competing aggressively with very strong offers at highly competitive prices,” he said.
“That is clearly putting pressure on the market. With White Mountaineering, as with the other projects I work on, my approach is to try to offer a point of differentiation,” he said. “You have to be yourself.”
