'Russia wants more than Ukraine,' Germany's Merz warns, as he says Moscow is 'escalating on a massive scale'
In his wide-ranging budget speech, Merz talks about Germany “neglecting fundamental issues for far too long” on defence, infrastructure, demographic change, and the economy – and the need to address these issues urgently.
But the starkest part of his speech comes as he talks about the external situation – and particularly the threat from Russia - to explain why some decisions, particularly on defence, are needed.
“Russia knows exactly what it wants and Russia wants more than Ukraine,” he warns.
“Anyone who fails to grasp this either does not understand the world as it is, or is pursuing an entirely different agenda – one that stands in opposition to the freedom, openness, and liberal values of our own country.”
Merz warns that “Russia is escalating tensions in the Baltic region, in Poland, in the Black Sea, and even in the outer space.”
“It is escalating on a massive scale – including here within our own borders, even at the cost of human lives. That is the situation, ladies and gentlemen.
And that is why we, as Europeans, must—and will – set clear boundaries for Russia.”

Kremlin brushes off reports of up to 700,000 Russian war deaths
The Kremlin also declined to comment on reports that up to 700,000 Russian soldiers have been killed in its almost five-year-long offensive on Ukraine.
After analysing a trove of hacked data from Russia’s official statistics agency, several Russian and international media outlets say Moscow’s own data shows 470,000-700,000 soldiers have been killed in the war.
Russia has not given an official death toll among its army since September 2022, when it said almost 6,000 soldiers had been killed in the first six months of fighting – a figure widely seen as a large undercount even at the time.
Asked about the new reports, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Wednesday:
“The only entity that can officially disclose casualty figures is the defence ministry of the Russian Federation.”
“I suggest not to pay attention to any other data,” he added.

Germany has 'begun to change course' amid major challenges, Merz says
Merz also touches on this week’s EU summit, starting tomorrow, and the likely contentious negotiations over the next EU budget.
He pushes back on “impressions” that he thinks Germany spends too much on EU contributions.
But he says he is “pushing for us to limit and focus our spending on what truly strengthens Europe today,” specifically on security and competitiveness.
In a pretty passionate defence of European integration, he says only such focused spending will give Europe “an assurance that we will not simply become a plaything in this world, but have a hand in shaping it.”
Summing up, he repeats his earlier point that Germany faces major challenges that have been unresolved for too long, and so one budget “is far from solving all of our problems,” but says “we have begun to change course” as economic indicators are starting to improve.
'By supporting Ukraine, we are supporting and protecting ourselves,' Merz tells parliament
Merz goes back to last week’s meeting of the Coalition of the Willing in Berlin, saying the US delegation was “it seemed to me, quite clearly surprised by how united … the Europeans are on this issue.”
“It is precisely this determination, ladies and gentlemen, that allows for a cautious optimism regarding the prospect of peace – a genuine peace,” he says.
He keeps coming back to his main message: “by supporting Ukraine, we are supporting and protecting ourselves.”
“This is true in a sense that may not yet be fully understood by many in our country,” he stresses.
