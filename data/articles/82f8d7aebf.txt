These are the stories making headlines in fashion on Tuesday.
Danish fashion house Cecilie Bahnsen is launching its first kidswear collection on Dec. 4. The limited-edition line includes four core styles for ages 3 to 10, and uses fabrics from the brand’s archive. “Creating a universe of kidswear has been a dream of mine for a long time,” Founder and Creative Director Cecilie Bahnsen said in a statement. “I love the idea of revisiting some of our most beloved silhouettes and bringing them to life in beautiful fabrics from our archive, giving these materials a new story.” This move also builds upon the response to the brand’s kidswear collaboration with Uniqlo this past spring. {Fashionista inbox}
Hailey Bieber’s conversation with Andrew Ross Sorkin at The New York Times’ Dealbook conference has caused online debate, with some saying the questions about Rhode’s $1 billion sale to E.l.f. Beauty were condescending toward a female founder and others saying she seemed unprepared for a journalist’s scrutiny. Critics of Sorkin, including Dieux Founder Charlotte Palermino, argued that the beauty industry is underestimated at business events, while others said the Rhode founder leaned on ideas like manifestation instead of explaining an actual sound business strategy. {Business of Fashion/paywalled}
J.Crew Chief Marketing Officer Julia Collier is leaving the company and, per WWD’s sources, is said to be returning to Skims. J.Crew confirmed that her last day will be Dec. 18 and that it is searching for a replacement. Collier has led the brand’s marketing since January 2025 and previously was a senior vice president of marketing at Skims. {WWD/paywalled}
This holiday season, retailers are using artificial intelligence to set prices and decide when and how deeply to discount. According to Reuters, AI pricing firms find that smaller markdowns won’t hurt demand, which could mean lower Black Friday deals for shoppers already strained by inflation. One firm said its software advised a billion-dollar beauty brand to cut a skin-care discount to 30%, down from a planned 40%, marking a growing sign of AI tools’ influence in demand forecasting. {Reuters/paywalled}
British retailer Next reported that one of its online suppliers experienced a breach that may have provided unauthorized access to some of the company’s information. Next said none of the affected information includes customers’ personal details, and that it couldn’t be used to defraud shoppers or the business. The incident adds to a run of retail cyber incidents in Britain, including a breach at Asos last month and the 2025 attacks on Marks & Spencer and the Co-op. {Bloomberg}
