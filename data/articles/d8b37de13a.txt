In 2022, Ukraine bombed the Kerch bridge linking mainland Russia to the Crimean Peninsula, using explosives hidden in a truck. In 2023, naval drones struck the bridge. In 2025, covert Ukrainian operatives planted bombs on its undersea support pillars. But none of these attacks caused major damage to the structure, which Russian President Vladimir Putin described in 2018 as a “miracle”. But earlier this week, two back-to-back Ukrainian attacks, involving drones and missiles, brought down a crucial span of the road bridge and damaged the parallel rail bridge. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy hailed it as an “unprecedented operation”. Russian authorities acknowledged the damage, announcing a suspension of road traffic and restrictions on train services.
The 19-km Kerch bridge, across the Kerch Strait, which connects the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov, is the longest bridge in Europe, built at a cost of $3.6 billion. It was opened in 2018 by Russian President Vladimir Putin, four years after Russia annexed Crimea from Ukraine through a contested referendum. Mr. Putin drove an orange truck across its length and said “a miracle has come true”.
