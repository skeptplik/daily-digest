A man has been charged with murder after a delivery rider was stabbed to death while working, leaving his family bereft and a city in mourning.
On Friday night, HungryPanda delivery rider Feng Jianming, 47, and another rider confronted a man allegedly tampering with their bikes in Melbourne’s city centre.
An altercation ensued and the man, a 51-year-old, is alleged to have stabbed both riders.
Emergency services rushed to the scene on Frederick Way, but Feng could not be saved.
The second rider, 34, was taken to hospital with serious injuries, where he remained on Monday.
The 51-year-old was arrested by police on Saturday and on Monday morning was charged with one count of murder and intentionally causing serious injury.
Feng, originally from China’s Shaanxi Province, had been in Australia for about nine years.
His wife, Xu, their parents and two children all relied on his work to get by at home.
The news of Feng’s death reverberated through Melbourne and gig worker communities in Australia and abroad.
Mourners left floral tributes and notes near the state library over the weekend in his memory.
Some cards expressed sympathy for his family, while others vented grave frustrations with messages such as “city of criminal” and “Asian lives matter”.
A GoFundMe fundraiser set up by the Melbourne Chinatown Business Association had raised almost $390,000 by Monday morning to help Feng’s family with emergency travel and funeral expenses.
Christina Zhao, head of the Melbourne Chinatown Business Association, said Feng’s passing had weighed heavily on his colleagues and gig worker communities in China, Japan and Korea.
Food delivery company HungryPanda said it was assisting the family with visa arrangements, funeral expenses, financial support and insurance.
The Transport Workers Union said HungryPanda need to confirm they properly assessed risks to their workers.
“HungryPanda has legal obligations to do whatever it can to minimise risk as food delivery riders carry out their work,” national secretary Michael Kaine said on Monday.
“It must urgently confirm whether it has done this, and we are calling on WorkSafe to investigate whether it has failed in its duties here, as we have seen in the past with its failure to report a workplace death.”
The 51-year-old man was remanded in custody to appear before Melbourne magistrates court on Monday.
