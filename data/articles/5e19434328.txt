The midterm general elections held this Tuesday marked a major shift for the United States. A historic “blue wave” reflected the country’s overwhelming support for the Democratic Party, which now has control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate. And a number of Democratic candidates won their elections for governor too, in swing (and even non-swing) states like Texas, Georgia, Iowa, and Ohio.
Many of the surprise wins also brought about historic firsts for the nation. Keisha Lance Bottoms has become the first Black woman ever elected to governor, while Chris Pappas is the first openly gay candidate elected to the Senate. Other notable firsts include the first-ever Muslim Senate-elect (Abdul El-Sayed) and the first-ever Native American female governor to be voted in to the post (Deb Haaland).
But with so much excitement around the candidates, it can be easy to forget that yesterday also saw a number of important measures featured on ballots across all 50 states. And a few of the results were quite uplifting.
Virginia saw a complete victory for voters on the left, with citizens of the state voting to codify marriage equality, even in the case that the Supreme Court ever decides to overturn Obergefell v. Hodges; and Loving v. Virginia, which protects interracial marriage, was also codified. Virginia voters also chose to protect reproductive rights, and to restore voting rights to those who have been previously incarcerated.
Nevada citizens voted to protect abortion rights too, while those in Washington voted not to repeal a millionaire wealth tax. (The key issues that many of the ballots centered around this year included abortion rights, taxes, drug policies, voting, and transgender rights.)
There are still some ballot measures that have yet to be called, and votes will continue to roll in throughout the next few hours, meaning a few major issues remain hanging in the balance.
