Adults across Sussex will be able to access free mental health and recovery courses through a new county-wide project launching in 2027.
The Sussex Recovery College is offering courses covering mental health, wellbeing, recovery, personal growth and creativity from January.
It will bring together existing services across Brighton and Hove, East Sussex and West Sussex into a single programme.
Sussex Partnership NHS Foundation Trust, one of the partners in the project, said research involving people already using the existing services showed hospital admissions fell in the 18 months after they registered to take part.
The research showed that in the 18 months, average hospital admissions fell by 34% and the number of days spent in hospital fell by 55%, the trust said.
A 50% reduction in admissions under the Mental Health Act was also reported, it added.
The trust is developing the county-wide service with Southdown, which is the project's voluntary sector partner.
Matt Gough, the charity's chief operating officer, said his organisation was bringing more than 10 years' experience of running the Brighton and Hove Recovery College to the project.
Courses that were offered in 2024-25 looked at specific health conditions and included creative activities such as writing, music and singing.
"We've seen first-hand the difference recovery education can make, helping people build confidence, develop new skills, connect with others and find ways to support their mental health and wellbeing," he said.
Courses are co-produced and delivered by people with lived experience, NHS clinicians, peer trainers and specialists.
Students will be able to attend face-to-face local courses or take part online.
Dr Oliver Dale, chief medical officer at the trust, said: "Recovery colleges play an important role in supporting people's mental health, wellbeing and recovery."
He said the new service would create greater opportunities for people to learn, connect and support their recovery journey.
