New York Public Radio turned up the volume at The Glasshouse on Tuesday night, welcoming more than 500 guests for its annual gala honoring the organization behind some of the city’s best-known media outlets, including WNYC, WQXR, and Gothamist. Against the backdrop of the Manhattan skyline, supporters came together in celebration of the public broadcaster’s work keeping New Yorkers informed, engaged, and entertained.
This year’s gala theme, Make Waves, paid tribute to the voices, talent, and ideas shaping journalism and culture. Padma Lakshmi, Katie Couric, Lesley Stahl, and Candace Bushnell were on hand, alongside New York City officials and figures from the worlds of media and the arts. The evening’s honorees included lifestyle doyenne Ina Garten, who received the Cultural Advocacy Award, and Nobel Peace laureate Maria Ressa, who was honored with the Impact Award.
The recognition held particular meaning for Garten. The Barefoot Contessa said that being acknowledged for her work was nowhere in sight when she first arrived in town from Washington, D.C. after a career in government. “To be honored for cultural reasons in New York would have only been my wildest dreams,” she told Vogue. “It will take a little getting used to.”
For Garten, entertaining has always been about more than what’s on the plate. In an increasingly phone-obsessed world, the Happy Hour with Ina Garten podcast host remains a firm believer in the magic of gathering around the table. “There’s nothing like getting together in person. There’s something biological about it, and it makes us feel good,” she said. “That’s why I cook—I cook not for the food, but to keep people together. When you call somebody and invite them to dinner, they always say ‘yes.’”
With Thanksgiving just around the corner, Garten also had some timely advice for aspiring hosts. Her golden rule? Have a welcome cocktail or mocktail already prepared. “You want somebody who is relaxed, greeting guests at the door, and really happy to see you,” she said. “When people arrive at the door, it smells good, the music is good, and then everyone is happy.”
The Glasshouse certainly delivered an atmosphere that Garten could co-sign. Following a cocktail reception, guests took their seats at elegant round tables filled with calla lilies, roses, ranunculus, and hydrangeas. One of the room’s most striking features was a spectacular canopy of color-changing LED chandeliers, which rose and descended gracefully, shifting the space from an airy, light-filled setting to a more intimate, dramatic atmosphere.
Sara Bareilles got the festivities underway with a medley of her hits, including “Love Song” and “Brave,” setting an exuberant tone for the program. Michael Hill, host of Morning Edition, followed with welcome remarks before Anna Quindlen, vice chair of the New York Public Radio Board of Trustees, presented Garten with her award.
In her acceptance speech, Garten took guests on a nostalgic trip back to the New York of the late 1970s. While working in the capital, she would escape to the Big Apple whenever she had a few days off, drawn to its energy, creativity, and cultural offerings. She recalled visiting Dean & DeLuca, admiring the windows at Henri Bendel, and browsing the aisles of Zabar’s before returning to Washington to host dinner parties that, by comparison, felt a little less inspiring. One particularly memorable New York dinner party, where guests passionately debated a New Yorker article by writer John McPhee, made her realize just how much she wanted to be part of the city’s cultural conversation.
“I always believed culture is not something reserved for museums, concert halls, or grand institutions,” she told the audience. “Culture is how we live. It’s what we read, it’s what we listen to, it’s what we cook, it’s what we share around a table and how we welcome people.”
She also praised New York Public Radio for its role in fostering an informed and culturally engaged community, noting that independent journalism and cultural programming have never been more important. It was a fitting tribute from someone whose own career has been built around bringing people together.
Soon after, guests had a chance to put their generosity into action. A spirited live auction got paddles flying, with a little friendly competition that helped the gala raise more than $1.8 million. After all that bidding, everyone had certainly worked up an appetite. Dinner offered a welcome opportunity to settle in and reconnect over the main course: A French onion short rib that proved to be a crowd-pleaser.
Once the meal wrapped up, celebrated cellist Sterling Elliott took the stage with a moving performance of the third movement of Cassadó’s Suite for Solo Cello. Singer-songwriter Nia Drummond followed with a jazz rendition of “Summertime,” before the program turned to a special tribute to the Apollo Theater. The legendary Harlem institution received the Community Icon Award in recognition of its more than 90-year legacy as a global showcase for Black artistry and creativity. CEO Michelle Ebanks accepted the award on the Apollo’s behalf.
As dessert was served—pumpkin cheesecake bites, fruit tarts, and dark chocolate-raspberry truffles—Renée Elise Goldsberry brought the show to a close. The Tony Award-winning star of Hamilton performed her own renditions of “Satisfied” and “You’ll Never Walk Alone” from Carousel, providing a sweet finale to the formal festivities.
But the fun was far from over. The gala soon gave way to a lively after-party, with Questlove taking over the DJ booth and guests flocking to the dance floor. Between songs, partygoers refueled with a decidedly New York-inspired late-night spread of hot dogs, cheeseburgers, fries, mini doughnuts, and popcorn. With Questlove spinning and the crowd still going strong well past midnight, one thing was clear—New York Public Radio knows how to throw a party.
