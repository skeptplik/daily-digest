A rare phenomenon filmed last Saturday went viral on social networks. Moist sea air pushed up a slope cools, forming clouds that descend on the far side and create a waterfall-like effect.
The expression "Cidade Maravilhosa", used as an epithet for Rio de Janeiro, gained popularity in the early 20th century and, even in 2026, the city still lives up to the name.
At dawn last Saturday, the city offered a visual spectacle that took residents and tourists by surprise. A dense mass of cloud wrapped around Corcovado Mountain, crowned by the world-famous Christ the Redeemer statue, at around 710 metres above sea level.
Only the summit of the mountain, with the iconic statue, and the highest peaks remained visible above a white blanket that created the effect of a waterfall spilling down the slopes.
The moment was captured on a mobile phone by filmmaker and photographer Márcio Amaro, from the Santa Teresa neighbourhood, and quickly went viral on social media thanks to the beauty of the scene.
"I had never seen Corcovado like this", wrote Márcio Amaro, making it clear that "the image is real, no AI".
The scene looks as if it has come straight out of a film, but it is very real and has an explanation in atmospheric science. The technical term is orographic cloud, a rare phenomenon triggered by winds coming in from the ocean that push a mass of moist air against a natural barrier.
In this case, that air rose up the slopes of Corcovado. On its way up, it cooled very quickly, a change that leads to the condensation of water vapour and to the formation of dense cloud near the top of the mountain.
As it crosses the summit, the mist is projected over to the other side and, as it begins to descend, it follows the contours of the terrain, creating the illusion of falling water.
As it continues to drop, the air warms up again and the droplets evaporate, which reinforces the effect of a veil or curtain sliding down through the vegetation, until the mist eventually dissolves.
Given that Corcovado Mountain is close to the Atlantic Ocean and covered in Atlantic Forest, the orographic cloud phenomenon is actually frequent in Rio and the surrounding region, especially when cold fronts pass through or in the early morning.
Other photographers had already captured a moment like this in August 2026, showing Christ the Redeemer rising above a tangle of clouds at daybreak. However, that was only the result of static fog trapped in the valleys.
The moving waterfall image depends on a precise combination of factors: the height of the cloud, which has to be exactly at the level of the statue's feet, with Christ clear of the mist, the density of the vapour and a constant current of wind that drives the mass of air down the hillside without dispersing it immediately.
Rio de Janeiro was already an unmissable destination for nature lovers. After this spectacle, there really are no excuses left. You just have to get up early.
