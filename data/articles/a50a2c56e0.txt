The UK, Australia, Canada and Italy have issued a joint statement urging Israel to "allow immediate, rapid and unimpeded humanitarian access throughout Gaza", where they say the situation on the ground is "dire".
The foreign ministers warn recent floods have brought "further suffering" to the war-torn territory, which recently had its first winter storm.
The statement says 150,000 tents and other vital supplies are being held at the border.
The Israeli military authority which controls the Gaza crossings has said it is co-ordinating with the US-led Board of Peace and the UN on a winter plan, and that 93,000 tents and 8,600 aid trucks with winter supplies had entered the territory.
It added that reconstruction "can only begin after Hamas fully disarms".
The UN Office for the Co-ordination of Humanitarian Affairs said the widespread flooding and a landslide led to a number of deaths, thousands of damaged shelters and the suspension of critical services.
The statement from the British, Australian, Canadian and Italian foreign ministers says, external: "This situation is entirely reversible - and there is still time to act before the winter fully sets in."
"The crossings must be opened, and the tents allowed in. Dual-use items necessary for humanitarian operations and early recovery must be granted entry before it is too late."
The European Union issued a similar statement on Saturday, external, reiterating its "grave concern" over what it called the "persistent and devastating humanitarian crisis".
The EU also urged Israel to comply with international law.
The war in Gaza was triggered by the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, in which about 1,200 people were killed and 251 others were taken hostage.
Israel responded by launching a military campaign in Gaza, during which more than 74,690 people have been killed, according to the territory's Hamas-run health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the UN.
Israeli air strikes and other attacks have continued to take place almost every day despite the ceasefire agreed in October last year.
