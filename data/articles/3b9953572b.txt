A Victorian Coalition government would privatise two sewage plants and sell off state-owned land, renewable energy assets and the “air rights” between Richmond train station and the Melbourne Cricket Ground if elected.
The opposition leader, Jess Wilson, announced a $14.47bn “asset recycling” plan as part of the Liberal and National parties’ pre-election policy costings on Thursday.
She said the plan included a sale of state land to developers, and a $9.2bn, 90-year lease of the Eastern and Western treatment plants that process the vast majority of Melbourne’s sewage.
Wilson said there would be “no change” to water bills as a result of the 90-year lease.
“The reasons why we have made this decision is because we can reduce Victoria’s debt, and what does that deliver? Reduction in interest payments, which cannibalise the state budget,” she said.
The air rights between Richmond station and the MCG would be sold on the condition the station was “revitalised”, while the renewable energy sector would buy the assets owned by the State Electricity Commission, which was only recently revived by the Labor government.
The Coalition has also proposed savings of $5.73bn from drawing down on contingencies and $1.23bn in reprioritising several planned level crossing removals.
The independent Parliamentary Budget Office has found the Coalition’s plan would lower net debt to $176bn by the 2029-30 financial year, compared with $196bn outlined in Treasury’s pre-election budget update this month.
Wilson said it would save the state $4.2bn in interest payments.
More than 1.5 million Victorians have already voted ahead of Saturday’s election.
