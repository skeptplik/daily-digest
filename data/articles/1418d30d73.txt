Skydance has implemented an undisclosed number of cuts to its games division.
A spokesperson told Variety the company is "implementing reorganisation and operational shifts across a few areas of [its] business" following last month's $111 billion merger between Paramount and Warner Bros. Discovery.
"We have stepped back and taken some time to consider where we are investing our efforts and have made the difficult decision to scale down some of our projects. As a result, there are roles impacted."
The spokesperson continued: "This is not a reflection of the talent, dedication, or creativity of those affected, and we are grateful for their contributions. We are committed to approaching the process with transparency, care, and respect for those who are leaving the company."
According to Kotaku and Insider Gaming, approximately 30 positions may be affected. This includes associate design director David Ballard, who announced his departure on LinkedIn.
"My chapter with Skydance and Paramount came to an end today with a layoff," wrote Ballard. "I'm super grateful for the opportunities I was given over the past almost 4.5 years. A shipped title, several awards, two promotions, an incredible team, and an unbelievable amount of creativity and collaboration."
Skydance CEO David Ellison previously stated that the merger would require the company to make "difficult decisions."
"Bringing together two companies of this size and complexity will require difficult decisions. There will be changes, and there will be impacts. I'm not going to pretend otherwise."
Paramount Games Studio was established this summer and is led by EVP and head of games Dan Brigg. The studio has combined Paramount's IP with Skydance Interactive and Skydance New Media.
Following the acquisition, it merged with WB Games to form a single unit led by Tony Driscoll.
