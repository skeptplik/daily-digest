It’s crunch time for retailers — and the challenges this holiday season loom large.
Retailers are up against cost-conscious, cash-strapped consumers demanding greater value, deeper discounts and more conveniences. They’re also dealing with rising logistics and fuel costs, shifting tariffs, El Niño disrupting the weather and sudden concerns that the anticipated surge in online retail orders and AI could cause outages, following the Federal Aviation Administration, JPMorgan Chase, Microsoft, and other outages this month.
Yet the positive outlook for decent holiday sales gains remains intact — at least for now — with consumers showing resilience so far and a greater inclination to visit stores, a trend particularly true for Gen Z. They’re helping retailers keep it together. Positive third-quarter results from Walmart, Target, Abercrombie & Fitch, Gap brand, and even Kohl’s, as well as lower temperatures this week, suggest there’s momentum ahead of Black Friday.
Retailers also have 29 days between Thanksgiving and Christmas this year, one more than last year.
Coats and cold-weather merchandise have not been selling well, but consumer demand for rainwear and wet-weather categories will increase this holiday weekend, according to Planalytics, which advises retailers on the weather and its impact on business. “Overall, the weather over the entire Black Friday weekend will favor winter-driven categories such as outerwear, boots, hats and gloves, blankets and seasonal food and beverages as shoppers respond to both promotions and the more wintry feel,” said Evan Gold, executive vice president of global partnerships and alliances at Planalytics.
“By midweek colder air will slide across the central and eastern U.S. and the West will turn warmer again. Finally the colder air will settle over the Southeast at the end of the week and warmer conditions will prevail over the rest of the U.S. Rain will fall in the East and West Coast for much of the week, including heavy rain and high winds in the Midwest and Northeast on Thanksgiving Day and into Black Friday, including New York City, Boston and Philadelphia. Most of the U.S. will be warmer than last year and warmer than normal with a sliver of colder anomalies along the Gulf Coast. Showers will continue near all three coastlines, while the interior of the country is dry.”
The National Retail Federation projects holiday sales will grow 4.2 percent to $1.01 trillion this year, though about half of that is driven by price inflation. The NRF expects Thanksgiving weekend will draw out 182.7 million consumers, in line with the average seen over the past few years. Sixty percent of consumers commenced holiday shopping in early November. Black Friday remains the most popular day to get out and shop, with 69 percent planning to participate, followed by Cyber Monday at 36 percent, though it’s not expected to be a barnburner given all the early Black Friday promotions seen as far back as October.
Retail price promotions have been widespread and persistent for weeks, and are likely to accelerate with deeper cuts as the season progresses. However, inventories are being managed well, so there’s not expected to be as much leftover merch as in past years. Up to now, most price promotions have ranged from 25 to 50 percent off, often billed as storewide with “exceptions.”
According to Deloitte, 87 percent of shoppers surveyed plan to participate in Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales events, up from 82 percent in 2025, and the highest level since 2016. Deloitte sees spending per consumer at $624, flat to last year, suggesting shoppers will be taking home fewer items. Ninety-four percent of Gen-Zers plan to shop on Black Friday and Cyber Monday, and spend $753, which is 23 percent above a year ago and more than other cohorts expect to spend. Seventy-two percent plan to shop in stores on Black Friday, versus 50 percent in other generations. Deloitte sees clothing and accessories as the most popular gift item, followed by electronics and accessories, then toys and hobbies, and gift cards.
“This holiday season, consumers are being especially thoughtful about how and where they shop,” Rosalia Bucaro Polizzi, chief merchandising officer at HSN, told WWD. “They are looking for value, but they also want discovery, inspiration and products that feel relevant to their everyday lives.”
HSN has a new initiative, HSN Holiday Helpers, connecting customers with personalities and experts in fashion, beauty and culinary, offering “curated gift ideas, seasonal advice and inspiration,” said Polizzi. “We’re seeing particularly strong momentum across beauty and culinary, categories that lend themselves to both meaningful gifting and everyday enjoyment.”
Fashion that can be worn for different occasions and settings and well after the holidays is more important this year.
	“Today’s consumer is looking for pieces that feel both special and versatile, with an emphasis on quality and thoughtful design,” said Jill Norton, chief commercial officer at Vince. “During the holiday season, we see an opportunity to invest in wardrobing staples that offer a sense of occasion while remaining relevant well after. At Vince, that translates to a refined approach to dressing, balancing timeless silhouettes with elevated textures, rich seasonal colors like violet and ultramarine, and considered details.” She said Vince’s design team continues to complement fabrications like cashmeres, leathers and silks “with the right touch of nuance.”
“This holiday season, we’re seeing consumers place greater importance on the meaning behind the gifts they give,” said Claudia Cividino, president of Jared Jewelers. “They are looking for pieces that feel personal, celebrate important relationships and create memories that last well beyond the holidays.”
Ashley Bigbee, vice president of merchandising at the Kay jewelry brand, had a similar message. “For today’s consumer, it’s about more than price. Consumers are increasingly gravitating toward jewelry with sentimental value and personal significance. We’re seeing interest in both accessible personalization, such as birthstones, zodiac signs and initial pendants as well as more customized options, including engravings and custom-designed pieces. Outside of customization and personalization, important categories include men’s jewelry, watches, classic styles and gifts $500 and under.”
LuxExperience, the online platform for luxury fashion, sees three key themes driving consumer demand this season: gifting, including self-gifting; ski, including performance and après-ski styles, and “winter sun.”
The company expects strong demand in fine jewelry, statement coats, and elaborate bags, and has already launched a winter edit with Brunello Cucinelli featuring exclusive styles, alongside menswear “alpine” exclusives with Saman Amel, and a Mytheresa x Goldbergh exclusive ski capsule collection, among many others. In early December, LuxExperience’s Net-a-porter will launch a “comprehensive” ski campaign with exclusives from Arch4, Arma and Nour Hammour, among others. And Mr Porter will have a ski edit featuring Bogner, Kjus, Brunello Cucinelli and Moncler Grenoble.
Also in early December, Net-a-porter will launch its “winter sun” campaign featuring brands including Dolce & Gabbana, Zimmermann, Pucci and Missoni, with Mytheresa’s cruise campaign launching shortly after.
	J.Crew is focusing on new takes on icon pieces, with fresh proportions and fabrications, styles that can be worn day into night. Cashmere sweaters, barn jackets, Fair Isle roll-neck sweaters, wool trousers, overcoats, pajamas, beanies and socks are being emphasized.
According to WalletHub, a tool for monitoring credit, debt, credit cards and other financial products, the best places to shop on Black Friday, at least for discounts, are JCPenney, Macy’s and Target. They will average at least 38 percent off on average at those stores. Consumer packaged goods will offer the most value on Black Friday, while computers and phones should have the least rewarding deals, according to WalletHub.
Generally, consumers will be spending more for fewer gifts. According to WalletHub, 85 percent of consumers will spend the same or less on the holidays this year compared to last year.
	In the luxury sector, HSBC reported in October that this holiday season, “Chanel has superior reach, resilience and engagement, Louis Vuitton has rebounded, while Hermès, Versace, Dior, McQueen and Balenciaga ‘remain weak.’”
“Consumers have entered the holiday season with a plan, and they appear to be sticking to it,” said Kiara Barrett, head of global thought leadership at Circana, which provides technology, AI, and data to consumer packaged goods companies. “Black Friday and Cyber Monday remain critical shopping moments, but consumers are increasingly focused on making sure each purchase delivers meaningful value. Retailers still have significant opportunities ahead as shoppers continue evaluating purchases and completing their holiday lists throughout December.”
“Holiday shoppers are approaching every purchase with intention,” Barrett said. “The fact that dollar sales have recently turned positive while unit sales remain under pressure tells us consumers are still willing to spend when they see the right value proposition. Black Friday, Cyber Monday and December remain critical opportunities for retailers because shoppers are actively comparing prices, promotions and fulfillment options before committing their dollars. The winners this holiday season will be the retailers and brands that make consumers feel confident that every purchase is worthwhile.”
