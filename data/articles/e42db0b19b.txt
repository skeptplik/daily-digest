Anything can be the star of a Far Side comic. While most of them featured stupid humans or talking animals as their main characters, the surrealist streak in cartoonist Gary Larson sometimes gave life to inanimate objects as well. For example, there’s a strip where a jogger tells a living pair of scissors that they can no longer run together. And if scissors can be alive in The Far Side comics, just about anything can, including food.
Here are the seven funniest Far Side comics that star food, some of which feature food that has even gained sentience for the sake of a perfect joke.
1 Mar. 16, 1994
I love this comic strip because it is just unabashedly silly. Not only are sandwiches alive in this one-panel reality, but they have such a complex social structure that they even have their own organized crime. The sandwich mafia is so cruel that it punishes its victims by hurling them into a pit of fourth-graders. If I were the sandwich, I’d be especially worried about the fourth-grader in the green stripey shirt. He looks vicious.
2 Jan. 14, 1987
Yet another food item that has turned to a life of crime. Here, Larson interprets potato salad going bad in the silliest way possible.
3 July 8, 1985
This is a twist on that classic gag where, with two people in a life boat, one begins to imagine the other as food. This one features a man and a cow in a lifeboat, so the man naturally imagines steak while looking at the cow. As for the cow, well, he dreams of the man as a pile of man-shaped grass.
4 July 23, 1982
I love how adorable-looking these man-eating bears are as they stumble upon a group of campers in sleeping bags and exclaim “Sandwiches!” Personally, I think of sleeping bags as more burrito-like or even more like pita bread than sandwiches, but perhaps these bears aren’t terribly educated about the ways other cultures eat.
5 June 11, 1984
One of the reasons why The Far Side endures more than 30 years after Gary Larson concluded his strip is that he never spoke down to his audience. Sure, he had some dumb jokes sometimes, but they were never dumbed down for our sake; they were just dumb for the sake of being dumb — like a sandwich mafia. Larson could pull off the lowbrow and the highbrow, often in the very same comic, as this one illustrates.
At a glance, it doesn’t even seem like there’s a joke here, despite the simple caption: “Soup of the day is ready!” This is one of those comic strips by Larson that demands that you go looking for the punchline. Larson seems to trust that the audience will find the joke (or maybe he doesn’t care if some don’t, which I applaud). Eventually, you see that this chef is missing his shoe, meaning he used it to make the soup. So for all that highbrow thinking you had to do to find the punchline, the joke is incredibly lowbrow. It’s the perfect mix.
6 July 9, 1981
Larson also got into really dark humor at times. He once drew a comic strip where a snake killed himself by becoming his own noose, which is both silly and incredibly macabre. Yet this food-centric panel is so dark that it borders on creepy. Who is this guy that wants to buy this young employee of the butcher? And who is this butcher that is willing to sell him?
7 Feb. 28, 1987
I first read this comic strip when I was a teenager, around the time when I first discovered The Far Side and quickly became obsessed with it. Since then, every time I make a waffle, I cannot help but think of this comic strip. I’ll mix the batter, spray the waffle iron, pour the mix in, close the top, and wait for that little light to go off. Inevitably, as I’m staring at that little light, I’ll murmur, “Slave to the waffle light.” It's one of those pieces of art that just speaks to a real-life truism.
When making waffles, we are all Wendell Zurkowitz.
