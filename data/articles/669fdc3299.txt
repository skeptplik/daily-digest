Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced increased Ubisoft's Q2 net bookings to €389 million, exceeding the estimated guidance of €370 million.
Ubisoft stated that the new Assassin's Creed leadership within its Creative House structure "made clear, decisive choices in the final stretch of development," contributing to this outcome.
"This enabled the game to achieve an elevated level of quality and a commercial performance that positions Black Flag Resynced among the industry's most successful action-adventure remakes," it said.
The numbers
For the first half of FY2026-27
- Net bookings: €644.7 million, down 16.5% year-on-year
- Digital net bookings: €548.2 million, down 20.1% year-on-year
- Back-catalogue net bookings: €438.8 million, down 40.8% year-on-year
- IFRS operating income: -€189.2 million (vs -€120.2 million YoY)
- Non-IFRS operating income: -€155.9 million (vs €27.1 million YoY)
The highlights
Ubisoft's net bookings for H1, covering the six months ending September 30, 2026, declined 17% year-on-year to €645 million.
The company maintained stable monthly active users and active players during the period, at 34 million and 88 million, respectively.
In the associated report, Ubisoft announced that it had amended its Creative House structure by merging House 3 (focused on live service games) and 5 (focused on casual games) into a single unit to "leverage synergies between Live experiences across platforms". The two units were already overseen by a single GM, Julian Bares; the firm said that the Creative House Structure was now "fully in place" with the remaining GM for Creative House 4 joining in January. It also established Motherbrain, a subsidiary focused on AI-driven "gameplay innovation", along with an unnamed dedicated business unit "to maximize the value of brands not included in a Creative House with an agile and investment-light model".
The company stated its cost reduction program is "on track" and has entered the third phase. The fixed cost base for the period was €660 million, a 6% year-on-year decrease, with a target of €1.25 billion "on a run-rate basis" by March 2028.
Ubisoft's headcount was down close to 1,100 over the period following layoffs and studio closures. Shortly before the earnings announcement it laid off 166 staff in its Montreal and Quebec studios, which followed the closure of studios in Winnipeg and Belgrade in June alongside job losses worldwide including at its San Francisco office, and subesquent layoffs at Ubisoft Barcelona in July.
In Q2, Ubisoft's back-catalogue bookings declined 55% to €218 million, attributed to "a high comparison base that included partnerships and the significant contribution of Assassin's Creed Shadows."
The Rainbow Six Siege franchise "performed in line with expectations" and has remained stable into the third quarter. However, Ubisoft announced the shutdown of Rainbow Six Mobile in October.
During an earnings call (via Seeking Alpha), CFO Frédérick Duguet said the decision was made because the company is "in the mindset of having a stronger focus in terms of capital allocation and financial returns" and "putting our resources on bigger opportunities".
"Today, we can say that Console and PC remain our core focus for our biggest franchises," Duguet continued. "Mobile is still an opportunity for the medium to longer term with games such as AC Jade on Assassin's Creed."
"But the focus is really to make sure that we come with strong growth on Console and PC, and notably PC, which is a more dynamic platform than ever."
Tom Clancy's The Division 2 "saw net bookings growth this quarter, building on monetisation improvements initiated in Q1," while the Ghost Recon franchise "delivered a strong performance" during the period.
"Our new operating model is now fully in place, with leadership secured across all Creative Houses," said Ubisoft CEO Yves Guillemot. "In an increasingly competitive entertainment industry, gamers require exceptional quality, distinctive creative experiences, uncompromising gameplay and a defined audience for games to stand out and succeed.
"By repositioning our talents at the centre of decision-making and fostering a more spontaneous and direct link with our communities, our new model is designed precisely to deliver on these imperatives. Its first benefits are already visible, with the commercial success of Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced and the renewed momentum of Rainbow Six Siege."
Guillemot continued: "We remain on track with our cost reduction program, reflecting our commitment to a focused and disciplined organisation as we are preparing for a significantly stronger content cycle. In parallel, we have made progress on our refinancing plan with the objective of securing competitive terms and putting in place an efficient medium-term plan over the coming months."
"We are confirming our FY2026-27 objectives. By combining our iconic brands and creative talent with a more focused, agile and accountable operating model, we are building stronger Ubisoft."
