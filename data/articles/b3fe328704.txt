It was just after 09:00 on a warm February morning when CCTV picked up a man dressed as an UberEats rider hovering on a sidewalk in one of Sydney's wealthiest neighbourhoods.
He dawdled for around 20 minutes, "steeling himself for what he was about to do" prosecutors say, before disappearing out of sight. Minutes later another CCTV camera captured the sound of gunshots ringing out.
Former police officer Beau Lamarre, 31, was on Wednesday found guilty of murdering his former partner Jesse Baird, a TV presenter, and Luke Davies, Baird's new partner, with his work-issued gun.
In February 2024, the bloodied belongings of Baird and Davies were found in a skip in the suburb of Cronulla, sparking a frantic search for the couple. It culminated in the discovery of their bodies on a rural property, partially buried beneath sticks and debris.
During a two-month trial filled with at-times extraordinary testimony and a wealth of CCTV evidence, the court heard Lamarre had planned his crime over months.
Jurors watched CCTV of the then police officer purchasing a surfboard bag in which to put Baird's body, after casually sizing it up against a friend in the shop, and heard from a stream of former friends and colleagues who testified that Lamarre asked them for advice about everything from mobile phone signals to where he could buy a gun.
One recounted the chilling moment Lamarre told her that if he were ever to murder someone, he would leave their belongings in a dumpster and hide the body in bushland where they would never be found.
"What would you do if I told you I killed somebody? Would you help me hide the body?" he asked, according to her testimony.
Later, Lamarre asked for help in covering up the killings; his friends - none of whom have been implicated - say they began receiving panicked calls and odd requests from him.
One told the court Lamarre persuaded her to go for a drive in the country, during which he tried to dispose of the bodies at the family farm of a man he'd met on a dating app. Over the speakers, he played Taylor Swift's No Body, No Crime.
Dark infatuation
Baird, 26, and Lamarre - a one-time celebrity blogger who also had his own swimwear line on the side of his police work - met in a nightclub in October 2022. For Lamarre, it quickly became an infatuation, but for Baird, an AFL referee and former Network Ten presenter, the relationship appeared to be casual.
After discovering in April 2023 that Lamarre had gone through messages on his phone, and unfollowed other men on his Instagram account, Baird broke it off.
But Lamarre's obsession only grew. He watched Baird having sex with another man through his bedroom window, broke into his house multiple times, deleting contacts on his phone and turning on location settings so that he could engineer meetings with the TV presenter.
On one occasion Baird woke up to find Lamarre in his bedroom; not realising who it was, he chased him down the street. Lamarre was also convicted of aggravated break and enter over that incident on Wednesday.
Prosecutor Brett Hatfield said Baird was "oblivious to the accused's scheming and manipulations", and the pair briefly rekindled the relationship before Baird ended it definitively in November.
He'd discovered Lamarre had, again, been accessing his phone and had attempted to ward off a love rival by pretending to have received flowers from Baird.
'The beginning of the plan for murder'
His pride wounded, Hatfield said Lamarre's thoughts turned to a "darker place": "revenge".
He asked an old friend if her ex-partner could get him a gun: "This was the beginning of the plan for the murder," the prosecutor said.
In the months leading up to the killings, Lamarre bought a burner phone, a motorcycle helmet, and practised shooting in the woods. On holiday with a friend in Bali in January 2024 he tried to buy a silencer.
While his murder plans percolated, he also began lashing out with petty acts of malice, prosecutors said, such as putting water in the fuel tank of Baird's car.
But at the same time as he plotted, Lamarre was still trying to win his ex back.
On one occasion he pressured a friend to send Baird a 1,600-word message he had himself composed, claiming that he had cancer, and may need to have his arm amputated.
Under questioning in court, he said a naturopath he couldn't remember the name of had diagnosed him with "a form of sporadic cancer".
By the end of 2023, Baird had met and hit it off with Davies, 29. "It was early days but they were both smitten with each other," his mother, Helen Baird, told the court. He sent her a picture of the pair at a Pink concert. "I just sent a big love heart and said 'I'm so happy for both of you'."
Early on the morning of 19 February, Lamarre picked up a hire car he had asked his mother to rent and drove to Baird's home. After waiting several hours for Baird's housemates to leave, while disguised as an UberEats driver, he let himself in with a key he had copied.
Lamarre told the court he had been at Baird's home to pick up some belongings, including a A$25,000 (US$16,500, Â£12,600) Cartier ring, and had disguised himself to avoid the embarrassment of being recognised if anyone was home.
He claimed he let himself in when no one answered the door, and the two men were killed in separate struggles. He argued that Baird had been holding a knife and the first shot was fired to disarm him, and the second by accident. Davies shot himself when he grabbed the gun, Lamarre said.
But prosecutors told the jury Baird was shot in the back and then once again through his temple as he lay gravely wounded on the kitchen floor.
The evidence, Hatfield said, pointed to one thing only: "an execution".
Davies was then shot through a bedroom door as he tried to call emergency services. The court heard the panic in his voice as the seconds-long call was replayed before it dropped out.
Covering tracks
After that, Lamarre set about trying to hide the evidence of what Hatfield called his "brazen and shocking murders".
One of his first actions was to call a friend. He testified Lamarre confessed he had "killed people" and asked him to take a day off work "to help him clean up his mess".
Later Lamarre pretended the call was a joke, saying, "Did you like my Oscar performance? Got you".
Meanwhile, Lamarre went to a DIY store to purchase mops, bleach and bin bags. After returning to Baird's house to clean up, he left the bodies wrapped in surfboard bags and tarpaulins in the courtyard.
Baird had been set to move out that week, and Lamarre used Baird's phone to reassure his housemates he had hired an Airtasker to move his belongings, adding, "I might be flying to Perth tonight".
Baird's housemates quickly spotted the surfboard bags when they returned home, snapping a photograph tendered in court which showed "red liquid" seeping out.
"I thought it was rusted water from the outdoor furniture," one of the women said.
The surfboard bags were gone the next morning.
Lamarre had hired a van, and over the next two days made more trips to the DIY store and a sports shop to buy gumboots, a wheelbarrow, bigger surfboard bags and 10kg weights. He squeezed in a hospital appointment for a minor procedure and paused for a sushi date with a friend, CCTV showing him laughing and eating as the bodies lay in the back of his van.
On 21 February, he picked up another friend in the van for what she thought was lunch.
There were blue tarps in the back and it smelled "lemony", she told the court. She "wasn't allowed to put the windows up or turn the aircon off" as they motored towards Goulburn, 200km southwest of Sydney, stopping only at another DIY store, where he asked her to buy an angle grinder.
His friend told the court she remembers Lamarre playing No Body, No Crime at some point: "It's a very niche song."
Arriving at a farm owned by a Hinge date's family, Lamarre used the angle grinder to cut through a padlock, making his way to a dam where he unsuccessfully tried to sink the bodies.
It was now late at night and he drove his friend, by this time upset and confused, home before returning to retrieve the bodies. He then left them in the van, parked in Sydney overnight, before driving once more to Goulburn, where he dumped the bodies near a monastery.
Later that day he visited another friend, telling her to look up the definition of schizophrenia and asking whether he could use her hose, because "there was a bit of blood he had to scrub off" the hired van, the court heard.
Lamarre faces life in prison
By this time police were closing in, and on 23 February Lamarre went to turn himself in at Bondi police station. On the way, he called a police helpline.
"I've been bullied by multiple New South Wales police force members," he told the woman on the other end of the line. "All this stress and trauma and psychological abuse and trauma - did I say trauma already? - has caused me to not sleep, get anxious, it's caused me to have some symptoms of schizophrenia."
"Actually sorry, you know what, can you just... delete that whole thing. We'll just keep it short and sweet."
He wasn't sure when he'd be back to work, he said. Shortly afterwards he was arrested and charged with murder, and the bodies of Baird and Davies were found four days later.
Lamarre's lawyer John Stratton insisted he'd acted in self-defence and had "panicked" realising how the scene would look to outsiders.
The former police officer did plenty of "foolish" and "unusual" things - "He was, it's fair to say, a poorly behaved boyfriend," Stratton said - but that did not make him a murderer.
Ultimately, however, the jury was not convinced and took just over an hour to convict him. He will be sentenced in March and faces life in prison.
