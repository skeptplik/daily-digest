Drops for Nvidia, Anthropic and other AI stocks drag the markets back down
S&P 500 falls 0.5% to 8th loss in last 9 days
Stocks tied to the artificial intelligence industry dragged the U.S. market lower Tuesday after their surge from the day before quickly fizzled. That's even though much of the rest of Wall Street rose, along with Americans' confidence in the economy.
The S&P 500 fell 0.5 per cent to its eighth loss in the last nine days. Tech stocks once again led the way and yanked the Nasdaq composite down a market-leading 1.5 per cent. Stocks outside of AI held up much better, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 423 points, or 0.9 per cent.
In Toronto, the S&P/TSX composite index slipped 0.35 per cent.
They're the latest swings for a market that's been mostly sinking since hitting its all-time high last month. The worry is that prices for AI-related stocks shot too high into a possible bubble and need to deflate. That carries immense consequences for the broader market and workers' 401(k) accounts because AI companies have grown into Wall Street's most influential stocks.
While the surprise return of Jensen Huang as Nvidia's CEO announced Monday does bring back a charismatic leader who championed the entire AI industry, it does not settle the deep concerns dogging it. For one, doubts remain about whether AI can possibly produce enough profit and productivity to make the massive investments underway in data centres worth it.
At the same time, high interest rates make it more difficult to borrow money to pay for the industry's growth. Sweeping victories for Democrats earlier this month in the U.S. midterm elections, meanwhile, have raised concerns about more regulation for AI, including possible halts to further construction of data centres.
Tech stocks fizzle
To be sure, AI stocks and the broader U.S. market have been prone to extreme reversals since the summer, not only week to week but even hour to hour.
For now, at least, the momentum is back downward.
Nvidia fell 5.9 per cent day after soaring 31.5 per cent to its biggest gain in more than two decades. Other stocks around the AI industry fell by similar amounts.
CoreWeave, which buys Nvidia chips and rents out access to them over the cloud, tumbled 11.8 per cent. GE Vernova, which helps power AI data centres, dropped 4.8 per cent.
And Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI chatbot, sank 6.5 per cent to drop below $83 US. That's how much it first sold its stock for in its initial public offering last week. The stock had briefly jumped as high as $115 US in its first day of trading amid a frenzy of hype.
Such drops were powerful enough to drag the S&P 500 index lower, even though most of the stocks within the index climbed. They rose following the latest report to say confidence among U.S. consumers is improving by more than economists expected.
Confidence jumped sharply for Democrats and Independents following the midterm election results, according to the Conference Board. That narrowed the wide gap between them and Republicans, whose confidence fell slightly.
The results echo a report from the University of Michigan released earlier in the month, which also showed a rise in overall sentiment following the midterm elections and a drop in oil prices from late October.
Consumer stables show strength
Companies in industries across the economy, meanwhile, continue to report strong growth in profits for the summer.
Dollar Tree climbed 11.2 per cent after reporting bigger earnings for the latest quarter than analysts expected. The average shopper spent slightly more in its stores during the three months than they did in last year's third quarter, and more of them came in than last year.
Kohl's rallied 19.5 per cent after reporting a profit for the latest quarter, when analysts were expecting basically zero. It also gave forecasts for revenue for the final three months of its fiscal year that were better than expected.
Businesses in the S&P 500 are on pace to report overall growth in earnings per share of more than 25 f per cent for a third straight quarter, according to FactSet. That's a big deal because stock prices tend to follow the path of corporate profits and interest rates over the long term. But emotions of investors can also overwhelm them in the short term, as the last few weeks have demonstrated.
All told, the S&P 500 fell 36.08 points to 7,201.09. The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 423.39 to 50,018.70, and the Nasdaq composite fell 356.02 to 23,733.54.
In the bond market, U.S. Treasury yields held relatively steady. The yield on the 10-year Treasury rose to 4.93 per cent from 4.92 per cent late Monday after drifting up and down earlier in the day.
Oil prices rose, which tend to push upward on inflation and on bond yields. The price for a barrel of Brent crude oil climbed 2.4 per cent to $88.78 US. It's been yo-yoing between $72 US and $114 US since the United States and Israel attacked Iran in February.
