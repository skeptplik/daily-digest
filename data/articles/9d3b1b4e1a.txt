Getting quality sleep is just as crucial as getting enough sleep. And how you feel in the morning only tells part of the story. Smartwatches, smart bands, smart rings and sleep earbuds can all help fill in the details: how long it took you to doze off, how long you spent in deep sleep, how restless you were, your resting heart rate, even if you have night-time breathing difficulties.
Armed with this information, you can learn how your lifestyle affects your sleep. Maybe your after dinner cappuccino triggers a night of tossing and turning, or you’re more restful after evening workouts. As the wise GI Joe once said, knowing is half the battle. And the more you know about your sleep, the better chance you have of improving it.
Why you should trust me
As a consumer technology journalist for more than two decades, I began wearing fitness trackers in the early 2010s and have since reviewed hundreds of products in the sleep and fitness space. After spending more than 15 years tracking my sleep nightly, I have a deep understanding of how wearables work, which brands and products are reliable and how to best interpret app data.
How I tested
I wore up to five wearables at a time (two wrist devices, two rings, and earbuds) for at least seven nights each. Every morning, I plotted sleep data into a spreadsheet, including duration, sleep stages (light, REM, deep, awake), along with average sleep heart rate and heart rate variability (HRV) when available.
The Oura Ring 5 served as our benchmark for accuracy, since it is widely considered to be among the most accurate consumer wearables for sleep tracking. A study by the Multidisciplinary Digital Publishing Institute (MDPI), for example, found Oura Ring’s data to be virtually identical to a polysomnogram (PSG) for all sleep stage measurements – that’s the gold standard method they use in sleep labs.
I also evaluated each tracker for design (is it comfortable to wear, especially overnight?), battery life (do I have to charge it frequently?), and the companion app (is it confusing or easy to read?).
Overall, I tested 11 wearables, some of which I spent significant time with before while reviewing them individually. Other wearables I have recently evaluated that I am confident would not make the cut or that have been replaced by newer models are listed as well.
I keep certain products on hand for future comparative reviews. Some products were sent specifically for consideration in this piece and were returned to the manufacturer if requested, except earbuds for hygienic reasons.
Best overall sleep tracker: 
Oura Ring 5
Not only is the Oura Ring 5 the sleekest and slimmest smart ring out there, it’s also the most accurate and easiest to live with.
Why we love it: Part gadget, part fashion accessory, the Oura Ring 5 comes in six color options, from traditional black to elegant Deep Rose. The barely-there design is so minimalist you’ll forget you’re even wearing it, yet it accurately captures detailed sleep data, including efficiency, restfulness, latency and timing.
Easy-to-read bar graphs in the app let you know what areas need improvement. Had restless sleep? Maybe you need to refrain from consuming liquids before bed to avoid nightly trips to the bathroom. Do you take too long to fall asleep? Look into softer lighting, cooler temperatures, white noise or less screen time right before bed. I love the “sleep debt” figure that continuously calculates the discrepancy between how much sleep you got v how much you need. It motivated me to make better decisions to close the gap.
If the info from the app isn’t enough, the Oura Advisor AI assistant is like having your own personal (virtual) sleep coach. Ask it questions about your sleep, such as how to improve, or even ideas for a good before-bed snack.
It’s a shame that … You need a $6 monthly subscription to benefit from all the great features of Oura Ring 5. And even though the battery lasts for about six to nine days, some competitors last longer. Plus, it only comes with a wired charging dock – you need to buy the wireless charging case separately.
Sizes: 6-13
Finish options: Silver, Brushed Silver, Gold, Deep Rose, Black, Stealth
Material: Full titanium
Battery life: Six to nine days
Subscription: Required for all meaningful data, $6 monthly or $70 annually
Best budget:
Fitbit Air
This incredibly thin wristband is inexpensive and free of distractions, but also impressively accurate.
Why we love it: With no screen, the Fitbit Air doesn’t bombard you with notifications and data; it just works passively in the background. When you want to know more – about sleep, steps, heart rate – open the app, sync and explore. The sensors sit on the inside of a thin strap that is incredibly comfortable to wear all day and overnight. It comes in fabric or metal mesh, but Google also offers a silicone accessory band.
The Fitbit Air’s data matched the data from our benchmark Oura Ring 5, closer than any of the other device I tested. This includes total sleep, deep sleep, HRV and near-identical heart rate readings throughout the testing period. The only big discrepancy came from awake time, where it deviated from the Oura by about 40 minutes on average. For example, one night, the Oura Ring 5 logged 58 minutes of awake time, while Fitbit Air only counted 16 awake minutes.
It’s a shame that … You need a $10 monthly subscription to Google Health Premium to unlock detailed sleep analysis and an AI coach, but it does provide enough value to justify it. Battery life of seven days is decent, but it’s not as good as other screenless models.
Sizes: One size (130-210mm wrist size for textile/polyurethane), 130-175 or 165-210 mm (silicone)
Finish options: Obsidian, Berry, Lavender, Fog
Material: Recycled polycarbonate and PBT plastics, silicone
Battery life: Up to seven days
Subscription: Google Health Premium, $10 monthly or $100 annually (optional)
Best for iPhone users: 
Apple Watch Series 12
The Apple Watch may be best known for flashy apps, fitness tracking and endlessly customizable bands, but it also makes a great sleep tracker.
Why we love it: The newest Apple Watch looks similar to the last few generations, but it now comes in ceramic, titanium or scratch-resistant aluminum, along with new finishes, including the lovely Dark Bronze of my review sample. I found it comfortable to wear overnight, and the data is easy to read from the watch face and the Health app.
After many years of wearing different Apple Watch models, I have noticed they often overstate deep sleep and understate awake time. This remains true for the newest model, but it comes pretty close to the Oura in tracking other sleep data, with the smallest differential for sleep duration, stages, sleep heart rate and HRV. It also had the closest REM readings to Oura of any device.
Beneath the basic stats, you can also dive deeper and compare your average sleep for the year to date with the year prior, providing value for longtime Apple Watch wearers. Like only a few peers, the Apple Watch can detect the breathing anomalies that align with sleep apnea. It’s not an official test – but it provides enough info to help you decide whether to consult a medical professional.
Besides tracking sleep, the Apple Watch is also a perfect iPhone companion for notifications, fitness tracking, Siri AI voice assistance, and with Apple’s new Health Sensing System, accurate heart-rate tracking.
It’s a shame that … The battery only lasts 24 hours per charge. You also need an iPhone, so Android owners are out of luck.
Sizes: 42mm, 46mm
Finish options: Dark Bronze, Light Gold, Black, Space Grey (aluminum), Radiant Gold, Natural (titanium), Pearl White, Night Blue (ceramic)
Material: Aluminum, titanium, ceramic
Battery life: Up to 24 hours (38 hours in Low Power Mode)
Subscription: None
Best for Android users: 
Google Pixel Watch 5
One of the most beautiful Android smartwatches you can buy is also an excellent tool to collect accurate sleep data and get AI-based guidance for adopting better sleep habits.
Why we love it: This comfortable-to-wear smartwatch for Android owners does a fairly good job of calculating sleep and stages, and especially HRV. You can also see how various factors such as restlessness and time-to-sleep compare with the typical range for people of your same age and gender. Its biggest weakness is inconsistent REM times, which were sometimes way above or way below, and other times spot on.
You need a Google Health Premium subscription ($10 monthly) to unlock sleep breathing trends, and AI Coach to ask questions about your sleep. But you get most of the core sleep metrics for free, including sleep duration, stages, score, restlessness, heart rate, and breathing quality. Since you can log meals, stress, alcohol or caffeine consumption and more, AI Coach can pick up on potential triggers for poor sleep, like having coffee too close to bedtime, and bring them to your attention.
Like the Apple Watch, the Pixel Watch 5 puts all sorts of other features on your wrist, from notifications to controlling smart home devices, and even answering questions via Gemini voice assistance. If you want those bells and whistles, it’s worth getting.
It’s a shame that … It isn’t a huge step up from the Pixel Watch 4. The 40-hour battery life means you need to charge it every day and a half. And you need a Google Health Premium subscription to unlock deeper sleep analysis. It also does not natively work with the iPhone.
Sizes: 41mm, 45mm
Finish options: Matte Black, Polished Silver, Satin Pyrite, Champagne Gold
Material: Aluminum
Battery life: Up to 30 hours, 48 hours in Battery Saver mode (41mm), up to 40 hours, 72 hours in Battery Saver Mode (46mm)
Subscription: Google Health Premium, $10 monthly or $100 annually (optional)
Best for no subscription: 
Garmin Cirqa
If you hate the idea of paying a monthly fee, the Garmin Cirqa works great without a subscription and also excels at tracking workouts.
Why we love it: The Cirqa offers accurate sleep tracking, activity tracking and vitals for one low price of entry. Garmin does technically offer a subscription service called Connect+, but it’s not worth it for most people and doesn’t offer any advancements in sleep tracking.
While thicker and wider than others, wearing the Garmin Cirqa is still comfortable. The battery lasts up to 10 days, and the two band sizes can accommodate wrists of any size.
As for accuracy, this band tracked closely with the Oura on light sleep, and fairly close on deep sleep, REM and HRV. Like the Fitbit Air, awake times are its biggest weakness, as it consistently recorded significantly less awake time.
Along with providing data incuding average SpO2 (blood oxygen saturation) while sleeping and number of restless moments, the Cirqa provides sleep guidance in the app. It might say you need more sleep after a short night, for example, and nudge you to get to bed at a specific time tonight to make up for it.
It also tracks whether the midpoint of your sleep was behind, ahead of or at your usual internal rhythm. This helps determine your circadian rhythm, and whether you’re a morning person or a night person. It can help shift workers and other people with inconsistent schedules track how this affects their natural rhythm.
It’s a shame that … It’s not smaller and more fashionable. Since it’s among the clunkier bands, it looks out of place on daintier wrists.
Sizes: S-M (120-200mm), L-XL (145-240mm)
Finish options: French Grey, Captain Blue, Mauve, Black
Material: Fabric ComfortFit band
Battery life: Up to 10 days
Subscription: Garmin Connect+, $6.99/month, $69.99/year (optional, not necessary for sleep)
Best for battery life:
RingConn Gen 3
You can’t go wrong with two weeks of battery life per charge, a nicely laid-out app, no subscription and a reasonable price.
Why we love it: While the RingConn Gen 3 isn’t quite as slim as the Oura Ring 5, it’s one of the thinner, nicer-looking smart rings I have tested. The 14-day battery life is a dream – and there’s also a handy wireless charging case included. It tracked close to the Oura Ring’s data for total sleep, while deep sleep, REM, heart rate and HRV were all very close as well.
Its biggest issue is severely under-reporting awake time, mistaking this for light sleep. One night, the Oura Ring 5 said I spent 80 minutes awake (not a great night’s sleep!) while the RingConn Gen 3 thought I was only awake for 12 minutes. That said, total duration and heart-rate metrics are on point.
The app shows data including sleep efficiency, skin temperature, sleep goal gap (similar to Oura’s sleep debt) and even sleep stability, which incorporates the number of times you wake up. Like the Apple Watch, there’s also sleep apnea detection to check for breathing abnormalities consistent with the condition.
It’s a shame that … It can vibrate to alert you of low battery or if you have been sitting too long, but it doesn’t work as a wake-up alarm. It’s a missed opportunity.
Sizes: 6-15
Finish options: Future Silver, Royal Gold, Matte Black, Brushed Silver, Brushed Rose Gold
Material: Titanium epoxy resin
Battery life: Up to 14 days
Subscription: None
The best of the rest
Best premium: 
Whoop MG
The Whoop MG looks and feels a cut above the rest. It has a comfortable and premium band, up to 14-day battery life, and the coolest charging system I’ve seen. The Wireless Powerpack slides on to the sensor and band to charge while you’re still wearing it, so you never have to take it off. It takes about 90 minutes to fully charge, so you can get it done before bed.
The main downside is the expensive annual membership of $359. It’s worth it for serious athletes or those who prioritize advanced features such as ECG and blood pressure insights, but probably too much data for casual users.
As far as sleep tracking accuracy, it aligned with the Oura on sleep duration, but deviated by quite a bit for light, deep, REM and awake times. It consistently overstates deep sleep, a crucial metric for recovery. However, it handles other physiological measurements including heart rate and HRV much more accurately.
Whoop supplies loads of supplemental information, such as a percentage representation of hours slept v hours needed, sleep consistency, efficiency and sleep stress. You can even see the number of times you woke up throughout the night and review weekly trends graphed out for making sense of the data.
It didn’t make the final cut because … $359 a year is too much to pay, if you’re only interested in sleep tracking.
Sizes: 9.5in
Battery life: 14 or more days
Subscription: Whoop Life, $359/year (mandatory)
Best for travelers: 
Ultrahuman Ring Pro
The notably beefy Ultrahuman Ring Pro is still comfortable to wear during sleep, but the thickness stands out on longer, thinner fingers like mine. The charging case is lovely, however, and it can hold up to 45 days’ worth of extra charges. Together with the 15-day battery life of the band, that makes the Ultrahuman a fuss-free option for travelers.
Aside from consistently higher HRV numbers, the Ultrahuman Ring Pro did a decent job across the board for sleep tracking. One neat feature is the ability to tag sleep reports with insights on sleep quality including “hot bedroom”, “big day” or write your own. The smart alarm feature can wake you in your lightest sleep stage, to ensure you arise feeling refreshed.
It didn’t make the final cut because … it’s thicker and more expensive than the comparable RingConn 3.
Sizes: 5-14
Battery life: Up to 15 days
Subscription: Ultrahuman Max optional, $10 monthly or $100 annually; some PowerPlug app add-ons are extra
An affordable option with no subscription: 
Luna Band
While it’s as comfortable as others, the Luna Band feels a little cheap. But overall sleep metrics are what is most important, and they were generally in line with Oura Ring. In fact, Luna Band had the closest sleep heart rate readings of any device tested. But HRV was oddly inconsistent.
The app, which is constantly improving with updates, also reports latency, toss and turns, circadian midpoint, respiration rate and skin temperature. One downside is that the band is frustratingly slow to sync to the app, so it might measure your patience along with your sleep.
With decent 10-day battery life, no subscription and new features always being added (sleep alarm is listed as coming soon), Luna Band is a decent and affordable alternative to the bigger brands.
It didn’t make the final cut because … the Fitbit Air is even more affordable, but feels more premium.
Sizes
 : M (240mm), L (295mm)
Battery life: Up to 10 days
Subscription: None
What about the sleep buds?
You may have noticed that no sleep earbuds I tested made the cut. If your goal is primarily to accurately track sleep, they’re not the best option compared with bands, smartwatches and rings. They do not capture as much data, and what they do capture is less accurate. That said, if you view them primarily as sleep aids with tracking as a bonus, or don’t like sleeping with something on your wrist or finger, they’re worth looking into.
Anker Soundcore Sleep A30
These buds recorded much longer sleep duration than others, and do not record REM nor heart rate data. They’re not the most accurate nor consistent for sleep tracking, but they have some interesting sleep-related features.
Active noise cancellation (ANC) can quiet ambient sounds, such as a snoring partner or outside traffic noise. And if snoring or other disruptions are detected while you slumber, the noise-masking feature can turn out automatically. You can play calming stories or white noise right from the app. In Bluetooth mode, they can function like regular daytime earbuds, too.
They are comfortable and generally remain secure, though one did fall out one night when I was tossing and turning. The included charging case keeps the batteries topped off for multiple nights, and the app is nicely laid out. Reporting sleep movements is a highlight. See how many times you rolled over and a breakdown of how long you spent in left, right, back and prone (flat on your stomach with head turned) sleep positions. These buds are a good choice if you have a snoring partner or want to know how much you move during sleep.
Ozlo SleepBuds 2
The Ozlo SleepBuds 2 often overstated light sleep. There is no heart rate data, but they analyze room lighting and ambient noise levels overnight. It is slow to sync to the app in the mornings, which can be frustrating. They plug your ears well enough to soften some external noise, though there’s no ANC. And as a nice bonus, they never fell out, no matter how much I tossed and turned.
The strongest aspect of these buds is the more than 50 sleep sounds and the private in-ear alarm. Play the calming audio all night or long enough to help lull you to sleep. They last up to 14 hours and the charging case will keep them going for about three nights. These are a good choice if white noise helps you sleep, and you get decent sleep duration and stage reporting.
