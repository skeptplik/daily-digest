Nvidia’s board is set to decide the future of its founder Jensen Huang after revelations that he had a romantic relationship with a company executive, days after it emerged that he had an affair with a suspected Chinese spy.
The board held urgent talks on Wednesday night after news broke of the Nvidia chief executive’s relationship with Kimberly Powell, the chipmaker’s vice-president of healthcare, according to people familiar with the matter.
The relationship with an Nvidia employee deepens an already damaging scandal for the $4.2 trillion semiconductor group. Huang on Sunday admitted to having an affair, two days after the FT reported his romantic links to an executive at an Nvidia client, who US officials believe is a Chinese intelligence asset.
Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Powell, who has worked at Nvidia for more than 18 years and is among its more prominent public figures, could not be reached for comment.
The board, which unusually has no chair in a testament to Huang’s singular role at Nvidia, this week hired a former federal prosecutor to investigate his affair with the suspected spy. But the board has thus far backed Huang to remain as chief executive.
The directors could opt to have Huang step back from his duties as CEO while the investigation proceeds and the board intensifies succession planning. Or they could now push for his immediate departure.
Nvidia’s annual reports and corporate governance paperwork have until now included no specific reference to “succession planning” and set out no details for how it would replace its CEO, even in an emergency.
