PRESS REVIEW – Monday, November 23: Papers react to the breakdown of the COP31 climate talks. Israeli paper Haaretz reports on the struggles of crossing the Qalandiyah checkpoint. The New York Times talks about the mathematical "formula to stop the apocalypse". Finally, the Bond universe keeps making headlines.
"The Fossilised COP" reads the headline of the Catholic paper La Croix. An editorial in the same paper calls out the "stubbornness of dinosaurs" sticking to fossil fuels. Les Echos writes "COP31 turns into a fiasco". British paper Metro has another catchy headline: "Complete COP-out: It's all hot air as climate talks collapse". An analysis piece in the Guardian writes that COP31 failed, but in "the real world, the transition away from fossil fuels is inevitable". The Guardian interviewed a Brazilian Indigenous leader who said that the summit failed because leaders "refused to name the issue at the core of climate change: fossil fuels". An opinion piece by Der Spiegel says that "Oil-producing nations are afraid and the Europeans are making excuses". It says that renewables are posing a real threat to fossil fuels and those who make money out of them. The only option left for these businesses is to delay the process – "the longer, the better". The article says that the outcome is sobering: "Climate diplomacy is not dead, but it has reached a new low".
We turn next to Jerusalem, where the Qalandiyah checkpoint crossing has become "unbearable" for Palestinians. Israeli left-wing daily Haaretz reports that residents who work in the neighborhood of Kafr Aqab are forced to wake up before dawn and wait for hours to cross the checkpoint. About 100,000 people live in the neighbourhood and their jobs are across the checkpoint. The situation has been an ordeal since the outbreak of the war in Gaza, but it has worsened in recent weeks and nobody knows why.
Next, The New York Times tells the story of Mike Winer, a mathematician trying to solve "the last meaningful math problem of his career". This is about "how to make sure artificial intelligence behaves in ways humans want, before it becomes too powerful to control". At a conference, Winer warned his colleagues that AI is progressing much faster than we thought and "it could be heading towards a catastrophe". Most of the scientists he works with have concluded that "humans are living through their final months or years as the most intelligent beings on Earth". He is now searching for "the formula to stop the apocalypse".
Finally, British paper UnHerd says that "James Bond and GTA prove that Britain still has soft power". The Wall Street Journal writes that "James Bond's bare wrists set off watch industry war". The British spy has worn an Omega watch for three decades, but Omega's owner Swatch Group is struggling, so Pattinson might be swapping it for another brand: Jaeger-LeCoultre. The Independent, meanwhile, is interested in who is going to be the next Bond girl.
