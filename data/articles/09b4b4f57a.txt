Helsinki – Mao Shimada led all the way to claim her second win of the season at the Finlandia Trophy Grand Prix figure skating event in Helsinki on Saturday.
Shimada, a four-time world junior champion, secured the second win of her debut season after taking gold at Skate Canada earlier this month to book her ticket to the Grand Prix Final in China next month.
The 18-year-old’s free skate performance to “Waloyo Yamoni” by Christopher Tin earned her a score of 150.43 points for an overall total of 229.49.
European champion Niina Petrokina of Estonia, who was in second place after the short program, held onto her spot to finish 15.43 behind Shimada. American Isabeau Levito moved up from sixth to take bronze.
In the men’s event, Estonia’s Mihhail Selevko held his overnight lead after the short program to win gold with 254.99 points.
Sweden’s Andreas Nordeback took silver (240.94) and Kazuki Tomono (237.75) earned bronze.
France’s Adam Siao Him Fa had been 12th after the short program but moved up to fourth overall after scoring second highest in the free skate. The two-time European champion had won the season-opener at home in Angers.
However, ankle inflammation has since disrupted his preparation.
Germany’s Annika Hocke and Robert Kunkel won the pairs event, pushing Canada’s Lia Pereira and Trennt Michaud into second place.
The Canadians were in the lead after the short program but scored third highest on the free skate to finish just 1.12 points behind the Germans.
Olympic champions Guillaume Cizeron and Laurence Fournier Beaudry won the ice dance category, their second success of the season.
Also European and world champions, Cizeron and Fournier Beaudry began their season a month ago with a promising win in Angers.
The French pair finished with 212.91 points, 22 ahead of Finnish runners-up Juulia Turkkila and Matthias Versluis. Third place went to Americans Oona and Gage Brown.
They took the lead in the competition following the rhythm dance with a flamenco set to music by Spanish star Rosalia.
The Finlandia Trophy is the fifth of six Grand Prix events with the last being the NHK Trophy in Tokyo next week.
The top six in each discipline qualify for the Grand Prix Final in China next month, with Cizeron and Fournier Beaudry assured of a place following their win on Saturday.
The competition in Helsinki is taking place without Russian skaters who were forced to withdraw following the Finnish government’s decision not to grant them visas due to the war in Ukraine.
