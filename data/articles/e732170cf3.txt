A Moscow court on Wednesday ordered Murmansk region Governor Andrei Chibis to be held in pre-trial detention for two months on charges of embezzlement and bribery on a particularly large scale.
Chibis is the first sitting governor to be arrested since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. He has served as governor of the Arctic region since 2019 and survived a knife attack in 2024.
A judge at Moscow’s Meshchansky District Court dropped earlier charges of fraud and illegal currency transactions mentioned by Russia’s Interior Ministry in a press release on Chibis’ detention earlier this week, his defense lawyer Ruslan Zakalyuzhny told the RBC news outlet.
“I don’t agree with the accusation,” Chibis said via Zakalyuzhny. “I’m confident that both during the investigation and in court I’ll be able to prove my innocence.”
Chibis was reportedly arrested at Moscow’s Sheremetyevo International Airport after arriving in the Russian capital for a work trip. His wife, Yevgenia Chibis, called the accusations against him “absurd” in a social media post.
According to RBC, the case against Chibis involves at least 10 defendants and centers on the alleged embezzlement of more than 39 million rubles ($472,500) in regional construction funds and 1 billion rubles ($12.1 million) in transfers tied to companies in Kyrgyzstan, as well as state contracts involving roads and construction.
Murmansk’s Deputy Governor Nadezhda Aksyonova will reportedly serve as acting governor. Later on Wednesday evening, President Vladimir Putin officially relieved Chibis of his duties.
