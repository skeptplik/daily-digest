With its smell of fresh-cut wood and après-ski, Italian Alps motif, a “Loft Lodge” holiday pop-up opens Thursday on 49th Street off Fifth Avenue, opposite Saks Fifth Avenue.
It’s Loft’s first pop-up and an attempt by the women’s specialty brand to transcend the cacophony of Christmas promoting, and create some buzz as Black Friday nears.
The 2,800-square-foot Loft Lodge, situated on the site of a former Vince store, will only operate through Sunday. But Loft executives believe even in that short time it will help put Loft top-of-mind among holiday shoppers for the season.
“There’s no better place than our hometown of New York City to launch such a special experience,” Loft Brand president Erin Landon said in an exclusive interview. “We set out to create an immersive physical extension of our winter philosophy. We wanted to capture the nostalgic warmth and après ski-like magic of the season.”
In addition, “The location itself creates this high-traffic, customer acquisition opportunity,” Landon said.
The brand president has a positive outlook for holiday, which this year is filled with conflicting dynamics. Surprisingly, there’s been many positive retail performances this year, largely fueled by more affluent shoppers, even as the vast majority of Americans are feeling increasingly pinched and stressed out by rising food, gas, housing and health costs. They’re likely to spend more on holiday gifts and end up with fewer gifts than last year to put under the Christmas tree.
Landon suggested that business at Loft is proceeding well, though the company does not provide specifics on selling results. “We’re seeing gifting categories trending and that’s a good indicator for us,” she said. “So the (categories) we need to win with, from a business perspective — sweaters, cold weather, gifting — is where we’re seeing encouraging signs. Gifting categories for us typically (go) beyond sweaters, which is a large business. But there are also sweat sets, and our Lou & Gray department is our number-two category. We’ve been seeing double-digit comp trajectories on a consistent basis in sweat sets, fleece, cozy knits, and outerwear is also doing really well for us right now — coats, ski jackets and puffers.” Lou & Gray, a subbrand of Loft, is focused on cozy and tactile lounge, sleepwear and sweats for the indoors. Loft is part of the Sycamore Partners-owned Knitwell Group which also includes Ann Taylor, Chico’s, Talbots, Haven Well Within, Lane Bryant, Soma and WHBM.
Landon also pointed to the “gifting tree” by the cash wrap at the Loft Lodge. It features impulse items recently introduced primarily for the holiday season, and it’s a top-performing category serving as a UPT (units per transaction) builder, she said. “It’s been something that we’ve been slowly building over the past two years, but we’ve seen a lot of growth during the November into December period.” She said the gifting tree is merchandised very thoughtfully, including matching socks, gloves and scarves. Ten different categories of merchandise are shown, including socks, candles and jewelry, as well as ornaments which are exclusive to the pop-up. Prices on the gifting tree range from $10 to $34.
Regarding overall pricing this holiday season versus last year’s, Landon said, “It’s slightly up for us, but pretty flat.”
The Loft Lodge pop-up is distinct from Loft’s other stores because, as Landon said, it’s “experiential,” offering embroidery, monogramming, an Instagramable ski lift photo moment, free Bluestone Lane hot chocolate which will be distributed outdoors on the corner of Fifth and 49th Street, as well as some exclusive items. A celebrity (whose identity is being kept secret) is expected to appear at the pop-up on Friday.
Despite operating for only four days, there’s a comprehensive marketing campaign to promote the Loft Lodge, involving city buses wrapped in Loft Lodge ads, social media, paid media including YouTube, and influencers including ShopMy gifting outreach. There’s also a sweepstakes for a trip for two to a luxury lodge of choice, “elevated” gifting at concierges at 11 hotels, and street teams will be roving Rockefeller Center to get the word out.
Asked why the pop-up is just for four days, Bill Miller, Loft’s chief marketing officer, said, “We really wanted this to not take away from our Black Friday moment with our stores here in the city. This is really more experiential. It’s a very focused, edited collection. We actually have a special limited-edition collection that you only can purchase here,” under the label Loft Lodge X New York City. It’s a capsule line of five sweatshirts, two mugs, two tote bags and ornaments.
Roughly, 10 percent of the Loft Lodge assortment is unique to the shop, and about 90 percent is sold through the Loft chain. Miller said the capsule line isn’t regarded as a collection but more as “a memory… We want her to come in, shop this, and leave taking back with her the memory of Loft Lodge in New York.” In addition to the exclusive merchandise, the pop-up reintroduces several recently sold-out items like the “Merry” sweater, on sale for $35.98, from the original $79.95 price.
Wood paneling with a vintage sled, a pair of snowshoes, a stack of logs by the cash wrap, and snow-covered pine trees, evoke a cabin in the woods wintry yet comforting setting.
Miller said 49th Street by Fifth Avenue for Loft Lodge was selected because, “This is Loft’s very first pop-up so we wanted to be here in the heart of New York City (where Loft is based and started operations, though the first Loft store, originally marketed as an Ann Taylor Loft, launched in 1998 at Great South Bay on Long Island.)” Loft is considered more casual and priced less than Ann Taylor. Currently, Loft operates 494 stores in the U.S.
With Loft Lodge, “We wanted to create a different experience for our customer and new customers — an immersive moment,” said Miller. “This really harkens to nostalgia. It’s a big trend happening right now. We want to play in that relevancy space.”
