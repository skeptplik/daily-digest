Pope Leo XIV was struck in the eye by what appeared to be a rosary as his popemobile made its way through crowds of onlookers in Peru on Monday.
Video footage showed the 71-year-old pontiff rubbing his eye after the object was thrown towards him before a farewell mass at Las Palmas air base.
Chicago-born Leo was able to continue with his schedule and appeared to be uninjured.
"My journey is coming to an end, but the mission continues," Leo later told the crowd.
"At the conclusion of my Apostolic Journey, I too wish to confirm you in faith and charity and assure you that I will always carry you with me. Yes, the Peruvian people are my people too!" he added.
Leo's tour of South America has also seen him visit Uruguay and Argentina and came just weeks after he underwent lung cancer surgery in Rome.
The Vatican said in October that the pope's "lower lobe of the right lung was removed" during the procedure, while also confirming a diagnosis of a "pulmonary adenocarcinoma, as suspected from the pre-operative tests."
Doctors later said a chest X-ray had shown "good recovery" of the affected lung, but the operation had nevertheless cast some doubt over Leo's South America trip.
His performance on the tour will have done much to ease fears about his recovery.
Leo was also able to return to his former hometown of Chiclayo during the trip, presiding over an open-air mass for around 500,000 people in the northern Peruvian city. He previously worked as a missionary and then bishop in Peru and holds Peruvian citizenship.
