In short:
Jannik Sinner has undergone knee surgery and his Australian Open comeback has all but been scrapped.
The two-time Melbourne Park winner hasn't played since claiming the Wimbledon title in July.
What's next?
The Australian Open is set to begin on January 17.
Jannik Sinner's planned Melbourne comeback is in considerable doubt, with the former world number one going back to the operating table for more surgery.
The two-time Australian Open winner had targeted the major for his big-time comeback after nearly six months out with a right knee injury.
Sinner last played in July at Wimbledon, where he won his fifth grand slam title. During his absence he has lost his world number one status to Alexander Zverev.
"Over the past few months, together with my team we've tried everything to ‌treat my injury without surgery," Sinner said in ‌a statement after the operation on Tuesday.
"After some very encouraging progress early on, my knee unfortunately didn't respond as we'd all hoped, that's ‌why today I underwent a minor surgery to improve the condition of my iliotibial band."
The thick band of connective tissue runs along the outside of the leg from the hip to the top of the shinbone. It stabilises the knee when functioning properly but can cause irritation when too tight, or when associated muscles are weak.
The winner in Melbourne in 2024 and 2025, Sinner said a fortnight ago: "The aim is to go to Australia."
But he now could be sidelined for months. The 2027 Australian Open main draw begins on January 17.
Sinner ‌said recently that he had lost a significant amount of weight after having his tonsils removed and was in the process of rebuilding muscle.
"I have to respect the protocols because if we rush things we could have more serious problems," he said earlier this month.
"I have a ‌lot of faith ‌in the process, and I'm working toward that objective.
"Initially, we had an impossible goal for my return in mind, so we took a different path. I'm a tennis player, not a doctor, so my only option is to trust them.
"I miss playing tennis. Right now, however, it is crucial that I take care of my body, because I want to keep playing for another 10 to 15 years."
