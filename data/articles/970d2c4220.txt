A Russian attack struck an apartment building in a village near Ukraine’s capital on Sunday (November 22, 2026), killing at least two people and wounding four others, authorities said. Nearly a dozen people were feared trapped beneath the rubble.
The morning strike destroyed the five-storey building in Dymer, which is north of Kyiv. Rescuers have evacuated nine people, including a child, authorities said, while another child was hospitalised with injuries. Search and rescue teams worked to find other survivors. Ukraine’s State Emergency Service said 11 people were believed to be trapped.
“When (the explosion came), I literally flipped over in bed,” said Mykola Osadnyi, a relative of some of those trapped. “I live about 200 metres from here. Then we ran over here.” “My father-in-law … was screaming, ‘Save me, I’m burning!’” Mr. Osadnyi said.
“And now we’re just waiting again to see what happens.” Another resident, Olena Kharkavliuk, recounted that “a terrifying sound woke us up, and then there was an explosion”. “My husband and I have been waiting here since about 4 am, because my older daughter’s classmate and her mother are in there,” Ms. Kharkavliuk told AP.
Russia reports air alert in Kaliningrad
Meanwhile, a rare air alert sounded in Kaliningrad, a Russian exclave near Poland and Lithuania, according to the local Governor, as claims circulated online of drone activity near a major naval base there.
The alert late on Saturday (November 21, 2026) came hours after a drone strike in the Gulf of Finland set fire to a Russian cargo ship bound for the region, a key base for Russia’s Baltic Fleet. Finnish emergency services were still working to extinguish the blaze on Sunday (November 22, 2026).
Photos and video posted online show drone activity around the city of Baltiysk, where much of the fleet is stationed, but AP could not immediately verify their authenticity. According to local media, air raid sirens were heard in Kaliningrad for the first time since Russia’s all-out invasion of Ukraine on February 24, 2022.
Governor Alexei Bezprozvannykh said “a suspicious object coming from the direction of Lithuania” triggered the alert in the Nemansky district near the Lithuanian border, about 170 km north-east of Baltiysk.
On Russian Telegram channels, posts by users saying they were Baltiysk residents described air defences working there during the night, with photos and videos of bright streaks against the night sky.
Kaliningrad authorities on Sunday (November 22, 2026) denounced as fake one photo, supposedly from Baltiysk, showing clouds of smoke rising into the night sky, telling Russian State media that it “had nothing to do with the region.” As of Sunday morning (November 22, 2026), Russian authorities reported no casualties or damage in Kaliningrad.
Lithuania denies involvement
Lithuanian Foreign Minister Kestutis Budrys wrote on X that Lithuania “categorically rejects false claims” regarding the country’s involvement in a drone attack against the region.
“The Republic of Lithuania has never authorised the use of its airspace for attacks against targets in the Russian Federation, including in the present case,” he said. Lithuania’s authorities also said they had not recorded any drones or similar objects flying through the country’s airspace the previous night.
Finnish rescuers battle fire aboard Russian cargo ship
Emergency services tried to extinguish the fire aboard the Russian cargo ship Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland on Sunday morning (November 22, 2026), authorities in Finland said. The ship drifted into Finland’s territorial waters following the attack, the Finnish Border Guard said. The contents of its cargo remain unknown.
A Border Guard representative told the Finnish broadcaster Yle that the crew had been transferred to a Russian rescue ship, and that once the fire is extinguished, the vessel would be moved out of Finland’s waters.
Both Finland and Russia have suggested that the drones that struck the ship were Ukrainian. As of Sunday morning (November 22, 2026), Ukrainian officials had not provided any comment.
