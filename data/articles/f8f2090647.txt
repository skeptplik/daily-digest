Pick 4: Arki Butler - Richmond
An electric and damaging small forward who can change a game in a quarter. Can be a weapon in stoppages but will need to build tank if midfield minutes are to become an option. Highly competitive and a little bit of a pest, Butler is set to be a forward-50 star.

The Verdict: Cody Walker to Carlton
The son of Andrew is now officially a Carlton player, though he is been one at heart for many years before tonight. Walker will not need to be introduced to anyone at the Blues next week because he was ever-present at Princes Park in 2026.
Walker is an electric midfielder, as tough and competitive as they come. Kicking needs some work, but it'll improve. If it was a little better at this point he probably would have gone at pick one, because he's got everything else going for him already. Another long-term piece for Josh Fraser's engine room, which is growing stronger by the year.

The Verdict: Ethan Drever to Essendon
The Bombers have tried to keep a bit of suspense around this pick for the past couple of months, but it has become increasingly clear as the draft approached that Drever was the man.
So he should be. He had arguably the strongest season of any of the top pick contenders, his blend of speed and skill and athleticism making him an offensive weapon in the midfield. But I also love his hands in close and his ability to use his height and composure at stoppages. Robey, Sharp, Drever. You've got a bit of everything there, and on paper by 2030 it looks pretty complete.

Pick 2: Ethan Drever - Essendon
An electric, hard-running midfielder whose trademark has become the explosive burst out of contest and into goal. Viewed as a future leader, Drever has excellent hands in the contest and is extremely damaging in space. At a good height, he is the prototype modern midfielder.

The Verdict: Dougie Cochrane to Port Adelaide
Absolutely no surprises here whatsoever. This was effectively guaranteed from the moment Port Adelaide gained access to Cochrane via their NGA at the end of last season.
He has been the number one pick elect for at least two years, and even though he hardly played a game this season due to injury, Cochrane never lost that status. He is big, he is quick, he is fit, he can kick, he can mark, he boots goals, he takes intercept marks, he sticks tackles. He does all of it, and he can do it pretty much anywhere on the field.
If he's fit, he'll play round one in 2027. And many, many more games beyond that.

The big questions to answer tonight
Hello folks, I'll be joining forces with Chris tonight to chuck my two cents in about these picks and players as it all unfolds. I reckon this draft is an absolute lottery from about pick 10 onwards, so here are a few questions to keep in mind before it all kicks off?
- Where does Harry van Hattum go? Sounds like Richmond's second pick is the likely landing spot, but if he isn't taken there this prodigious ruck talent could slip all the way out of the top 10. Which would be mad.
- When are the bids coming? The Cochrane bid will come at pick one, but what about Cody Walker? Ethan Matthews? Caylen Murray? A surprise or two late in the night?
- What about the trades? We think GWS will get Port's pick 12. We think Adelaide will get up the order somehow. We think Collingwood and Sydney might be up to something. The order posted above will shift dramatically tonight.
- Who is the slider and who is the bolter? There will surely be one player taken tonight that nobody saw coming, and one that will dip far below the position experts had them.
