Feijóo dared Sánchez to give up his seat and his legal protection if he loses. The prime minister declined.
One pair acted as if they were perfectly in sync, while the other behaved as if they did not know each other. The leaders of the two right-wing parties chose to ignore each other completely in a final election debate that reflected a reality in which, apparently, all four candidates to become Spain's prime minister feel comfortable: in the elections on 29 November, what really seems to be on offer is two different models of the country.
The four-way showdown began with Ada Colau, the leader of Frente Amplio, wearing an LGBT pin on her lapel. The former mayor of Barcelona used her opening remarks to ask her rivals to condemn killings linked to gender-based violence. The International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women was marked on Wednesday. Only Santiago Abascal, the leader of the far-right Vox party, ignored her request.
Buoyed by the previous debate with Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, which the media consensus said he had won, a visibly self-assured Alberto Núñez Feijóo even dared to nod to the meme culture of millennials, the most progressive demographic group in the population pyramid. The candidate of the conservative People's Party (PP) made references to the Luna Rossa pizzeria and used a phrase that would make any Gen Z voter cringe: "Amiga, date cuenta" (roughly, "Girl, wake up").
Feijóo had one main challenge in this debate: to avoid mistakes after his earlier win, in a campaign in which the polls, as in 2023, make him the favourite. One exchange in the public services segment did not help him. Colau accused him of hypocrisy for not demanding the resignation of Carlos Mazón, the former head of the Valencia regional government, who was widely criticised over his handling of the deadly floods of October 2024. She also criticised Feijóo for not asking Mazón to give up his status as an aforado, an office-holder who can only be tried by a higher court.
"Good management is taking charge in a national emergency, not running away from Paiporta while you stay quiet," Feijóo replied. Paiporta is the town near Valencia, in eastern Spain, that was worst hit by the 2024 floods. The reply provided another viral clip in a campaign that seems to be followed more on live streams than on traditional broadcasts. The candidate, who was not quick enough to respond immediately, returned to the remark during the final segment of the night.
The synergy between Sánchez and Colau was almost complete throughout the programme, recalling the July 2023 campaign, when Yolanda Díaz was in the spotlight. Díaz was then the candidate of Sumar, the left-wing platform allied with Sánchez's Socialists. Only in the closing moments of each segment – housing, the economy, public services, corruption, foreign policy and immigration – did the Barcelona politician gently criticise certain policies of the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE).
Abascal, who used the far-from-casual term Reconquista in his closing statement, focused his contributions on three common enemies of the country: immigration, environmental policies and corruption. Reconquista refers to the medieval Christian campaigns to retake the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rule. At no point in the debate did Abascal address his hypothetical coalition partner, whether inside or outside government, and he directed most of his attacks at Sánchez.
The Vox leader also tried to reach out to working-class voters. He attacked the profits of IBEX 35 companies, the firms in Spain's benchmark stock index, and contrasted them with the economic conditions that, he claimed, the rest of society has to endure.
Sánchez had a better night than in his previous head-to-head with Feijóo, but he was slow to go on the offensive, leaving several awkward silences after attacks from his two rivals on the right. At the outset he again tried to strike a conciliatory tone, a strategy that had already failed him in the previous debate. Although he adjusted as the debate went on, he appeared much more comfortable taking on the far-right leader than the conservative one.
In the last segment of the debate, the opposition leader laid down a challenge to the prime minister. Feijóo said he would resign from his post, give up his seat in parliament and renounce his legal protection if he did not become prime minister, and he demanded that Sánchez commit to doing the same. Sánchez did not accept the challenge.
