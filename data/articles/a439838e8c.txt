Denis Villeneuve isn't thrilled about having to share a release date with Marvel's next Avengers movie. The Dune: Part Three director has admitted he's "deeply disappointed" after Disney scheduled Avengers: Doomsday for the same December release date as his upcoming sci-fi sequel. In an interview with Time, Villeneuve discussed the unusual box-office showdown between the two highly anticipated films, both of which are scheduled to open Dec. 18.
"The first Dune came out at the end of the pandemic. We made a great box office in a very rough environment. But we had the winds against us," Villeneuve told Time, pointing to the challenges surrounding the first two installments. Dune: Part Two was originally expected to arrive in the fall before being pushed to the end of winter because of the Hollywood strikes.
Villeneuve said he had hoped the third movie would finally enjoy a more straightforward theatrical release, without a pandemic or industry shutdown complicating its prospects. Instead, Marvel's biggest franchise is now going head-to-head with his film.
"So I was deeply disappointed," he said. "But we had committed to this date, to IMAX. It was impossible to move. So I accepted my fate, praying to the gods of cinema."
A major box-office showdown is coming
Villeneuve acknowledged that Dune doesn't have the same commercial muscle as the Marvel Cinematic Universe, describing Avengers as a "T. rex" and his franchise as a "sexy little animal that has a lot of firepower." Villeneuve's first two Dune films made $411 million and $715 million at the worldwide box office, while Marvel's Avengers movies have consistently grossed in the billions. "I'm no fool. Those movies are a mammoth," he said, adding that he hopes Dune: Part Three will survive the box-office competition.
Still, the filmmaker isn't necessarily viewing the simultaneous releases as a straightforward rivalry. He suggested the two movies could combine to create one of the biggest box-office weekends of all time. "Am I in competition with the Avengers? Absolutely not," Villeneuve told Time. "But I stand proud because I think the film delivers."
Dune: Part Three will adapt elements of Frank Herbert's Dune Messiah, picking up years after the events of Dune: Part Two. Timothée Chalamet returns as Paul Atreides, alongside Zendaya, Florence Pugh, and other familiar faces. Villeneuve has also described the movie as a significant departure from the source material, explaining that the adaptation required substantial creative choices.
Avengers: Doomsday marks the next major ensemble installment in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, with Robert Downey Jr. returning to the franchise in a new role as Doctor Doom. Both films are currently scheduled to hit theaters on Dec. 18, setting up a potentially massive "Dunesday" weekend for movie theaters.
