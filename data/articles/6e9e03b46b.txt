Trump threatens to penalize U.S. tech companies that build AI data centres in other countries
Democrats debating best way to quickly establish guardrails in the next Congress
U.S. tech companies that build artificial intelligence data centres in other countries will face "a tremendous tax," U.S. President Donald Trump said on Friday.
"We're not allowing data centres to go into other countries because China will take all of them, and we're not allowing that to happen," Trump said in a White House press briefing. "And we're going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try and do it."
Trump did not provide details on how he would enforce such a policy and said he hadn't yet told his own officials about his plan.
The president urged Americans to welcome data centre construction in their communities and extract concessions from the developers, repeating a point he made before the midterm elections.

"You ought to take advantage of the data centres," Trump said. "Make them pay you, make them pay your community, but don't shut them off."
"We want them to build it here," he added.
The U.S. Senate on Thursday approved bipartisan legislation aimed at speeding up permitting reviews to build power plants, transmission lines and other energy infrastructure as lawmakers seek to increase the nation's energy supplies to meet surging demand and lower electricity prices.
The legislation's sweeping changes would shorten the timeline to start big energy projects and make sure data centres pay for the energy they use and upgrades to the larger transmission network.
Trump praised the Senate bill, which now goes to the House.
"It's going to cause tremendous amounts of new building," he said.
A tougher regulatory environment for AI giants and data centre developers is expected to follow, even if Trump — a champion of the industry — stalls any federal crackdown. The data centres powering the AI boom have become politically toxic.
Democrats weighing how to regulate AI
Anger over data centres and rising electricity prices helped usher in a midterms wave of Democrats pledging to clamp down on the energy-hungry behemoths.
Democrats have been publicly and privately discussing what their first steps could be on artificial intelligence when they take the majority in January. Top policy proposals could be regulating data centres, safety standards and stepping up monitoring and research.
Colorado Sen. John Hickenlooper said Friday that there hasn't been enough progress in Congress since Democrats formed a working group on AI three years ago and convened tech leaders from across the country to figure out next steps.
While Republicans who have led the House and Senate for the past two years have said that AI should be a priority, they have focused mostly on innovation and there has been no major push to establish safeguards or increase monitoring of the technology.
"There's a perception that we haven't done anything," Hickenlooper said.
House Democrats have also been discussing a way forward on AI and have proposed a robust package of proposals. This week, three Democrats announced the launch of the House AI Safety and Innovation caucus to address security risks.
With host communities across the U.S. turning against the gargantuan data centres, America's technology giants are promising to take fewer taxpayer handouts, keep fewer secrets or shower more money onto the communities willing to allow the server warehouses.
Besides ramping up financial incentives, officials with Google and Meta said in interviews with The Associated Press they are trying to address mistrust by being more transparent with communities where they would like to build.
Companies are also making pledges to reduce their impact on the environment and offering assurances that data centres will boost local property values — all while maintaining AI is a positive force for the American economy.
The Trump administration on Monday issued new rules to restrict remote access to advanced U.S. computing chips and servers, marking the latest step to limit China's abilities to use U.S. chips to train its best AI models.
