The twin brother of a man whose body lay undiscovered for 12 days after he was killed in a suspected hit and run in Sydney says it has left him feeling like half a person.
Joshua Howard was last seen at a bus interchange in Parramatta, in western Sydney, about 10pm on 7 November.
The 31-year-old was reported missing the following day and detectives later established he had been walking along Garfield Street East in Rouse Hill, in the city’s north-west, about 12.45 that morning.
An extensive search for began in the Rouse Hill area on Thursday with help from Blue Mountains Rescue, the New South Wales State Emergency Service and the Rural Fire Service.
His body was found by police in a bushland gully near Windsor Road in Rouse Hill about 2.45pm on Friday.
Superintendent Jason Joyce said police believe Howard was hit by a car as he was making his way home from a friend’s Diwali party.
“We believe he’s been walking along the side of the road and then been struck, and that’s propelled him over the guardrail into the bushland,” Joyce told reporters on Sunday.
“Based on his injuries … we believe there was some level of force of him being struck by the vehicle.”
It took police 11 days to start the land search because they were initially trying to track Howard’s movements through CCTV footage and doorknocking houses in the area, Joyce said.
Howard’s parents, Peter and Sue, described him as a loving son and sibling to his twin David and older brother Matthew.
He was a talented artist who graduated from the National Art School and always carried a sketchbook and pen with him, they said.
“When he was missing, David went through his room to see whether all those books were gone,” Sue Howard told reporters.
“But they were all there, indicating he was coming home that night.”
She said her family was heartbroken to learn they had likely driven past him the day after he was killed, without knowing his body was there.
“Our poor boy was there for 12 days,” she said.
She said David was particularly devastated, telling her: “I feel as if I’m half a person now.”
Peter Howard pleaded for anyone with information about the suspected hit and run to contact police.
“We don’t want Joshua’s passing to be wasted,” he said.
“We want drivers to know the impact of speeding or whatever happened.
“Think of others. Stop. Ring triple zero and take responsibility for [your] own actions.”
