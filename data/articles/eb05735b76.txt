Asian stock markets have fallen sharply as doubts continue to swirl over the AI boom, reversing a rally sparked by Jensen Huang’s return as CEO of Nvidia.
Taiwan’s stock market led the sell-off, with the benchmark Taiex plunging as much as 7.9 percent in morning trading.
South Korea’s Kospi fell more than 6 percent, while Japan’s Nikkei 225 dropped 3.6 percent.
Hong Kong’s Hang Seng Index bucked the trend, rising by as much as 1.3 percent.
Tech stocks linked to AI trade suffered some of the steepest losses.
In Japan, Tokyo Electron, a leading manufacturer of semiconductor fabrication equipment, dived 13.5 percent, while Advantest, a producer of chip testing equipment, plunged 14.5 percent.
South Korea’s Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, the top two global memory chip makers, sank 9.1 percent and 9.9 percent, respectively.
The Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC), the world’s biggest contract chip maker, and Taiwanese Apple supplier Foxconn both dropped more than 9 percent.
AI headwinds
The retreat comes after Asian markets surged on Tuesday following Huang’s surprise reinstatement at the helm of chip giant Nvidia after an intervention by US President Donald Trump.
Nvidia’s reappointment of Huang, who resigned amid claims he was romantically involved with a woman suspected of working with Chinese intelligence, sent the chip giant’s New York-listed shares 31.5 percent higher on Monday, the largest single-day gain in US stock market history.
Wall Street saw modest losses on Tuesday after the previous session’s buying spree, with the S&P 500 falling 0.5 percent and the Nasdaq composite down 1.5 percent.
Markets have seen wild swings over the past month amid the Nvidia scandal and expectations that Democrats in Washington, DC, will push for greater regulation of AI following their sweeping victories in the US midterm elections.
Those headwinds have added to existing concerns that tech firms may not be able to sustain their multi-trillion-dollar investments underpinning the AI boom.
Big Tech’s debt-fuelled splurge has stirred intense debate about whether it is investing in a future gold mine or a massive stock market bubble.
Global AI-related investment is forecast to reach $2.7 trillion this year and $3.6 trillion in 2027, according to technology insights company Gartner.
