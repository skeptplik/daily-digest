In an extraordinary decision, the Federal Communications Commission on Wednesday granted SpaceX conditional authorization to deploy a constellation of up to 1 million Starmind satellites designed to serve as orbital data centers.
If fully deployed, this constellation would increase the number of satellites in orbit by roughly 50-fold.
“The United States is moving decisively to ensure that this breakthrough technology creates opportunity, drives investment, and delivers lasting benefits for the American people,” FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said in a statement on Wednesday. “This action opens a bold new frontier for the computing infrastructure needed to power America’s leadership and growth.”
The decision, announced a day before the Thanksgiving holiday in the United States, represents a major regulatory victory for SpaceX and its founder, Elon Musk, who has allied himself with the Trump administration.
A long deployment runway
This announcement clears a major hurdle to deploying data centers in space, although it does not establish that orbital data centers are technically or economically viable. Nevertheless, the FCC authorization appears designed to give the company wide latitude as it works through these remaining issues.
For example, SpaceX has seven years to deploy a single operational Starmind satellite under the order. SpaceX has until November 2035 to deploy 100,000 operational satellites, and November 2040 for the full constellation. SpaceX, therefore, has a 14-year regulatory runway to deploy the most ambitious industrial project ever approved for spaceflight.
The authorization includes some conditional aspects. SpaceX has proposed operating in six orbits, ranging from 550 km to about 1,000 km. The company sought a waiver from FCC rules to allow satellites above 600 km to be disposed of into “Earth disposal orbits or heliocentric orbits.” That is, instead of burning them up in Earth’s atmosphere, SpaceX wants the flexibility to move retired satellites into other orbits around Earth, or potentially into orbit around the Sun.
