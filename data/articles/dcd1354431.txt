More than Â£160m is needed to clean up and maintain one of Europe's largest illegal landfill sites, government officials have said.
The contaminated Mobuoy dump at Campsie in County Londonderry, discovered and shut down in 2013, was used to bury thousands of tonnes of rubbish at a site spanning more than 100 acres.
Last year, two businessmen were jailed for their part in the operation.
Northern Ireland's Environment Minister Andrew Muir said his department had now received an updated cost estimate of Â£161m for the clean-up over a 30-year period.
Muir described what happened at Mobuoy as "an absolute scandal" and said "the community rightly expects and deserves remediation of the site".
He is now seeking a meeting with the Finance Minister John O'Dowd to discuss how the work will be paid for - with funding from Westminster a possibility.
Once the finances are agreed, they will work towards appointing a contractor.
Investigations indicate an estimated 1.6m tonnes of waste was present at the site and 627,000 tonnes of that was believed to have been dumped illegally.
The Mobuoy site is beside the River Faughan, which supplies a significant proportion of Derry's drinking water but officials have not found any contamination to it.
The area of polluted ground stretches to about 100 acres, or the equivalent of about 65-70 football pitches.
'Road map for remediation'
"This is a complicated project, as you'll understand, given the complexity of the site," Muir told BBC Radio Foyle's North West Today programme on Tuesday, .
The minister said he took environmental protection very seriously and would continue to push for the establishment of an independent environmental protection agency.
"One of the massive lessons that needs to be learned from the Mobuoy site is the need for stronger environmental protections, so people have confidence that our environment is being looked after."
Muir said the next assembly election was fast approaching in May, so he understood there might be some concerns about the potential costs involved.
"I want to finish this mandate with a degree of certainty for the local community, so we have a road map here for remediation," he said.
"I am pushing ahead with real enthusiasm with this."
Muir said the drinking water was tested regularly to ensure it was safe and that he would continue to work with all agencies to provide the local community with greater assurances about the site.
Muir said the Mobuoy site remained in private ownership, but they were "actively" considering ways of transferring it.
One option is vesting, which allows government departments to obtain compulsory and legal ownership of land through a court order.
