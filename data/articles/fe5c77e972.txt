Yatsushiro, Kumamoto Pref. – Citizens of Yatsushiro in Kumamoto Prefecture enjoyed a cheery moment at a traditional shrine as an event was held to pray for recovery from a major earthquake that hit the prefecture in July.
On Monday, visitors enjoyed traditional dance performances by a huge doll called kida, a mythical creature that combines a turtle and a snake, and shishi lion dolls during the event, held at Yatsushiro Shrine in place of the shinko gyoretsu parade. The parade, a highlight of the shrine’s annual autumn festival, Yatsushiro Myokensai, was canceled this year due to the quake.
Dating back some 400 years, Yatsushiro Myokensai attracts more than a hundred thousand spectators from across Japan each year. It was added to UNESCO’s Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2016.
The festival’s shinko gyoretsu, usually held in late November, is a major local traditional event in which around 1,600 people, including citizens, dressed in historical costumes parade around the city.
This year, however, the parade was canceled in consideration of those affected by the temblor, which registered a seismic intensity of up to 7, the highest level on the Japanese scale. An event to commemorate the 10th anniversary of the UNESCO registration has also been canceled.
“Next year, we hope to show a proper festival with shinko gyoretsu,” said Katsuhiko Kobayashi, 42, chief priest of Yatsushiro Shrine.
Many Yatsushiro residents visited the shrine Monday to watch the dance performance.
Mitsuru Kubo, 71, whose home was partially damaged by the quake, expressed pleasure to see the performance and people’s happy faces.
In the evening, some 50 lanterns with written messages wishing for Yatsushiro’s reconstruction were released into the sky.
