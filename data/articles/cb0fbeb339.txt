Melbourne Cup 2026: Point of Law wins Cup, Ciaron Maher trains trifecta
Point of Law, a horse sold by King Charles III in July, has won the Melbourne Cup in just its sixth start.
The three-year-old colt trained by Ciaron Maher firmed as the favourite just before the jump, taking the tag away from Hopewell Rock, which began the day as the most fancied to win the famous 3,200m race.
Ridden by Alice Springs native Ethan Brown, Point of Law stormed home late, chasing down long-time leader Royal Supremacy with 150 metres to go, to win ahead of Zahrann and Juja Kibo.
Mahar became the first trainer in Melbourne Cup history to train the trifecta.
Point of Law, the son of famous champion Frankle, was bred in England and owned by King Charles III before being sold in August to an Australian consortium following his Group 3 Bahrain Trophy win at Newmarket.
Its two runs in Australia have both been victorious after winning October's Geelong Cup, becoming just the fourth horse to claim the Geelong-Melbourne Cup double after Media Puzzle (2002), Americain (2010) and Dunaden (2011).
Earlier, Chris Waller-trained Asterix was scratched from the Cup after stewards deemed medication had been used on the horse today. Waller explained it was a simple mistake in the heat of the moment.

The last runs for all 24 Melbourne Cup horses
One of the guides to the Melbourne Cup each year is recent form, in particular the last run before the Cup for each horse in the race.
Let's look at the Melbourne Cup dating back to 2009, when Shocking was the last horse to run on Derby Day — a first-place result in the Hotham Handicap over 2,500m — and go on to win the Cup.
In the 17 Cups from 2009 to 2025, 12 of the winners had placed (come first, second or third) in their previous race before the Melbourne Cup.
Six horses — Shocking, Americain (2010), Dunaden (2011), Almandin (2016), Without A Fight (2023) and Half Yours (2025)— won their last race before winning the Cup.
You don't have to have a top-three finish before the Cup to win the whole thing. Five have done this, the most recent was 2024 winner Knight's Choice, who backed up at Flemington after a fifth in the Bendigo Cup (G3, 2400m).

How often do favourites win the Melbourne Cup?
As you might imagine in a race with 24 (or even 23) runners, there are a lot of factors in a Melbourne Cup, including the barriers, the track surface, who can get cover (not get caught wide), the tempo of the race and the runs that different horses get (or don't get).
Back in 2014, my friend and colleague Daniel Franklin, who puts together the marvellous interactive Cup Guide every year for ABC, did an interesting fact-check story before the 2014 Melbourne Cup, which was won by Protectionist.
Checking the exhaustive stats, he found that to that point, 34 favourites had won out of 153 Melbourne Cups, or a tick over 22.2 per cent (a Richie Benaud stat, there).
Since that point, however, it's been a bad run for favourites. Fiorente, in 2013, was the last Melbourne Cup favourite to finish first.
Before the 2014 Cup, 70 of the 153 Melbourne Cup favourites had run at least a place (come first, second or third). That's equal to 46 per cent of the time.
But again, the intervening 12 years have not been kind to the horses on top of the betting. Only three of the last 12 favourites (Hartnell in 2016, Tiger Moth in 2021 and Incentivise in 2022) have finished in the top three. That means the total equals 73 favourites out of 165 races have run a place — or 44.2 per cent.
An explanation? With the internationalisation of the Cup, we have a lot more horses coming from overseas, plenty still rejecting a pre-Cup run Down Under.
This means that favouritism often is led by rumours out of the quarantine centre at Werribee, where the internationals do their trackwork after landing in Australia.
That's racing, but it does make it harder and harder to line up form between horses from various countries around the world and those that have run here and are used to conditions.
