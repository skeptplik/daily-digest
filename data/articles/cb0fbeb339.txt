Can an Australian-bred horse win the Melbourne Cup again?
It's been an interesting time for the Melbourne Cup — or maybe things go in cycles.
Even five years ago, pretty much everyone agreed that Australian-bred stayers were such a low priority in local racing that many of the big trainers had decided to look overseas for their challengers for the Cup.
The whole tone of the race was changing, and yet — not quite.
Two years ago, 100-1 outsider Knight's Choice, trained by Sheila Laxon and John Symons, struck a blow for local breeding when he won the Cup in a tight finish.
Was it an outlier? No, it turned out, as Half Yours went on from his Caulfield Cup win to take out the big one at Flemington as well.
So, can Australia make it three on the trot, so to speak?
Well, there's a non-zero chance. There are four locally-bred entries - Knight's Choice, So You Are, Strictly Business and Zakouma.
Knight's Choice is back for a tilt at a second Cup; So You Are has won four races in a row, capped by the Bendigo Cup victory that got him into the Cup; Strictly Business is a mare whose last win was in the 2025 Victoria Oaks at Flemington; and Zakouma has been given the Bart Cummings treatment by trainer Grahame Begg, flying under the radar in a 10,000m-raced prep.
Below is the list of the seven Australian-bred Cup winners since the internationalisation of the race in 1993. We'll find out in a few hours whether that number can increase to eight or not:

Melbourne Cup storylines: The trainers
There are five chances to add to Ireland's four wins in the race — and they're all for the same man.
Joseph O'Brien could become the youngest trainer (at 33) to win a third Melbourne Cup with Sons And Lovers, Trustyourinstinct, Goodie Two Shoes, James J Braddock or Kizlyar.
If he breaks the record, O'Brien would take the place of French trainer Etienne de Mestre, who won five Cups in the 1800s, with his third in 1867 with Tim Whiffler at the age of 35.
There are a number of trainers who could add to England's sole win in the Cup (Charlie Appleby with Cross Counter in 2018).
Roger Varian has Enfjaar, Harry Charlton has Wine Dark Sea, and George Boughey has Hopewell Rock.
Between O'Brien and Ciaron Maher, the pair literally have half the field.
Added to O'Brien's five entries are Maher's septet — Middle Earth, Royal Supremacy, Juja Kibo, Zahrann, Point of Law, Mr Monaco and Shockletz. If any of these come home, it would be Maher's second win after Gold Trip in 2022.
Maher is a leading proponent of the Aussie "imports" strategy for the Cup. Of his seven horses, only Shockletz (NZ-bred) is vaguely local, while the rest are from Britain or Ireland.
Chris Waller no longer has Asterix, but he does have Irish-bred Soul of Spain in the running. If it runs first, Waller would have a second Cup win to add to Verry Elleegant in 2021.
Gai Waterhouse could also add a second victory if (French-bred) Vauban gets up (and a first for co-trainer Adrian Bott). A win would see Waterhouse join Sheila Laxon (Ethereal, Knight's Choice) as the only female trainers to win two Melbourne Cups.
Laxon is going for a third Melbourne Cup — with the same horse as her second as Knight's Choice goes for a second win in three years. Her co-trainer John Symons would win his second.
Then there are the trainers looking to break through and add their names to the honour roll for the first time (aside from those already mentioned).
These are Bjorn Baker with So You Are, Tim Fitzimmons with Onesmoothoperator, Gregory Hickman with Future History, Thomas Carberry with Strictly Business and Grahame Begg with Zakouma.
Melbourne Cup storylines: The international raiders
While several international horses have been withdrawn from the race in the lead-up to the field being finalised, there are still plenty of "raiders" willing to try their luck to take the Melbourne Cup overseas.
Last year, Joseph O'Brien-trained Goodie Two Shoes fell one place short of taking the Melbourne Cup back to Ireland, losing to Half Yours by a couple of lengths.
This time round, a third of the field are internationally trained, not to mention the Aussie "imports".
And where are these horses coming from? Ireland and England.
The first two modern "raiders" who came to Australia for the sole purpose of winning the Melbourne Cup were Vintage Crop and Drum Taps in 1993. Their campaign — and Vintage Crop's win for Irish trainer Dermot Weld — was seen as the start of the internationalisation of the Cup.
There are a few horses trying to do what Vintage Crop did and win without a lead-up race in Australia.
Aside from Goodie Two Shoes, whose last run this time round was 10th in the Kilternan Stakes (G3, 2,414m) at Leopardstown, trainer Joseph O'Brien has Sons And Lovers, who came second in the Kilternan Stakes; Trustyourinstinct, who came fourth in the Irish St Leger at the Curragh (G1, 2,816m); James J Braddock, who came sixth in the Kilternan Stakes.
O'Brien also has Kizlyar, who had to race in Australia to make the field. He came 11th in the Moonee Valley Cup (G2, 2,500m), but still got in after withdrawals.
The other true raider — here just for this race — is Hopewell Rock, trained by Englishman George Boughey. Hopewell Rock last ran second in the Ebor Handicap (G2, 2,786m), England's biggest handicap race.
English trainer Roger Varian has brought over Enfjaar, who produced a nice run to come third in the Caulfield Cup two-and-a-half weeks ago (G1, 2,400m). Varian's compatriot Harry Charlton trains Wine Dark Sea, who also ran in the Caulfield Cup, finishing 11th.

How does the track rating system work?
If you're new to racing, or if you only tune in once a year on a day like today, the description of the track rating may confuse you.
The system uses different ratings on a scale from 1 to 10.
Rating 1 is a dry, hard track and 2 a firm track. Ratings 3 and 4 are "Good", with 3 a better covering of grass and 4 a track with some give in it.
For everyday races, track managers aim to have the surface at a Good 3 or 4.
Ratings 5 to 7 are Soft, meaning that there is more moisture and give in the track. By the time you get to a 7, it's a more rain-affected track that will start to chop up during the course of the meeting.
Right now, we are at a Soft 5, which means there is some moisture in the track but it's not deteriorating yet. In fact, the lack of recent rain means we could see an upgrade to a Good 4 at some stage.
The last three ratings, 8 to 10, are Heavy.
That would come after a lot of rain and increasing of deterioration in the track.
When it gets to a Heavy track, only so-called "mudlarks" will cope with it. Organisers will be hoping we don't get to that stage today.

Chris Waller says 'very simple mistake' led to Melbourne Cup scratching
The biggest news so far today is the scratching of Chris Waller-trained Asterix.
The horse was found to have Penetrene, a medication for sprains and muscle relief — applied to its forelegs.
Because that is a medication, it is illegal to apply without permission on race day.
Waller has told News Corp journalist Gilbert Gardiner that it was an honest mistake from the person who looks after Asterix.
"The horse gets the same treatment every day, it's a very basic liniment most stables use and I guess the pressure of race day, the person that looks after the horse, put it on his legs right in front of the security guard, nothing to hide," Waller said.
"The pressure of raceday, a very simple mistake.
"Nothing was hidden, the security guard was right there, as soon as it happened we knew there was a problem.
"It's not her fault. I've got to wear the responsibility. It's my team. It's rare for us to make mistakes but the pressure of raceday, you can't explain how it affects everybody. Everyone is on edge on Melbourne Cup Day and you think a little bit differently."

Stewards inquiry as Melbourne Cup contender scratched
— reporting by AAP
High-profile trainer Chris Waller has been questioned by stewards after his Melbourne Cup contender Asterix was found to have had the medication Penetrene administered to both forelegs in its stable just hours before Australia's greatest race.
Asterix, part-owned by former New Zealand international cricketer Mark Greatbatch, was scratched from today's $10 million Flemington showpiece.
Penetrene is generally allowed to be used on horses, except on race days.
"Penetrene is a topical product marketed as an anti-inflammatory and analgesic liniment," Racing Victoria said in a statement.
"Penetrene is used topically on horses for symptomatic relief of muscle strains, sprains and muscle soreness.
"Any substance administered to a horse on raceday, including any topical application amongst any other route of administration is considered a medication."
Stewards will continue their inquiries into the circumstances with Waller later on Tuesday.
Asterix's scratching leaves Waller with only one contender - $21 fancy Soul Of Spain - in the Cup. Asterix had been a $51 chance.
It also leaves 2020 Cup-winning jockey Jye McNeil without a ride in Flemington's $10 million showpiece.
McNeil rebounded from the news to finish second with Royal Press in the opening race on the card, behind Damian Lane on Amelia's Affair.
Meanwhile, popular jockey Harry Coffey has arrived at Flemington to ride Shockletz in the Cup, just two days after having a plate inserted to repair his fractured collarbone.
Coffey required surgery after his scary Victoria Derby day fall, but was all smiles as he flapped his 'wing' at punters when he walked towards the mounting yard.
Rival jockeys lauded Coffey's bravery after he was declared fit by Racing Victoria's chief medical officer Dr Ben Barresi.
"They always say jockeys are made of different stuff and he's proving that," said Rachel King, who will partner So You Are in the Cup.
"It just proves how much it means to us jockeys ... Melbourne Cup day is the biggest day of the year."
Superstar jockey James McDonald, who will ride Trustyourinstinct for Irish trainer O'Brien and billionaire owner-breeder JP McManus, rates Shockletz among the main challengers.
"(Coffey) is tougher than most of us," McDonald said.
"It's like a grand final for us jockeys and trainers. This is the race that stops the nation, so we'd get up for anything."

30 years since Saintly's Melbourne Cup win for Bart Cummings
While we're all about today's big race, sometimes it's good to look back at some of the great stories that have come out of the Melbourne Cup.
Thirty years ago this week, the "horse from heaven", Saintly, ridden by Darren Beadman, won at Flemington to give "Cups king" Bart Cummings his 10th victory.
Cummings, the unchallenged master of training Cup winners, always had a plan. But he broke his own hard-and-fast rules to guide Saintly's campaign.
Cummings's long-time track rider, Joe Agresta, spoke to the ABC about those days and his initial thoughts about the horse.
"He was a nice horse, but he was a gangly type of three-year-old," Agresta says.
When he turned four, things changed and the big-striding chestnut gelding won the Australian Cup earlier in the year before an unlikely win in the Cox Plate leading into the big day at Flemington.
