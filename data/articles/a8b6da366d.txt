The Leonid meteor shower reaches its peak on Tuesday night, giving UK sky gazers their best chance to see it this year.
The celestial spectacle runs until 30 November, but the greatest activity is expected on the night of Tuesday 17 November, into the early hours of Wednesday.
There could be the chance to spot up to 10-15 meteors per hour.
The shower is made up of dust particles from the Comet 55P/Tempel-Tuttle, which leaves a trail of debris as it follows its path around the Sun.
These particles enter the Earth's atmosphere at up to 44 miles (71km) per second, meaning these streaks of light are the fastest meteors in the sky.
How to spot the Leonids
Experts advise finding a safe, dark location away from sources of light pollution such as street lights.
The meteors will be visible with the naked eye, with no binoculars or telescope required.
However, giving your eyes some time to adapt to the dark, perhaps 20 or 30 minutes, will give you the best chance of spotting them.
A wide open space is the best place to be, giving a full view of the night sky.
Meteors could be visible anywhere but the Leonids' radiant, the point from which they seem to appear, will, as the name suggests, be around the head of the constellation Leo, meaning this is the best part of the sky to focus your gaze. Leo does not rise until around midnight, so the best time to look is after midnight and in the hours before dawn.
As with all celestial spectacles, clear skies will be a must.
What will the weather be like on Tuesday night?
Viewing conditions could be particularly good around the peak. The Moon will set at around 23:30 GMT on Tuesday, meaning its reflected light is less likely to spoil the view in the hours after midnight.
The best chance of seeing the Leonids will be in the far north of Scotland, including Orkney and Shetland, where cloud is expected to be well broken with only a small chance of a passing shower.
Parts of Northern Ireland, Wales, the Midlands and southern England will also have clear spells at times but there will be periods of cloudier weather and some splashes of rain.
In Northern Ireland, the Midlands and North Wales, the best chance of clear breaks will be earlier in the night.
Much of Scotland away from the far north, and northern England, will have limited opportunities. A few places may see the odd gap in the cloud but for most it will be cloudy and damp.
