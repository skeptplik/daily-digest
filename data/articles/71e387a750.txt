Russia’s Justice Ministry on Friday designated the left-wing online news outlet Rabkor, which was founded by jailed anti-war sociologist and Marxist theorist Boris Kagarlitsky, as a “foreign agent.”
The ministry accused Rabkor of disseminating information created by “foreign agents” and “undesirable” organizations, as well as working with those organizations and spreading “false” information about the Russian government.
Kagarlitsky, who himself was designated as a “foreign agent” in May 2022, is currently serving a five-year prison sentence on charges of “justifying terrorism” in a YouTube video about a Ukrainian attack against the Crimean Bridge.
A well-known academic who has spoken out publicly against Moscow’s invasion of Ukraine, Kagarlitsky chose to remain in Russia despite the Kremlin’s crackdown on dissent.
Besides Rabkor, the Justice Ministry on Friday also designated four activists and media figures who live abroad as “foreign agents.”
They include exiled Chechen human rights activist Fatima Gaziyeva, Finland-based Chechen opposition activist and video blogger Musa Lomayev, Russian-Georgian media manager and political analyst Yegor Kuroptev and Ukrainian journalist Alexei Artyukh.
The Justice Ministry accused them of spreading false information about Russia’s political system and sharing content from “foreign agents” and “undesirable” organizations.
Hundreds of cultural figures, journalists, businesspeople, as well as news outlets and organizations, have been branded “foreign agents” since Russia introduced the label in 2012.
Russia applies the designation to individuals it views as enemies of the state. Those branded as “foreign agents” are required to include a lengthy disclaimer in all of their public appearances and statements, including posts on social media.
