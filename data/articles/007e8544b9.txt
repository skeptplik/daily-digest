The government plans to submit to next year’s ordinary parliamentary session starting in January a bill to revise an existing law, in order to have the Japan Transport Safety Board (JTSB) investigate self-driving car accidents, sources said Tuesday.
The move comes as the country’s first driverless taxi services are planned to be launched in 2027 by Google-linked Waymo, taxi-hailing app operator Go and taxi operator Nihon Kotsu.
A committee to investigate self-driving car accidents was set up in fiscal 2020, but it is said to lack sufficient authority.
In contrast, the JTSB, established as an affiliate of the transport ministry under the national government organization law, possesses far stronger investigative and recommendation authority.
The envisaged bill to revise the law for the establishment of the JTSB would add self-driving car accidents to its investigative scope, which currently includes aircraft, train and ship accidents, informed sources said.
The board’s probes would target serious accidents caused by vehicles equipped with Level 3 autonomous driving technology, which requires drivers to take over control upon system request, or with more advanced self-driving technology.
Specifically, the JTSB would investigate accidents that resulted in death or serious injury, those that could have resulted in death or serious injury and other serious incidents such as road traffic law violations, according to the sources.
In addition, the government plans to soon establish a panel under its regulatory reform promotion council for discussions on victim compensation for self-driving car accidents, with the focus expected to be on who would be liable for damages payments.
