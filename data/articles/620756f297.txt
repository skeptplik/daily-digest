As businesses increasingly turn to AI agents to automate work, the vast amounts of information locked in complex documents remain a challenge. Unsiloed AI has raised $7.5 million in seed funding led by Felicis to tackle that problem, aiming to help enterprises make their documents more accessible to AI agents through specialized technology.
Silicon Valley venture capital firm Felicis led the round, with participation from Y Combinator, Orange Collective, and Uncorrelated Ventures. Angel investors Aarthi Ramamurthy and Beth Turner also participated.
Business documents often include tables, charts, and complicated layouts that AI systems can struggle to interpret accurately. But getting the information wrong can lead to costly mistakes for businesses, and the impact could be severe in the case of a bank, hospital, or a law firm, as they rely on these documents for their daily work.
Unsiloed aims to solve this problem with software that breaks down complex documents, extracts information from tables, charts, and other elements, and turns it into structured data that AI agents can use. The technology also shows where the extracted information came from in the original document, so businesses can check the results rather than take the AI’s output at face value.
“The problem with LLMs is they’re inherently probabilistic,” said Unsiloed co-founder and CEO Aman Mishra (pictured above, right) in an interview. “There is going to be some deviation induced. There is going to be some hallucination involved, and that has a massive negative impact downstream.”
Mishra co-founded Unsiloed with Adnan Abbas (pictured above, left), his former classmate at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Kharagpur. The two joined Y Combinator’s Fall 2025 batch after spending some time developing the technology.
Unsiloed is already working with customers in finance, legal services, and healthcare, Mishra told TechCrunch. One bank is using its technology to process documents, while a law firm has deployed it on its own servers to handle contracts and old case files, he said, without naming either customer. Mishra, however, named voice AI startup Bolna AI as a customer, saying it uses Unsiloed to ingest knowledge bases for building voice agents.
The San Francisco-headquartered startup has entered a market that is already crowded and includes specialized document-processing startups such as Reducto and Unstructured, as well as offerings from Microsoft and Amazon. Businesses also sometimes use general-purpose AI models to extract information from documents, giving Unsiloed another set of competitors.
Mishra told TechCrunch that Unsiloed’s advantage lies in how it processes complex documents. The startup uses what it calls “dual-stream” vision models, which handle text and image data separately, along with a technique called “heat map-based chunking” that helps identify the most important parts of a document.
“In one of the streams you have the text tokens, in the other stream you have the exact image tokens,” Mishra said. “This is something that we have spent a lot of time architecting.”
The technology then directs more computing power toward areas such as figures, numbers, and charts. Unsiloed also uses a specialized decoder that takes industry-specific terminology into account, helping it distinguish between terms that can mean different things in different business contexts.
Supporting those claims, Mishra pointed to a test involving 542 financial documents supplied by an enterprise customer. Unsiloed achieved 99.78% accuracy across roughly 84,000 extracted data points, per a technical brief shared by the startup. The test compared Unsiloed with major AI models, Microsoft’s Azure Document Intelligence, and the customer’s in-house extraction tool. The results have, however, not been independently verified.
Unsiloed also claims to have outperformed competing AI document-processing tools on olmOCR-Bench, a public benchmark developed by Allen AI. In tests conducted by the startup, Unsiloed scored 88.0, compared with 66.0 for Reducto’s agentic system and 39.9 for Unstructured. The benchmark covered 1,403 PDF pages and tested how well the systems handled tables, mathematical expressions, scanned documents, and complex layouts.
Unsiloed’s pricing, Mishra said, starts at one cent per page. He asserted that the startup’s approach also helps keep its costs down by directing computing power toward the parts of a document that need the most attention, instead of processing every section in the same way.
Currently, Unsiloed has a headcount of seven people. The startup, Mishra said, plans to use the fresh funding to hire more researchers and engineers, expand its sales efforts, and develop proprietary datasets to improve its technology.
