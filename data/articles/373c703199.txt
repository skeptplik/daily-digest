He is the next James Bond. She is a successful model/actor/singer-songwriter. Together, they are set to become the next big British power couple in Hollywood.
This week Robert Pattinson and Suki Waterhouse, who have been in a relationship since 2018, were thrust into the global spotlight after the news that he had landed arguably the biggest and most glamorous role in film.
RPatz is well known – this year alone he has starred in The Drama, The Odyssey and Primetime.
Alongside modelling, Waterhouse has played a leading role in Daisy Jones & the Six, the TV adaptation of Taylor Jenkins Reid’s Fleetwood Mac-inspired novel, and is touring Loveland, her third album, which includes a cameo from Mick Fleetwood. She previously opened for Taylor Swift at Wembley.
However, the 007 news means the couple’s level of fame will receive a turbocharged jolt, catapulting both of them into household names.
The last British twosome to transform into a power couple on a global level were the Beckhams. In May their empire, which spans football, fashion and property, passed the £1bn mark.
Instead of following the Brand Beckham blueprint, Pattinson, 40, and Waterhouse, 34, are taking their own newgen approach to power. Rather than trying to turn themselves into a joint brand for commercial gain, the couple are focusing on their own individual careers and successes.
It’s a master plan created by fellow big screen stars Tom Holland – who, like Pattinson and Waterhouse, also hails from west London – and Zendaya, who, despite their global fame and estimated combined net worth of £85m, have garnered a reputation as Hollywood’s most low-key couple. Pattinson previously worked with Zendaya on The Drama.
“For decades Hollywood power couples have leveraged their relationships to build joint brands, attract media attention and pursue lucrative business opportunities,” said Shaun Harper, a professor at the USC Marshall school of business at the University of Southern California. “But there is also tremendous business value in keeping their professional identities separate.”
When the Beckhams got engaged in 1998 they held a press conference. A year later, OK! magazine bought the exclusive rights to their wedding photos in a record-breaking £1m deal. They also sold the first photos of their oldest son, Brooklyn. In January, he published a statement in which he accused his parents of prioritising Brand Beckham. He is now estranged from the family.
Pattinson and Waterhouse are navigating fame in a more restrained way. They take a zero-sharenting – in other words no sharing on social media – approach to their daughter, who was born in 2024.
Pattinson has been an ambassador for Dior for more than 10 years and in September signed a deal with the Swiss watch brand Jaeger LeCoultre. Waterhouse has her own deals with Michael Kors and Miu Miu.
The duo were four years into their relationship before they made their red carpet debut at a Dior show in 2022 and since then, they have done so only a handful of times. When they do, it is fruitful for brands. According to the fashion data platform Launchmetrics, their joint appearance at the 2026 Venice film festival, where Pattinson wore Dior and Waterhouse Saint Laurent, generated £4m in media impact value for the luxury houses in one week.
Harper said the couple realised that success did not rely on their relationship becoming a commercial enterprise. “Their approach allows each to build a career, pursue business ventures and maintain a fanbase without being overly dependent on the other,” he said. “It also protects their individual brands from some of the reputational and financial risks that come with being marketed as a couple.”
Holland and Zendaya have starred in the Spider-Man film franchise together and regularly support one another on the red carpet, but they have never done a brand deal. Instead, Holland runs his own alcohol-free beer brand. Zendaya is an ambassador for brands including Prada Beauty and Valentino, and she owns properties separate to Holland. They have never shared their wedding photos.
Zoe Gaffney, a brand director at the marketing agency Leopard Co, points to how a shifting media landscape is influencing this new generation of celebrity couples. While the Beckhams rose to fame during the tabloid era, when “audiences eagerly awaited the latest glimpse into their glamorous lives”, today, owing to social media, they can be more in control of their own narrative.
This new age of optional exposure created a paradox, said Gaffney. “Perhaps being less visible can actually make you more desirable,” she said.
Holland and Zendaya use their platforms solely to promote their latest projects. Pattinson doesn’t have any social media accounts, while Waterhouse has almost 6m combined followers on Instagram and TikTok where, alongside promoting her work, she posts self-deprecating behind-the-scenes posts but never shares images of husband, whom she refers to as her “favourite weirdo”.
Gaffney said this made the couple “all the more compelling”. While the Beckhams do multiple congratulatory posts on any family achievements, Waterhouse has so far remained stumm.
However, it hasn’t stopped Bond fans from weighing in. “Can’t believe this was filmed by James Bond,” reads one comment below a video of her sipping a cappuccino. “Privacy creates intrigue and intrigue can be more powerful than exposure,” Gaffney said.
Harper describes the way they have kept their relationship away from public intrusion so far as impressive. “Sometimes the smartest business decision celebrities can make is to keep their romantic relationships out of their businesses,” he said.
