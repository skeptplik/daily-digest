Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi at a meeting hosted by the French government Monday urged the international community to join hands in dealing with security issues regarding artificial intelligence.
Joining the meeting online, Takaichi said that cutting-edge AI is posing new security challenges, such as its use in cyberattacks and loss of control.
Its impact transcends national borders, making international cooperation essential, she said.
Takaichi stressed the importance of deepening discussions in the international arena on ways to facilitate innovation and handle risks at the same time.
Explaining the Japanese government’s aim of hosting an AI summit at an early date, Takaichi said Japan hopes to help achieve progress in international cooperation under an evolved free and open Indo-Pacific.
The meeting, held from 10 p.m. on Monday was attended by French President Emmanuel Macron as well as leaders, government institution representatives and AI-related business officials from interested countries.
Takaichi has been under fire at home for skipping an online summit of the Group of Seven major nations held from 9:30 p.m. on Oct. 2.
At a news conference Tuesday, Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara said that Tokyo was informed of the schedule of Monday’s meeting by the French government in the second half of last week.
The prime minister decided to participate in the meeting, considering it is important to “communicate Japan’s stance and efforts” to the international community, the top government spokesperson added.
