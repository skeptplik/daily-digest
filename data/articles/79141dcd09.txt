Ukrainian explosive naval drones sank a Russian warship docked in the port city of Benghazi in Libya while also striking a Russian cargo ship that it was escorting. The incident demonstrates the expanding scope of Ukrainian military strikes on Russian ships from the coast of North Africa to the Gulf of Finland.
The Russian Navy’s missile corvette Merkury sank at port after being struck by at least two Ukrainian naval drones on November 22, according to The Associated Press. Ukraine’s Security Service described using seven naval drones launched from a mother ship to target the Merkury, and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy shared video footage showing the drone boats approaching and exploding against both the burning Russian warship and a Russian dry cargo ship, MV Angara.
Zelenskyy noted that a Ka-27 combat helicopter onboard the Merkury was also destroyed. He described the cargo ship MV Angara as transporting weapons—Russia provides material support to the Libyan National Army that currently controls Benghazi and is battling the internationally recognized Libyan government based in Tripoli.
Casualties from the strikes included at least 10 people, including Libyan and Russian nationals, Libyan National Army officials told The Associated Press. They also mentioned some wounded crew members of the Russian warship being transferred to local hospitals.
Merkury was a Steregushchiy-class missile corvette, designated by Russia as Project 20380, that can carry both anti-ship and air-defense missiles. They are also armed with torpedoes to counter submarines and have a naval cannon mounted on the deck.
Corvette-class warships are typically smaller than the destroyers or frigates that often act as workhorses of modern navies, although NATO has designated Russian missile corvettes such as the Merkury as being equivalent to frigates.
Merkury was originally meant to join Russia’s Black Sea Fleet. But after Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the Russian warship was unable to enter the Black Sea because Turkey invoked the 1936 Montreux Convention to restrict warships from entering or leaving the Black Sea. Instead, the Merkury mainly operated from Russia’s Northern Fleet bases in Arctic waters, according to naval analyst HI Sutton.
