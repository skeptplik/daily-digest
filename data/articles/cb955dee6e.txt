This cannot possibly be right. I just sat next to a guy on a bus, and his scent was definitely not phwoar. Let me ask you a question: are you in a long-term romantic relationship with this man?
No, of course not. Well, there’s your answer, then. If you were, you would have scientifically found his smell irresistible.
I’m not sure about that. Do not question science, especially not the paper just published by the Behavioural Science Institute at Radboud University in the Netherlands, which claims that the longer a couple have been together, the more likely they are to find each other’s body odour sexually arousing.
This doesn’t sound right. Trust me. Researchers got their subjects to fish out a T-shirt worn by their partner and sniff deeply from it, rating the smell by its pleasantness, intensity, and its soothing and sexually arousing aspects.
And what did they discover? Well, although you might assume that the smell of someone you’ve been going out with for a while would primarily feel safe and familiar, it actually just makes you quite horny.
So it isn’t like getting used to a bad smell? Absolutely not. The study found that odour-evoked sexual arousal grew throughout the duration of a relationship. People who had been together for 400 months were almost four times more likely to be aroused by smell than people who had just got together.
This is astounding. I just have to get someone used to my smell and they’ll fall in love with me. Hang on, it isn’t quite that simple.
Why is that? Because the study also found that smell wasn’t the be-all and end-all. When it comes to overall relationship satisfaction, there are more significant predictors.
Such as what? Such as personality and competence.
Oh no. And looks too, come to think of it.
