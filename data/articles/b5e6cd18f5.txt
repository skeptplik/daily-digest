As Jordan Bardella denies fresh allegations concerning antisemitic remarks he allegedly made as a teenager/young adult, how much damage could the affair do to Marine Le Pen's presidential prospects? Ana Navarro Pedro, correspondent for Portuguese media, believes its electoral impact may be limited, despite Le Pen's defence of Bardella and her choice of him as a future prime minister. “What they most regret in this whole episode is that Bardella is denying it,” she says of prospective Le Pen voters.
Navarro suggests that some would have preferred him to acknowledge and express regret for his alleged past remarks. She also examines why accusations of antisemitism appear to have greater repercussions for Jean-Luc Mélenchon, whose electorate, she explains, is particularly sensitive to questions of racism, antisemitism and human rights.
Drawing a parallel between Bardella's denials and Donald Trump's approach, she speaks of “a kind of tribal loyalty” among voters. She attributes Le Pen's recent dip in the polls instead to the emergence of David Lisnard, the mayor of Cannes, whom she describes as “a kind of softer' Javier Milei and a potential competitor for Le Pen's electorate.“We are in France. Politics here are particularly vicious,” Navarro explains, as she considers another possibility: could Le Pen's continued support for Bardella eventually give her grounds to replace him as her prospective prime minister? She also discusses the enduring influence of the Le Pen family within the National Rally and the possibility of another family member rising within the ranks of the party.
