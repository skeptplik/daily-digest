Nvidia’s former CEO Jensen Huang will testify before several congressional committees on 1 December, setting the stage for a high-stakes hearing in the wake of his resignation following an affair with a suspected Chinese spy.
Tim Teter, Nvidia’s general counsel, has also agreed to appear for a closed-door transcribed interview the following day, 2 December.
“We look forward to hearing from Jensen Huang and Tim Teter on their roles in a potential breach of our national security,” Congressmen Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford, who chair the committees, said in a press release. “The American people need answers on Mr Huang and Nvidia’s role in allowing dangerous [People’s Republic of China] actors to access our most advanced chips, as well as the role of a potential Chinese intelligence asset in this scandal.”
The three congressmen chair the foreign affairs committee, the select committee on the Chinese Communist party, and the intelligence committee of the House of Representatives, respectively. On 12 November, they had signed a letter warning Huang that he could face a subpoena if he did not agree to the closed-door meeting.
Nvidia chips are key to powering the artificial intelligence boom. The Trump administration has prohibited the selling of advanced artificial intelligence chips to China.
Huang stepped down as Nvidia’s CEO after allegations of extramarital relationships with the suspected Chinese spy Alice Huang and two Nvidia employees surfaced.
Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, had been Megaspeed International’s executive director. The Singapore-based cloud computing company has bought nearly $5bn in AI chips and advanced hardware. The company said Alice Huang stepped down in 2025.
Megaspeed ultimately facilitated about $12bn in chip sales, in violation of US export controls. On Friday, Malaysia stated that they were investigating whether the company diverted advanced AI ships to other countries.
A recent joint Department of Commerce and Department of Justice letter, stated that the Department of Commerce, under its secretary, Howard Lutnick, first started an investigation in Megaspeed on 2024 for potential export control violations. The justice department, led by the attorney general, Todd Blanche, took over in 2025.
In his first remarks since his resignation, Jensen Huang said on Thursday he would be willing to return to Nvidia as CEO. Elon Musk and other Silicon Valley tech giants, along with 165,000 signatories to an open letter, have called for his reinstatement. Trump has also signaled his openness for the disgraced tech CEO’s return.
