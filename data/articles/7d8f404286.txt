There are two reasons why a good grinder makes for great coffee: First, coffee starts losing the volatile compounds that contribute to its flavor as soon as it's ground, so grinding your beans just before brewing ensures as many of those compounds are contributing to your cup as possible. Second, if your coffee grounds aren't a uniform size, they make an uneven brew: Inconsistently sized grounds—like those produced by cheaper, blade-based grinders that smash the beans apart—means your coffee can be simultaneously underextracted and overextracted, muddying the flavor with all sorts of undesirable notes.
A proper hand grinder gives you the best of all worlds: Fresh grounds on demand, quality conical burrs that produce more uniform grind sizes, and a cheaper price point than high-end grinders that might be five to ten times as expensive—or more. I'm in the market for a grinder upgrade myself, and that's why this Black Friday deal on the Timemore Chestnut S5 Pro caught my attention.
I've been happily using a cheaper Timemore grinder for a couple of years, so I can already vouch for the brand's quality. The S5 Pro brings some additional features that'd offer some quality-of-life upgrades over my trusty old C2: For one, it's got a bigger capacity. More interesting to me, though, is its precision external grind adjustment with numbered steps. My current grinder's adjustment dial is unlabeled, which can be irritating when I'm switching between coffees. That numbered grind adjustment will make it easier to dial in and keep track of coffee-specific grind sizes.
Timemore also boasts that the S5 Pro's reengineered burrs represent "a new benchmark in grinding." And if there's anything I love, it's progress. If that $143 price tag is too expensive of an ask for starting your coffee grinding journey, however, there are similar deals on Timemore's more affordable options: This $60 Chestnut C2S is a great entry point that'll give you a running start towards better coffee.
