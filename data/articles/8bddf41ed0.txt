Japan’s Senate on Friday rejected a nonbinding censure motion filed against education minister Yoshihiro Seki over a political fundraising scandal.
The failure likely ends opposition efforts to unseat him, as the government turns its attention back to getting its key bills passed before parliament closes on Dec. 12.
Of the 244 votes cast, 124 were against and 120 were in favor. Nine parties in the opposition-controlled upper chamber supported the motion, with eight of them having agreed to the move against Seki on Wednesday. The two-member Conservative Party of Japan and three independents voted against it.
“Given the motion was rejected by just a four-vote margin, we take the result very seriously,” said Masaji Matsuyama, the Liberal Democratic Party’s Senate chair.
Seki has been criticized for inviting bureaucrats to fundraising parties in 2024 to serve as guest lecturers while charging corporate guests ¥90,000 per person. He initially told parliament he had only organized two fundraising parties, but it was later revealed he had held three additional events.
He faced backlash from within the ruling LDP and the opposition over the revelation, with the latter calling on him to resign.
Seki, backed by Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, vowed to continue on as minister. Seki was appointed education minister by Takaichi in a September Cabinet reshuffle.
Some in the ruling coalition were also concerned that a successful motion against Seki would halt parliamentary discussions on the April food tax cut bill — from 8% to 1% for two years — and another one to cut 31 Lower House seats.
Passing those two bills during the current parliamentary session is a top priority for the ruling coalition. The food tax cut bill in particular will need the approval of at least one opposition party in order to be approved by the Senate, formerly known as the House of Councilors, and time is running out to reach an agreement.
Even if the censure motion had passed, Seki would not have been required to resign. However, in addition to holding up parliamentary debate, remaining would have created further political headaches for Takaichi over her choice of Cabinet members.
In October, Former Agriculture Minister Kazuo Yana resigned after less than a month in office over remarks about cutting road budgets for two Tochigi Prefecture municipalities whose mayors did not back him in February’s Lower House election.
With the Senate’s rejection of the motion, however, Seki may be more secure in his job, at least in the short term, forcing the opposition parties to recalibrate their parliamentary strategy in the remaining time left for debate.
