PRESS REVIEW – Tuesday, November 24: Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia dominates the papers. In other news, leaked records shed new light on Russian military losses in Ukraine. Also, the UK will host the 2035 Women's World Cup. Finally, a devilled eggs world championship crowns its winner.
Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia is hard to avoid in the press, and it features on a number of front pages. The Financial Times calls it a stunning reversal for the co-founder, while the Daily Telegraph runs an AI-generated image posted by Donald Trump on Truth Social, showing the CEO rising again with a red bra in hand. The Economist says nothing will be the same for Huang, who faces a hostile Congress, rattled employees and wary business partners. Despite their initial relief at his reinstatement, it argues, investors may see trouble looming. Wired is also concerned about the company culture at Nvidia. Despite the hero's welcome for Huang, it says, many employees had seen his departure as a chance to change a culture of "unfettered unity and minimal bureaucracy".
Several papers have been looking into Russian casualties since the start of the war in Ukraine, based on data from the Russian state statistics service – including 11 million death certificates – released by two pro-Ukrainian hacking groups. The Guardian found that more than a thousand Russian soldiers have died by suicide since the full-scale invasion began. The New York Times obtained the same records but headlines on another number: 700,000 Russian soldiers killed, a major increase on previous assessments. It adds that although Russia was losing more soldiers than it was recruiting, Moscow remains on track to recruit about 409,000 troops in 2026.
We turn next to some sports news. The Independent reports that the UK has been confirmed as host of the 2035 Women's World Cup, the first World Cup held in the country since England hosted the men's tournament in 1966, and the largest single sporting event ever staged there. It will be twice the size of the men's Euros the UK will host in 2028, with 48 teams playing 104 matches over five weeks and around 4.5 million tickets available. The Guardian says maximising the tournament's societal impact, especially combating misogyny, should be a top priority. The paper urges organisers to "put it in your biggest and best stadiums".
Finally, FRANCE 24 reports that there is a cracking new international event: the Devilled Eggs World Championships. It was won by Paris-based chef Hugo Riboulet, who came out of his shell with a winning recipe of confit egg yolk, enhanced with puffed spelt grains for texture, on a bed of verjus jelly. Most competitors opted for eight minutes and 40 seconds of cooking, reputed to be the sweet spot for the perfect devilled egg.
