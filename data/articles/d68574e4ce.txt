Amazon will extend a 3.5 percent fuel and logistics surcharge to transportation fees for two bulk inventory services used by third-party sellers starting Feb. 1, 2027, widening a charge it introduced in April without raising the rate itself.
The surcharge currently applies to fulfillment fees for the company’s Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) service in the U.S. and Canada, and was extended to users of its Multi-Channel Fulfillment and Buy with Prime offerings in May.
The expansion, announced to sellers on Nov. 5, will apply the surcharge to transportation fees for those accessing the Amazon Warehousing and Distribution (AWD) and Multi-Channel Distribution (MCD) services in the U.S.
AWD is an upstream bulk storage service that holds seller inventory at a lower rate than Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) and automatically replenishes fulfillment centers. MCD is the outbound leg from that service that distributes the inventory to a seller’s own warehouse or third-party channel outside Amazon.
The surcharge is calculated on the fulfillment fee, not on the selling price of the item. Amazon puts the average impact at about 17 cents per unit, according to its surcharge announcement to sellers.
Amazon told sellers that costs, especially fuel and line-haul trucking, have remained elevated since April, and that it will keep reviewing the surcharge as conditions change.
That lines up with remarks from the company’s third-quarter results, where fuel and line-haul inflation contributed 3 percentage points to an 11 percent annual increase in shipping costs.
Chief financial officer Brian Olsavsky said on the Oct. 29 earnings call that fuel and line-haul inflation in the fourth quarter would be higher than in the third.
“We instituted a fuel surcharge at the beginning of the year for FBA sellers that recovers some, but not all of the costs that we’re seeing on fuel for those shipments,” Olsavsky said. “Then the remaining part is on the retail inbound shipments and outbound shipments.”
The February expansion also lands on top of a wider fee reset that starts Jan. 15, 2027, when FBA fees will rise by 19 cents per unit sold on average, or about 0.8 percent of the average item’s selling price.
Amazon is also eliminating its storage utilization surcharge from Feb. 1 and folding the aged inventory surcharge into a single age-based rate.
The e-commerce giant said the elimination “will make it easier to stock up ahead of high-demand events,” and that the combined rate “will result in flat or lower fees for inventory that turns in under six months, higher fees for aged inventory, and a new age-based rate that will make your storage costs easier to predict.”
Carlton Hawkins, principal consultant at Vantage Business Consulting, wrote on Tuesday that the storage split was the real test in the 2027 changes.
“We read 2027 as Amazon pricing for behavior. Amazon says it is adjusting fees ‘to better match our cost of serving different products,’” Hawkins wrote. “In practice, fast-turning inventory that stays in stock is rewarded, and slow, aged stock pays more. The fee change is mostly an inventory management test.”
Hawkins called the 19-cent-per-unit increase on FBA fees “overhyped” in both directions.
“It is not nothing, and it is not a crisis,” Hawkins said. “For most established brands the bigger risk is aged inventory sitting into February and running low enough to trigger the low-inventory fee, at the same time.”
Amazon is not alone in turning a temporary April fuel charge into a longer-term part of pricing. The U.S. Postal Service also installed an 8 percent transportation-related increase in April, and said this month it would convert that charge into a permanent fuel surcharge at 11 percent from Jan. 17, 2027. That comes alongside adjustments that would raise the price to domestically ship a package by 5.9 percent on average.
Both FedEx Ground and UPS Ground Domestic implement their own fuel surcharges in accordance with weekly average on-highway diesel fuel prices. Effective Monday, the companies incorporate 30 percent fuel surcharges as diesel prices sank to under $6 per gallon for the first time in 10 weeks. Diesel prices are still 56 percent above the $3.81 per gallon calculated by the U.S. Energy Information Administration on Feb. 23.
The TD Cowen and AFS Freight Index forecast fourth-quarter ground parcel price increases of 5.8 percent year over year, with express prices expected to jump 13.8 percent.
While fuel has become a major driver for the increases in pricing in recent years, AFS Logistics’ chief analytics officer Mingshu Bates indicated that the surcharges themselves have become more of a revenue lever for carriers, as well as a pass-through mechanism to cover the cost of doing business.
