Fun fact: More people are wearing booze than drinking it. Or rather, they’re preferring boozy perfumes to the bar. Global alcohol consumption is historically at the lowest it’s been in 94 years (according to Gallup), but boozy perfume releases are selling in record numbers. Gen Z and younger generations are far less likely to drink alcohol nowadays, preferring low- and no-alcohol beverages and social settings, and they’re also more likely to spend more money on perfumes.
One might call it sober hedonism—I think, based on cost per ounce, it’s simply a good purchase decision. A great cocktail you drink, and it’s gone. A perfume inspired by the most perfect night out? You can wear it for years, and years to come, and instantly be transported back to that time and place. Which perfumes pull off this boozy trend? I asked some of the most discerning perfumeheads and perfumers around.
“From an evaluation perspective, a successful boozy fragrance is the one that recreates the atmosphere, the experience of enjoying a spirit. But also, the one has the right contrast and balance,” explains Jorgelina Propato, Head of Evaluation Fine Fragrance North America at Givaudan. Perfume evaluators like Jorgelina are essential to analyzing perfume drafts before they reach the consumer, working closely between the client and the perfumer. “When thinking about a boozy fragrance performance on skin, It’s important to maintain tension between the dark and light elements. When the boozy accord is connected to fresh spices, woods, aromatics, or fruity nuances, the fragrance preserves sophistication and indulgence.” Propato notes that as consumers become more sophisticated and adventurous in their fragrance choices, they’re moving past traditional sweet and fruity gourmands, and instead “gravitating toward more nuanced forms of indulgence.”
Jake Levy, co-owner of the niche perfume store Stele in New York, agrees. “You’d think New Yorkers would be over the bar scent, with all our nights out, and still we want more. My thought is that we want the booze without the hangover,” he muses. “There is nostalgia in a good night out. Booze was there for some of our best memories: a parental figure who used to sip a certain drink, or a drink tied to an experience you’ll never forget.” Levy’s picks for the category are Thin Wild Mercury’s Whisky 1969 and Art Brut’s Weltschmerz.
Cathleen Cardinali, the perfumer and owner of Thin Wild Mercury, has perhaps the most perfect bangs you’ll ever see, and her work on Whisky 1969 is as cool as she is. “Whisky 1969 is the only boozy scent you will need. It’s a lumberjack at the end of the day enjoying whisky and a solid cigarette,” notes Levy. Another of her creations, Girl of the Year, is rumored to be Chappell Roan’s favorite.
Weltschmerz is, according to Levy, “a boozy fragrance for those who hate booze. See yourself on the way to dry land, and this one will give you the memory but not the appetite.” There’s peach, cinnamon, whisky, rose, vanilla, and cedar—something quiet and moody, like a cozy but empty bar.
Steve Gontarski, manager at Luckyscent, finds that the draw to boozy fragrances can be divisive. “I’ve met some people who’ve decided to kick drinking and love wearing boozy scents as a substitute … but I’ve equally met those who avoid them because they don’t want to be reminded at all of the experience of drinking. Personally, I love boozy scents throughout the year,” says Gontarski.
He continues: “I love Bonne Chauffe from Frapin, for its perfect balance of cognac and plum. Frapin has been making cognac for generations and more recently added perfume to its portfolio: a very logical and fruitful choice.” it’s a powerful, smoky wood honoring the second distillation in cognac production. Another hitter: Eau Capitale by Diptyque, a decadent rose chypre that feels like drinking wine so dark it's nearly black. In a large, crystal goblet.”
And of course, one cannot forget the boozy perfume par excellence: Angels’ Share by Kilian. “Angels’ Share makes me want to throw on some tartan and hang out by a fireplace,” notes Gontarski. Based on sales units alone, so does everyone else: It’s a runaway bestseller. This feels completely appropriate, considering Kilian of Kilian Hennessy is, in fact, heir to the Hennessy cognac empire. He launched his perfume line in 2007, and boozy liquor notes have been woven into every perfume that debuts. Angels’ Share is a reference to the small amount of alcohol that evaporates as the liquor ages—delivered straight to the angels’ mouths.
My own favorite boozy scent is a little lesser known, but I’d love to put you on to it: Peach Tobacco Oud by Indianapolis-based Maison Saint Charles, one of the most exciting American perfume houses right now. Charles Bradshaw, a self-taught perfumer with just a few years of experience, is very talented at different presentations of boozy perfumes. My favorite of his is Peach Tobacco Oud, with brandy peaches, vanilla, orange blossom, and caramel. It doesn’t sound like it should work—and yet it does, gloriously. As a certified gourmand skeptic, it’s one of the most persuasively sexy perfumes I’ve ever tried. It is decadent and moody, sweet but not—and never—simpering. I think about it with the fondness of a lover who taught me well.
I suppose that’s the distinction I look for in gourmand, boozy perfumes: I don’t want to smell edible, but I’d like to be savored with something close to worship. Liquor notes, in just the right amount, make darkness in perfume feel delicious. They pull depth into sweetness and atmosphere to fruity florals. Peach Tobacco Oud balances the darkness with tenderness, with a self-assured panache that simply gets it right. I don’t drink brandy, but I’ll wear this one, thank you.
