A judge in central Russia’s Tambov region has sentenced former Governor Maxim Yegorov to 11 years in prison after finding him guilty of taking large bribes, state media reported Friday.
Investigators accused Yegorov of accepting around 84 million rubles ($1 million) in cash, property and services between 2022 and 2024 from his friend Roman Stefanov, who at the time headed Gazprom’s subsidiary in the Tambov region.
Yegorov, who served as governor from October 2021 until his sudden resignation in November 2024, denied the corruption charges in court.
Law enforcement authorities arrested Yegorov at his home in July 2025. Since then, he has been held at a pre-trial detention center.
State prosecutors requested a 14-year prison sentence for Yegorov.
In addition to his 11-year sentence at a maximum-security prison, Yegorov was fined 406 million rubles ($4.9 million) and banned from holding public office for 10 years.
The judge also convicted two other defendants in Yegorov’s case after finding them guilty of acting as intermediaries in the bribery scheme. They received prison sentences of seven to eight years and fines of 21 million and 19 million rubles.
