As the discount season begins, DECO warns of irregular promotions: some shops offer sale items that were cheaper days earlier. Check prices and plan purchases to avoid being misled.
Black Friday officially takes place on 27 November, but promotions begin several days earlier, with different retailers announcing discounts and campaigns from the start of the month.
For anyone looking for a "bargain" a few weeks before the Christmas season, it may be possible to find one, but the Portuguese Consumer Protection Association (DECO) issues a warning: not everything that glitters is gold.
On 2 and 3 November, the consumer protection association analysed (source in Portuguese)products from the online shops of four major brands: Auchan, El Corte Inglés, Euronics and FNAC, detecting several infringements of the so-called promotions law, which sets the rules for commercial practices involving price reductions.
"The rules are clear and say that the same shop has to apply the discount to the lowest price of the last 30 consecutive days before the start of the price reduction", Nuno Pais de Figueiredo, DECO spokesperson, told Euronews.
The association's analysis found that in some cases this did not happen, and identified products that are now in promotion that were cheaper a few days before the supposed "sale".
One of the examples listed was a pair of headphones at FNAC, which were being sold for 249.99 euros as part of a Black Friday campaign but which, according to DECO, between 12 and 18 October were sold in the same shop for 199.99 euros, in other words, 50 euros less than at the start of the campaign.
"What often happens is that a week, 10 days before Black Friday, product prices go up", the spokesperson explained. "What actually happens is that in the final accounts the retailer is not giving any discount, it is selling at precisely the same price", he stressed.
Another infringement detected by DECO involves using the recommended sale price set by the manufacturer, producer or supplier (PVPR) as the term of comparison, thereby misleading the consumer, who thinks he or she is buying a product at a discount when in fact that is not the case. The association explains that there are many cases in which the PVPR is accompanied by a percentage or value of discount that does not correspond to a reduction actually applied, which, according to DECO, breaches the law on sales with price reductions.
In addition, even if they do not know the price history of certain products, the information provided by retailers must be clear and transparent, so that consumers can understand how prices have changed. While stating the discount percentage is optional, the new price and the lowest price previously applied must be explicit and visible.
DECO stresses that any consumer can complain when he or she detects irregular practices. "He or she can and should", Nuno Pais de Figueiredo emphasised. "In the shop itself, using the Complaints Book, or by lodging a complaint with DECO PROteste or with ASAE. There are a range of tools available to do this", he explained.
"The fine pays off"
According to DECO, irregularities during Black Friday campaigns cut across different product sectors and happen in different ways, because "the fine pays off".
"The fine is not a deterrent and this is the problem most of the time", Nuno Pais de Figueiredo explained, stressing that every year the association finds irregularities. "It is common and has not decreased, not by a long shot, because again, the amount companies have to pay is not sufficiently dissuasive".
As a result, the association recommends caution and above all preparation before starting the shopping journey.
At a time when all consumers are constantly targeted with information and stimuli to buy, the trick lies in popular wisdom and taking an old saying literally: "When the handout is too big, the poor man gets suspicious".
"Be suspicious first and look closely afterwards", the spokesperson explained. "There are situations where we look at the prices and think: 'how do these people manage to make money, they're almost giving things away'. So you need to be careful", he stressed.
"Everything depends on how people behave. By making an impulse purchase, the consumer risks falling for the trick behind those so-called 'super, mega, hyper promotions'," he said, underlining the need for consumers to know the product they intend to buy well. DECO even offers an online price comparison tool (source in Portuguese)designed to help consumers make more informed decisions.
"In this specific case of Black Friday, avoiding impulse buys and being wary of offers that seem too good to be true ends up helping people to make the best purchase", the spokesperson said who, despite everything, insisted that "there are good deals" at this time of year.
"If you know what you are going to buy, and have clearly decided what you want, you will get good deals".
Marked on the last Friday of November, the well-known Black Friday originated in the United States and marks the start of the Christmas shopping season.
Boosted by online commerce, the occasion has gained ground in European countries, particularly in Portugal, where it is no longer just one day but a month-long event: promotions start at the beginning of the month and, alongside Black Friday, you are likely to hear about Black Week, the week leading up to the main day, and Cyber Monday.
