With World of Warcraft: Forever's first raids set to release in early December, developers of the game have spoken about endgame itemisation and their desire to disrupt the "best-in-slot" (BIS) mindset that's been at the core of WoW's progression for years.
This information stems from the latest episode of the official World of Warcraft: Forever podcast. In it, senior game designer Aidan Moon revealed his philosophy around the game's itemisation, which he played - and plays - a major role in designing.
"I essentially want to get rid of BIS," Moon said. "I think BIS is not a good thing for a video game that's supposed to be about role-playing. That's why I've introduced the creature subtype system, where things are more effective against murlocs [for example]. We've introduced the biome system too, and in the raid it's different."
He gave an example from upcoming raid Barrow Deeps, which will be split into different biomes that offer opportunities for such creature subtype gear to be put to use. "If you go to the left wing that's snowy; if you go to the right wing, that's haunted; if you go down below, that's cavernous. ... I think your BIS should be different for every single boss if you're making a theoretical BIS list."
Moon acknowledged that the system is open for players to interact with to the extent they're comfortable with. Some players might bring optimised gear for certain bosses, while others might not, opting instead for generally good gear to perform well regardless of the fight.
It's an excitingly different approach that goes against the established norms of WoW's endgame raiding. In other versions in World of Warcraft, gear progression is more linear: people go into raids with an idea of what gear drops are best for their character, and intentionally hunt these pieces to replace the set they're wearing. It's a vertical sense of progression. Will theoretically having multiple sets of equipment, for multiple situations, change this?
Best-in-slot gear is an especially pertinent topic in WoW Forever at the moment, given the most dedicated players are reaching max level, or nearing it, and are hunting for items in preparation for the upcoming raid. I'm currently ticking off equipment from a best-in-slot list for a Balance Druid. Whether this player behavior will change when the changes are implemented remains to be seen. Perhaps the approach will expand, with players continuing to hunt down generally accepted 'good' items while also seeking side pieces that provide situational bonuses along the way.
The Blizzard discussion on raid itemisation went beyond standard power items. Chase items, those with especially low drop rates, were also talked about by Moon, noting that Barrow Deeps continues several such items that he hopes will keep players returning to it for years to come.
The items Moon is likely referring to here probably relate to mounts, pets, situational gear, and perhaps campfire bonuses.
