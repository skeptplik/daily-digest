"Oh, it's so on now!" is the tagline in the latest trailer for the video game "Grand Theft Auto VI," or "GTA VI" for short.
"GTA VI" is a game of superlatives that's been crashing through records. The hype around it is unparalleled. No game in history has clocked so many pre-orders. It's thought that around 10 million people have already purchased it. There's no denying it: "GTA VI" is a global cultural phenomenon.
A few days before its release on November 19, fans can already start downloading the game. The file is so large that downloading it can take several days, depending on the internet connection. Providers have already registered a significant spike in data traffic.
As soon as the game is unlocked, players can start speeding through the fictional US metropolis of Vice City in a stylish sports car with criminal couple Jason and Lucia.
Miami gets cast as Vice City
To mark the launch of "Grand Theft Auto VI," its real-life inspiration — Miami — is getting a full-scale "Vice City" makeover. This promotional push includes the Miami Heat basketball team stepping onto the hardwood in custom "Vice City" jerseys for a home game, a local festival featuring artists from the game's official soundtrack, and roadside "Vice City" signage installed across the area.
Expectations for "GTA VI" are sky-high. Nearly 5,000 people have been working on the game for more than 10 years. Development costs are estimated at around $1 billion (€900 million).
"Based on those figures alone — as well as the series' extremely successful and popular track record — this can certainly be described as a truly exceptional release," says Rudolf Thomas Inderst, cultural studies scholar and professor of Game Design and Game Studies at Germany's Neu-Ulm University of Applied Sciences.
When such an effort is put into a product, it's only natural for people to expect great things, he adds. Inderst is expecting "GTA VI" to showcase the absolute best of what modern gaming can do across gameplay, storytelling, visuals and tech.
Social satire and gameplay freedom
This video game series has been around for almost three decades, setting new standards through its different versions. A biting satire on US society and the American dream, "GTA" parodies politics, culture and the media.
The game is also renowned for its gameplay freedom and spectacularly staged chaos in a crazy world where players can do whatever they please. Shoot down helicopters with a bazooka, go to the hairdresser's, take a stroll on the beach, go to a nightclub, take part in car races and much more.
What makes it a standout among video games is how its NPCs — non-player characters, those not being controlled by a human player — are brought to life. They respond based on a player's interactions with them, displaying individualized behaviors and traits ranging from fear to hostility. "GTA VI" has an estimated 600,000 animations for the civilians in the fictional state of Leonida.
'Grand Theft Auto,' a long-running hit
"GTA VI" is essentially a huge adult playground, striking a chord across several generations. Millennials grew up with "Grand Theft Auto III," "Grand Theft Auto: Vice City," "Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas," and "Grand Theft Auto IV," while Gen Z grew up with "Grand Theft Auto V." Generation Alpha might still be too young for the latest edition, but will likely find ways to play it anyway.
Social media has certainly played a part in the series' ongoing success, particularly "GTA V" and its multiplayer spin-off, "GTA Online." Numerous content creators stream gameplay sessions on Twitch or YouTube, and particularly funny or crazy gameplay moments end up on TikTok.
At the same time, a dedicated role-play community has grown around the game. Players gather inside the world of "GTA" to act out specific characters, such as taxi drivers, judges or small-time criminals. They invent realistic backstories for their personas, and many even use accents to stay in character.
Perfectionism has its downsides
The hype surrounding "GTA VI" is not only good news for the game itself but could also boost the entire games industry by drawing more people back to their consoles. This is because the boom triggered by the COVID-19 pandemic was followed by studio closures and redundancies. Worldwide, it's estimated that 50,000 people in the games industry have lost their jobs since 2022.
But while publisher Rockstar Games is well-known for the meticulous polish of its titles and attention to detail, the perfectionism so prized by fans also has its drawbacks, especially for those working on the game. Rockstar, like many other game developers, has frequently faced major criticism regarding its labor practices.
Former developers report extremely long working hours and a heavy workload that resulted in health issues. Trade unions accuse company managers of employee surveillance, resulting in a culture of workplace stress and low morale.
Coinciding with the launch of Rockstar's largest game to date, the developers behind it are pushing for trade union recognition. That move would give them a collective voice to address working hours, pay, pensions and parental leave. If successful, their campaign could set a major precedent for the wider video game industry, which continues to face challenges around fair labor practices.
