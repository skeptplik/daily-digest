Jensen Huang's future rests in the balance as Nvidia board reportedly meet
Nvidia’s board is meeting to decide whether to oust CEO Jensen Huang, a source familiar with the matter told the Reuters news agency, as the world’s dominant supplier of AI processors grapples with reports of two extramarital affairs involving its longtime leader.
The reports mark a sharp escalation in a controversy that began with revelations about Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, a woman US intelligence has said was involved in facilitating shipments of Nvidia chips to Chinese firms.
In a memo to employees on Sunday, Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife and took “full responsibility,” but gave no details about the relationship.
Huang, 63, has no obvious successor and owns about 3.6% of Nvidia’s stock. He also has dozens of direct reports, underscoring the tight control he maintains over the company.
The company’s shares slid about 8% in after-hours trading after the Wall Street Journal first reported that Nvidia was preparing to oust Huang. Including Wednesday’s after-hours selloff, Nvidia has lost about $600bn in stock market value this week since the board announced its investigation into his relationship with a Chinese businesswoman.
Earlier in the day, the New York Times reported that Huang had also had an extramarital affair with a company executive who reported to him.
In pre-market trading, US chipmakers Intel, Marvell Technology and Micron are down between 1.8% and 2.7%, while AI infrastructure company CoreWeave slid 5.7%. The Nasdaq-listed Dutch company Nebius reversed earlier losses to trade 1% higher.
The declines, if maintained when Wall Street opens, would wipe out Wednesday’s gains, when strong results from CoreWeave, an American specialised GPU cloud-computing provider, lifted AI stocks.
Last night, we reported that Nvidia appointed a leading US law firm to conduct a “thorough and independent” investigation into the relationship between its chief executive, Jensen Huang, and an alleged Chinese spy.
The $4.1tn (£3tn) chipmaker’s board has hired New York-based Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz for the investigation, which will be led by the Wachtell partner Joon Kim. The firm’s website describes Kim, a former government prosecutor in New York, as a specialist in areas including internal investigations, white-collar criminal and regulatory defence, and crisis management.
A committee of independent board directors, assisted by Kim and Wachtell, will oversee the investigation.

Who could potentially replace Jensen Huang if Nvidia ousts him?
Jonathan Yerushalmy
Jensen Huang has led Nvidia since it was founded in 1993, but with the CEO now unexpectedly on the backfoot, and with no known successor in the wings, the field is wide open should the board decide to oust Huang.
Agence France-Presse has looked at some of the names being suggested most frequently as Huang’s potential successor.
Internal hire
With more than 40,000 employees, Nvidia could opt to go with an in-house pick to take the helm, including one of Huang’s dozens of direct reports.
Tech publications frequently cited Nvidia’s chief financial officer Colette Kress as an easy choice. Another longtime Nvidia executive is Ian Buck, who oversees the company’s datacentre clients.
Business magazine Inc. also lists Nvidia’s vice-president of applied deep learning research, Bryan Catanzano, as a possible pick.
A family affair
Huang could also try to position a member of his own family as his replacement. His daughter Madison Huang is currently the senior director of product marketing at Nvidia and is among the names most suggested by analysts.
His son Spencer Huang could also be a potential successor. He currently works at Nvidia as the director of product management in the company’s robotics division.
Huang’s cousin, Lisa Su, could also be a viable candidate for selection; she is the longtime CEO of semiconductor giant AMD.
Tech CEO swap
Su is not the only tech chief being bandied around as a possible replacement for Huang.
Rene Haas, CEO of UK chip design firm Arm, is another candidate being suggested by the tech press.
Speculation runs wide in Silicon Valley: former Apple CEO Tim Cook – who stepped down from leading the tech company in August after 15 years at the helm – is another person insiders say could be tapped.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has been one of the poster boys for the AI revolution, because his company’s chips are essential for the technology. But the $4tn company had more humble beginnings.
Huang was born in Taiwan and then spent some time in Thailand because his father moved the family there for work. His parents then sent him to live with an uncle in the US when he was 9, along with his older brother. Huang’s parents later came to the US and the family reunited in Oregon, where he attended high school, graduating early after skipping two grades.
At Oregon State University, Huang studied electrical engineering in a class that had about 250 students, only three of whom were women, including his future wife, Lori, who was on his lab team.
The couple went on to work as chip designers in Silicon Valley, with Huang earning his graduate degree from Stanford University in 1992.
Nvidia got its start when three engineers – Huang, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem – gathered in 1993 at a Denny’s diner in San Jose, California, in what is now the heart of Silicon Valley.
Huang worked at a Denny’s restaurant when he was young and has spoken about how working there helped him overcome his shyness and to learn the value of hard work. On his LinkedIn profile he lists only two work experiences – as Nvidia’s founder and CEO, and as a dishwasher, busboy and waiter at Denny’s.
At that first meeting, the three men discussed building a computer chip that would make graphics for video games faster and more realistic. That conversation, and ones that followed, led to the founding of Nvidia.
Nvidia’s specialised graphics processing units (GPUs) were designed to rapidly process computer graphics for video games, but are now also widely used to power the booming spread of artificial intelligence systems.
Huang is now one of the world’s wealthiest people, with a personal fortune that Forbes estimates at more than $150bn.

Taiwan, a dominant player in the global semiconductor industry, sits at the centre of strategic competition between the US and China, which claims the self-ruled island as part of its territory.
