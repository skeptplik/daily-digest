Rachel Dixon’s article (The root-to-tip revolution! An A-Z of how to eat plants in full – for extra flavour, fibre and savings, 17 November) shows how we can make healthy food go further, and the opportunity is bigger than many of us realise. The food we throw away at home costs an average household of four around £1,000 a year, and much of it is good fruit and vegetables that could have been eaten.
It’s not just peel and stalks we bin. WRAP found that date labels on fresh produce got in the way of people’s judgment, so more people said they would throw it out. Retailers have since removed those labels at scale, making it easier for all of us to use more of what we buy by default, and WRAP estimates that could save 50,000 tonnes a year.
With food prices still rising, WRAP’s research found the majority of people are cost-conscious at Christmas. The government’s Be Ready campaign asks us to stock up, but that only works if we use what we store.
Another F is for fridge: keeping most fruit and veg below 5C keeps it fresher for longer. Making the most of what we already have adds up – for our pockets, our health and the planet.
