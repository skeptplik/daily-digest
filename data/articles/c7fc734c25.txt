Seoul – South Korea’s education minister resigned Sunday over an AI-assisted cheating scandal that erupted ahead of the country’s key college entrance exams last week.
The country has been gripped by fears of AI-assisted exam cheating since last Sunday, when the top scorer in a national tax accountant exam confessed to using a miniature camera and smartphone to achieve a near-perfect score, just four days before the crucial college entrance exam known as the Suneung.
The annual nine-hour Suneung is widely seen as a ticket to prestigious universities, well-paid jobs and even better marriage prospects in the highly competitive country.
The confession sent officials scrambling to roll out a slew of preventive measures — including blocking public Wi-Fi in testing areas and placing stickers over clothing buttons — but Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin struggled to reassure the public, particularly after remarks he made three days before the exam.
“The exam concluded without any major incidents. ... However, as the minister in charge, I do not believe I am immune from public criticism that we failed to prepare adequately in advance,” Choi wrote on X.
“Now that the exam is over, I take full responsibility and hereby offer my resignation,” he added.
President Lee Jae Myung has accepted his resignation, the presidential office said.
Asked during a parliamentary hearing on Monday whether he was confident that authorities could catch would-be cheaters on exam day, the 72-year-old replied: “Due to my age, I am not confident I could.”
His response quickly drew public backlash.
Choi did not appear at press conferences in the days leading up to Thursday’s exam, where a vice minister instead announced the preventive measures.
Officials said 111 cases of misconduct involving electronic devices were identified during Thursday’s exam, nearly half of which were detected through three emergency alerts sent by the government to identify smartphones that test takers had failed to hand over.
