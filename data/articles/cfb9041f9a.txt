Anthony Mackie’s Sam Wilson/Captain America will be leading the Avengers in Avengers: Doomsday, guiding Thor (Chris Hemsworth), Ant-Man (Paul Rudd), Shang-Chi (Simu Liu), and Falcon (Danny Ramirez) in the fight against Doctor Doom. But while Sam is getting a big promotion, Mackie said he was lied to about his next starring role.
Mackie plays FBI Special Agent Jack Strickland in 12 12 12, a heist thriller that premiered on Apple TV on Nov. 13. Jack’s partner was killed in a bank robbery gone wrong, and since then he’s been doggedly hunting the perpetrators, criminal mastermind Paul Galvin (Jamie Dornan), and his older brother and gunman Bobby Galvin (Jack Kesy). Jack tracked them to Zurich, where he believes they’re planning their next big job.
“This was pitched to me as ‘Imagine you in The Italian Job,’” Mackie told Polygon in a virtual interview. “I was like, ‘I’m in.’ It wasn’t that at all. They lied to me. I would have never taken this job.”
Mackie said that last part with a laugh, but he is bummed he doesn’t get to cruise around in a Mini Cooper, which is what he said makes The Italian Job his favorite heist movie. Series creators Dudi Appleton and Jim Keeble tried a different way to get Dornan into his role as the master thief, but the actor didn’t do the homework.
“Jim and Dudi sent me [a movie] that I didn’t watch,” he told Polygon. “It was French. That’s not fair. I did watch some of it. I can’t remember the name of it, but it felt removed from our show where it’s in Europe but all the main players were Americans. It felt to me not incredibly useful. I’m an Oceans 12 guy. Oceans 12, Oceans 13, and 14.”
Filming 12 12 12 was “headache-inducing”
12 12 12 takes its name from the FBI Principles of Crime, which hold that it takes 12 months to plan a high-value robbery, 12 hours to pull it off, and that law enforcement only has 12 days after the crime to successfully catch the culprits. Each episode of the show is split into three different timelines representing these periods, and filming was similarly scattered.
“It was all over the map,” Dornan said. “It was tough. You’d have days where you’d be doing scenes from different blocks, so you’d be working with different directors on one day. That’s problematic for everyone involved. That was just the situation because of the locations we were in, and the way the timelines jump around. It’s tricky. There’s a lot of needing to check in with people and track where your characters were at any given point and what had happened and what hadn’t happened. As you can imagine, it was quite headache-inducing a lot of the time. It was different from a lot of other jobs you do, which are a bit easier to track.”
Appleton and Keeble hope that structure will keep viewers on their toes, splitting more methodical planning with high-stakes action and chase scenes. The first two episodes of 12 12 12 are streaming on Apple TV now, with new episodes releasing weekly on Fridays through Dec. 25. The big Zurich heist takes place on Christmas Eve and the chase starts on Christmas Day, making it perfect holiday viewing.
