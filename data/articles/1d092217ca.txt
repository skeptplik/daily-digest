Cyberleek has returned for the launch of GTA 6 to allegedly release a near complete Xbox build of the game — and has threatened to release its executable in just days.
It was thought that the person or group responsible for the online Cyberleek persona had ended their spate of GTA 6 leaks last month, after their memecoin crashed despite efforts to boost its market cap.
In October, Cyberleek had tied various types of GTA 6 gameplay leaks to the Cyberleek market cap, and threatened to release a build of the game — supposedly over 200 GB in size — if the memecoin hit a $30 million market cap. Gameplay clips reportedly leaked at the time allegedly showed Lucia fully naked — something that is not possible in the final launch version of the game.
Yesterday, November 19 — the GTA 6 release date — Cyberleek returned to drop a GTA 6 Xbox build without the game's executable. In a statement reportedly published online, Cyberleek claimed this Xbox build is a “full November 2026 build of GTA 6.” They predicted this Xbox build would be used to emulate GTA 6 on PC “within a week, running far past 30fps.”
The console version of GTA 6 targets 30fps across all platforms, although struggles at times to hit that on base PS5 and Xbox Series X and S.
Cyberleek has revived their crypto scheme, promising to release the executable immediately if it hits a $15 million market cap. While the CyberLeek memecoin was boosted in value by the news to $1.9 million yesterday, it is unlikely to get anywhere near the target.
Still, Cyberleek has vowed to release the final file anyway on November 23, whether the memecoin hits the market cap or not.
The release of a full and recent build of GTA 6 would of course be hugely damaging for Rockstar and parent company Take-Two, which appears to have been unable to deter Cyberleek’s actions in any meaningful way since their spate of leaks began.
It would also run counter to the thought that Cyberleek only had access to an early development build of GTA 6, a feeling that was reinforced not only by the videos that were released, but by the differences that have emerged following the game’s official launch.
Fans are calling on people not to spend money on Cyberleek, pointing out the leaker cashed out back in August and did again in October. Analysis of cryptocurrency transactions in late August pointed to the GTA 6 leaker cashing out having earnt around $270,000.
IGN had reported on fan skepticism around the alleged leaked gameplay that reportedly showed both Jason and Lucia fully naked. A previous leaked gameplay video allegedly showed a tank spawning into the world, which suggested access to a dev menu of some kind. The leaked videos allegedly showing both Jason and Lucia fully naked also show NPCs failing to react to that in any way, which suggested this effect was achieved with either a dev menu or cheats.
Cyberleek had threatened Rockstar and Take-Two with the release of a full playthrough of the game’s story if they failed to release GTA 6 on-disc and issue a public apology — and the market cap of the memecoin hit $30 million. Of course, few believe these GTA 6 leaks are about campaigning for physical media — rather they're about making money from a crypto scam.
Cyberleek appeared to go dark after a spate of high-profile GTA 6 leaks in August that sparked an investigation from Take-Two. The company issued subpoenas to the likes of Discord as part of the hunt for the person or group of people responsible. It remains unclear how the gameplay footage, or any potential build of the game it's taken from, escaped the confines of Rockstar's infamous tight security.
In August, Rockstar addressed the GTA 6 leaks, describing the situation as "heartbreaking" for its development team and "unfortunate" for fans. At the time, the developer acknowledged that its intended experience for players "may now be impacted by some spoilers," but reiterated GTA 6's previously-confirmed launch date of November 19.
Whether the culprit or culprits are ever caught may rest on how successful Take-Two's legal team and the relevant authorities are at forcing social networks or crypto exchanges to divulge any personal details they hold on those responsible.
