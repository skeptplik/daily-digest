Final Fantasy 14 is between expansions at the moment, with Evercold preparing for release in February. So, in order to keep the game populated for the next few months, Square Enix is running a special promotion in hopes of tempting players back in.
This promotion, which began yesterday, offers players a choice between two enticing freebies. Lapsed players who haven't logged in for over six months are to be given 30 days of free game time to hop back in and catch up with the main story quest ahead of Evercold's release.
In addition, there's an alluring character skip available too. If your FF14 character is at least level 90 on one class - the current level-cap is 100 - you can skip all the way to the final part of the latest Dawntrail expansion. You still need to grind to level 100 in preparation for Evercold, but the aforementioned free month of game time should provide plenty of time to do so.
In a producer letter written to accompany this promotion, Naoki Yoshida provided insight into why this was being implemented, stating that retaining players in an MMO like FF14 was of the "utmost importance".
"Encouraging players to return is no mean feat", Yoshida wrote. "People pause their adventures for all kinds of reasons, including dissatisfaction and loss of interest. Although we continually update the game to address such issues, the fact remains that getting players to resume their journey is a difficult task indeed.
"For one, I feel that the pace of change in our lives is growing increasingly fast. Back when A Realm Reborn launched, I set policies in place based on the idea that significant changes in life usually happened in roughly three-year cycles. With that in mind, we've done our best to avoid heavy-handed attempts to secure player retention through game design, and we still maintain that philosophy even today."
Yoshida went on to explain that promotions such as this are meant as a "tiny nudge". He notes that those who have retained subscriptions continuously remain "the most important" players in the development team's eyes, and acknowledges that the process of catching up in an MMO can seem "daunting" for many.
This problem of player retention is nothing new for MMOs, especially in the tricky time between the end of a current expansion and the beginning of a new one. MMO popularity often waxes and wanes, with a new expansion acting as an exciting call to action, with the months leading up to it often serving as a natural break-point for many. Still, monthly subs pay the bills, so keeping folks around even during these quiet months is important.
Final Fantasy 14: Evercold is looking like an especially exciting expansion. On top of the new story and two classes being added, the game is getting an overhaul to combat, with both classic Reborn and Evolved action modes, as well as a new raid difficulty meant to bridge the gap between normal and savage tiers.
The game has certainly come a long way since its initial release back in 2010. Dawntrail proved divisive among fans too, though with Evercold there's a lot of hope that a new overarching storyline can bring the steal back the hearts of players old and new.
