Midwives who will graduate in Wales next year have been told there are fewer jobs available for them than expected.
The Royal College of Midwives said this is in addition to the 66 newly qualified midwives who didn't manage to find a job after they graduated last year.
Director of RCM Cymru Julie Richards said the situation was "unacceptable" when maternity services are "facing pressure from every direction".
The Welsh government said it recognised the concerns around employment uncertainty and it was working with NHS Wales "to understand workforce pressures and requirements".
Kimmy Williams, 37, from Swansea has had to return to her old job working in the kitchen at a local college because there aren't enough midwifery graduate jobs available in Wales.
Williams said she's aware that almost half of her peers on the midwifery course at Swansea University are yet to find a job despite the promise of employment for everyone once they graduated.
Her dream job was to become a midwife after having children of her own 16 years ago, but she has instead been left with "a mountain of debt and no job at the end of it".
"Now I'm just working in the same job I was previously but worse off financially," she added.
Williams said she would accept minimum wage to do the job initially just to be able to get into the system and progress her career.
The Royal College of Midwives said students across Wales are facing the same "deepening crisis".
Megan Lewis, from Cardiff, said it's hard to understand why so many newly qualified midwives in Wales, like her, are not able to get a job after witnessing first hand how "stretched" services can become.
"Midwives regularly told us how much they valued having students on shift because we were another pair of hands to help with care," she said.
Midwifery students have their tuition fees paid by NHS Wales on the condition they commit to working in the country for up to two years after they graduate.
Although a number of jobs may come up at some health boards, RCM Cymru said it had not been confirmed how many vacancies will become available, but they were "challenging the suggestion that improved retention is behind the lack of jobs for newly qualified midwives in Wales".
Richards said RCM Cymru's members had told them they were working "under enormous pressure".
"They are working above their contracted hours, they're missing breaks, many are having their training cancelled," she added.
"Quite clearly there is a need for these graduate midwives to enter into the workforce now."
Health Education and Improvement Wales (HEIW) said the NHS would not be running a national recruitment round in spring 2027 as "very few roles" are expected to become available.
HEIW said midwife turnover in NHS Wales is at its lowest level for over 16 years meaning "fewer roles have become available than expected".
It also said financial pressures have limited the number of Band 5 roles health boards can offer to newly qualified midwives.
Chief executive Alex Howells said HEIW was "working with health boards to establish what roles they can offer and how graduates can apply."
