In short:
Two men have been charged after Melbourne Cup trophies and a Caulfield Cup were stolen during a series of break-ins at Melbourne racecourses during the Spring Racing Carnival.
Police today revealed a fourth Melbourne Cup was taken during the heists, adding to the theft of three cups won by Makybe Diva.
What's next?
The stolen cups remain missing.
A fourth Melbourne Cup has been stolen as police reveal the brazen theft of Makybe Diva's three Melbourne Cups and a Caulfield Cup are allegedly linked.
Police have charged Sama Mirzaagha and Chrysovalandis Tassakos over the heists, which unfolded over several days during Melbourne's cup carnival.
The 1948 Caulfield Cup was allegedly swiped from Caulfield Racecourse during several break-ins at the track between November 4 and November 11.
On Monday, thieves believed to be dressed as tradies stole legendary mare Makybe Diva's three consecutive Melbourne Cups, worth $3 million, from Flemington Racecourse.
Police now allege both robberies are linked, and revealed that a fourth Melbourne Cup, a replica, had been stolen.
It was initially believed three cups won by Makybe Diva in 2003, 2004 and 2005 were the only items stolen from Flemington but police also allege a cup was stolen four days earlier.
The Victoria Racing Club has remained tight-lipped about whether it was aware of the first robbery before Makybe Diva's cups were taken.
Charged over the Flemington heist, Mr Mirzaagha, who was out on bail at the time, was not present for a brief hearing in the Melbourne Magistrates' Court, where his lawyers hit out at police.
"There seems to have been a rush to charge, and investigating later," lawyer Justin Riazaty said.
Mr Riazaty called the prosecution brief "flimsy" and said there were "serious deficiencies" in the case against his client.
However, prosecutor Jonathon McCarthy said the evidence included DNA, fingerprints and a large amount of CCTV footage. He also referred to Mr Mirzaagha and Mr Tassakos as being co-accused.
Further details about the alleged offending were not read out in court, and Magistrate Simon Zebrowski refused to publicly release a remand summary filed by police.
Mr Riazaty did not apply for bail on behalf of his client but said an application would be made "in due course".
He said Mr Mirzaagha was normally prescribed medication and requested to be seen by a nurse in custody.
Mr Mirzaagha's charges include burglary, theft and dealing with property suspected of being the proceeds of crime.
During an earlier hearing, Mr Mirzaagha's co-accused, Mr Tassakos, was granted bail on charges of burglary, theft, handling stolen goods, dealing with property suspected of being proceeds of crime, obtaining financial advantage and obtaining property by deception.
Mr Tassakos is charged in relation to both alleged heists and was supported in court by his parents.
It comes after police raided a Mount Waverley home in Melbourne's east as part of their hunt for the stolen cups, which remain missing.
Both men are expected to face court again in February.
