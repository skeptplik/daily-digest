A threat actor breached the internal network of Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and accessed two systems containing customer, employee, and contractor data.
The attack exposed details of more than 1.5 million individuals before the company blocked access from external IP addresses and suspended the affected services.
KEPCO is South Korea’s main electric power company and a state-controlled, major operator of the country’s critical infrastructure.
The organization detected the attack on November 20, when it confirmed unauthorized access to its KEPCO ON electricity billing platform and its electronic procurement system (SRM).
“On Friday, November 20, Korea Electric Power Corporation (hereafter KEPCO), led by President Kim Dong-cheol, confirmed that some customer information held in its IT systems had been exposed,” the power company states.
“The company is making every effort to prevent harm.”
ON is an integrated customer platform that allows customers to view and pay electricity bills. SRM is the company’s procurement system for contracting services.
According to the announcement, published on Sunday, the following was exposed:
- for 650,487 KEPCO ON customers: names, ID information, phone numbers, addresses, bank names, bank account numbers, account holder names, card issuers, card numbers, cardholder names, and electricity charge and billing information.
- for 426,827 company representatives: names, ID information, email addresses, and company addresses.
- for 442,934 KEPCO employees: names, affiliation, employee ID number, mobile phone number, email address, company phone number.
The company says the data breach resulted from “advanced technologies such as AI,” but it shared no specific details about the initial access method or the attack chain.
KEPCO is notifying exposed individuals directly, has filed a report with the data protection authorities, and is coordinating with government and financial authorities to block abuse such as unauthorized transactions.
Potentially exposed individuals are warned that the exposed information could be used for targeted phishing and impersonation. They are advised to report any attempts to the police or Korea’s Internet and Security Agency.
It is also recommended to avoid installing unknown apps, entering information into suspicious forms, and sharing sensitive data or verification codes over calls or texts.
A threat actor using the “Noiem” alias has claimed the attack and advertised the breach on a hacker forum on November 19, saying that they had KEPCO user data, supplier records, and bidding and contract documents.
South Korea’s Personal Information Protection Commission separately confirmed that KEPCO received an email from a hacker claiming data theft on November 19.
While these claims match the available evidence, attribution to Noiem remains unconfirmed.
