Jai Opetaia's eyebrow-raising move to heavyweight to fight Deontay Wilder is over after the Australian withdrew from their bout.
The Gold Coast-based Opetaia (31-0) was set to move up from cruiserweight to face former world champion Wilder on the undercard to Tyson Fury and Anthony Joshua's Cardiff blockbuster next month.
"Fighter to fighter, I know the feeling, and it's frustrating. I've always prided myself on fighting anyone, anyplace," Opetaia posted on social media on Wednesday night.
He rubbished suggestions he'd pulled out of the fight because of Wilder's "big right hand".
"The goal has never changed and still, like I've always said, remains the same. Undisputed," he said.
"They all said I couldn't after signing with Zuffa Boxing, everything always against us, stripped and vacated multiple times … but you watch.
"I'm coming to spoil the party."
The fight was seen as a lucrative, profile-raising option — albeit a risky one given the power of Wilder — to stay busy before a potential cruiserweight clash with David Benavidez next year.
Opetaia's co-promoter Mick Francis said Opetaia had aggravated a hand injury first sustained during his September bout against Noel Mikaelian.
"This is a disappointing setback for Jai, who was looking forward to what promised to be a cracking fight. His focus now is on making a full recovery and continuing his quest to become the undisputed cruiserweight champion of the world," he said.
Benavidez and Opetaia agreed in the ring after Opetaia's last win to face each other next May.
The two-division champion holds the WBO and WBA's cruiserweight belts and will face WBC strap-holder Michal Cieslak in a unification battle next month.
And the WBC had installed the Australian as the mandatory challenger for the winner of that fight.
Opetaia was stripped of his IBF title for fighting for the unsanctioned Zuffa belt he now holds, alongside his enduring Ring champion status.
The Australian beat former WBC champion Mikaelian, who gave up his belt for a shot at Opetaia.
Opetaia remains in a court battle with the IBF to reclaim that belt and, if successful, could walk into a clash with Benavidez with all four major organisations' belts on the line.
Meanwhile, contenders have lined up to replace Opetaia on the card, with Derek Chisora, Richard Riakporhe and even fellow Australian and Tasman Fighters stablemate Justis Huni floated as options.
