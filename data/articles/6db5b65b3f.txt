Patrick Stewart's final on-screen performance will arrive in December's Avengers: Doomsday, and Marvel is making sure the late actor receives a fitting farewell.
Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige and directors Anthony and Joe Russo are planning to include a tribute to Stewart in the upcoming superhero blockbuster, according to a new interview with Variety. Stewart died on Oct. 29 at age 86 following a brief battle with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy Body Dementia.
The actor had already completed filming for Doomsday, in which he reprises his iconic role as Professor Charles Xavier for the ninth and final time. Speaking to Variety, Feige revealed that Stewart's last day on set was an emotional occasion, with the actor delivering a speech to the crew.
"He stood up and he gave a speech to the crew," Feige said. "We didn't know necessarily it would be his last day on a set, but he was sort of indicating to us that he thought it was."
Anthony Russo also recalled how difficult filming had become for Stewart as he struggled with his health. Despite the challenges, the actor remained committed to delivering his performance.
"For him to have muscled through and delivered the performance that he did in that movie, and the experience that he delivered for all of us as collaborators, it made it intensely emotional," Russo said.
Although principal photography has wrapped, Feige said Marvel is now figuring out how to properly honor its longtime collaborator.
"We're long done filming that movie, and he did an amazing job for us," Feige told Variety. "We're looking now at how we can put something together that honors and respects our friend."
Professor X and Magneto reunite one last time
The tribute will carry particular significance given Stewart's history with the X-Men franchise. He first played Professor X in 2000's X-Men, becoming one of the defining faces of the superhero series alongside Ian McKellen's Magneto. The two actors will reunite in Doomsday, with Joe Russo revealing that their characters share an emotional scene about forgiveness.
"In a lot of ways, Xavier's the conscience in the movie," Russo said. "He has a lovely scene with Ian McKellen, who's his best friend, in the film. It's a very emotional scene about forgiveness, and I think very resonant in the wake of his passing."
Stewart's death prompted an outpouring of tributes from fans, fellow actors, and gaming communities. Players of No Man's Sky, for example, began naming planets and entire star systems after the actor, with some creating virtual memorials inspired by his portrayal of Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation.
Avengers: Doomsday arrives in theaters on Dec. 18.
