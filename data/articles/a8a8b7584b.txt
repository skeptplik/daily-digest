Homes and properties on a picturesque remote island are under threat from an out-of-control bushfire that has sent smoke hundreds of kilometres south.
The blaze on Flinders Island in Bass Strait spread significantly on Wednesday after being started by lightning strikes.
Some structures had been destroyed but it was too early to tell whether they were homes, the Tasmania Fire Service incident controller Steven Richardson said.
A Bureau of Meteorology post on X featured a video showing a vast column of smoke being blown south across north-east Tasmania, as northerly winds pushed the plume across Bass Strait.
As of Wednesday night, three emergency warnings were in place, including for the coastal settlement of Killiecrankie and nearby areas Leeka, Mount Tanner, Mount Boyes and Carnacs Flat.
Some residents were warned it was too late to leave.
Roughly 3,000 hectares had been burned by the fire on Tuesday, before it spread south on Wednesday following a wind change.
Residents on the island, which is about 130,000 hectares in size and home to 900 people, posted photos of massive plumes of smoke on social media.
Mick Grimshaw, a farmer, helped friends move cattle from their property and said they had lost some fences.
“We were under the ashes. Right there. But we’ve got them safe,” he told ABC radio.
“The fire threat for this farm looks like it has abated a bit but certainly not further down the island.
“We can see the smoke front heading right down the guts of the island.”
Nearly 40 firefighters were working to control the bushfire, plus 12 parks and wildlife staff, five helicopters and three aircraft.
“Additional resources are being deployed and Tasmania Police is also helping evacuate people, particularly in the Leeka and Mount Tanner areas,” parks minister Nick Duigan said.
“There is a lot of smoke in the area and it’s now coming down Tasmania’s coast.”
Several roads have been closed because of poor visibility due to smoke.
Flinders Island is known for high-quality exports, including beef, lamb, wool and seafood.
