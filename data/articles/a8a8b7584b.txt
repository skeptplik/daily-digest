Residents in the north of Flinders Island in Tasmania have been told to take shelter, as an out-of-control bushfire destroys homes and sends smoke hundreds of kilometres south.
By Thursday midday, the uncontrolled fire involving 10-metre high flames had burned through 14,500 hectares – about 10% of the island – with at least six properties damaged or destroyed and authorities warning more were at risk.
Steve Richardson, the northern deputy regional chief for the TFS, said the fire front was making a “significant run” towards the south-west of the island on Thursday, after “horrendous conditions” on Wednesday made it too dangerous for firefighting crews to get close to the flames.
Emergency services warned residents in the Killicrankie and Mt Killicrankie recreation area it was too late to leave. “You are in danger and need to act immediately to survive,” the warning said, with the fire threatening evacuation routes.
Residents were told to enact their bushfire plan, shelter in a room with clear exits, bring pets inside, and close all doors and windows. Killiecrankie beach was identified as a place of last resort.
Communities in nearby Leeka, Mt Tanner, Mt Boyes, Carnacs Flat and surrounds have been warned it was not safe to return, while a smoke alert was issued for the island and the north-east and east coast of Tasmania.
Parts of the island in the path of the fire contain highly flammable material, which has strengthened the blaze.
“These conditions have made it unsafe for crews to undertake direct firefighting operations in some areas,” Richardson said in a statement.
“The situation is changing quickly, and emergency alerts are being updated regularly.”
Three other areas in the northern part of Flinders Island remain under a watch and act warning, with residents urged to prepare to take shelter or to leave.
Phone service may be disrupted due to impacts to communications systems, but officials said triple zero services were being prioritised.
Weather conditions were expected to deteriorate again on Friday, with strong winds expected.
The Tasmanian premier, Jeremy Rockliff, said there would be government emergency assistance grants for people on the island who had to relocate due to the fires.
Applications for the grants – which included $250 per adult and $125 per child, with a maximum of $1,000 for any one family – would open from 2pm Thursday, Rockliff said in a social media post.
“For those on the frontline fighting the fire, thank you,” Rockliff said. “We could not be more grateful for your efforts.”
Residents on the island, which is about 130,000 hectares in size and home to 900 people, posted photos of massive plumes of smoke on social media.
Mick Grimshaw, a farmer, helped friends move cattle from their property and said they had lost some fences.
“We were under the ashes. Right there. But we’ve got them safe,” he told ABC radio.
“The fire threat for this farm looks like it has abated a bit but certainly not further down the island.
“We can see the smoke front heading right down the guts of the island.”
Nearly 40 firefighters were working to control the bushfire, plus 12 parks and wildlife staff, five helicopters and three aircraft. Nine additional vehicles arrived on Thursday.
Flinders Island is known for high-quality exports, including beef, lamb, wool and seafood.
