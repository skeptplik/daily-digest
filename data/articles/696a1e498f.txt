Actors in the UK are to be given more rights over their “digital replicas” as part of a new agreement on AI protections with TV and film producers, secured after a growing on-set backlash.
Under a wide-ranging new deal secured by Equity, the actors’ union, performers will have the rights to be paid when their digital replicas are used and to consent to whether their digital replica is used or not.
Approval must be secured from an actor if a digital double or digital alteration is used to make “substantive changes” to scenes involving agreed levels of nudity or simulated sexual activity.
The agreement also makes clear that producers may not use a digital replica during a strike “in any circumstance where consent is required for such use”.
Contracts will now make clear that producers only obtain permission for a specific project, rather than obtaining “blanket consent” to an actor’s digital replica.
The deal, signed after years of talks with the production body Producers Alliance for Cinema and Television (Pact), marks an attempt to create some ground rules for the use of an actor’s image, with AI developing rapidly.
The three-year deal will be put to a vote among 8,000 Equity members, with the union’s leadership recommending they approve it. It also includes a 24% increase in pay rates for major films and a 9% increase for TV projects.
It comes after Equity members voted 99% in favour of taking industrial action and refusing to be scanned on set without further AI protections, in an indicative ballot last December.
However, Equity said there were areas that a new agreement simply could not cover, such as digital scanning traditionally undertaken in production for digital effects added later.
There have been mounting concerns on set about the use of AI, which has made many actors more wary of undergoing body scans. Many fear the data could be used to train AI models on their likenesses or poses, paving the way for the technology to take away work.
Last year, the actor Olivia Williams told the Guardian that actors should have as much control over the data harvested from scans of their body as they do over nudity scenes.
The star of Dune: Prophecy and The Crown said she and other actors were regularly pressed to have their bodies scanned by banks of cameras while on set, with few guarantees about how the data would be used or where it would end up.
Equity said that “despite our efforts”, the new agreement did not establish explicit contract protections regarding the use of actors’ performances for training AI systems.
In a note to its members, Equity explained that digital replicas could be made using the same photography captured for the production itself, without the need to scan artists.
However, producers must limit access to digital replicas to people with a legitimate business need and “use commercially reasonable efforts” to protect digital replicas from unauthorised access, use or copying.
The new deal also includes a commitment to safe casting and audition spaces, as well as training and reporting on sexual harassment. Producers also have a new obligation to provide artists of all ethnicities and cultures with adequate hair and makeup provision.
Paul W Fleming, Equity’s general secretary, said: “We set about to radically improve and modernise the terms and conditions for performers across UK TV and film. The result is groundbreaking.
“We’ve achieved the best AI provisions in the business – protecting performers and ensuring that human performance is valued and prioritised across the industry.”
