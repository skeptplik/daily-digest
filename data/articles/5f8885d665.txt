UK blood services have issued an urgent appeal for 150,000 donations to help the NHS cope with the increase in demand that occurs during the winter.
Supplies of blood have run particularly low, with reserves of certain blood types only half of what is considered necessary, officials say.
They include supplies of O negative, B negative, O positive and RO blood. People of black heritage have also been asked to donate to help the NHS treat people with sickle cell disease.
The health service needs 5,000 donors every day – who either give blood already or are new volunteers – from now until the new year in order to ensure hospitals have enough for patients. That is because blood has a shelf life of 35 days and platelets of just seven days.
Blood service officials want people to fill 150,000 donation appointments over the coming weeks and help boost what they stress are “life-saving supplies” of vital products.
Each donation takes an hour and can help save three lives, they say.
Winter’s cold weather brings two challenges for blood supply agencies. The greater prevalence of colds, coughs and respiratory illnesses such as flu stops people who are unwell from donating.
It can also leave people with long-term conditions, including sickle cell disease and cancer, needing to receive more blood or transfusions of platelets to safeguard their health.
“Right now, as we move into winter, there is an urgent need to boost our life-saving blood, platelet and plasma supplies”, said Gerry Gogarty, the director of blood and plasma supply at NHS Blood and Transplant.
“With patients reliant on regular transfusions, particularly vulnerable to colds and respiratory illnesses, it is vital that we stay well stocked to ensure the blood is always there when it is needed.”
Earlier this month, the Scottish National Blood Transfusion Service announced its first amber alert for O blood types. Stocks had dropped to “unprecedented low levels” because of a shortage of donors and greater use of the blood type over recent months, it explained.
Health chiefs also want to ramp up supplies of blood plasma. That is needed to make medicines for people with serious immune conditions, which become harder to manage in winter.
Bigger donation centres in town and city centres offer the most appointments, especially for first-time donors and those with common blood types, Gogarty said.
He asked those who cannot find an appointment immediately to keep checking back to help take up last-minute availability or cancellations.
