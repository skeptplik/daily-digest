The Salvadoran president welcomes the destruction of the home of Jeff Larose, one of Haiti's main crime leaders. It is not clear whether Jeff was in the building during the bombing.
The president of El Salvador, Nayib Bukele, confirmed on Wednesday that his forces had attacked and destroyed the luxurious mansion of one of the main leaders of Haiti's criminal gangs, known as "Jeff" Larose. For now, it is not clear whether the leader was killed in the strike or whether he was even in the house when it was bombed.
Bukele posted a video on the social network X showing images of Jeff posing in front of his mansion, followed by Salvadoran helicopters that approach the building and open fire. The footage shows several columns of smoke rising above the property.
"My gift to the Haitian people on the anniversary of the Battle of Vertières," the president wrote, referring to the 1803 battle that helped secure Haiti's independence from France and make it the world's first black republic.
Thousands of Haitians took to the streets of Port-au-Prince for the second day in a row to celebrate the operation and chant the Salvadoran president's name. "Jeff is a criminal and I hope he is dead. We asked for help and Bukele answered us," said Mario Jean-Pierre, a 45-year-old vendor.
Jeff Larose, also known as Jeff Canaan, Jeff Gwo Lwa or Taliban, leads a gang that has controlled the community of Canaan, north of Port-au-Prince, for years. According to Haiti expert Diego Da Rin of the International Crisis Group, speaking to AP, it is one of the country's most structured criminal organisations and one with the greatest territorial control.
The strike comes after Salvadoran forces also attacked the residence of Wilson Joseph, leader of the 400 Mawozo gang, on 8 November. The two leaders were allies and are considered two of the main criminal bosses in the capital.
Salvadoran deployment endangers civilians
Bukele deployed about 2,500 troops to Haiti last month, after four Salvadoran soldiers were attacked on 18 October in gang-controlled territory and went missing.
The use of helicopters and explosive drones has made it possible to step up operations against criminal organisations, but it has also increased the risk to the civilian population.
Experts warn that one of the main challenges remains keeping control of the territories that have been retaken and preventing the gangs from taking reprisals against residents.
