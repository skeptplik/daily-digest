To be sure, there’s no shortage of quaint, storybook villages in any part of the Alps—but over on the French side, tucked away in the Haute-Savoie region, there’s something special about Megève. “I’ve been coming for years; I enjoy it just as much in summer as in winter,” says Vogue France’s Head of Editorial Content, Claire Thomson-Jonville. “I love that it still feels like a small, cozy village. It’s postcard-perfect with a family-friendly atmosphere; it’s discreet, not flashy, with incredible nature.”
While the hiking—and generally stunning landscape—is a great draw for year-round tourism, travelers in the winter really have one reason to visit: the skiing. Thomson-Jonville has her routine down: “One of my favorite things to do is to take a moniteur de ski for the day and head into the mountains, around Mont d’Arbois.” (On her more adventurous days, she’ll brave the challenging pistes a little farther out at Mont Blanc.) In between stints on the slopes, she’s developed a list of local favorites, for everything from designer fashion to where you can find the best raclette; ahead, she offers us a peek into her Megève address book.
For a one-of-a-kind clothing find: Allard is a classic—a Megève institution. It’s on the square beside the church, the maison behind the original ski fuseau created in the 1930s. It’s a lovely place to find a beautiful sweater. Blu&Berry is where I go to find something more unexpected; they have pieces from The Row and Celine. Big Boss is another Megève institution, with a great skiwear selection. It’s one of those shops that everyone who comes here regularly knows.
For a worthwhile investment piece: The Hermès store in Megève is particularly well-stocked, and the Loro Piana boutique in the center of the village is great for après-ski shopping!
For unique souvenirs: Again, Allard for something classic that feels connected to the village. For something more unusual, there’s Max et Isa; it’s great for antique jewelry, vintage Murano lamps, and decorative objects. It’s a good address to browse for an unusual gift or a special piece for your chalet.
For the magpie: Guilhem Joaillier is my favorite jewelry store in Megève. I’ve picked some meaningful pieces here over the years; it’s a solid gift shop, too. Someone always has a birthday when we’re in Megève, and, of course, there’s always Christmas.
For your next great read: Scarlett Megève has a good curation of art books, as well as fun decorative objects. It’s a good place to buy gifts. The Maison de La Presse in town is the place we all go to buy magazines and novels; it’s super simple but you’ll always bump into someone you know there. Megève is a small place!
For the quickest way to impress an out-of-towner: I spend a lot of time in the Pharmacie de la Tour on Place de l’Église—it’s full of little treasures. I love an après-ski French pharmacy haul. Vitamins, moisturizers, supplements, hair masks… I love trying out new products when I’m in the mountains.
For a pick-me-up treat: Le Hibou Blanc is a great address for brunch or a coffee. It’s in the center of town with a big, heated terrasse to sit outside with a cappuccino and watch the world go by.
For a luxurious lunch: L’Idéal 1850, up on Mont d’Arbois, for a lunch break between runs with a beautiful mountain view. It’s very social; wear a great ski outfit the day you book a table.
For a fabulous dinner reservation: I love Le Vieux Megève. It’s a cheerful, traditional spot in the village—it’s noisy, yummy, and so much fun. Book early to share a raclette or fondue with family or friends. It’s my favorite spot in Megève—in fact, everyone in my family loves it there. It’s a unanimous favorite.
For a different kind of evening, I love Kaito at the Four Seasons. The sushi is delicious, and it’s a bit of a scene. Dress up and people watch!
For a post-slope spa day: I always love to try new places, but to me, Les Fermes de Marie has the best spa in Megève. The facials are great. The Susanne Kaufmann spa at Les Chalets du Mont d’Arbois is also good for bodywork.
For an apéro.. or a nightcap: The bar at the Fer à Cheval. It’s cozy and welcoming—a perfect place to settle by the fire after a day in the mountains, plus it’s in the center of town. The bar at the Four Seasons after dinner is also a vibe for the people watching.
For your packing list: My mountain packing list is tried and tested: One ski look, one puffy ski jacket, a (fake) fur coat, cashmere joggers, and big sunglasses. I also pack UGGs and cashmere socks for warmth, plus some great earrings to liven up a low-key dinner look.
