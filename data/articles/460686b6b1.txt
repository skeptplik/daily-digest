Match fined $10 million for alleged Tinder privacy violations
Ireland’s data protection regulator will fine the Match Group €11 million ($10 million) as a result of the dating app Tinder allegedly failing to provide users with their personal data held by the company upon request.
The country’s Data Protection Commission (DPC) alleged Tinder offers a self-service tool known as Download My Data that purports to let users access a complete copy of their information, but in reality fails to provide all of it.
Some categories of data were left out without users’ knowledge or were only supplied as summaries, the regulator’s press release said. The agency also alleges data provided by the tool did not comply with Europe’s General Data Protection Regulation’s right of access standard, which requires controllers to let consumers know if their personal information is being processed.
“These tools do not need to provide a complete copy of personal data, but they must be fair and transparent to individuals regarding the extent of the data provided,” DPC deputy commissioner Graham Doyle said in a statement. The probe was launched in January 2020.
Tens of thousands of users accessed the tool during the nearly six-year-long inquiry, according to the press release.
The DPC issued an order requiring Match to begin complying with the data processing rule within three months. The company did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
