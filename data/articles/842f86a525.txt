Good morning, and welcome to the UK politics live blog. The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has taken some services offline this morning after what it described as a “cyber incident”.
There is currently little information about which services have been affected by the incident or how long the disruption is expected to last, but the DWP said benefit and pension payments “will be paid as normal”.
“We have taken some DWP online services offline as a precaution after we identified a cyber incident affecting our systems,” it said in a statement.
The department added that it was working with cyber security experts to investigate the incident, restore services and limit disruption.

- MPs are due to consider a bill in the Commons today aimed at tackling strategic lawsuits against public participation, known by its acronym Slapps. It aims to protect whistleblowers, journalists, victims of sexual assault and others who speak out on matters of public interest from being silenced by costly or intimidating legal action brought by wealthy or powerful individuals and organisations. The bill, introduced by Conservative MP John Whittingdale, would make it easier for courts to dismiss such cases at an early stage.
The incident comes days after the government issued an alert warning of an increased risk of Russian sabotage attacks in the UK, particularly against businesses supporting Ukraine.
The DWP’s most senior official told a committee of MPs this month that cybersecurity and resilience was one of the issues “that keeps a new permanent secretary awake”.
Sarah Healey, the DWP permanent secretary, was appearing before the work and pensions select committee, where she was asked who would ensure that DWP systems would “not be the frontline in some sort of hybrid warfare from the Kremlin”.
Supermarket fridges and freezers in seven European countries malfunctioned earlier this month, with their thermostats remotely cranked up in some cases to 50C (122F), in what was blamed on a cyber-attack. The EU has condemned what it said was “a deliberate attempt to disrupt our food supply chain and undermine consumers’ confidence in it”.
According to Downdetector, which tracks online service outages, reports of problems with the government website appeared to begin last night and peaked at about 8am this morning.
Tory MP Neil Shastri-Hurst has also commented on the disruption at the DWP, saying the incident highlights the importance of protecting essential public services and ensuring they can recover quickly when systems fail.
He wrote on X:
Today’s cyber incident at DWP is a stark reminder that national resilience is not a tomorrow issue. It means protecting the essential services people rely on every day, with secure systems and the ability to recover quickly when things go wrong.”
Online portals for services including universal credit, personal independence payment (PIP) applications, the pension credit checker and the “Tell Us Once” bereavement service are unavailable.
Lib Dem leader Ed Davey has described the DWP incident as “deeply alarming”.
We do not yet know the nature of the DWP cyber incident, but Davey said the department holds huge amounts of sensitive personal and financial information and stressed the importance of protecting people’s data.
Speaking to Sky News, he said:
We’re seeing cyber attacks on the health service, on other parts of government, now on DWP who holds huge amounts of records of individuals, financial situations, their bank accounts and so on, huge invasion of people’s privacy and confidentiality.
So I mean, let’s see the details.
Obviously I don’t want to prejudge what’s happened, but this is deeply alarming.”
Almost all pensions and benefits systems have been knocked offline following a “cyber incident” which hit the department of work and pensions. Anyone trying to check their state pension, make an application for disability benefits or claim universal credit was not able to do so on Friday morning as a result of the incident. DWP officials insist benefit and pensions payments will continue to be made as normal.
Turning to the growing divisions within Labour over the Rosebank oilfield, the environment minister Emma Hardy has been asked about the issue during this morning’s media round.
As reported by the Guardian, 92 backbenchers and at least four frontbenchers have written to the prime minister urging him not to approve the project.
Speaking to Sky News, Hardy acknowledged her party was divided on the issue. “There are different views in the Labour party when it comes to Rosebank,” she said.
“I know from chatting to some of my friends and colleagues, some Labour MPs in Scotland feel differently to some Labour MPs elsewhere. But I know Andy [Burnham], as our prime minister, will be listening to everybody.”
It is not the first time a government department has faced major disruption to its computer systems. Just a few months ago, hackers stole more than 600,000 lines of data from the Department for Education’s helpdesk portal, including contact details belonging to parents and staff.
Here is the statement from the DWP on the “cyber incident”:
We have taken some DWP online services offline as a precaution after we identified a cyber incident affecting our systems.
We are working closely with the National Cyber Security Centre and Government Cyber Coordination Centre to investigate and to bring services back online as quickly as possible and to minimise any disruption.
Regular benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal.
If an online service you need is unavailable, please try again later. As always, we will never message you to ask for bank details or ask you to click on links.”
