Super Netball players and umpires have released a joint statement opposing suggested rule changes to the competition.
It is a rare move from the umpires, who typically keep quiet on such matters.
Heading into its 11th season, there is a desire from stakeholders to increase entertainment value and keep pushing the sport forward in order to hold onto its tag as the world's best league.
However, there is a clear divide in how that should be done.
In April, Netball Australia shelved plans for expansion and signed a five-year broadcast deal with Nine to return to free-to-air TV audiences.
These are the kinds of ideas the player and umpire group prefer.
"Netball has a bright future. Getting there will take big ideas and bold thinking," the statement read.
"We don't want less innovation. We want much more of it.
"Why not more teams, more games, new competitions and exciting formats? Why not fresh broadcast opportunities, technology, digital engagement and events that bring new audiences to our sport?"
The last rule changes fell six years ago when the super shot was announced six weeks before the 2020 season start. The decision was controversial and created distrust between the national governing body and the athletes, who felt their opinions had been disregarded.
Since then, England and New Zealand have also added two-point shots to their top-flight competitions.
Other innovations, such as tactical timeouts and rolling subs, have been much better received.
In May this year, Netball Australia ran a fan engagement survey, asking how they could elevate the 2027 spectator experience. Of the 3,000-plus fan submissions, there were consistent themes around speed, scoring and technology.
That same month, it formed a League Innovation Forum — comprised of Netball Australia staff, as well as Super Netball club, coach, umpire and player association representatives — to discuss potential modifications.
Former Big Bash League boss Anthony Everard is head of the project.
Although the sport has been reluctant to confirm details, three key proposals have been leaked:
- Stop the clock: Time will be held when the ball is out of play in the last two minutes of a match.
- A shoot-off: If a match is still tied at the end of extra time it will go to a shoot-off. Allowing two shooters each two attempts at a two-point super shot or giving one shooter three attempts at a regular goal.
- The centre pass heist: Players can request two centre passes in a row up until the last two minutes of a match.
The last of the three was trialled in early November in Victoria's men's premier division. Those involved said it did not make a compelling case as it had little impact, interrupted flow, and created confusion and inconsistency in the game.
When ABC Sport asked Netball Australia how these trials went, it was told that executives were finalising their recommendations to present at a December board meeting.
The players and umpires have concerns the trial didn't appropriately replicate the demands and standard of Super Netball and that fundamental changes will be made again without proper consultation.
By taking this stance now, they hope it's not too late to change the executive's minds.
"Netball is a team sport … That's it's magic," the statement read.
"Reducing a hard-fought contest to a shootout involving a small number of players moves away from what makes netball great. It also unnecessarily exposes individual players to additional pressure and scrutiny.
"The centre pass heist raises similar concerns. Possession should be earned through the contest, with all players having the opportunity to influence the outcome. Close games should be decided by skill and teamwork.
"Rule changes aren't off the table. But surely we should first ask: What problem are we solving? Will it improve the contest, reward skill, entertain fans and grow the game? And have we considered those who play and officiate it?
"If the answers are convincing, let's get on with it. If not, let's keep exploring."
