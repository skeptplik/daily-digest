A paint inspired by the skin of fast-swimming tuna is attracting attention, as the product is seen improving the fuel efficiency of ships amid soaring energy prices.
The paint, Fastar, developed by Nippon Paint Holdings, resembles the slimy surface characteristic of tuna. Applying it to the bottom of vessels helps reduce frictional resistance and therefore speeds them up, improving fuel efficiency, according to the Japanese company.
After studying the mechanism of high-speed swimming by tuna in the ocean, Nippon Paint mixed a jellylike material containing abundant moisture with the paint, mimicking the slime covering the tuna body, as a means to reduce friction.
By preventing the surface of a vessel’s underwater hull from becoming rough to a significant degree and protecting it from fouling by marine organisms including barnacles and algae, the paint is estimated to improve fuel efficiency by up to 14.1%.
Since its release in 2021, the paint has been used on more than 2,000 vessels.
The company plans to sell the paint in Asia outside Japan as well. “The product has growth potential, reflecting the need to enhance the fuel efficiency of ships as well as meet tighter environmental regulations,” a Nippon Paint official said.
Yusuke Kodama, who oversaw tanker painting at a dock of Tsuneishi Shipbuilding in Fukuyama in the western prefecture of Hiroshima at the end of October, said, “It’s significant to manage the work so that the product can deliver the intended performance.”
