Erasca’s pitch is straightforward: The U.S.-based biotech says it went to China and found a more potent, better-tolerated pan-RAS inhibitor than Revolution Medicines’ Rasonque. To date, Erasca has backed up its claim with scant data from an early-stage study disseminated via press releases. The story might be true, it might not be, but for now that’s less important to investors than Erasca’s $5 billion market value, which is a fraction of the $35 billion carried by Revolution Medicines. The valuation disparity makes sense given Revolution’s lead, but it also stokes speculation that Erasca could be an attractive takeover target.
On Friday, the company is presenting another slug of data on its drug, called ERAS-0015, at the EORTC-NCI-AACR “Triple” meeting in Barcelona. Erasca knows what it’s presenting already, obviously, so without breaking the meeting’s embargo policy, CEO Jonathan Lim has been signaling to investors at investment banking conferences that a “4-handle” — Wall Street lingo for 40%-ish — overall response rate from ERAS-0015 in second line-plus pancreatic cancer would be “meaningfully different” from Rasonque.
