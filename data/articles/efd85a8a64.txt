Suspected of informing the RN about probes into the party, the top official asked to be relieved of his post. He condemns a "manhunt", while Marine Le Pen says she has "never received confidential information" from him.
Five months before the French presidential election, the Interior Ministry is in turmoil. Hugues Moutouh, prefect of Centre-Val de Loire, is suspected of having passed confidential information to the National Rally.
On Saturday, the senior official announced that he had asked to be relieved of his duties.
In a statement, shared by the X account of the prefecture based in Orléans, Hugues Moutouh denounced a "campaign of destabilisation" that "goes far beyond" his own case and would target the institutions as well as the prefectural corps. "Through me, it is the very authority of the state that people are trying to attack", he said.
"This offensive comes at a time when some people are methodically working to weaken our institutions, to delegitimise those who embody them and to challenge the very foundations of the republican order", the prefect added.
"Calling me a mole is vile"
At the same time, in an interview with the weekly Journal du dimanche (JDD), published online on Saturday evening, Hugues Moutouh said he was "the victim of a manhunt".
"I have always served the state of one country, France, which I love deeply. Portraying me as a henchman in the pay of a party is odious, calling me a mole is vile," he said.
A former secretary general of the Interior Ministry between April 2025 and May 2026, Hugues Moutouh is suspected of having informed National Rally about investigations targeting the far-right party.
National Rally MP Jean-Philippe Tanguy, for his part, rejected these allegations on France Inter, describing them as "baseless rumours in the middle of an election campaign".
"I have the feeling that some people want to make Mr Moutouh pay for having done his job", he said, referring to the security arrangements for the National Rally congress held in Orléans at the end of October, which also prompted a counter-demonstration by opponents of the far-right party.
Le Pen denies having benefited from secret information
Marine Le Pen, National Rally candidate in the presidential election and well ahead in the opinion polls, responded to the affair on Sunday on France Inter and France 2. She said she had "never received any confidential information" from prefect Hugues Moutouh and had "never asked for any".
She added that she was "unable to say" whether anyone else in her party might have benefited from such information, as the investigation was still "ongoing".
The Paris public prosecutor's office confirmed this week, after revelations in the press, that it had opened a judicial investigation in July for suspected breaches of professional secrecy, breach of secrecy of investigations and handling the proceeds of those offences. The case has been handed to the General Inspectorate of the National Police (IGPN), nicknamed the "police of the police".
The premises of National Rally were searched at the beginning of the month, the prosecutor's office announced on Thursday. The Interior Ministry was also searched, according to a source close to the case quoted by AFP.
The investigation into the alleged leaks to National Rally stems from another search carried out as part of a separate procedure by the European Public Prosecutor's Office. According to AFP, confirming information published by Le Monde, an email from the office of prefect Hugues Moutouh was discovered at the Rome home of Frédéric Chatillon, an associate of Marine Le Pen who long handled the party's election communications.
In her interview on France Inter and France 2 on Sunday, Marine Le Pen said she did not know how this email could have been found at Frédéric Chatillon's home.
Earlier this week, AFP, citing a source close to the case, also reported that investigators had found a confidential Interior Ministry document during a search carried out as part of this European investigation. It concerns suspicions of the misappropriation of European funds by the former far-right group Identity and Democracy (ID), which included, among others, National Rally MEPs. This group was replaced in July 2024 by Patriots for Europe.
On Friday, Hugues Moutouh was summoned by minister Laurent Nuñez. Speaking on BFMTV, the latter said that if the facts were "proven", they would be "obviously very serious", while stressing that at this stage no one had been placed under formal investigation in the case.
These suspicions of links between National Rally and a former number two at the Interior Ministry, who is still prefect of Centre-Val de Loire, come amid a particularly tense political climate, five months before the presidential election that will choose Emmanuel Macron's successor.
According to recent opinion polls, Marine Le Pen would receive more than 30 % of the vote in the first round.
