A nine-metre-tall replica of a town hall in Fayetteville, North Carolina, takes centre stage at American rapper J Cole’s world tour set, which has been created by production company Sturdy.
The set for The Fall Off Tour was designed by Los Angeles-based Sturdy to realise J Cole’s ambition to take his hometown, Fayetteville – or “the ville”– “to the world”.
To do this, the studio drew on the historic, two-storey Market House building, which has stood as a local landmark in the city since 1832. Sturdy created a weathered, ivy-covered replica of the structure, which is neoclassical with gothic revival details.
“The Market House was chosen because it’s an iconic piece of architecture historically as well in the town that J Cole’s from,” Martinez told Dezeen.
“I think a big part of the intention is that he takes home with him wherever he goes; conceptually, the idea was to take ‘the ville’ to the world,” he continued.
“And what better way to do that than something that lives basically at the centre point of the entire city?”
Other nods to the rapper’s life in Fayetteville include the presence of a Honda Civic car, which Martinez describes as a “supporting actor in the show”.
Positioned in front of the building at centre stage, the car is supported on ties that allow it to be suspended above the stage during specific moments of the performance.
“The car is like a supporting actor in the show that adds a little bit of an authentic take to the performance,” he said.
“His fans know the black Civic, and, at least from my perspective, with this being The Fall Off, the car needs to have a place in the story. It’s part of ‘the ville’,” Martinez added.
The set was designed by Sturdy to be easily dismantled, shipped and rebuilt between shows across the US, Europe, South Africa, Australia and New Zealand.
This determined the scale and design of the neoclassical Market House replica, which stretches just over 15 metres and is composed of easy-to-assemble wood and metal components.
The materiality of the structure also allows for J Cole’s performance to extend to the building’s first-floor terrace.
Alongside the structure is a 23-metre-long walkway lined with grass, rocks and a tree stump that doubles as a seat for the artist.
“Any show that we are doing, especially of this scale, has to be designed from a ‘rigging perspective’ to how this thing breaks down,” Martinez explained.
“That all has to be thoroughly thought out because we’ve got to be able to have the proper Legos. We can’t really just show up and hope that it’s going to piece together,” he added.
Also contributing to the set is a large LED screen, which, according to Martinez, is used “rather sparingly” throughout the performances to bring the stage design to the fore.
“I think for a long stretch of time shows were just about crazy visuals and putting stuff on screens and just letting people see stuff moving in the background,” he said.
“We never use the entirety of the screen for the whole show,” Martinez continued. “It’s to add a little bit of depth to what we have going on scenically on stage.”
J Cole’s The Fall-Off World Tour has been running since July, with recent shows at London’s O2 Arena. The tour will end in December in Johannesburg, South Africa.
