Parts of northern England and southern Scotland are getting an early taste of winter with snow falling over some hills on Tuesday morning.
A Met Office yellow warning for snow is valid until 11:00 GMT with 5-15cm expected at some locations above 300-400m elevation.
Major routes like the A74(M), A66 and M62 could all have problems with transport disruption.
Some railways are also likely to be affected with longer journey times for train services.
Cold health alerts are also in place, external for northern England issued by the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) until 09:00 GMT on Thursday.
What's causing the snow?
A weather front is pushing north and east across the UK bringing a spell of rain to many areas. Over northern England it is encountering a reservoir of colder and drier air.
Some of the rain is evaporating in this drier air, cooling the atmosphere further by a process known as evaporative cooling - the same process involved in cooling your body down when you sweat.
Rain has turned to snow over parts of the Pennines, Cumbrian Hills and Scotland's Southern Uplands.
The snow could come down in big, wet, chunky flakes which could stick to power lines and tree branches.
The extra weight of the snow combined with wind gusts of 40-50mph over high ground could bring down tree branches and power lines, bringing an additional risk of some localised power cuts.
How long will the snow last?
Some snow may accumulate down to 100m elevation or so, with around 1-5cm possible.
Any snow in these lower hills will probably be quite short-lived, turning back to rain by mid-morning in many parts, however the highest hills and mountains of Scotland and northern England could continue to see wintry weather into the afternoon.
Cold outbreaks of rain will be left across most northern areas of the UK through Tuesday afternoon with some hill fog and a chilly wind.
Meanwhile the south will have sunshine and blustery showers but it will also be milder with temperatures up to 14C.
Rain and showers will continue to impact Northern Ireland, England and Wales for the next few days.
There will still be some rain in Scotland on Wednesday but then high pressure will take over with sunnier weather returning.
This drier, sunnier weather will then push southwards across most of the UK by the end of the week with cold air returning, along with some overnight frosts and a few coastal showers.
