NSA continues response to interruptions in classified internal network
Disruptions at the National Security Agency entered their second day on Wednesday as the spy organization worked to get its internal communications network back up and fully running.
The system, known as NSA Net, experienced intermittent outages on Tuesday, leading the agency to tell its work force to stagger arrivals at its headquarters at Fort Meade, Maryland. It eventually closed its campus to most employees by the evening, according to sources.
Non-essential personnel were sent a "Code Gray" notification late Tuesday night, informing them to arrive at 10 a.m. Wednesday. However, that time has already been moved to the afternoon and could change again, said a source familiar with the ongoing situation.
Meanwhile, the agency’s cafeteria, and a Starbucks located on its campus, remain closed.
The same person said the network is working but NSA leaders are still staggering personnel because the login process is the most taxing process on the system.
They also said comments made Tuesday by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton — that the outage was due to maintenance affecting PHP, a common programming language — were correct.
It remains unknown if the vulnerability was AI-derived or human-driven.
The source speculated that it might take another day or two for full restoration of the classified network, but insisted that the spy agency’s classified national security work remains unimpeded.
The NSA did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
The outage is the latest in a series of high-profile technical disruptions in the last week, including a global outage of Microsoft products, a nationwide flight stoppage and problems with bank services.
Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) on Wednesday called on President Donald Trump and Clayton to “form a dedicated red team of trained intelligence professionals to challenge the assumption that these outages are just business as usual.”
Schumer, who is lobbying his colleagues to become Majority Leader following the outcome of the midterm elections, said it is “too coincidental that all these outages happened within a few days for us to think it's business as usual. We have to get to the bottom of this.”
He urged the administration to form the team and report its findings to Congress by December 1.
Testifying on Wednesday before the House Homeland Security Committee, Clayton said he understands that the “timing of these incidents can raise questions and it should.”
“The American people should ask us these questions, but, based on what we know today, after a great deal of diligence, these are separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes,” he added. “They are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.”
