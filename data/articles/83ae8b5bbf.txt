2 men charged in break-in at country residence of Prince William, Princess of Wales
Charges include trespassing, entering a dwelling with intent to steal
Two men have been charged with burglary at the country home of Prince William and Princess Catherine, police said Wednesday.
The Norfolk Police force said Mirgen Doku, 34, and Arilen Ndoci, 32, were charged with entering a dwelling with intent to steal and trespassing on a protected site. They are due in court later Wednesday.
Police officers were called Monday evening to reported "suspicious activity" at an address on the royal family's rural Sandringham estate in eastern England. After a search involving police dogs, drones and a helicopter, two men were arrested on suspicion of burglary and trespassing.
No members of the Royal Family were on the property at the time.
William and Kate's Kensington Palace office declined to comment on security matters.
Anmer Hall, a 10-bedroom house on the Sandringham estate, was a wedding present to the couple from the late Queen Elizabeth.
William and Kate use the secluded red brick Georgian mansion about 171 kilometres north of London as a country residence on weekends. The couple and their three children spend most of their time at Forest Lodge in Windsor, west of London, which they have described as their "forever home."
Intruders have breached royal properties several times over the years.
Britain's Prince William and Catherine, Princess of Wales, are shown in Mumbai on Nov. 17. The couple are now back in Britain, but were not at their country residence Monday when a break-in occurred. (Rafiq Maqbool/The Associated Press)
In October 2024, burglars broke into the Windsor Castle estate west of London and stole a pickup truck and a quad bike from a farm building. Charles and Queen Camilla weren't at Windsor Castle at the time, but it was reported that William and Kate were in their nearby house.
Three years earlier, a man armed with a crossbow was detained on the castle grounds. Jaswant Singh Chail was later sentenced to nine years in prison after admitting he had been encouraged by a chatbot to kill Elizabeth.
In 1982, intruder Michael Fagan managed to sneak into the queen's private chambers while she was in bed. The queen spent 10 minutes chatting with the burglar before raising the alarm when he asked for a cigarette.
As well, there was a security incident earlier this year at Sandringham when Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor, the former prince, was allegedly threatened by a masked man while walking his dogs.
A court case for the 39-year-old man who was charged in connection with the incident is ongoing. Andrew, who was stripped of his royal titles and removed from his longtime Windsor Castle residence, now lives at Marsh Farms on the Sandringham estate..
