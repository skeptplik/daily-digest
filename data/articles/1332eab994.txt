China’s economy really is unique. The weak spot for the world’s second-biggest commercial power is not simply the low inflation you’ve repeatedly heard about. It’s that the type of inflation authorities are dealing with is the wrong kind.
The price of goods as they leave factories is climbing, largely reflecting the spike in energy costs from the Iran war. Yet it’s hard to say that China has entirely escaped the menace of too-low inflation. The problem is that the increases aren’t flowing through into what consumers pay for products and services.
That’s a malady that’s almost as alarming as too high a reading and something that Group of Seven authorities responded to with vast monetary easing between the Global Financial Crisis and COVID-19. Beijing doesn’t appear ready to go there, despite the already soft economy demonstrating a marked deterioration in recently released figures.
