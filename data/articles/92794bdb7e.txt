Shabana Mahmood pledges to tackle ‘corrosive and growing perception of two-tier policing’
Good morning. Shabana Mahmood has done two of the most difficult jobs in the government. With Labour under pressure from anti-migrant populists, in Reform UK, in the Conservative party and in the media, as justice secretary and home secretary she has been on the frontline in all the debates where the fight between the right and progressive politics has been most intensive. And she has developed a bold, novel, and largely successful strategy for winning these battles; she disarms her critics by agreeing with them.
Not entirely, of course. She is still a Labour cabinet minister. But sometimes she can sound a bit like Chris Philp.
And today we are going to get another example. In a speech to the National Police Chiefs’ Council (NPCC), she will tell officers that she wants to do something about so-called two-tier policing – the theory that, under pressure to be politically correct, the police have become biased towards leftwing causes and nervous about enforcing the law against minority ethnic people for fear of being seen as racist.
On the right, the existence of this version of two-tier policing is now an article of faith, even though most of the time actual policing is very different. This is how Robert Hutton from the Critic satirised the idea in a sketch two years ago, during 2024 summer riots when racists were trying to burn down hotels housing asylum seekers.
This is about fairness, Ed, and it’s a very important point. When I talk about two-tier policing, I’m talking about the way that different groups of marchers are treated. For instance, if you go to a Pride march, there are police with rainbow flags on their cars. During the Black Lives Matter protests, the police took the knee. But you go to these protests, and do you see the police waving swastikas? No you don’t. When was the last time you saw a film of a policeman kicking a brown-skinned person in the head? Well, OK, but when was the last time you saw a video of a policeman trying to set fire to a building full of refugees? Exactly. That’s what we mean by two-tier policing.
Here is a Guardian article looking at whether policing really is two-tier. In some respects, it is, the article points out. “Black people remained more likely than any other ethnic group to be subjected to almost every form of force, from handcuffing and dog bites to firearms and Tasers, when compared with population or arrest figures.”
In her speech today, from the extracts released in advance, Mahmood is not going to say that there is two-tier policing. But she will argue that it is important to respond to the “perception” it exists. She will say:
We must tackle head-on the corrosive and growing perception of two-tier policing in this country.
Now let me be clear: I know the vast majority of officers police impartially and that policing without fear or favour is their creed.
But there have been examples of differential treatment and they have harmed policing, allowing people – often motivated actors – to whip up the idea that there is two-tier policing in this country.
In its report previewing the speech, the Press Association says:
Mahmood will lament the low levels of black officers in police forces, while also warning against “misguided attempts at course correction” that lead to “differential treatment”.
And she will warn officers against engaging with “activist groups”, telling them to “leave the politics to the politicians”.
She will also set out plans to fast-track talented recruits – including from outside policing – to senior ranks to address sometimes “inadequate” leadership.
A new “impartiality standard” is being developed to ensure all members of the public are treated equally, she will tell chief constables and police and crime commissioners.
This will bring guidance on key impartiality principles into one place and aims to give police more confidence in how they handle cases, regardless of someone’s race, faith or other characteristics, and to give the public confidence they will be treated fairly.
A third of Britons believe police treat people from ethnic minority backgrounds more favourably than white people, a More in Common poll found earlier this year, noting this marks a doubling in the last two years.
Mahmood will tell the National Police Chiefs’ Council (NPCC) that it is “shameful”, 33 years after the murder of black teenager Stephen Lawrence, that the proportion of black officers remains virtually unchanged.
But Mahmood will argue that some approaches intended to promote equality, such as the former police anti-racism commitment, have risked undermining confidence in impartial policing.
Mahmood will say:
We must also be wary of misguided attempts at course correction, where the pursuit of equality leads instead to differential treatment.
To the public, it was patently absurd to tell police officers that fair policing does not mean treating everyone the same.
This is a reference to the Police Anti-Racism Commitment, a document that has frequently been criticised by the Conservatives and Reform UK. It says producing equality of policing outcomes “does not mean treating everyone ‘the same’”.

Lammy claims 'many' Tory voters could switch to Labour at next election if party committed to rejoining EU
David Lammy has claimed that Labour could win the support of pro-European Tories by proposing rejoining the EU at the next election.
Speaking at an event at the Chatham House foreign affairs thinktank, Lammy also argued that Andy Burnham should run the campaign to rejoin the EU out of No 10 North in Manchester to avoid it being seen as a project based in London and the south-east.
Lammy has explicitly said that rejoining should be a manifesto commitment for Labour at the next election. (See 9.49am.) He is the second senior figure from the Keir Starmer government to say this, after Rachel Reeves recently said being out of the EU left Britain “poorer, weaker and less secure”.
In his speech, Lammy pointed out that the Conservative and Reform UK proposals to leave the European convention on human rights meant that at the next election they would be arguing for their own renegotiation of the Brexit settlement – because the current post-Brexit trade deal with the EU is based on the UK remaining in the ECHR.
He said:
We may be in a position at the next general election where the Labour party is effectively arguing for rejoining and to get growth as a result, and Reform and the Conservatives are arguing effectively for a harder Brexit because both those parties want to pull out of the ECHR, which would mean a renegotiation of the TCA (trade and cooperation agreement), which is in fact a hard Brexit. I say bring it on.
He predicted there would be “a consolidation in the polls around progressives, around the Labour party” and he claimed:
I think you’ll also find that many Conservative voters, mainstream Conservative voters, vote Labour to get back into the European Union.
There is some polling to back this up. A recent poll by Lord Ashcroft found that 28% of people who voted Conservative in 2024 now say they would vote to join the EU.

In her speech today, Shabana Mahmood, the home secretary, will also announce plans for a new fast-stream police leadership programme for up to 200 recruits a year. Vikram Dodd has the details.
Tories and Reform UK dismiss Mahmood's bid to assuage concerns about 'two-tier policing'
The Conservatives and Reform UK do not seem very impressed by Shabana Mahmood’s proposal to meet their concerns about two-tier policing.
This is fromNick Timothy, the shadow justice secretary.
If you don’t want two-tier justice, stop legislating for it.
Stop training people for it.
Stop hiring people for it.
Two-tier justice comes from the equality duty, human rights laws, DEI policies, differential sentencing policies, conscious choices like the “Islamophobia” definition, the decision to use the Public Order Act as a de facto blasphemy law, the deliberate decision not to enforce the law during the anti-Israel marches, appointing communalist interests to police and CPS advisory boards, and more.
The issue is not training the police to treat people equally - it’s all the decisions that stop the criminal justice system treating us as equal before the law.
And this is fromRobert Jenrick, the Reform UK Treasury spokesperson.
Last year the Home Office believed claims of “two-tier” policing were “far-right”. Will they now apologise?
Even in her statement Mahmood still can’t bring herself to say it’s wrong.
Lucy Powell criticises media organisations for playing prison message from Russell Brand
Lucy Powell, the Labour deputy leader, has criticised media organisations for playing a message from rapist comedian Russell Brand.
As the Press Association reports, Brand’s wife Laura Gallacher shared the message from the jailed sex offender in which he insisted he was not guilty of the offences for which he is serving a 10-and-a-half year term. Gallacher read out the message on a YouTube podcast on Sunday, prompting an official investigation into whether Brand has breached prison rules.
Asked about this in an interview on LBC this morning, Powell said:
I was appalled at that and actually I think mainstream media shouldn’t have transmitted that either.
Brand was “someone who’s not showing a jot of remorse, a jot of any sensitivity at all to their victims”, she said.
This is somebody who’s been convicted and found guilty of very, very serious offences and rape.
Asked why she wanted to censor that message, she said: “It’s a platform I don’t think … should have been amplified, to be honest.”
Lammy rules out standing to be Labour's next candidate for London mayor
In his Today interview, David Lammy also said that he would not be standing to be Labour’s next candidate for mayor of London. He said he was still enjoying national politics. There had been speculation that he might apply if Sadiq Khan decided not to stand for a fourth term in 2028. Today voting ends in the Tory election to choose their candidate and James Cleverly (like Lammy, a former foreign secretary), is expected to win.
Khan had been expected not to seek a fourth term. But, with Andy Burnham beefing up the powers available to mayors, making the job more attractive, there is increasing speculation that he will try to stay on.
Lammy says he doesn't think there is two-tier policing in Britain, but he supports 'tidying up' guidance on this
David Lammy was on the Today programme this morning talking about his call for the UK to rejoin the EU. (See 9.49am.) Asked about Shabana Mahmood’s speech on two-tier policing (see 9.04am), Lammy, a former justice secretary, replied:
I don’t think that there’s two-tier policing in Britain.
But I think there’s a preception [of two-tier policing] because of too many guidelines that are not straightforward. If [Shabana Mahmood] is tidying that up – I haven’t seen the detail [of her speech later] – then I would welcome that.
Lammy says Burnham should put rejoining EU in Labour's next manifesto
David Lammy, the former foreign secretary and former deputy PM, has used an article in the Times to say that Andy Burnham should proposing rejoining the EU in the next Labour manifesto. He says:
Burnham has to show how joining the EU passes the Makerfield test. I believe to do that it should answer three questions. What will deliver the most growth? What will deliver the most security? And what will help prevent uncontrolled irregular migration?
The customs union doesn’t deliver major growth on its own. The single market without EU membership gives Britain economic growth but with little influence over the rules we follow. Only membership answers all three questions. On migration, it would put Britain back inside the EU’s asylum returns system, giving us access to the tools we lost in 2020 and have only been rebuilding piecemeal ever since. The ability for Brits to live and work in Europe and Europeans to live and work in Britain with free movement would return. But I believe, like Ireland, we can remain outside Schengen, and, like Sweden, we can stay out of the eurozone.
Burnham’s Labour should go into the next election clear that its intention is to rejoin the EU. Forming the next government would give it the mandate to begin formal negotiations. Once a deal is agreed, this should be put to the people against the status quo. Unlike the last referendum, the public should have the opportunity to decide between two clearly defined choices.
Lammy also says, towards the end of Keir Starmer’s time in office, he tried to get Starmer to agree to abandon Labour’s red lines on Europe (ruling out rejoining, or joining the single market or customs union) at the next election. But Starmer resisted, he suggests.

Mahmood to tell the police 'leave politics to the politicians'
Here is another quote from what Shabana Mahmood will say in her speech to the the National Police Chiefs’ Council (NPCC).
Community engagement should mean going to a residents’ association meeting and discussing local issues. It can be over tea and biscuits, or tea and samosas, but it doesn’t mean engaging with activist groups and campaigners who are pushing an agenda and not representing a community.
