CD Projekt Red has said people can expect a similar qualitative and technological leap from The Witcher 3 to The Witcher 4 as has been witnessed between Grand Theft Auto 5 and Grand Theft Auto 6.
CDPR joint-CEO Michał Nowakowski made the remark during a Q3 earnings call this afternoon, during which he and CDPR faced a handful of questions related to GTA 6.
"First of all, obviously this is a call about us, not about Rockstar," Nowakowski began by saying, "but I do understand the connection here. Having said that, I'm not going to make a deep-dive and a full-blown review of GTA 5 versus GTA 6. But of course we did look at the game, we did see the differences.
"We believe there's going to be a comparable leap between The Witcher 3 and The Witcher 4 - I think so, yes. It's been 11 years, we are working on The Witcher 4 to make it significantly better looking and improved in terms of pretty much every possible facet of the game, so yes you should expect us to make a much bigger jump between Witcher 3 and Witcher 4."
Another question was about whether GTA 6 had affected development plans for The Witcher 4 and Cyberpunk 2 - a question well-timed after Cyberpunk 2 creative director Igor Sarzynski joked at the weekend that "Cyberpunk 2 is cancelled" because of how impressive GTA 6 is.
"Is seeing GTA 6 launch making us make the games more expensive?" said Nowakowski. "We have certain ambitions when it comes to Witcher [4] and Cyberpunk 2; they have not changed when it comes to GTA 6. If anything happened as a result of GTA 6, it's [that it's] always great to see games like that because they show you how much cool stuff can be done, and that's for sure an inspiration in many ways for our developers."
Specifically in terms of budget, Nowakowski said nothing had changed, and also echoed a point made earlier this year when he said Witcher 4 and Cyberpunk 2 are "not budget-driven developments".
"I'm not saying we don't care about the budgets: we do, and I'm pretty sure [Rockstar] did as well. But you cannot make games like that having in mind 'this is the fixed number of money I want to spend and that's it, otherwise I'm gone'," he said.
"They're ambition-driven developments and money's important - the budgets are not indefinite, that's not what we're saying. But the philosophy is like I said, and honestly that philosophy has not changed for us because of GTA 6's release; it has just always been like that."
When asked whether the arrival of GTA 6 had impacted sales of The Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077, Nowakowski answered "yes and no". "You do see a certain dip in engagement on the PlayStation and Xbox for sure," he said.
Statistics apparently show a drop of "at least" 25 percent in CDPR's players as they "engaged themselves with GTA 6". "But you do have to remember that a lot of our players are PC-based," Nowakowski pointed out. "GTA has not launched on PC; we have not seen any impact whatsoever on the PC sales or engagement. So it's a bit of a mixed answer.
"And also on the PS5 and Xbox, it's not like our sales came to zero," he added. "Some short impact, yes, on the consoles. Long-term, we do not really see that affecting ourselves in any material way in Q4."
Finally, the questioning turned to marketing, which Rockstar has shown can reach far and wide if you have the brand-power of GTA and some imagination. We've seen prestigious department stores like Selfridges taken over by Rockstar for GTA 6, trailers premiering on Netflix, Miami launch festivals - Rockstar's marketing department has gone all out.
CDPR is no stranger to grabbing headlines with glitzy actor hirings such as Keanu Reeves, but will Rockstar's approach to marketing GTA 6 push it a step further?
"The most important takeaway - I'm not sure if I'd call it a lesson because this is our playbook as well - is that if you want to reach a certain critical mass, you do have to get out of just the core gaming bubble, something we did in the past as well," Nowakowski said. "For sure, GTA 6 went there.
"If the underlying question was if we're going to repeat or copy-paste their playbook: no, for sure not. Our games are different and they require a certain different approach - we just do things differently than they do. But in terms of the ultimate effect, in terms of breaking out to the mass market awareness: this is for sure something we consider pivotal to whatever we're going to do with our marketing campaigns in the future."
CDPR's Q3 financial results were positive. The company made less money than this time last year, but the "fresh momentum" - as Nowakowski put it - provided to The Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077 by the release of the Remastered edition of Witcher 3 and the Edgerunners anime series on Netflix, and associated game update, went a long way.
The Witcher 3 reached its highest concurrent player count on Steam as a result, and Cyberpunk 2077 reached its second highest concurrent player count, which represents serious staying power for both games so long after they were first released. And with Witcher 3 expansion Songs of the Past on track for 2027, and The Witcher 4 for 2028, the future is loaded.
