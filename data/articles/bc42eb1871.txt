Lando Norris wins wet F1 Brazil Grand Prix, Oscar Piastri sixth
Lando Norris was near perfect to claim victory in Brazil, but the McLaren driver was left to celebrate his win with a shattered champagne bottle.
Norris recovered from a poor start from pole, where he dropped to third, to claim his third grand prix win of the season in a São Paulo Grand Prix that went from wet to dry, and back to wet.
Max Verstappen finished second after starting from 21st on the grid, while his Red Bull teammate Isack Hadjar was third.
McLaren teammate Oscar Piastri bounced back from a disappointing qualifying to place sixth.
One week after watching Mercedes driver Kimi Antonelli be crowned world champion, taking the title off him, Norris responded like a champion should in very tough conditions.
But if the Briton wanted to celebrate his 14th career win in style, his champagne bottle had other ideas.
Norris jumped from the top step and slammed the bottle on the ground, trying to produce the spray of wine that signifies victory.
But the bottle exploded on impact into shards of glass, leaving Norris defenceless as Verstappen and Hadjar soaked the race-winner with French bubbles.
But for the two hours prior to the exploding champagne bottle, Norris was excellent.
He suffered a poor start in the wet and dropped from first to third as Antonelli established a nice lead.
Once the track dried out and all drivers went onto slick tyres, Norris charged while Antonelli struggled.
The Italian, who had spun on his way to the grid, then spun out of the lead on lap 20 of 71.
Antonelli would continue to struggle, while Norris never looked like losing.
"It was a very tough race, obviously not the best of launches off the line," Norris said.
"A lot of challenges today … going onto slicks in the wet is tough.
"This weekend, we had an opportunity to get a pole yesterday, an opportunity to win and this year when I've had an opportunity, I've mostly taken it."
Two years after winning from 17th, and earning a podium from the pit lane last year, Verstappen again produced a stunning drive from the back in Brazil.
From 21st on the grid, he roared up the order in the opening laps, picking off drivers one by one in wet conditions.
Once up to second, he could not catch Norris, with Red Bull reportedly believing Verstappen's car had mysteriously lost rear downforce.
"It was a challenging race. The first few laps were very important; I overtook a lot of cars,' Verstappen said.
"The first stint on the inters was very, very strong. After that, I don't know if I picked up some damage ... The balance went off."
Hadjar was third, followed by the Mercedes duo of George Russell and Antonelli.
Oscar Piastri was sixth, while Charles Leclerc (Ferrari), Liam Lawson (Racing Bulls), Lewis Hamilton (Ferrari) and Nico Hülkenberg (Audi) rounded out the top 10.
Antonelli and Russell spin as Mercedes seal constructors' title
Mercedes would have loved to seal the Formula 1 constructors' championship with their drivers on the podium, but both were caught out by Brazil's wet weather.
Kimi Antonelli, one week after becoming drivers' world champion, looked destined for victory when he took the lead off the start.
He held the lead for 19 laps, but on soft tyres in conditions that were still slippery, the young Italian spun and handed the race lead, and victory, to Lando Norris.
That was not the first incident Antonelli had on Monday morning (AEDT). On a wet track, he slid his Mercedes off the circuit and onto muddy ground on his way to the grid.
He was able to find an escape road and take his place at the front of the grid.
George Russell also suffered a race-ruining spin. The Briton, exiting the pit lane after putting on slick tyres, spun his Mercedes before returning to the track.
He was facing back towards the garages as several rivals passed him on the narrow slip lane, costing him any chance of a podium.
But the duo still managed to finish in the top five, and ahead of both Ferraris to seal the F1 constructors' championship.
It is Mercedes's ninth constructors' title, equalling Williams for the third-most all time.
"It is amazing; it was a very strong beginning of the season, and that got us across the line," team principal Toto Wolff said.
"Overall I'm so proud of the organisation; we have taken a few years to get back to the top spot."
Wolff has been team principal for all nine of Mercedes's constructors' championships, and now equals the record with the late Sir Frank Williams for most ever.
Wolff was once a shareholder in the Williams team, and had a relationship with Frank.
"Frank [Williams] is such an unbelievable icon of the sport, to be tied on constructors' championships, I never thought it would be possible."
