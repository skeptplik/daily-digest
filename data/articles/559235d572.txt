The European Union added Russia's wealthiest woman, online retailer Wildberries' CEO and owner Tatyana Kim, to its Russian sanctions list on Monday.
Kim's EU assets will be frozen as a result and she is barred from making financial transactions in or traveling to the bloc.
Wildberries is Russia's largest e-commerce retailer.
Kim, born in Grozny, Chechnya, to ethnic Korean parents, has an estimated net worth of $8.1 billion (€7.4 billion).
The sanctions were part of a larger regime targeting some 50 banks and oil export enablers and nearly 100 business leaders, propagandists and a major pro-Russian hacking group.
EU authorities say they targeted Kim due to the fact that Wildberries is "one of the largest taxpayers" in Russia and "operates as a supply network for the Russian military by facilitating the sale of military and dual-use goods."
An EU statement went on to say that "Kim is supporting, materially or financially, and benefiting from Russia's military and industrial complex."
Why is the EU targeting an online retailer?
In its defense against invading Russian forces, Ukraine expanded its military target list this summer to include Wildberries warehouses inside Russia, saying the company's product list — which includes night-vision goggles, ammunition pouches and helmets alongside regular clothing, cosmetics and electronics — is supporting Russia's illegal war.
Kim defended her company at the time, saying its offerings were no different than other online retailers like Amazon in the US and Alibaba in China.
The Kremlin, too, denied that the company and its warehouses were involved in the war effort.
Who else is on the EU's newest sanctions list?
In a statement, Wildberries said Brussels' sanctions would have no impact on the company since it has no assets in the bloc nor does it conduct business there.
Other entities on Monday's sanctions list include firms in China, Oman, the Seychelles and the United Arab Emirates linked to the sale of oil from Russia's so-called shadow fleet of illicit tankers.
The sale of that oil also fuels Russia's invasion, which the EU is seeking to hamper through sanctions as well as direct financial and military assistance for Kyiv.
Brussels has imposed more than 20 sanctions packages in response to Russia's February 24, 2022, invasion of Ukraine, targeting thousands of individuals and companies to pressure President Vladimir Putin to negotiate an end to the assault.
The 27-nation bloc intends to introduce another sweeping sanctions package by year's end.
