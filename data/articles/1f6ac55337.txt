Sat, 21 Nov, 1:05 pm, Final score, Kangaroos 83, Swans 51
With the league best and fairest medal around her neck, Georgie Prespakis looked down at her sister Maddy from the stage.
"To think that she's been up here and been able to achieve this. I'm full of emotion and trying to hold back some tears for sure," the Geelong star said into the microphone.
"I've always wanted to follow in Maddy's footsteps, and at times, I probably felt a little bit in her shadow."
The camera then panned to Essendon's Maddy, who mouthed "not anymore" with a face wet with tears. The room erupted with laughter before focusing back on Georgie.
"It's obviously a hard game. It's a hard game at times, so to be able to lean [on] and support one another is something that's meant a lot to me, particularly this year," she said, after the Cats finished in the top two but exited the finals in straight sets.
When asked what made the pair so good, Georgie replied: "Certainly some good genes. I'll take those for sure.
"[But] no, no doubt my family, my parents particularly, are feeling super proud, and yeah, I can't wait to obviously share this moment with them."
The Prespakis pair became the first sisters in AFLW history to both win the league's highest honour, the W Award.
The only brothers to win the men's equivalent, the Brownlow, were Albert and Harry Collier, back in 1929 and 1930 respectively.
Maddy won it back in 2020. With the awards held via Zoom due to the COVID-19 pandemic, Maddy was presented with the medal by Georgie in their shared backyard.
A year later, Georgie was drafted to Geelong.
In her early years at the Cats, Georgie was constantly being compared to her older sister. When Geelong and Essendon (or Carlton when Maddy was still there) played each other, it was referred to as "the Prespakis cup".
As the years went on, Georgie burst out of that shadow, as she does from stoppages, and began to represent a new era of AFLW talent.
Inaugural player Jasmine Garner was the favourite on Monday to take out the W Award, a medal that has eluded her over her career despite long being regarded as the best player in the competition. She earned her ninth All Australian blazer this week, the most of any player in the history of the competition.
Garner polled 24 votes, falling behind the other two frontrunners, Charlie Rowbottom (28 votes) and Georgie (30 votes).
Charlie and Georgie were drafted in the same year, with picks one and two respectively. They are the first players not to have played in the first two seasons of the competition to poll so high.
"I've always set really high standards for myself," Georgie said.
"And I've always wanted to be the best that I can be, and I will always work incredibly hard to kind of get myself in that position. And no doubt, my first season of AFLW was a real eye-opener just as to how difficult it is.
"That was something that I really struggled with, and knew that off the back of that, I had to lose a little bit of chubby, baby fat and get to work, and that was something I did.
"It was really difficult to move out of home and go up to Geelong and start a life down there. And I think that's why I feel so grateful to have landed at such an amazing club… [that} allowed me to become not only a better person but a better footballer."
