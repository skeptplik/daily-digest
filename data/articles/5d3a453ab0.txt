One reason 197,000 people, including Elon Musk, signed a petition over the weekend calling on Nvidia to reinstate the company’s ousted CEO Jensen Huang is that he wasn’t just championing the AI boom. He was using Nvidia’s enormous profits to help finance it.
And Nvidia has a lot of money to spend. The company reported on Tuesday net income of $64.9 billion for the third quarter, which was up from $59.7 billion the prior quarter.
But Nvidia’s continued success depends on having enough customers building and expanding data centers to keep buying its AI chips and systems. Under Huang, the company aggressively invested in AI startups and neoclouds and, more recently, began guaranteeing massive loans to finance new data centers. The biggest deal yet was the $105 billion backstop for OpenAI’s PORTS-Pike data center campus in Ohio, announced in August. In exchange for that loan guarantee, OpenAI promised that the campus would exclusively use Nvidia’s equipment, the company said.
Huang’s abrupt departure last week raised questions about whether Nvidia would continue bankrolling its customers at the same pace. There were reports that CFO Colette Kress, now interim CEO, had tried to push back on Huang’s increasingly ambitious dealmaking. She reportedly reduced the backstop guarantee for the OpenAI project from $250 billion down to $105 billion.
Under Kress, Nvidia clarified in its quarterly CFO commentary that guaranteeing a loan doesn’t mean the company is providing the financing itself. The guarantee only takes effect once the customer has secured the financing needed to build the facility and install Nvidia’s products. If the project fails to meet those conditions, the guarantee “doesn’t exist” she said.
“There is the raising of capital that they may need through equity and or debt, in order to build out any of the buildings, get them ready for use, but also the ability to procure the compute, install the compute, and get ready. What we are indicating in our statement there is that is when our guarantee starts. So if all of that begins and unfortunately they are delayed or they have a challenge in terms of building that, our guarantee doesn’t exist anymore,” Kress said to analysts on Tuesday during the quarterly conference call.
She pointed to OpenAI PORTS-Pike campus as an example of where such terms apply. When asked, she also said this wasn’t a change in policy, but simply a clarification of existing terms. However, these disclosures have never been public before.
Kress also made clear that Nvidia wasn’t bailing on all the ways it was financing the AI boom, especially new data centers. But, she said, Nvidia was going to be far more selective in the future.
“The availability of land, power, shell is such a critical part of the whole structure in terms of building out those data centers. So they may need the assistance. That’s a great asset for anyone to hold. So we will look at that as probably one of our top priorities,” she said. At the same time, she insisted that Nvidia was “taking a very strong look in terms of the types of companies that we would want to help in terms of the financing.”
One of the ongoing criticisms Huang faced was his love of circular deals: invest in his customers so they would turn around and buy his products. Kress is now trying to strike a delicate balance. She isn’t saying she will pull the rug out from under Nvidia’s customers. After all, the company’s investments undoubtedly spurred the AI boom that has been so lucrative for Nvidia. But she’s also signaling that the days of Huang’s freewheeling dealmaking may be over.
