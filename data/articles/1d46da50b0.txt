Gap Inc. saw top- and bottom-line gains during the third quarter, but on Thursday issued a cautious outlook by lowering expectations for the year.
The company also revealed that Ashley Kechter, global brand president at Vuori, will join Gap Inc.’s troubled Athleta division as brand president and chief executive officer on Dec. 14. Kechter succeeds Maggie Gauger, who last October became president and chief product officer at Lululemon. Kechter will report to Gap Inc. president and CEO Richard Dickson. Both Vuori and Lululemon are direct competitors of Athleta.
For the third quarter ended Oct. 31, Gap Inc. saw a 2 percent increase in sales to $4 billion, while comparable sales were flat.
Operating income rose to $396 million from $334 million in the year-ago period. Net income rose to $311 million from $236 million a year ago.
Lowering its expectations for all of 2026, net sales are now seen flat versus the earlier forecast of a 1 percent to 1.5 percent increase. Last year Gap Inc. generated $15.4 billion in sales.
Adjusted earnings per share are now seen coming in at $2.29 to $2.34, down from the previous forecast of from $2.35 to $2.45.
Sales for the fourth quarter are projected flat to down 1 percent year-over-year, which is in contrast to forecasts from retail pundits and trade groups predicting low midsingle industry gains for the 2026 holiday quarter.
By the close of the stock market Thursday, investors had pulled Gap Inc. stock down 4.7 percent to $18.89.
	Despite the lowered outlook, Dickson was mostly upbeat about last quarter. “We exceeded our profit expectations. We delivered over 30 percent earnings per share growth versus last year, and we returned over $800 million to shareholders year to date,” Dickson told WWD. “Importantly, we gained market share, which really reinforces the strength of our product and relevance with our customers.
“When you look at our portfolio, we maintain the number-one market position in kids and baby. We also rose in the ranking to number two in denim and in active. Gap brand delivered another outstanding quarter — 9 percent comp sales growth. That’s the 12th consecutive quarter of positive comps for Gap.”
At Old Navy, Dickson pointed to “meaningful improvement to a flat comp and that’s up against a six-point comp from last year, so I feel very good about the progress we’re making on Old Navy.” Old Navy’s net and comparable sales fell 4 percent in the second quarter of this year. In addition, Michael Francis this month stepped up as president and CEO of Old Navy, succeeding Haio Barbeito.
However, Banana Republic’s performance, down 3 percent, “was very specific to the women’s assortment, which was impacted by a delayed demand with cold weather categories,” Dickson said.
“Athleta just underperformed,” he said.
Asked if there’s a sense of the business overall slowing down after a long run of robust gains, Dickson replied, “Demand was very soft in the first two weeks of November, and really was attributed to the warmer weather that persisted from late October. In fact, according to Circana , the apparel market was down high single digits. [Circana is a market research, consumer insights, and data analytics company.]
“Given Old Navy’s more buy-now, wear-now sensitive consumer, particularly to weather, the brand’s impact was outsized relative to the market,” Dickson added.
However, lately, he said, the weather has turned favorable. “We’ve seen the business respond positively, with the seasonal assortments in particular performing and resonating well. We had a stronger fall assortment emphasizing key categories like denim, active and knits. We had much more compelling marketing and in-store activations, and the proof points are in our market share gains. In denim, we gained share. We moved from the number-three denim position in the U.S. to number two…In active, we launched Old Navy Sport, which marked a really important step for us in establishing a more distinct identity in the activewear category.”
The revised top-line outlook, he said, “reflected two drivers — the slow start to Old Navy, which now that we’ve seen the weather respond, we feel like we’re back on track. And we’re taking a more measured view on Banana Republic in light of the third-quarter performance and Athleta, where trends are just challenging as we navigate that turnaround.”
Gap brand was again the star performer in the portfolio, posting third-quarter sales of $1.1 billion, up 11 percent compared to last year. Comparable sales were up 9 percent, representing the 12th consecutive quarter of positive comparable sales. Broad-based strength across men’s, women’s, kids’ and Baby Gap was cited.
Old Navy, getting a lift from some improved fall product and marketing, reported sales were up 1 percent to $2.3 billion, with comparable sales flat.
On the negative side, Banana Republic’s third-quarter net sales of $449 million were down 3 percent, as were comparable sales. “Customer response to the women’s fall assortment and delayed demand for cold-weather categories weighed on performance,” the company indicated.
Athleta posted third-quarter net sales of $220 million, down 14 percent compared to last year. Comparable sales were down 15 percent “reflecting limited traction from efforts to modernize the assortment and marketing,” the company indicated.
“We are disappointed in the results of Athleta, and it ultimately does need to be recognized that we were in rebuild mode, but we’re officially in turnaround mode,” Dickson told WWD. “We do see the brand’s potential. It’s the sixth-largest active brand in the U.S. It has a fandom. It’s got a distinct and established position in the market, but at the same time, we’re very clear-eyed, and we need to think and operate differently to put the brand back on track. This is a category led by speed of innovation and relevance, which are critical.”
In his prepared statement on the appointment of Kechter, Dickson said, “Ashley is a proven leader with deep activewear expertise, exceptional product instincts, and the operational rigor required to move Athleta forward with clarity and conviction.”
“Athleta’s commitment to empowering women and girls is at the heart of what makes this brand so special and represents a powerful opportunity for its future,” Kechter said in her statement. “I’m excited to return to Gap Inc. and partner with Richard and the team to strengthen our connection with customers and shape the brand’s next chapter.”
Earlier in her career, she held senior roles at Gap Inc., Old Navy and Banana Republic, including chief business officer and general manager of Banana Republic. She also served as chief merchandising officer and general manager at RH.
Before Vuori, she served as global brand president of Fabletics.
