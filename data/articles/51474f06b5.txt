Colombian security forces have stormed a supermarket in the Caribbean city of Cartagena, ending a daylong hostage siege, with the president saying all those held had been rescued and one hostage-taker killed.
Several explosions were heard before security forces entered the building, where about 20 people were believed to be being held by armed men.
President Abelardo de la Espriella, said: “In an extraordinary joint operation between the national police and the armed forces, all the hostages were rescued.”
Shortly after midnight on Wednesday, a police helicopter was hovering low over the shopping centre as security forces massed outside the supermarket. Explosions then echoed around the complex as tactical teams moved in.
Ten of an estimated 30 people that were initially held had already been released during the 17-hour standoff, including several elderly women.
Moments later, soldiers could be seen inside the Éxito supermarket as security forces secured the building.
De la Espriella, who appeared at the scene wearing fatigues and carrying a sidearm and a walkie-talkie, described the operation as a success. He said: “We gave them the opportunity to surrender and free the hostages. They rejected that opportunity and we acted decisively.”
Cartagena’s mayor said all the hostages were “safe and sound” and would be reunited with their families before undergoing medical checks.
The siege gripped Cartagena, a Caribbean port famed for its colonial walled old town, beach resorts and cruise-ship tourism. Supermarket employees had been among those feared trapped inside.
“My son is there,” Jacob Pérez, the father of one of the workers, said earlier in the standoff. “He didn’t leave work and he’s not answering his phone. I think they took it from him.”
Images broadcast by Colombian media and shared online appeared to show masked gunmen in tactical gear alongside zip-tied hostages inside the store.
De la Espriella was quick to rule out the involvement of guerrilla or paramilitary groups. “It’s not that a terrorist group carried out a kidnapping – no, these are criminals who were caught off guard, had no way out, and then kidnapped a group of people,” he said.
Authorities said the siege began after an attempted robbery on an armoured vehicle at the shopping centre.
According to local media, the hostage-takers were demanding nearly US$2m (£1.5m), two armoured vehicles and safe passage to the city of Barranquilla, about 80 miles (130km) north-east of Cartagena. AFP could not independently verify those reports.
The siege began on the 10th anniversary of Colombia’s landmark peace accord with Farc guerrillas amid a series of attacks across the country. Despite the deal to end the region’s largest insurgency, dissident guerrilla groups and criminal organisations still fight for control of parts of the country.
On Monday, authorities blamed a dissident Farc faction for a car bomb attack in the city of Villavicencio that wounded 25 people and caused extensive damage.
Separately, the presidency said a “terrorist attack” on electrical infrastructure had disrupted power supplies in several south-western departments and prompted additional measures to protect energy facilities.
