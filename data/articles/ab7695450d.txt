London architecture practice Tuckey Design Studio has renovated and extended Bay House in rural Yorkshire, using a rich, moody palette intended to evoke the feeling of a wine bar.
The project involved reconfiguring and expanding the country house dating from the early 2000s, while introducing a garden spa and sauna.
Tuckey Design Studio was tasked by the clients to recreate the feeling of their favourite wine bar, which informed a palette of dark timber carpentry, terracotta-painted walls and a bespoke wine storage cabinet alongside a tasting area.
“The ambition was clear from the beginning: to bring the atmosphere of a much-loved London wine bar into the home,” project lead Fraser Biggins told Dezeen.
“The clients’ enthusiasm for wine and music became important touchstones for the design, informing both the arrangement of the spaces and the character of the interiors,” he added.
The home’s previously cramped interiors are now centred on a large, double-height atrium, which serves as both a point of arrival and a gathering and tasting space with a bespoke timber “wine wall”.
This hall is finished with dark timber panelling, terrazzo floors and terracotta paintwork, with a wall alongside an arched composite stone staircase punctured by small square openings.
This central space leads through into the social spaces, with a kitchen and dining space finished using similar dark timber panelling and concrete countertops.
The stairs lead up to the first floor via a landing with a built-in bench, where a balcony connecting the bedrooms enjoys views of the hall below. It also looks across the garden through windows in a new bay window extension.
“The central hall forms the organising principle of the house, providing a shared space from which the surrounding family rooms and activities unfold,” Biggins told Dezeen.
“A series of interconnected spaces, galleries and overlooking balconies create visual connections across different levels, allowing the house to feel both connected and varied,” he added.
On the first floor, two of the existing bedrooms have been converted into a games room and bar space, finished in dark shades of green and grey.
Alongside them are four ensuite bedrooms finished in warmer shades of pale pink and yellow, with each featuring a hand-painted mural designed to “reflect the character of each occupant”.
“Material choices were guided by a desire for honesty, durability and longevity,” Biggins told Dezeen.
“We wanted the spaces to feel timeless and comfortable, without relying too heavily on traditional materials or references.”
“Instead, the palette brings together robust, tactile finishes that feel natural and lived-in, allowing the character of the materials to speak for themselves,” he added.
The exterior of the extension has been finished in composite stone and dark accoya wood, and looks across the home’s garden to the standalone spa and sauna, which was designed as a “complementary counterpoint” to the main home.
Previous projects by Tuckey Design Studio include a home in Wiltshire constructed using walls of unstabilised rammed earth and a residence within a converted chapel in Devon.
