In short:
Tony Popovic is hoping to extend his time as Socceroos coach.
This week's friendly against Jordan could be Popovic's last game in charge, after the Asian Cup was postponed by 12 months.
What's next?
Popovic says he will hold discussions with FA "in the next couple of months".
Tony Popovic has suggested his time in charge of the Socceroos is not over, saying he would be hard pressed to find a job that gives him as much satisfaction as coaching Australia.
Popovic's future has been a topic of hot debate since the delay of January's Asian Cup by 11 months due to the conflict in host nation Saudi Arabia.
The 53-year-old is without a contract beyond Wednesday's (AEDT) friendly with Jordan, which was relocated to Doha after Socceroos players raised security concerns.
"I don't expect it to be the last game, but you never know," Popovic said.
"As it stands, it is, with the Asian Cup postponement."
Popovic turned down a new four-year deal with Football Australia (FA) on the eve of the World Cup this year.
He instead committed to a short-term deal that ran until the end of the Asian Cup. But the tournament's delay has prompted a stand-off over which way the federation goes next.
The initial signs according to FA sources are that the governing body wants Popovic to stay, despite fellow coaches Kevin Muscat being off contract and John Aloisi being out of a job following his sacking by Chinese Super League side Chengdu Rongcheng.
FA chief executive Martin Kuegeler has been in the Middle East in the past week but has yet to sit down with Popovic and go through further details.
"We never had any plans to discuss my future during this period," Popovic said.
"So the Asian Cup postponement doesn't change that and I'm sure that'll all happen in the next couple of months."
Popovic, who boasts a record of 12 wins, six draws and seven losses in charge of Australia, had never coached a national team before succeeding Graham Arnold in 2024.
"I don't think I could ever have a job that would be as good as the one I have now," he said.
"I'm in such a privileged position, I don't take it for granted and I'm very grateful.
"I'm coaching my country, the Socceroos, and it doesn't get better than this, so I don't think I'll ever have something that feels as special as this.
"But I also know football things change, whether you have a contract or not."
