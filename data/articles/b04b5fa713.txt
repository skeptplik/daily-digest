People caught littering or illegally dumping waste in a north-west London borough are set to face higher fines as the council steps up its efforts to tackle persistent fly-tipping.
Harrow Council has approved plans to increase penalties for littering, breaches of Public Space Protection Orders (PSPOs) and the most serious fly-tipping offences.

The penalty for breaching a PSPO will increase from Â£100 to Â£500, while a new Â£5,000 penalty will be introduced for the most serious fly-tipping offences.
The changes follow an increase in the maximum penalties available to councils under government rules.
The highest fly-tipping penalty will only be used in cases involving "aggravating factors", such as hazardous waste or repeat offences.
Other factors include the dumping of multiple bags, whether the waste affects vulnerable people, and whether it was dumped by a business for financial gain.
Speaking at a cabinet meeting where the proposal was approved last week, councillor Pritesh Patel said: "The reason for these changes is that the government has increased the maximum penalties available to councils. This gives us an opportunity to strengthen our response to environmental crime.
"Most people do the right thing, but there is a minority that don't, and it is important that the consequences are meaningful and encourage prompt compliance and resolutions."
Harrow Council has tried a number of approaches to tackle fly-tipping over the years, including installing CCTV cameras in hotspots, sharing images and videos of offenders on social media and taking people to court.
But in January 2026, the council was criticised for failing to prosecute enough offenders at a fly-tipping hotspot in Harrow Weald.
Residents said a site on Weighton Road had to be cleared every few days. Despite a CCTV camera being in operation, only seven people had been sanctioned in the previous year.
One resident told a cabinet meeting in February that seven fines could be issued in a week, "let alone 52".
At the time, the council described the situation as "disgusting" following the long-running problems.
