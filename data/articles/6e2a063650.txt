Roblox has struck a licensing agreement with UK music rights organization PRS for Music, opening up a new revenue stream for songwriters and composers whose work is used on the platform.
The deal licenses music use across Roblox, and PRS for Music will start distributing royalty payments to songwriters, composers, and publishers next year. Financial terms were not disclosed.
According to PRS for Music, the agreement establishes the principle that "when music helps create value within online worlds, the people who write and compose it should share in that value." Roblox is the latest platform to sign up as PRS for Music expands its license coverage across the games industry.
"Roblox is far more than a gaming platform; it is where entire communities create, share and play in immersive virtual experiences," said Dan Gopal, chief commercial officer at PRS for Music.
"Games are now among the world's most consumed entertainment media across various platforms," Gopal added. "And partnerships like this one with Roblox establish important foundations for how the industry moves forward - ensuring that as music helps drive value for creators and consumers, the songwriters and composers behind it share in that success."
"We're thrilled to welcome PRS as a partner as we build the future of the music economy on Roblox, championing the songwriters and composers whose work powers our community's culture," said Jessica Meehan, head of music partnerships at Roblox.
Roblox's latest financial results beat expectations for its third quarter, with revenue up 16% year-on-year to $1.6 billion. Bookings fell 12% to $1.7 billion after a stronger growth period in 2025, though Roblox noted Q3 bookings were up over 50% on a two-year basis.
Average daily active users (DAUs) declined 9% year-on-year, while hours engaged also dropped 15% to 34 billion, again due to "difficult comparisons" to 2025.
