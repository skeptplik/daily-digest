Dozens still missing in Russian, guided-bomb attack that killed more than 30 in southern Ukraine
Kremlin says talks with Ukraine, U.S. will take place Nov. 10-11 in Abu Dhabi
Rescuers in southern Ukraine continued an intense search for survivors Tuesday at a bombed-out aid facility and shelter where at least 31 people were killed and dozens were still missing.
Teams of emergency workers and volunteers picked through debris at the cratered blast site in Komyshuvakha, a village in Ukraine's southern Zaporizhzhia region, where bodies were pulled from the rubble and carried away in white plastic sheeting. More than 45 residents are still missing.
"The scale of the tragedy is terrifying," Mykhailo Semikin, acting head of the region's military administration, wrote in an online post. "This is one of the worst tragedies in terms of the number of civilian victims in our region since the start of the full-scale war."
Three days of mourning were declared in Zaporizhzhia, where Russian bunker-busting bombs, some filled with nearly 1.5 tonnes of explosives, were used in Monday's attack.
The dead included four police officers and two rescue workers, Semikin said Tuesday. At least five people were hurt.
Russia launched 289 attack drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles across nine regions, triggering emergency power cuts, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said in an online post that noted the barrage coincided with U.S. elections.
"The Russians think America won't notice this signal of theirs," Zelenskyy said. "But we need to notice and stop such terror. We need to help Ukraine defend its skies; otherwise, Russia will never stop its missile strikes."
Series of talks planned
Amid the attacks, diplomatic efforts aimed at ending the nearly five-year war are taking place.
Zelenskyy was in Berlin on Tuesday to meet with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who has made clear he intends to show his support for the Ukrainian leader a week before U.S.-brokered Russia-Ukraine peace negotiations resume.
Germany has become Ukraine's largest supporter in its war with Russia since Donald Trump returned to office last year and withdrew U.S. financial support. Washington still provides intelligence to Ukraine and allows other countries to buy U.S. weapons on Kyiv's behalf.
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy are shown arriving for a joint news conference in Berlin on Tuesday. (Fabrizio Bensch/Reuters)
Russia and Ukraine will head to Abu Dhabi in the United Arab Emirates on Nov. 10-11 for talks that include U.S. officials, it was confirmed Tuesday.
Zelenskyy said from Berlin that security guarantees, an "energy ceasefire" and a truce in the Black Sea to guarantee food exports will be discussed in Abu Dhabi.
The Kremlin said that Vladimir Medinsky, an aide to Russian President Vladimir Putin, will serve as Moscow's chief negotiator at the talks.

Previous attempts by Turkey to mediate an end to the war that started with Russia's invasion in February 2022 did not lead to a breakthrough, though the two sides made a number of exchanges of prisoners and war dead.
Ahead of those talks, the European Commission on Wednesday will host a meeting on the war in Ukraine. Representatives of the U.S., Ukraine, NATO and European allies are expected to participate.
White House envoys have talked to both sides in recent weeks as the U.S. looks to mediate a peace deal to end the war, Europe's deadliest conflict since the Second World War. But questions have emerged as to American loyalties in the wake of a diesel deal the U.S. struck with Russia, and antagonistic statements made by Trump regarding Zelenskyy.
Ukraine and Russia both agreed to a six-day ceasefire for humanitarian purposes last week in the southern Ukraine city of Oleshky, in the Kherson region.
Melania Trump, the wife of the U.S. president, helped push for that ceasefire and will host representatives from Ukraine and Russia at the White House on Thursday. It was not immediately clear who would be representing the two countries.
Ukraine hits Russian refinery again
Meanwhile, Moscow kept up its nightly attacks on power plants and energy infrastructure ahead of winter, causing extensive damage and electricity outages as it carried out 20 strikes in 10 regions overnight, Ukraine's Energy Ministry said.
In the northeast region of Kharkiv, a combined Russian missile and drone attack wounded at least 62 people, local authorities said.
A strike damaged a medical facility in the Nemyshlianskyi district, shattered windows in four apartment buildings and damaged cars. More than 200 patients were transferred to other medical facilities, head of the regional military administration Oleh Syniehubov said.
In the Dnipro area, rescuers pulled a woman alive from the rubble Tuesday morning, after a Russian attack killed one person and wounded 17, local authorities said.
The attack damaged an outpatient clinic and a hospital. Patients were transferred to other hospitals, said Oleksandr Hanzha, head of that regional military administration.

Russia's Defence Ministry said Tuesday that it had shot down 856 Ukrainian drones overnight. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said 493 drones were fired toward the Russian capital.
A Ukrainian drone attack wounded two employees at an oil refinery in the Russian city of Yaroslavl, regional Gov. Mikhail Yevrayev said Tuesday. He added that there was damage to the refinery's infrastructure due to a fire that erupted from an attack involving 124 drones.
The Slavneft Yaroslavnefteorgsintez (YANOS) oil refinery, which Ukraine has struck at least twice before, is located some 250 kilometres northeast of Moscow.
Rolling blackouts have been instituted in the south, west and east of Russian-occupied Crimea following attacks on energy infrastructure, Moscow-installed officials said Tuesday.
