In short:
The Wallabies have lost eight games in a row on European soil.
Australia lost to England (45-22) and Scotland (15-12) in successive games.
What's next?
ABC Sport will have live blog coverage of the Wallabies against Wales in Cardiff at 6am AEDT on Sunday.
The eternally upbeat Wallabies are relishing the promise of a rare northern-hemisphere dry track on which to fight fire with Wales in a pivotal spring tour showdown in Cardiff.
Looking to avert a ninth successive loss in Europe and a first season whitewash at the hands of the four UK home nations in 68 years, the Wallabies are intent on taking the fight to the suddenly formidable Welsh.
Strike forward Tom Hooper admits Les Kiss's side were too passive on attack in the tour-opening 45-22 defeat to England, before leaving their run too late in a defensively improved 15-12 loss to Scotland on Sunday.
With Wales on a high after downing the All Blacks for the first time since 1953, the Wallabies know a more clinical attacking display will be required this Sunday (AEDT).
"That first English game, we probably didn't move the ball around well enough, and when you're versing quality oppositions with quality defences, you've got to keep them guessing on what you've got to do," Hooper said on Wednesday, local time.
"We've got to make sure that we've got multifaceted weapons that we can throw at the Welsh this weekend and that's something that we'll be looking to do."
Australia's misfiring attack again proved costly in Edinburgh with Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii squandering an opportunity to set up a match-winning try after the bell at Murrayfield.
Instead of going through the hands, which probably would have resulted in Max Jorgensen clinching victory down the right sideline, Suaalii threw a cut-out pass straight to Jorgensen rather than drawing the last defender for fullback Tom Wright.
The backline's lack of execution under pressure has been a common occurrence during Australia's run of four successive defeats, including back-to-back trouncings from New Zealand.
"Sometimes we're trying too hard to maybe force something that's maybe not there," said playmaker Ben Donaldson, one of the best after coming on late against the Scots.
"So we're doing a lot of drills and training around that skill execution: short passing, long passing, ID-ing what's in front of us. We're trying to find cues to then attack off that.
"Sometimes you can go through the motions of running a play or throwing a pass, but not actually looking at what's in front of you."
Donaldson is hoping the Wallabies' attack will be sharper under a closed roof at Principality Stadium.
"It's a bit challenging — we trained today in the wet, on a muddy field," he said.
"But we know we've played in a lot of dry-weather conditions this year, so I feel like that's when we play our best footy. When we move the ball, we've got some great skilful forwards, some backs who like to get the ball in space.
"So it's really going to help us. Under the roof, I think you'll see some pretty good footy."
Coach Kiss will name his match day 23 on Friday.
