The scandal, which was reignited earlier this month by passages in Usman Khawaja's autobiography, is big news in Britain.
As the civil war rages Down Under, each day has provided blistering critiques in Blighty's press.
Filing from Perth last week before a one-day match between the old enemies, The Times's chief cricket correspondent, former England captain Mike Atherton, wrote: "Can there ever be a better time to be in this beautiful country than when Australian cricket is tearing itself apart?"
For those wondering, his answer was "obviously, no".
Perhaps Atherton is in a unique position to comment? In 1994, he was accused of ball tampering by using dirt in his pocket to scuff the pill during a Test match against South Africa. He was fined, but has always denied wrongdoing.

Around London's famous Oval cricket ground, which is set to host the fifth Ashes Test next year, fans of the sport were critical of the way the scandal was being handled in Australia.
"I think you guys over in Australia, your fans are probably going to be a bit quiet compared to the English fans [when the Ashes start next year]. And to be honest, I think you should be," said England supporter Charlie Banfield, who spoke to the ABC at The Rose pub a short walk from the stadium.
He said Australia had brought the game into disrepute and that the scandal would be unsettling ahead of the Ashes.
"Hopefully, they'll get what's coming to them," Banfield said.
Another cricket fan, Vikrant Kheterpal, was a little more sympathetic.
