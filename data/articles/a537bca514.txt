UK developers who released games, apps or content on the Google Play Store between August 2018 and July 2026 can now claim for their share of a £260 million settlement agreed in a class action suit earlier this year.
The case was brought by academic Barry Rodger, and alleged that Google was abusing its dominant position to prevent developers from using alternative storefronts, and charging an unfair commission. It echoed the previous US judgement that Epic won against Google in 2022.
The company settled the case before it went to trial, without admitting liability, and the funds are now being distributed to developers. The case was brought as an "opt-out" class action suit, which means that affected businesses were automatically included, and can claim for their share at no cost. Over 10,000 businesses are eligible.
The claim covers apps, games, subscriptions or in-app content sold through the Google Play Store since August 2018, and every eligible developer will receive a minimum payment of £200, a figure which increases based on their sales through the storefront. Payments are calculated from Google’s records of each developer’s Play Store sales and the commissions it paid between 22 August 2018 and 31 July 2026; the most successful titles are in line for million-pound payouts.
Developers can check if they are eligible and make a claim through the official claims portal until 24 March 2027. Those eligible should have already received an email to the address associated with their Google Play account.
“If an email about a Google settlement has landed in your inbox, it isn’t spam," said Rodger in a press release. "After more than two years of work, compensation is now ready for the UK businesses that sold apps and digital content on the Google Play Store, from one-person studios working at their kitchen tables to established games companies."
“For a decade, the big question about the UK’s class action regime has been whether the money ever reaches the people it is meant for," said Damien Geradin, founding partner of legal firm Geradin Partners, who acted for Professor Rodgers. "Today, thousands of UK businesses have the opportunity to claim their share of this settlement, free of charge."
The Google settlement is the largest pay-out for members of a collective action ever approved by the Competition Appeal Tribunal, which launched in 2015. Last week it dismissed a similar claim alleging uncompetitive practice by Sony on the PlayStation Store, saying that consumers had alternative buying options in the form of physical copies and releases on Xbox.
