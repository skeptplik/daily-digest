The Russian government announced Friday that it will impose a six-month ban on buckwheat exports amid a declining harvest and shrinking stockpiles.
“The decision was made to maintain stability on the domestic food market,” the government said in a statement.
The ban goes into effect on Saturday and expires on May 28, 2027. Russia last imposed a temporary export ban on buckwheat in the summer of 2021.
The ban makes an exception for exports to member countries of the Moscow-led Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU), the breakaway pro-Russia Georgian regions of Abkhazia and South Ossetia, as well as for humanitarian aid deliveries.
Russia exported $65 million worth of buckwheat in the first 10 months of 2026, according to the Interfax news agency, which cited data from the Agriculture Ministry.
China has been the top buyer, purchasing 157,000 metric tons worth $56 million, followed by Kazakhstan, Japan and Turkey.
Stockpiles of buckwheat at processing and procurement companies have fallen nearly 30% over the past year.
In September, a ton of raw buckwheat cost 15,259 rubles ($552.4), up by more than 50% year-on-year.
In 2025, Russia’s buckwheat harvest fell by more than a fifth, from 1.2 million metric tons in the previous year to 926,000 metric tons.
Known as grechka, buckwheat is a staple of the Russian diet.
Separately, the government announced Friday that it eliminated the 5% export duty on peas, chickpeas and lentils shipped outside the EAEU through March 31, 2027, citing falling global export prices and the need to boost the competitiveness of Russian goods abroad.
