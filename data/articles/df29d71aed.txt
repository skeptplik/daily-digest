SBC Medical Group has warned in a filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) that two cybersecurity incidents exposed sensitive customer and employee information, including personal data belonging to 1.6 million people.
SBC Medical Group is a clinic management company whose main business is in Japan, but it also has a limited presence in the U.S., is traded on Nasdaq as SBC, and holds a minority stake in the medical spa chain OrangeTwist. Its clinics receive 7 million patient visits annually.
The first compromise concerns the SBC databases, while the second involves unauthorized access to clinic administrator accounts on Tribeau, a third-party booking platform.
The company discovered the database breach on November 16 and was notified of the Tribeau compromise the same day. Both incidents affected its Japan-based businesses.
"On November 16, 2026, unauthorized access occurred to certain databases within a website system managed and operated by SBC Medical Group, Ltd., the Company’s, Japanese subsidiary, in connection with its management services in Japan," reads a FORM 8-K filing with the SEC.
"The Company confirmed that an unauthorized third party accessed and extracted customer information. The method of intrusion and full scope remain under investigation."
The incident impacting SBC databases exposed basic personal information belonging to 1,624,000 people, including:
- Full names
- Contact details
- Dates of birth
- Patient card numbers
- Member IDs
- Passwords
- Survey responses
Not all of the above data types were exposed for all 1.6 million people.
Additionally, the incident exposed 255,416 reservation requests, 217,769 inquiries submitted to SBC websites, and thousands of treatment description records, image filenames, and customer reviews.
The company said the attackers also stole approximately 20,000 employee account records, including names, IDs, and passwords. For a small subset of 459 employees, the affected data also included bank account and tax information.
The second incident involved administrator accounts on the Tribeau booking platform, a service used by 56 clinics.
The operator reported access to reservation information for 12,641 people, as well as access to treatment names, dates, and fees for 6,804 people who received treatment.
SBC noted that it has not confirmed unauthorized access to electronic medical records or information from its mySBC reservation site in the database breach.
As of November 18, when the Form 8-K was submitted, the company has not seen any evidence of misuse of the exposed data.
SBC said the investigation remains ongoing, and at this time, it could not estimate the financial impact of these cybersecurity incidents.
