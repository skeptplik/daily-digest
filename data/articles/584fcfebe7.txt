In Namie, a town in Fukushima Prefecture rebuilding after the 2011 Great East Japan Earthquake and nuclear disaster, a hydrogen station run by a private company is struggling to stay afloat, highlighting the financial challenges facing Japan’s push toward a hydrogen-powered future.
Along National Route 459, which runs from Namie toward the village of Katsurao in the prefecture, a sign catches the eyes of passing motorists.
“Hydrogen, close at hand and easy to use,” it reads. Another message promises free 30-minute test drives in fuel-cell vehicles.
