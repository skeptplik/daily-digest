Anthropic boss Dario Amodei reportedly once suggested that video games could be the solution to political strife caused by AI-fueled joblessness.
In a profile of Anthropic’s Dario and Daniela Amodei from the Wall Street Journal, it is reported that when Dario Amodei worked at OpenAI from 2016 to 2020, he allegedly suggested video games could pacify the masses after AI took their jobs.
Here’s the relevant section of the profile:
There [at OpenAI], he engaged in debates with his fellow employees, suggesting that immersive video games might be a solution for the political strife caused by AI-induced joblessness, according to widely circulated Slack messages.
The anecdote suggests a foresight from Dario Amodei about the potential of AI to cause mass layoffs, but also a rather dystopian solution that would see jobless people content to live out their lives playing video games rather than kick up a stink about losing their livelihoods.
While this section of the WSJ profile mentioned Amodei’s alleged thoughts on this issue from some time ago (potentially a decade ago), and he may have changed his mind since then, it’s interesting to consider his mindset in the context of growing concern around mass layoffs as companies turn to AI to replace human workers to save money and, they claim, boost productivity.
Survey data from Pew Research Center published in September showed how ordinary people around the world are worried about the economic impact of AI. In 34 of 37 countries Pew Research Center surveyed earlier this year, people tend to believe AI will lead to fewer jobs rather than more jobs. And people are getting more worried about this over time. In the U.S., the share of people who believe AI will result in job loss over the next 20 years increased seven percentage points in two years.
The profile also has a delicious timing, coming as it does around the launch of GTA 6, a game that you’d imagine Dario Amodei might have considered to be a video game "immersive enough" to pacify the jobless masses long term.
Reaction online to the profile is, predictably, a mix of outrage and mockery, with some posting The Matrix and Ready Player One memes in response. "Unless we’d get paid for playing video games, I don’t see how that would work. Boredom isn’t the problem with joblessness," one person said. “How do people buy games if they don't have a job that pays them?” another wondered.
The answer from people who are convinced of our AI-powered future to that common retort is, as Elon Musk is fond of saying, abundance for all. Musk believes that AI will create a post-scarcity economy where goods and services have become so plentiful that labor and money become redundant. In such an economy, no-one would need a job, because, so the theory goes, everyone has all they could ever want at the push of a button — for free.
In such an economy, could video games keep everyone occupied enough to prevent political strife? Could a game like GTA 6 keep you busy… forever?
