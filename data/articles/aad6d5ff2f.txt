Nelson Asofa-Solomona apologises for leaping into crowd after knocking out Paul Gallen in heavyweight boxing clash
A furious Nelson Asofa-Solomona has leaped into the crowd in sensational scenes following his knockout victory over fellow former NRL star Paul Gallen in Sydney.
Gallen, returning to the ring for the final time, was knocked out with 10 seconds remaining in the first round of their five-round heavyweight bout at the TikTok Entertainment Centre.
The 45-year-old was hit with a thunderous right that sent him back against the ropes, before four devastating upper cuts, the last of which was landed despite Gallen already being down.
As Gallen lay stricken on the ropes in considerable distress, Asofa-Solomona leaped onto the corner ropes to celebrate his win.
Then, incredibly, the 200cm, 137.5kg giant leaped over the ropes and ran to someone in the crowd.
Footage appeared to show the former Storm forward swing a wild punch towards a spectator, who was later led out the building by police.
"I just want to apologise for what I did, I acted like a bloody idiot," Nelson Asofa-Solomona said in the ring.
"I just got in a bit of the heat of the moment.
"I should not behave like that, I'm sorry."
Gallen, meanwhile, who surrendered a 20cm height advantage and more than 33kg in weight, was able to sit up.
He was later shown laughing with his shaken son in the dressing room, seemingly recovered.
Former world champion Tim Tszyu said the knockout was "frightening", while Gallen's former Sharks teammate Michael Ennis was in a state of shock at the sheer violence meted out on his friend.
"That was really hard," Ennis said.
"It was really hard to watch."
George Rose, promoter of No Limit Boxing, did not criticise Asofa-Solomona, but was adamant that attending his shows was safe.
"One thing that we love with our shows is that we like to make it a safe and enjoyable and entertaining experience for everyone," Rose said on Main Event.
"We want to make sure that it's something that people can be a part of and feel safe to come and enjoy."
Asked later, Asofa-Solomona said: "Someone was doing an ape gesture. I stood on the ropes and it looked like someone threw something at me.
"If I had my time back, I would, because that's not on. But what that guy did to me, in terms of that gesture, is not on either.
"I'd just seen red."
Earlier in the night, Paris Olympian and Commonwealth Games medallist Callum Peters, despite being slightly off colour, maintained his unbeaten record with a unanimous points win against rugged Colombian Juan Diego Ortiz.
Meanwhile Paulo Aokuso delivered a statement knockout to beat Mobin Khahrazeh in the second round of their IBF Inter-Continental light heavyweight title fight.
