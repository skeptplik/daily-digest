COPENHAGEN, Denmark—If we lived in the world that Volvo’s product planners expected back when the current XC90 debuted in 2015, both it and the smaller but related XC60 (which arrived in 2017) might have been the last of their kind as everything went electric. As we know, the future hasn’t turned out quite like that, especially in the critically important US market. At the same time, Europe and China are electrifying, which seems like an argument against spending resources on developing all-new replacements for models that might not have an eight-year lifespan in most markets.
So instead, Volvo has given the XC60 and XC90 another lifecycle refresh. The exterior styling tweaks are minimal; the bigger three-row XC90 had already been given a new look last year, and the XC60 now gains a similar grille and the same matrix headlights, for instance. And that’s no bad thing—both were, and remain, handsome shapes that are popular with Volvo’s customer base.
Instead, the Swedish automaker’s attention has been on bringing the technology up to date for model year 2028 standards. Which means a much bigger battery for the PHEVs, with sufficient capacity for a couple of days of daily driving for most of us before needing to be recharged. There’s also new, modern infotainment and interiors that are now even nicer to spend time in than before.
We first saw the revamped 2028 XC60 (and learned about its technical details and those of the XC90) in September, but with the cars going into production now and due to reach the first US customers in the first quarter of next year, we got the opportunity to spend some seat time with the updated cars.
