The Shortlist
PhotoVogue is proud to unveil the shortlist of 100 photographers and video makers selected for Brave New Visions:
Creativity as Rebellion, our Global Open Call dedicated to the power of creativity to challenge the status quo, question
established narratives, and imagine alternative futures.
With 95,758 submissions from 9,160 artists across 155 regions, the response to this year's Open Call was extraordinary.
But beyond the numbers, what moved us most was the imagination, urgency, and creative courage of the work we received.
We live in a world saturated with images. More images are produced and consumed than ever before, yet more images do not
necessarily mean more understanding, more imagination, or more freedom. What matters is not simply creating something
new, but finding a way to make us see, feel, or question something we might otherwise have overlooked. Perhaps today,
one of the most rebellious things an artist can do is refuse to give us another image we already know.
Rebellion does not always announce itself loudly. It can be political and confrontational, but it can also be intimate,
poetic, playful, uncomfortable, or deeply personal. It can take the form of tenderness, humor, beauty, or an unexpected
way of looking at something familiar. What makes it rebellious is the freedom to question what we have been taught to
accept and to imagine what might exist beyond it.
This year's Open Call brought together an extraordinary range of ideas, emotions, and visual languages. Some works
confront urgent social and political realities. Others explore identity, memory, belonging, desire, or the complexities
of human experience. What connects the 100 selected artists is not a shared aesthetic or a single definition of
rebellion, but a willingness to take risks, experiment, and follow their own vision.
Images do more than represent the world. They influence how we understand it, whose stories we recognize, and what we
believe is possible. The artists presented here remind us that creativity can do more than reflect reality. It can
challenge the assumptions through which we see it and make space for futures we have not yet imagined.

Meet Our Official Jury
Meet the international jury of Brave New Visions: Creativity as Rebellion, bringing together artists, photographers, editors, curators, and creative voices from different disciplines and backgrounds. Together with the Vogue and Condé Nast Committee, comprising more than 100 editorial and creative professionals from across our global brands, these jurors have brought their individual perspectives and sensibilities to the selection process. Their participation reflects something fundamental to PhotoVogue's philosophy: there is no single authority on what makes an image meaningful, no universal definition of beauty, and no one way of seeing the world. It is through the encounter of different perspectives that new possibilities emerge.
Photographer, Filmmaker & Curator
Born in Addis Ababa in 1974, Aïda graduated from Howard University with a degree in Communications, majoring in Film. Her photography is in major collections including MoMA, the Smithsonian, and the RISD Museum. She’s received numerous awards, including the EU Prize in Bamako, the CRAF Award, and the Royal Photographic Society Award. Aïda co-curated the Nobel Peace Prize exhibition in 2019 and was a commissioned artist in 2020. A Canon ambassador and founder of Addis Foto Fest and Africa Foto Fair, she also leads Africa Print House and advocates for health, the environment, and photography across Africa.
Model & Actress
Alex Consani is a model and actress who was named 2024 Model of the Year at the Fashion Awards in London, becoming the first transgender woman to receive the award. A face of Gucci and YSL Beauty, she has graced the digital cover of American Vogue and made history as one of the first transgender women to walk the Victoria’s Secret Fashion Show. Her acting credits include American Horror Story: 13 and A24’s upcoming film Peaked.
Curator, Founder & Artistic Director, Culture Art Society (CAS)
Awa Konaté is an independent curator, cultural strategist, and founder of Culture Art Society (CAS), a platform dedicated to research, exhibitions, and critical discourse on African and diasporic visual arts. Her practice draws on archival research, Black feminist pedagogies, and memory work, with a focus on interdisciplinary and lens-based practices by artists of African descent. She has collaborated with institutions including Guggenheim Abu Dhabi, Serpentine Galleries, Raven Row, and the Barbican, and served on juries for Rencontres de Bamako and BlackStar Film Festival. Her writing has appeared in Third Text, Foam Magazine, and Phaidon, among other publications.
Director, Foto Colectania (Barcelona)
Elisa Medde is an Art Historian specializing in Photography and Visual Culture, focusing on image, communication, and power structures. She has chaired juries for awards like the Luma Rencontres Book Award and MAST Foundation Grant. Elisa collaborates with European universities including ECAL, HSLU, and UWE Bristol. She has curated photobooks and exhibitions, with writings in FlashArt, Time Magazine, Vogue Italia, and more. Editor-in-Chief of Foam Magazine (2012–2023), she won two Lucie Awards and received the 2023 Royal Photographic Society Award for outstanding achievement in Photography Publishing.
Global Head of Photography, The Guardian
Fiona has over 20 years of picture editing experience across newspapers and is a curator, speaker on photojournalism, and mentor in the photography community. She was picture editor of The Guardian for ten years before becoming Head of Photography for Guardian News and Media Group. Fiona covered major historic events like 9/11, global conflicts, natural disasters, and refugee crises. She has judged awards including the Sony World Photography Awards, Taylor Wessing Portrait Prize, and chaired the 2024 World Press Photo Awards jury. Fiona also serves on the board of Stills Gallery, Edinburgh.
Director, Paris Photo
Florence Bourgeois has been Director of Paris Photo since 2015. With a background spanning fashion, luxury, and the art world, she previously held positions at Givenchy Parfums, Artcurial, and the Pavillon des Arts et du Design (PAD) in Paris and London. At Paris Photo, she has introduced initiatives including PRISMES, Emergence, and Elles x Paris Photo, developed in partnership with the French Ministry of Culture and Kering’s Women In Motion to increase the visibility and recognition of women artists. She has also overseen the fair’s international expansion in Los Angeles and New York and championed the acquisition of works by women artists through institutional partnerships. Actively involved in the photography ecosystem, she serves on numerous juries and commissions, supporting emerging talent and contemporary photography. In 2023, she was awarded the title of Officière des Arts et des Lettres by the French Minister of Culture.
Performance Artist & Choreographer
Florentina Holzinger is an Austrian performance artist and choreographer whose work spans dance, theatre, and opera, exploring the limits of the body through extreme physicality and theatrical experimentation. Her practice challenges the boundaries between high culture and counterculture, spectacle and subversion, often incorporating body modification and a wide range of body-based disciplines. Her productions have received the Nestroy Award for TANZ (2020) and the Faust Award for Ophelia’s Got Talent (2023), and have been selected four times for Theatertreffen Berlin. Since 2021, she has been an associate artist at Volksbühne am Rosa-Luxemburg-Platz in Berlin. In 2026, she represents Austria at the 61st Venice Biennale.
Freelance Writer, Host, Brand Consultant, The Messy Truth Podcast
Gem Fletcher is a writer, consultant and podcaster. Her work has been published in Foam, Aperture, Dazed, Creative Review, 1000 Words and The British Journal of Photography. She also hosts The Messy Truth podcast, a series of candid conversations that unpack the future of visual culture and what it means to be a photographer today.
Artist, Stylist & Creative Director
Ib Kamara is a Sierra Leone-born artist, stylist, and creative director known for his daring, interdisciplinary approach to fashion image-making. Raised in Gambia and educated at Central Saint Martins in London, he served as Editor-in-Chief of Dazed from 2021 to 2025 and Creative Director of Off-White from 2024 to 2026. His work spans editorial and commercial collaborations with artists including Beyoncé, Rihanna, Madonna, and Rosalía, and brands such as Chanel, Louis Vuitton, and Gucci. A contributor to international editions of Vogue, AnOther, and i-D, his work has been exhibited at Somerset House, Red Hook Labs, and the Victoria & Albert Museum. In 2021, he received the British Fashion Council’s Isabella Blow Award for Fashion Creator.
Stylist, Creative Consultant & Fashion Editor
Imruh Asha is a Paris-based stylist, creative consultant, and Fashion Director of Dazed, where he has been part of the team since 2021. Drawing on his Dutch-Caribbean roots and contemporary art, he brings a concept-driven and interdisciplinary approach to fashion image-making. Since his appointment as Fashion Director in 2022, he has pushed the boundaries of fashion imagery through ambitious collaborations, including projects that have broken three official Guinness World Records. In 2021, he presented Future Shock, his first sculpture exhibition, in collaboration with the Ere Foundation at Dover Street Market’s 3537 space.
Global Creative Director, Vanity Fair
Jennifer Pastore is an award-winning visual storyteller with more than two decades of experience shaping the imagery of leading magazines and cultural institutions. She is currently the Global Creative Director of Vanity Fair Magazine and previously held roles as the Visuals Director of Sotheby’s Magazine and the Executive Photography Director of WSJ. Magazine, where she commissioned photography, film, and illustration. Earlier in her career, she held roles at Harper’s Bazaar, Teen Vogue, and T: The New York Times Style Magazine. In 2022, she was awarded the Rome Prize in Design by the American Academy in Rome.
Photo Director, Artist & Curator. Former Director of Photography, The New York Times Magazine
Kathy Ryan was the longtime Director of Photography at The New York Times Magazine, where, over 39 years, she pioneered the integration of fine art photography and photojournalism, collaborating with leading photographers and championing new talent. She edited The New York Times Magazine Photographs (Aperture, 2011), which inspired an international touring exhibition, and is the author of Office Romance (Aperture, 2014), a collection of photographs made entirely on her iPhone. Since leaving the Times, she has focused on her own photographic practice, curating exhibitions, teaching at Yale, and speaking internationally. Her numerous accolades include the Royal Photographic Society Award for Outstanding Service to Photography (2012), the International Center of Photography's Spotlight Award (2024), and the Dr. Erich Salomon Prize (2025).
Visual Journalist, Researcher & Curator
Kira Pollack is a creative director and photo editor pioneering visual storytelling through photography, technology, and digital media. A 2025 Walter Shorenstein Fellow at Harvard Kennedy School, she explores AI’s role in preserving visual history. Formerly Creative Director at Vanity Fair and Director of Photography at TIME, she led iconic projects including The 100 Most Influential Photographs and TIME’s first documentary film unit. Pollack’s work earned Emmy, Lucie, Webby, and National Magazine Awards. She has served as a juror for World Press Photo and Tribeca Film Festival and is based in New York City.
Founder & Creative Director, Be Good Studios
Lina Kutsovskaya is a creative director whose work bridges fashion, art, and advertising. She has led influential campaigns including #MyCalvins at Calvin Klein and creative collaborations with Virgil Abloh at Louis Vuitton. After beginning her career at Vogue, she held creative leadership roles at Nylon, Teen Vogue, Barneys New York, and Calvin Klein, before founding Be Good Studios, a creative agency working across campaigns, brand identity, publishing, and exhibition-making. From 2024 to 2026, she served as Art Director of Another Man, and in 2025 was appointed Contributing Creative Director of Vogue Japan. She is also the founder of Little Big Man, a gallery and publishing project based in Los Angeles.
Curator, Writer & Editor
Melissa Harris is Editor-at-Large at Aperture Foundation, where she served as Editor-in-Chief of Aperture magazine from 2002 to 2012, earning the publication a National Magazine Award for General Excellence. During her more than twenty years at Aperture, she edited over forty photography books and curated exhibitions internationally. Her curatorial projects include Mary Ellen Mark: Encounters (2023–2024), A Kind of Language at Fondazione Prada (2025), and a retrospective of Graciela Iturbide’s work at C/O Berlin (2026). She has taught at Yale, Columbia Journalism School, and New York University, where she has been part of the Tisch School of the Arts for twenty years. She is also Creative Director of Magnum Foundation’s ongoing US Dispatches / Time Capsule project.
Writer, Art Curator & Professor, New York University
Nicole R. Fleetwood is a writer, curator, and professor at New York University, as well as a MacArthur Fellow and elected member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences. She is the author of Marking Time: Art in the Age of Mass Incarceration, which received the 2020 National Book Critics Circle Award, as well as On Racial Icons and Troubling Vision. Her curatorial projects have been presented at institutions including MoMA PS1, Aperture Foundation, the National Underground Railroad Freedom Center, and the Brown Arts Institute. Her work has received support from leading cultural and research institutions, including the Mellon Foundation, Ford Foundation, and New York Public Library’s Cullman Center for Scholars and Writers.
US Head of Photography, The Guardian
Noelle Théard is a photographer, educator, and the US Head of Photography at The Guardian. Previously, she was Senior Photo Editor at The New Yorker, where she also produced Photo Booth, the magazine’s photography column. Her work focuses on social justice, community engagement, and the intersection of education and visual storytelling. She co-founded FotoKonbit, a nonprofit empowering Haitians to tell their own stories through photography, and has held roles at Magnum Foundation, Parsons, and the Museum of Contemporary Art in Miami. She has also taught African Diaspora and Art History at Florida International University.
Member of the Board of Directors, Friends of Palais de Tokyo
Olivia Anani is a Paris-based curator, writer, and art market expert with a background in Asian Studies and Contemporary Art. She has worked at Christie’s, Sotheby’s, and Phillips, contributing to the sale of major masters and the reevaluation of African artists. Olivia serves on the Board of Friends of Palais de Tokyo and leads its Young Patrons initiative. She supports institutions like the Louvre and is involved in developing a new cultural institution in Benin. Anani has contributed to major monographs and was named among France’s 100 Most Influential Women in Culture in 2021.
Co-founder & Editor-in-Chief, Atmos
Willow Defebaugh is the co-founder and editor-in-chief of Atmos, an award-winning climate and culture magazine that seeks to re-enchant people with our shared humanity and the Earth. She is the author of The Overview, a deep ecology newsletter and book, and host of The Nature Of, a podcast from Atmos. She is a lifelong student of nature and graduated with a degree in creative writing from the University of Michigan. Her work has been featured in Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, Teen Vogue, V Magazine, Interview, i-D, BBC, The Guardian, Them, New York Magazine, and more. She lives in Brooklyn on unceded Lenape territory.
