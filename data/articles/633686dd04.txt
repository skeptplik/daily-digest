Nicalis has pulled AI-generated music from The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ after players discovered it in the game's latest update, prompting a wave of negative Steam reviews.
The music - tied to a run themed around Grand Theft Auto 6 - released on November 19, the same day GTA 6 launched. The following day, a player claimed the music was "100% generated by Suno (AI) (with proof)," highlighting tracks whose metadata referenced the AI music tool Suno.
Binding of Isaac creator Edmund McMillen responded on X/Twitter, saying he had woken up to "a shit show."
"Obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned," he wrote.
Nicalis has since pushed a new build with "critical fixes," including the "removal of generated music files," but it has not explained how the music ended up in the game. Neither The Binding of Isaac: Repentance nor Repentance+ currently discloses AI use on its Steam store page.
McMillen's most recent game, Mewgenics, was released earlier this year.
Last month, CEO of Epic Games, Tim Sweeney, criticized Steam's AI disclosure policy, calling the company's position on AI disclosure "a political act." Back in June, he also suggested that Valve's decision to have disclosure labels for projects on Steam that make use of AI was "really irresponsible."
