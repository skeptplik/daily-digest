Next month in New York, Bonhams will auction a 1960s Chanel couture skirt suit that’s more than just the sum of its parts. The suit was owned and worn by actress, singer and style icon Marlene Dietrich. She later passed it to her only daughter, Maria Riva, and it has remained in the family until now.
The suit is going under the hammer for the first time at the “New York Luxury: Handbags and Fashion” online sale, which is taking place online from Dec. 1 to 10.
Made by Gabrielle “Coco” Chanel’s studio between 1960 and 1964, the suit has a cream silk and lace exterior with white and blue sequin embellishments and a front button fastening. It is being offered with an estimate of $4,000 to $6,000.
Marissa Speer, Bonhams’ head of sale for handbags and fashion, U.S., said the Chanel suit brings together “an important fashion house and the personal history of one of the 20th century’s most iconic women.”
Speer added: “Its richly patterned cream silk and lace exterior and decorative sequin detailing embody the house’s celebrated craftsmanship and understated glamour. We are honored to have been entrusted with bringing this remarkable piece to auction.”
The German-born Dietrich was known for her gender-bending style, and her habit of flouting tradition. During the 1920s and 1930s she wore men’s tailored suits, tuxedos and hats, for her on-stage performances and in real life, and always caused a scandal.
She was a rule-breaker in other ways, too, becoming a naturalized U.S. citizen in 1939 after rejecting the Nazi ideology, and entertained Allied troops during World War II as part of the United Service Organizations, or USO. She sang in German, English and French and was known for songs “Lili Marlene,” “Falling in Love Again” and the Edith Piaf classic “La Vie en Rose.”
