California voters rejected a first-in-the-nation tax on the net worth of billionaires.
California billionaires won a contentious ballot measure campaign, defeating a proposed one-time tax on their wealth. The measure would have imposed a one-time 5% tax on the wealth of Californians whose net worth exceeds $1bn.
The bruising campaign over Proposition 40 was the most expensive in state history and witness to back-and-forth dealing from tech billionaires and democratic leaders, including Gavin Newsom, the governor, who steadfastly opposed the measure. While supporters of Prop 40 cast it as a solution to drastic healthcare cuts from Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill Act, opponents led by prosperous billionaires mounted an influential campaign saying the billionaire tax was instead “an everyone tax”.

The education department announced today that it has launched investigations into public school districts in Pennsylvania and New Mexico, alleging that the schools’ gender identity policies violate federal law. The Trump administration has taken repeated actions to restrict transgender rights.
In a press release, the education department cited “recent reports from Albuquerque Public Schools and Santa Fe Public Schools in New Mexico” that “raise concerns that these districts maintain policies that prevent school personnel from sharing information about a child’s ‘gender transition’ or ‘gender identity’ with their parents.”
It added that “in Pennsylvania parents allege that the Pennsbury School District facilitated their daughter’s so-called “social transition” to appear as a boy for three years without informing them” and said it was taking enforcement action with thejustice department against the Pine-Richland School District “for policies directing school staff, including nurses and psychologists, to facilitate ‘gender transitions’ for students without parental consent.”
“The Trump Administration has made upholding parental rights and transparency in the classroom a key part of our education agenda – and we will continue to do so,” said education secretary Linda McMahon. “Policies designed to conceal or hide the most sensitive information about a child’s well-being not only violate federal law but also educators’ moral obligation to let parents be the first decision-makers in their child’s lives.”
The justice department has sued Lyft, alleging the ride-share service discriminated against passengers with disabilities, including those who use service animals and mobility devices such as stowable wheelchairs.
In a press release, the department said its lawsuit “seeks $53 million for individuals with disabilities who Lyft has discriminated against, and who have submitted complaints to Lyft or the Department.”

The man accused of assassinating Charlie Kirk will stand trial in June 2028, a Utah judge ruled today.
District Judge Tony Graf allocated 105 days for the trial, with jury selection slated to begin on 21 June 2028. Graf said the 2028 date balances the defense’s right to prepare with the Kirk family’s desire for a speedy resolution.
Attorneys for defendant Tyler Robinson, 23, had proposed a trial starting in 2029. Robinson could be eligible for the death penalty if convicted.
In a statement, the Kirk family called the hearing date a “delay” and said that “every additional day without justice prolongs our family’s pain.”
