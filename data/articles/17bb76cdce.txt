The Prince and Princess of Wales’s second official visit to India reached its high point today, as William hosted the sixth annual Earthshot Prize ceremony in Mumbai. The event, which awards £1 million ($1.3 million) each to five winners tackling the world’s most pressing environmental challenges, was held on the subcontinent in recognition of the fact that it has offered more winners and finalists than anywhere else since the initiative was founded in 2020. It also marked Catherine’s first appearance at the awards since Boston in 2022, with the couple marking the occasion with a rare red-carpet (or, in this case, green) photo call.
And while the Princess’s wardrobe over the past few days has largely consisted of her tried-and-tested staples—a Roksanda dress here, an Edeline Lee suit there—tonight was the night to pull out all the stops. Catherine chose a hand-embroidered anarkali of sindoori-red georgette by New Delhi-based designer Mahima Mahajan, which she paired with a necklace and earrings from Queen Elizabeth II’s emerald tassel suite and a burgundy velvet Prada minaudière.
William similarly paid tribute to Indian design, wearing a black bandhgala by Mumbai-based Manish Malhotra—one of Indian cinema’s most influential costume designers, who later turned his hand to couture—and the man behind Simone Ashley’s custom lehenga for the evening. Also in attendance were actors Sonam Kapoor and Alia Bhatt, as well as the three remaining members of Take That.
Beyond today’s engagements, though, which included the inaugural Children’s Earthshot Awards—for which Catherine rewore a blue linen Gabriela Hearst suit last seen at Wimbledon earlier this year—the Prince and Princess will spend the remainder of their visit celebrating the deep cultural ties between Britain and India through cricket, film, and, of course, fashion. All while shining a light on the country’s remarkable environmental progress. (India reached its target of having 50% of its installed electricity capacity come from non-fossil fuel sources five years ahead of schedule, while its solar capacity has grown more than 50-fold in little over a decade.)
“Too often, the world’s emerging economies are presented with false choices: growth or the environment; prosperity or protection,” as William told a crowd of changemakers at the Earthshot Prize’s 2026 Impact Assembly yesterday. “But India shows us a different picture. A picture full of color, complexity, and possibility.”
