PARIS — The Champs-Élysées is betting on Hollywood star power, a new immersive light show and the return of its Christmas market to reinforce its standing as one of the world’s top tourist and retail destinations, as the city seeks to reassure visitors following weeks of protests and unrest.
Cate Blanchett inaugurated this year’s Christmas illuminations Sunday night with a splashy — if the rain puddles counted — ceremony that drew thousands of tourists to the avenue.
Blanchett greeted the crowd in French, calling it an “iconic” avenue ahead of the countdown. She was followed by a performance by French band Feu! Chatterton.
The lights will feature a new audiovisual display set to “The Four Seasons” by Vivaldi, and the Christmas market will return for the first time in 10 years, after it was suspended following the 2016 terror attacks.
“This is not a commercial operation. It is an operation of reputation and renown,” said Comité Champs-Élysées president Marc-Antoine Jamet at a press conference before the ceremony.
He said the event was less about boosting sales for retailers than strengthening the reputation of one of the world’s best-known shopping streets.
“I don’t think there is a genuine demand for an increase in turnover. On the other hand, there is a real demand among the shopkeepers around here for image, reputation, renown and attractiveness,” he added.
Jamet said the ceremony and the return of the traditional Christmas market are part of an overall plan to convey “a message of calm and tranquility.”
The event took place against a backdrop of continuing political tensions in Paris. Storefronts along the Champs-Élysées, some of which had been boarded up following the Oct. 17 protests, have since reopened and were decked out in holiday decorations by Sunday night.
Demonstrations have been taking place weekly on Saturdays since October, and while unions have called for the protests to continue, the most recent demonstrations have been largely calm.
Asked whether additional security measures had been introduced for this year’s lighting event, Jamet said the gathering was open to everyone and that no special security arrangements had been put in place around it.
“As you can see, it’s very open. Everybody is very welcome, and you don’t see any policemen around you,” he said. “We are here to celebrate and to be happy together.”
Blanchett’s appearance was intended to give the event a stronger international profile. The Oscar winner follows previous guests including French actress Léa Seydoux and Canadian singer Charlotte Cardin.
“We said to ourselves that, at a certain point…there has been a colossal effort made by the Champs-Élysées Committee. The idea was to find someone who could create sparks,” he said of Blanchett, noting that coverage would be “systematically” picked up in the key markets of the U.S., Japan and China due to her presence.
The actress “accepted quickly,” Jamet noted. The risers were set up just in front of the Louis Vuitton flagship.
Jamet said the illuminations were intended to send a message “of harmony, of coming together, of celebration,” he said, similar to the 2024 Olympic Games that were held in the city.
Again, he emphasized that it would elevate the avenue’s standing on the global stage.
“It is the same thing as the Times Square ball. It is the same thing as the Sydney Harbour Bridge when it lights up for the first time on the first day of the year,” he said. “We really do have this role, and we would like to amplify it.”
Jamet said the committee hopes the return of the traditional Christmas market — filled with wood cabins and mulled wine — will encourage visitors to stay on the avenue for several hours rather than simply stopping to see the lights.
Jamet said the illuminations had attracted an estimated 14 million visitors last year, and organizers hope to exceed that figure this year.
“We want Parisians to come back, and tourists to come,” he said, describing the ambition as creating an avenue that was “open to the world and open to everyone.”
The new display was developed by French lighting company Blachère and uses 500,000 individually controlled LED diodes to create moving patterns, shadows and images across the avenue every 15 minutes.
The committee also highlighted the event’s energy consumption, saying the six-week display used roughly as much energy as two Parisian families consumed in a year. A representative of the lighting company said energy consumption had fallen by almost 95 percent over 20 years.
For the avenue’s retailers, the event is part of a longer-term effort to maintain the Champs-Élysées as an international destination, at a time when Paris’ image has been repeatedly damaged by televised demonstrations and disruption.
Jamet said the committee wants to ensure that the historical avenue remains a place of celebration and gathering.
“This is the avenue of mourning, and it is the avenue of joy,” he said. “But very honestly, we’re going to be much more in the register of joy, happiness, coming together and celebration.”
