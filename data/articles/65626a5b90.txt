Introducing the Vogue Business People Moves Tracker, a running list highlighting the key executive appointments and exits across the fashion and beauty industries. From CEOs to creative directors to other key C-suite hires, we track the leadership shifts that are reshaping the industry.
November 2026
Background: Caroline Deroche Pasquier joins Dior in a newly created role as director of press, VIP and cultural relations. She began her career at Saint Laurent before joining LVMH, where she worked at Louis Vuitton and Givenchy. During the last six years, Pasquier has led global communications for Bottega Veneta and then Versace. At Dior, she will work closely with the house’s creative director Jonathan Anderson, reporting to Olivier Bialobos, deputy managing director in charge of global communication and image.
Effective: November 16, 2026.
Company comments: “[Deroche Pasquier’s] career spans communication, creative collaboration, international markets and cultural relationships, giving her a broad perspective on the development and expression of luxury brands,” the company said in a statement.
Background: Oliver Timm has voluntarily stepped down from Hugo Boss, where he’s been both chief sales officer and deputy CEO since 2024, as well as the company board. In the meantime, CEO Daniel Grieder, alongside CFO and COO Ivica Maric, will split Timm’s previous duties. Timm was initially appointed as chief sales officer in 2021, to oversee Hugo Boss’s two brands (Boss and Hugo), including wholesale, e-commerce, visual merchandizing, retail operations, and customer relations.
The announcement follows Hugo Boss’s 5% sales drop in Q3 — attributed to an ongoing turnaround at the company — and Frasers Group’s de-facto takeover of the company on October 23.
Effective: November 30, 2026.
Company comments: “Oliver Timm and I have been working side by side throughout our careers for almost 30 years, and it is a great honor for me to look back on this time and the many moments we have shared. I will miss working alongside him,” said Grieder in a statement. “During his time at Hugo Boss, he has repositioned sales with a future-oriented omnichannel approach and a strong customer-centric mindset, making a significant contribution to our growth and digitalization. As I look back at these achievements, I wish him, also on behalf of the entire managing board, the very best for the future.”
Michael Murray, Hugo Boss chair and Frasers Group CEO, also shared his appreciation: “On behalf of the supervisory board, I would like to thank Oliver Timm for his contribution to Hugo Boss over the past years. We appreciate his commitment and wish him all the best for his professional and personal future.”
Background: Ben Hallam, most recently SVP of marketing at Four Seasons Hotels and Resorts, has been appointed as Lululemon’s chief brand officer. At the sportswear brand, Hallam will lead global brand marketing, brand creative, store experience, and community. He will join the company’s senior leadership team and report to CEO Heidi O’Neill who joined in April 2026.
The announcement comes after the appointments of Maggie Gauger as president and chief product officer and Joseph Godsey as chief operating officer. On Friday, the company also announced the appointment of Emily Sunderland as chief strategy officer, effective November 23. Sunderland joins from Boston Consulting Group and will report to chief financial officer Meghan Frank. Searches remain underway for a chief technology officer and a chief communications officer.
Effective: November 9, 2026.
Company comments: “Ben brings an exceptional combination of creative vision, commercial discipline, and experience transforming iconic, premium brands at scale,” O’Neill said in a statement. “His proven ability to translate brand strategy into distinctive and compelling guest experiences will be critical as we strengthen how our brand comes to life across every touchpoint. Ben will play a central role in building a stronger and more competitive Lululemon.”
Background: Daisy Shaw-Ellis has been appointed CMO of Malone Souliers, where she will oversee the brand’s global marketing and communications, with an emphasis on expansion in North America. Shaw-Ellis joins from Condé Nast, where she was most recently accessories and jewelry director for Vogue and Vanity Fair.
Effective: November 2, 2026.
Company comments: “We are delighted to welcome Daisy to Malone Souliers as global chief marketing officer,” CEO Andrew Wright said in a statement. “Her experience in executive positions at some of the industry’s most established and influential titles, alongside her exceptional eye and proven track record in luxury brand-building, will both enhance and amplify our voice globally. This is a pivotal moment for Malone Souliers as we embark on a new and exciting trajectory, and we look forward to presenting the brand in new and exciting ways in the coming seasons.”
October 2026
Background: Clarisse Godbillon, who was CFO and COO of Lemaire from 2020 to 2025, is returning to the brand as managing director. She left Lemaire in 2025 to become COO of Jacquemus, and made the move back to the French label after Laetitia Mergui stepped down from the head job earlier this year.
Effective: September 2026.
Company comments: “Clarisse possesses an in-depth understanding of the company’s various activities and a profound knowledge of its identity,” the house said in a statement. “As managing director, she will oversee the development of the brand and lead its strategic and operational direction, working closely with [the house’s artistic directors and majority shareholders] Christophe Lemaire and Sarah-linh Tran.”
Background: Lady Gaga has moved into the CEO role at Haus Labs, the clean beauty company she founded in 2019. This comes after co-founder and former CEO Ben Jones exited the brand in early October. Gaga will now lead all commercial aspects of Haus Labs, alongside her prior responsibilities of creative direction and product development.
Effective: October 29, 2026.
Background: Ryan Lally has been promoted to chief brand communications officer at Ralph Lauren Corp. Formerly SVP of global public relations and brand communications, Lally will continue to build on the company’s global comms strategy in this newly created role, while overseeing talent partnerships, events and more. Lally has been with the company for more than two decades, and will continue to report to David Lauren, chief branding and innovation officer.
Effective: October 28, 2026.
Background: Alexis Hoopes, formerly global fashion VP at Ebay, has joined The RealReal as chief merchandising officer. At Ebay, she led the company’s $10 billion global fashion business for two-plus years, including the expansion of its Authenticity Guarantee program to new categories, price points and markets, strengthening trust among buyers and sellers. Hoopes will now lead The RealReal’s merchandising strategy, focusing on its luxury assortment and personalized product discovery — as the platform further advances its AI capabilities and unlocks growth opportunities.
Effective: October 28, 2026.
Company comments: “Alexis is a proven leader who brings a rare combination of luxury merchandising expertise, global marketplace experience and operational discipline,” Rati Sahi Levesque, president and CEO of The RealReal, said in a statement. “As we build on our leadership in luxury resale, her ability to connect exceptional merchandise with evolving consumer demand will be instrumental in accelerating growth.”
Background: Hector Muelas joined LVMH in 2015 as global VP of content and creative, after working for Apple. Between 2017 and 2019, he was chief brand officer of LVMH-owned luggage brand Rimowa under chief executive Alexandre Arnault. Muelas then returned to Apple as global group creative director in 2019, a role he held until 2021. He returned to LVMH in 2023 as Tiffany & Co.’s global chief brand and creative officer, reuniting with Alexandre Arnault.
He has now been named Louis Vuitton chief brand creative officer, a newly created position reporting to Louis Vuitton deputy CEO Damien Bertrand.
Effective: November 1, 2026.
Company comments: “Hector will define and steer our creative storytelling across all touchpoints, thereby ensuring strong overall image consistency. Leading creative and content execution, he will oversee all brand campaigns, with the art buying team led by Magena Chansarel and the content factory team led by Carole Fuchs joining his organization. In close collaboration with our business units, regions, and creative talents, Hector will further elevate our brand desirability, ensuring the house continues to set the benchmark in cultural resonance, creative boldness, and emotional connection with our clients worldwide,” Bertrand said in an internal announcement.
Background: Bruno Pavlovsky, who most recently exited the board at Chanel, but remains president of Chanel SAS and fashion, is now co-chair of The Fashion Pact. A non-profit organization, The Fashion Pact is a coalition of CEOs from more than 150 companies representing a third of the global fashion industry by volume. Pavlovsky will work alongside Inditex CEO Óscar García Maceiras and climate campaigner Paul Polman to build on the work of the pact in creating a net-zero fashion industry. (Chanel is among the coalition’s founding signatories back in 2019.)
Effective: October 20, 2026.
Company comments: “As we look ahead to 2027, our focus is increasingly on translating collective ambition into implementation, and scaling solutions across the industry. Bruno’s appointment marks an important step in this journey, bringing valuable leadership and perspective as we work to strengthen collaboration across regions, business models and supply chains,” The Fashion Pact wrote on LinkedIn.
Background: Bruno Pavlovsky joined Chanel in 1990 and became a board member in 2018, a duty he will now step down from. Since 2022, he has also been president of the Fédération de la Haute Couture et de la Mode. Pavlovsky remains president of Chanel SAS and of the house’s fashion activities.
Effective: October 15, 2026.
Company comments: “This latest move marks the completion of the board’s evolution toward a composition of independent directors, bringing diverse external expertise and perspectives, while retaining executive representation through the global executive chair and global CEO. Bruno Pavlovsky remains fully committed to his role as president of Chanel’s fashion activities and president of Chanel SAS,” Chanel said in a statement.
Background: Zankov has appointed Jack Tobin, founder of luxury brand development consultancy Jack Tobin Agency, as CEO and business partner of Zankov, formalizing a partnership that has developed since Tobin began consulting for the brand two years ago. Since beginning its partnership with Jack Tobin Agency, Zankov has increased its international business more than tenfold and doubled its global stockist network.
Effective: October 1, 2026.
Company comments: “Zankov is at an incredibly exciting stage in its growth. I have always envisioned having a partner who could join me in shaping the next chapter of the brand — someone who shares my ambition to build an enduring business while staying true to the creative vision that has always defined Zankov. Jack is the ideal partner to take this next step with, and I’m excited about what we can build together,” says Henry Zankov, founder and creative director.
Background: Net-a-Porter (NAP) has appointed Devina Foley as Americas president to oversee the retailer’s US and Canada strategy. With more than 20 years of experience across retail, merchandising and e-commerce, Foley will help strengthen NAP’s market positioning as it settles into a new era under LuxExperience Group. She has held senior leadership roles at Bandier, Rockets of Awesome and Gilt, following earlier positions at Loft and Gap Inc. Based in New York, Foley will report to NAP CEO Heather Kaminetsky.
Effective: October 8, 2026.
Company comments: “Devina brings an impressive breadth of experience and a proven track record of driving growth and leading businesses through transformation,” Kaminetsky said in a statement. “Her leadership and expertise will be invaluable as we continue to grow Net-a-Porter’s US business.”
Background: LVMH has promoted Clara Boissonnas, formerly women’s leather goods product director at Louis Vuitton, to CEO of Pucci. Boissonnas joined Louis Vuitton in 2002 as a product manager, before exiting four years later. After a decade working across Lanvin and Byredo, she returned to LV where she helped shape the maison’s coveted leather goods business. At Pucci, Boissonnas will strengthen the brand’s distinctive creative identity, desirability, and international reach, while reporting to Pietro Beccari, chair and CEO of the LVMH Fashion Group. As for Saar Debrouwere, who has led Pucci since 2022, her future role will be announced in due course, according to the group.
Effective: January 1, 2027.
Company comments: “Clara has made a significant contribution to Louis Vuitton through her deep product expertise, strong creative sensibility, and unwavering commitment to excellence. Her ability to combine creative ambition with a clear understanding of the business makes her ideally suited to lead Pucci into its next chapter,” Beccari said in a statement.
September 2026
Background: Yanina Novitskaya, who has spent the last 19 years at Cartier, most recently as CEO of South East Asia and Oceania, is the next chief executive of Richemont-owned Delvaux. Delvaux, the world’s oldest fine leather goods house, was founded in Brussels in 1829. In its next chapter, Novitskaya succeeds Jean-Marc Loubier, who returned to lead the brand after its acquisition in 2021, and will report to Richemont fashion and accessories CEO Philippe Fortunado.
Effective: December 1, 2026.
Company comments: “Yanina’s strong knowledge of the luxury industry will be instrumental in leading Delvaux into its next chapter, strengthening its distinctive positioning and continuing to drive product desirability and international growth,” Fortunato said in a statement.
Background: Marc Jacobs has appointed John Wattiker as CMO to lead the brand’s global marketing strategy. Wattiker joins the brand after almost 15 years at Hearst Magazines working in the company’s advertising business, where he most recently held the role of VP of fashion and luxury. Prior to Hearst, he worked in the banking sector. At Marc Jacobs, Wattiker will oversee efforts to strengthen brand positioning, deepen consumer engagement and further expand the recently acquired brand’s global reach and relevance under new owner WHP Global.
Effective: September 28, 2026.
Company comments: “John brings deep experience across luxury, fashion and media, with a clear understanding of how to build brands and engage consumers,” Marc Jacobs CEO Eric Marechalle said in a statement. “He pairs creative instinct with strategic and commercial discipline and brings an extensive network of relationships built over the course of his career. I am confident he will be a great addition to our leadership team as we grow Marc Jacobs globally.”
Background: Chanel has appointed a creative makeup director, Lucia Pieroni. Previously, Pieroni was creative director of makeup at Shiseido-owned Clé de Peau Beauté (2006-2019), which led to a series of partnerships with photographers and designers where Pieroni helped shape beauty direction for fashion shows, editorials, and campaigns. At Chanel, she will work alongside the makeup creation studio reporting to Simona Cattaneo, president of fragrance and beauty.
Effective: September 2026.
Company comments: “Lucia possesses an extraordinary instinct for beauty. She perceives the individuality in every face and understands with precision how to reveal it, creating makeup that feels intuitive and expressive, while always celebrating singularity. Her artistic vision and unique savoir-faire will make this collaboration a decisive new chapter for Chanel Makeup,” Cattaneo said in a statement.
Background: Alistair McCallum, formerly global communications VP at Burberry, has been appointed chief marketing officer of Givenchy. McCallum arrives with international comms experience at fellow LVMH house Fendi, and before that, at Alexander McQueen, where he worked with now-Givenchy creative director Sarah Burton. He will report to brand CEO Amandine Ohayon.
Effective: September 23, 2026.
Company comments: “The arrival of Alistair McCallum [...] on our executive committee brings recognized expertise in the luxury and fashion worlds to support our international ambition and teams worldwide,” Ohayon said in a statement.
Background: LVMH-owned jewelry brand Repossi has appointed Matthieu Soudan as managing director. The hire comes as the brand’s former CEO Anne de Vergeron returns to her management responsibilities at the LVMH group level. Soudan has been in the LVMH ecosystem for nine years, the majority of which were spent as general manager of Tag Heuer before moving to Bvlgari in 2025. At Repossi, he will lead the brand into its next phase within the world of contemporary high jewelry, while strengthening its positioning and global reach.
Effective: September 22, 2026.
Company comments: “I am delighted to entrust the leadership of Repossi to Matthieu Soudan as we open a new chapter in the development of this distinctive maison. At Tag Heuer and more recently at Bvlgari, he has demonstrated a strong strategic vision, together with the ability to execute strategies that support operational excellence and the desirability of our maisons,” Stéphane Bianchi, group managing director and CEO of LVMH Watches & Jewelry, said in a statement.
Background: Capri has appointed Catherine So as the company’s chief legal and sustainability officer. So joins from Tiffany & Co., where she most recently served as senior vice president and general counsel, where she oversaw legal, compliance, intellectual property, litigation, governance and brand protection. She spent 15 years at Tiffany across various roles.
Effective: October 19, 2026.
Company comments: “We are pleased to welcome Catherine to Capri Holdings. She is a highly respected legal leader with extensive experience with helping develop global luxury brands and executive teams through periods of transformation and growth,” CEO John D. Idol said in a statement. “Her expertise, leadership, and strategic perspective will be invaluable as we continue to strengthen our business and position our brands for long-term success.”
Background: Poshmark has made a string of executive hires. Creative consultant Jamie Hunter, who worked as a buyer and fashion director at Bergdorf Goodman, has been appointed fashion and creative director of the California-based resale company. This new role signals Poshmark’s ambition to become an authority in the fashion resale space, by bringing its curation, styling and creative direction in-house, informed by Hunter’s two-plus decades in the industry.
Other new hires include: Yoshitaka Matsuzawa as SVP of seller and community experience, leading product development across consumer experiences and AI; Kishore Kumar Mohan (formerly The RealReal) as engineering SVP; Lesley Clifford as VP of merchandising, also a new role supporting a more strategic approach to assortment and brand relationships; and Beth Newman (formerly Canada Goose) as PR and communications director.
Effective: September 14, 2026.
Company comments: “We’ve been deliberate about bringing in leaders who add deep expertise in their respective fields and fresh perspectives to Poshmark. With experience spanning fashion, technology and retail, they’re strengthening our leadership team and helping us sharpen our fashion point of view,” chief revenue officer Elizabeth von der Goltz said in a statement.
