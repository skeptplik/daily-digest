This article was originally published in August 2018. It has been updated with the latest information.
It's been five years since No Time to Die, Daniel Craig's final James Bond film, was released and we finally officially know for certain who his successor is for the role of 007: Robert Pattinson.
Even before Craig stepped down, the rumor mill was in overdrive speculating about who would succeed him in the role of 007. This has been a media tradition throughout the franchise’s long history ever since Sean Connery retired his license to kill. But while there have only been now seven actors to (officially) play 007 onscreen, there have been dozens that the media has erroneously labeled as being “the next James Bond.”
For the purposes of this feature not running longer than SPECTRE, we’re going to just narrow it down to those actors most often reported as “the next James Bond” in the Daniel Craig era, starting from the time Pierce Brosnan exited the franchise in 2002 until the present.
Here then are all the actors who had been pegged as the Next James Bond … and who never ended up playing Bond (well, not yet anyway).
Nicholas Hoult
James Bond is the second major role that we know of that Hoult has lost out on to Robert Pattinson, the first being the title role in Matt Reeves' The Batman. Hoult wound up getting cast as Lex Luthor in James Gunn's Superman, although we'll have to see now if Hoult will stick around as Lex after Man of Tomorrow comes out in the wake of Gunn's exit from DC Studios.
Callum Turner
The Fantastic Beasts, Masters of the Air, and One Night Only actor seemed like the front runner to star in James Bond 26 when word spread of the screen tests for director Denis Villeneuve. Turner also seemed like he had the edge given that his wife is pop star Dua Lipa, who could've also done the movie's theme song. But it wasn't meant to be.
Jack Lowden
The Slow Horses star's passionate fanbase campaigned hard for Lowden, as did his co-star Gary Oldman, but in the end Robert Pattinson prevailed.
Josh O'Connor
O'Connor starred opposite Daniel Craig in the Knives Out sequel Wake Up, Dead Man, who told IGN he started the rumor about O'Connor being up for the Bond role just to annoy him while doing press. But O'Connor still ended up screen-testing for the role, ultimately losing out to Pattinson.
Jack O'Connell
The Sinners and Ink actor's name was brought up in recent reports as being a contender for 007 but, as far as we know, he never screen-tested for the role.
Jacob Elordi
The Frankenstein, Euphoria and Wuthering Heights star was widely seen as a leading and bankable contender for the role, but whether or not he ever seriously considered the role or was formally offered it remains unclear.
Aaron Taylor-Johnson
The Nosferatu, Kraven the Hunter and Werwulf actor had been the subject of Bond casting buzz going back to before EON Productions parted ways with the franchise. His name seemed to disappear from being in contention once the new producers and Amazon MGM Studios came aboard.
Tom Holland
Was the Spider-Man star ever truly up for the role or was it all just rumor-mongering by the press? Holland always did seem a bit too young and too short for the role.
Idris Elba
The Dark Tower and Thor actor’s name resurfaced recently as Daniel Craig’s successor, something the actor already called “a very old rumor” way back in 2011! "I would do it, but I don't want to be called the first black James Bond. Do you understand what I'm saying? Sean Connery wasn't the Scottish James Bond and Daniel Craig wasn't the blue-eyed James Bond. So if I played him, I don't want to be called the black James Bond,” Elba said in 2011. "If [then Bond distributor] Sony decided to call me and said hey, we wanted to do [James Bond], I'd definitely consider it." (Note: Sony is no longer the distributor of the Bond movies and it will be the producers’ not the distributor’s call on who plays 007.) Many fans certainly want Elba for the role, but they have also said the same thing about …
Tom Hardy
The Fury Road and Venom star’s name has come up many times through the years, most often in association with director Christopher Nolan, an avowed Bond fan who has made no secret of his desire to direct a Bond film. While Nolan and Hardy already made their own Bond-esque film with Inception, Hardy was linked with the role again as recently as July 2018. Hardy, for his part, has made cheeky comments about the rumors in interviews and on social media. Time will tell if Hardy ever takes on the secret agent.
Tom Hiddleston
The Loki actor has made no secret of his desire to play Bond. The rumors really heated up in 2016 when he reportedly met with EON Productions about the role. Of course, the producers could have just been meeting with Hiddleston as leverage against a then-reluctant to return Daniel Craig. In any case, Bond producer Barbara Broccoli reportedly thought Hiddleston wasn’t tough enough for the role, and Daniel Craig eventually signed on for Bond 25.
Henry Cavill
The Henry Cavill-James Bond rumor was actually legit at one point. The Superman star was the runner-up for the role back when Casino Royale was being cast but, then at only age 23, he lost out to Daniel Craig. Cavill, however, has made absolutely no secret about still wanting the role, reiterating his interest in it as recently as the Mission: Impossible - Fallout press tour, telling Yahoo!, “I would love to do it of course. I think Bond would be a really fun role. It’s British, it’s cool. I think that now that I have my Mission: Impossible badge we can do real stunts and really amp it up as well.”
Clive Owen
Long before Idris Elba was the actor everyone seemed to believe was the inevitable choice as the next James Bond there was Clive Owen. The actor’s tuxedo-clad turn in the late ‘90s film Croupier kicked off years’ worth of rumor-mongering that he would succeed Pierce Brosnan as 007. Even now, at age 53, Owen still gets asked about the chances he’ll be the next James Bond despite having said he was never even offered the role to begin with.
Hugh Jackman
The Wolverine actor was rumored for the role quite a bit during the 2000s, but last year he admitted to Variety that he’d actually turned down the part years earlier: “I was about to do X-Men 2 and a call came from my agent asking if I’d be interested in Bond. I just felt at the time that the scripts had become so unbelievable and crazy, and I felt like they needed to become grittier and real. And the response was: ‘Oh, you don’t get a say. You just have to sign on.’ I was also worried that between Bond and X-Men, I’d never have time to do different things.” The timing of Jackman’s approach would have coincided with the release of Pierce Brosnan’s final Bond film, Die Another Day, in 2002.
Damian Lewis
The Billions actor has been a reported “next James Bond” since his days on Homeland, with bookies reportedly naming him the odds-on favorite for the role back in 2015. When asked about the rumors in 2017, Lewis quipped, “By the time they make a decision [on Bond] I’ll be dead. … So no one need worry any more.”
Michael Fassbender
While the X-Men and Prometheus actor had long been rumored as Daniel Craig’s successor, he essentially ruled himself out of the running in a 2016 interview: “No, I think the franchise needs something new." Fassbender praised Craig for having done “a cracking job” with the role.
Dan Stevens
The Beauty and the Beast star was first linked with the role back during his Downton Abbey days, but it was his lean, mean, butt-kicking turn in The Guest that led the press to proclaim him “the next James Bond.” But then Daniel Craig signed back on for one more go as 007 …
Eric Bana
The Aussie actor, who parlayed his acclaimed turn in the true crime flick Chopper into lead roles in Hulk and Troy, was a reported frontrunner to succeed Pierce Brosnan after Die Another Day. Bana repeatedly denied the rumors back in 2004, and his co-star in Munich, Daniel Craig, won the role in the 2006 reboot Casino Royale.
Ewan McGregor
The Star Wars and Trainspotting actor reportedly turned down the role of 007 back when Casino Royale was gearing up, but his views on the opportunity have evolved since then. "I think it would be quite cool to play James Bond," McGregor told The Hollywood Reporter in 2015. "At this stage in my career (being typecast) is not really an issue anymore. Early on, when you start off you don't want your horizons to be narrow, but I think people know I have some kind of width in my work, so I'm really not worried about that anymore."
Julian McMahon
The late Doctor Doom and Nip/Tuck actor said back in 2004 that he’d been “in talks” for the role but later reportedly passed on the role due to his TV commitments.
Sam Worthington
Before he starred in the highest-grossing movie of all time, Avatar, Worthington was one of the reported finalists who did costumed screen tests for Casino Royale along with Daniel Craig and Henry Cavill.
Christian Bale
Bale’s name first surfaced in 2002 as a possible replacement for Pierce Brosnan, with the actor reportedly saying at a convention that he’d be open to playing the role. In 2014, however, Bale shot down old reports that he was offered the role before Daniel Craig.
Goran Višnjić
The Croatian native and ER actor screen-tested back in 2005 for Casino Royale and was reportedly a favorite of director Martin Campbell’s. The role went to Daniel Craig, of course.
Rory McCann
Long before playing Sandor “The Hound” Clegane on Game of Thrones, the Scottish actor reportedly screen-tested for the role of Bond back in 2005 for Casino Royale.
Jamie Bell
The Fantastic Four and Turn actor made headlines as a Bond contender in 2016 when he reportedly met with producer Barbara Broccoli about the role. Broccoli and Bell were making the movie Film Stars Don't Die in Liverpool at the time, and the producer was said to have been a fan of Bell’s for sometime.
Luke Evans
Dan Stevens isn’t the only Beauty and the Beast star who’s been linked as a frontrunner for the role. Luke Evans was reportedly on the shortlist to replace Daniel Craig in 2016 before the latter signed on for one last film.
Aidan Turner
The Poldark star reportedly had preliminary meetings with Bond producers back in 2016. After Daniel Craig ended up agreeing to return for Bond 25, Turner informed the British press in 2018, “There’s someone else playing the role at the moment, but when it’s free I’ll have a look at it.”
James Norton
The McMafia and Happy Valley actor was reportedly eyed for the role back in 2016 before Craig signed on for Bond 25. Norton told the press in July 2018, “I would love to play a Bond villain. I love Daniel Craig and I’d love him to do more films. He’s brought a whole other angle to the character — and long may that continue. But people love to speculate.”
Alex O'Loughlin
The star of Hawaii 5-0 and Man-Thing (yes, there was a TV movie based on the Marvel comic) told the press he did a full costume screen test for Casino Royale but that he was probably too young for the role at the time.
Gerard Butler
The 300 star has fielded countless questions through the years about possibly playing James Bond, ultimately telling the press in 2009 that he wasn’t interested in the part: “I enjoy doing an array of films. If I was to play Bond, that would quickly stop. How likely would I be accepted doing those roles after having played Bond?" Butler later accepted roles in Geostorm and Gods of Egypt.
Jude Law
The Sherlock Holmes and future Captain Marvel actor was a much talked about contender back in the early 2000s before Daniel Craig took on the role in Casino Royale. "To be really honest, I don`t know if it`s for me," Law said of the role in 2003.
Dougray Scott
The Mission: Impossible 2 actor confirmed in a 2004 interview that years of Bond rumors were true. Scott said he was approached for the role back when it looked like Pierce Brosnan was going to bail on the franchise but then he decided to do one last Bond film with Die Another Day.
James Purefoy
The Following and Rome actor was an oft-reported Bond contender years ago, first for GoldenEye and later Casino Royale. Purefoy said in a 2006 interview that he blew his audition for Casino Royale, believing the producers probably saw him as “Brosnan Lite”.
