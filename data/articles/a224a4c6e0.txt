One of the best lines of dialogue in Marvel Studios history almost didn't happen.
On the set of WandaVision, the cast and crew were preparing to film one of the show's most pivotal scenes: a heartfelt conversation between Wanda Maximoff (Elizabeth Olsen) and Vision (Paul Bettany). After refusing to confront the grief over her dead husband and instead channeling those emotions into a powerful hex that brings him back to life but also transforms an entire town into a series of sitcom spoofs, Wanda finally faces her emotions head-on. The only problem was, they still hadn't nailed one crucial line.
Speaking at a Space Con panel in 2025, Olsen recalled the last-minute script changes.
“Paul was like, ‘I think we really need to figure out this line. I feel like there’s something we’re missing.’ And then he and the showrunner’s assistant really landed on that line,” Olsen said.
Bettany added: "[Showrunner Jac Schaeffer’s] assistant walked in with a coffee, and I can’t remember what I had got to. It was like, ‘What is grief, if not love, something.’ And she came in and said, ‘Persevering.’ And we were like, ‘That’s it.’"
The final version is almost identical: "But what is grief, if not love persevering?"
(If you're wondering, the assistant in question was Laura Monti, who got promoted to staff writer when Schaeffer returned to the Marvel Cinematic Universe for her follow-up series, Agatha All Along.)
Marvel fans (and the internet at large) instantly latched onto those words. Not only because it's rare for the quippy MCU to feel so heartfelt, but because it's simply a great line of dialogue delivered to perfection by a phenomenal actor like Bettany. (Bettany was quick to share the credit with Olsen. "I get lots of love for that line," he said at the panel, "but when you watch it, you’ll see that the entire thing actually plays on Lizzie’s face.”)
Five years later, the MCU is having a full-circle moment. VisionQuest, which aired its finale on Nov. 10, serves as the conclusion to a story that began with WandaVision and continued through Agatha All Along. (The new series also feels like a natural stopping point for Marvel's entire uneven streaming series experiment that WandaVision kicked off, although we still have one more season of Daredevil: Born Again coming in early 2027.) So it's only natural that showrunner Terry Matalas would want to revisit that iconic line. What comes as a surprise is how he twists it into something that's both darker than the original and somehow also even more beautiful.
'Grief isn’t love, it’s fear'
In the final episode of VisionQuest, Ultron finally manages to escape from his virtual prison, taking the form of a murderous swarm of nanobots. In a last-ditch effort to stop Ultron's evil plan, which involves killing everyone in London, Vision traps them both in a simulated sitcom, which is both a clever WandaVision callback and hopefully the perfect setting to change Ultron's mind.
Initially, Ultron resists, taking control of the simulation and turning it into something horrific, but eventually Vision wears him down. In a last-ditch effort, Ultron throws Wanda's words back in Vision's face with a cruel twist.
“Grief isn’t love, it’s fear,” Ultron says.
Vision doesn't miss a beat.
"Yes, you're right," he says. "It's fear. Fear that without them you're no one. Inconsequential. An untethered thing that can all too easily float away and disappear. Alone."
Just like in WandaVision, Bettany's character perfectly captures the experience of a human struggling with loss — despite the fact that he's not a human at all. Not only is it a beautiful line, but it's a reminder of why Vision is such a great and criminally underutilized part of the MCU. His struggle to become human (or something close to it) makes him appreciate the emotions and experiences that others take for granted.
I don't have much more to say about those words, which I hope will live on beyond VisionQuest in the same way as that WandaVision quote. Instead, I'll leave you with the full conversation between Wanda and Vision from that scene, which makes it clear just how closely these two scenes are interlinked, as if Vision is somehow speaking directly to his lost love through space and time.
Wanda: "I'm so tired. It's, it's just like this wave washing over me, again and again. It knocks me down, and when I try to stand up, it just comes for me again. And I... It's just gonna drown me."
Vision: "No. No, it won't."
Wanda: "How do you know?"
Vision: "Well, because it can't all be sorrow, can it? I've always been alone, so I don't feel the lack. It's all I've ever known. I've never experienced loss because I have never had a loved one to lose. But what is grief, if not love persevering?"
VisionQuest and WandaVision are both streaming on Disney Plus.
