'European investors, together with Jordanian businesses', can create economic value. Emphasise Dubravka Šuica, European Commissioner for the Mediterranean, and Tareq Abu Ghazaleh, Jordan's Minister of Investment in an exclusive piece for Euronews. Jordan could be Europe’s gateway to the Middle East.
Sustainable investment requires more than capital. It requires trusted partners, predictable political and economic conditions and a long-term vision. European businesses looking to invest in the Middle East have a trusted partner in Jordan.
Jordan offers these foundations – and an opportunity for Europe to turn its longstanding partnership with the Kingdom into a new generation of regional investment that strengthens our shared prosperity and security.
New opportunities for Jordanian and European businesses
Jordan’s skilled workforce and trade relationships make it a natural base for businesses seeking regional and international markets. Its location can help European companies diversify supply chains and connect with emerging economic corridors. In the first half of 2026, Jordan’s industrial exports reached approximately €5.5 billion (USD 6.2 billion), up 8.9% year on year. Its industrial products reached 150 global markets in 2025, supported by an extensive network of free trade agreements.
European companies are already part of this story. An EU mapping study provided a snapshot of the contribution of 80 European companies operating in Jordan: approximately €2.5 billion in annual turnover, close to €230 million in corporate taxes and 22,000 direct and indirect jobs created. This presence provides a foundation for deeper economic ties, creating jobs and opening new opportunities for Jordanian and European businesses alike.
Expand productive capacity and create quality jobs
Building on these advantages requires commercially viable projects, reliable infrastructure, a predictable business environment, and access to finance. Jordan’s economic modernisation vision aims to attract investment that expands productive capacity and creates quality jobs.
The economy grew by 3% year on year in the second quarter of 2026. The dinar’s peg to the US dollar, maintained since 1995, provides a monetary anchor, while Fitch reaffirmed Jordan’s BB- sovereign rating with a Stable Outlook in May 2026. Foreign GCC-owned company registrations increased by 23% in the first half of 2026, compared with 7% growth in total registrations.
The EU–Jordan Strategic and Comprehensive Partnership, launched in January 2025, provides an ambitious framework to translate political trust into economic opportunity. Its €3 billion package for 2025–2027 combines €640 million in grants, €1 billion in concessional macro-financial assistance and around €1.4 billion in additional investments to be mobilised. These funds will act as wind on the sails of growing businesses, creating jobs and leading to real returns.
Jordan’s strengths meet European expertise
European cooperation can help make this happen. Through investment grants, guarantees and partnerships with development finance institutions, the EU can help reduce investment risks, improve project viability, and mobilise fresh financing. This also means more opportunities for European businesses and the Jordanian economy.
Our shared priorities include renewable energy and water infrastructure, digital technologies, logistics and high-value manufacturing – sectors where Jordan’s strengths meet European expertise, and where investment can reinforce regional connectivity. This is even more important as Europe is diversifying its supply chain, making businesses more resilient to shocks while strengthening Jordan’s role as an anchor of regional stability.
The Jordan-EU Investment Conference on 19 November at the Dead Sea is an opportunity to further turn this ambition into tangible projects. The European contribution to Jordan’s National Conveyance Project, a major step towards strengthening the Kingdom’s long-term water security, will be presented at the Conference, demonstrating the scale of what we can achieve together.
Europe’s trusted gateway to the wider Middle East
The Aqaba Digital Hub, supported by the EU, is another example of how our partnership delivers concrete benefits. The Hub is expanding data-centre capacity and digital infrastructure, alongside plans for an academy to develop ICT and cybersecurity skills. The Conference will provide opportunities to build on these projects and forge new investment partnerships.
By bringing European investors together with Jordanian businesses and project promoters, we can lay the groundwork for ambitious investments across more sectors, creating lasting economic value for both sides. Jordan can be more than a stable destination for investment: it can be Europe’s trusted gateway to the wider Middle East. Investing in this partnership is an investment in our shared prosperity and security.
