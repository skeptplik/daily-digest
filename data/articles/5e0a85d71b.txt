This gently moving and beautifully animated drama is adapted from an award-winning graphic novel by Californian illustrator AJ Dungo. His book combines a history of surfing with a memoir of love and grief; it is an account of his girlfriend Kristen Tuason, who died from bone cancer at just 24. There is something desperately sad in the film’s nostalgic mood: Dungo still in his 20s, already looking in the rearview at his youth like it’s a different universe.
AJ (voiced by Will Sharpe) is a gawky teenager skateboarder who meets fun, outgoing Kirsten (Stephanie Hsu); she’s a surfer and helps AJ to overcome his fear of the ocean. Pretty soon, they’re a couple, hanging out with friends, surfing with a mixtape of sunny bright pop tunes soundtracking their teenage years. Then Kirsten is diagnosed with cancer and undergoes surgery to partially amputate her leg. When the doctors tell her she’s in remission, life goes on. Kirsten trains to be a nurse, while her brother opens a surf shop; another friend starts a craft beer business; AJ is working hard at art school. Life is ahead of them.
Arguably, the film, directed by Phuong Mai Nguyen, is light on detail: characters’ facial expressions are illustrated in simple lines, the finer details of their personalities unsketched. By contrast, the ocean is rendered in spectacular depth and detail, in all its moods. There is a reverence for the sea and surfing here, and a gentle tapping into surfing’s spiritual side; a handful of gorgeous scenes in a palette of mushroom brown and grey tell snippets of its origins in Polynesia. At heart, In Waves is an idealistic, emotional film, handling its subject delicately, avoiding too much of the rawness of grief. I can imagine older kids and teenagers falling deeply in love with it.
