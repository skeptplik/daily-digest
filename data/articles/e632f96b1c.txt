Sarah Gibson Tuttle was never allowed to paint her nails while growing up, but that didn’t stop her from founding one of the top nail brands in the country. After beginning her career in finance, working for the likes of J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley, Tuttle noticed a white space for a Los Angeles nail salon delivering stylish (and affordable) manicures, so she opened Olive & June’s Beverly Hills flagship in 2013 to fill that gap.
In 2019, Olive & June introduced the Mani System, a DIY kit for salon-quality nails at home, at just the right time as the Covid-19 pandemic forced nail salons to close only months later. Olive & June salons stayed permanently closed as Tuttle focused on a growing product instead.
Today, Olive & June reigns as the top nail brand sold at Target, and also counts major retailers like Ulta Beauty and Walmart among its stockists. With help from innovations like its Poppy tool — a universal polish bottle handle offering more control — Olive & June has been at the forefront of the DIY nail movement. It also recently launched non-acrylic, at-home dip powder, since it is “almost as popular as gel manicures now,” Tuttle said. The brand intentionally serves consumers’ nail tastes via trendy polishes and press-ons, but it also aims to encourage its audience to step outside of their comfort zones, as Tuttle referenced a “90% catering and a 10% inspire balance formula” when developing new products, shades and press-on patterns.
“We’re not here to create nail polish colors for ourselves because we could do that, and that would be a very limited collection and assortment. We’re here to really cater,” Tuttle explained. “If you’re going to be the leader of the DIY nail movement and you’re going to be the most consumer-focused nail brand, you have to actually live those words every day, and we really do.”
