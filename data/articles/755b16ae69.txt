Abstract
Gene transcription within the viral core is a distinctive feature of poxvirus replication. Following entry into the cytoplasm, the poxvirus core dissociates from the lateral bodies and undergoes expansion, functioning as a compartment for early transcription1. However, how molecules are exchanged between the viral core and the host cytoplasm remains poorly understood. Here, using cryo-electron tomography and sub-tomogram averaging, we determine the structures of the portal complex and its central pore on the vaccinia virus core at 6.7 Å and 4.4 Å resolution, respectively. Guided by structural predictions and experimental density, we identify three viral proteins, E8, E6 and L3, as the principal components of the pore and construct a structural model of their organization. The pore contains a narrow, positively charged constriction formed by conserved lysine residues in L3, substitutions of which impair viral gene expression and propagation. The dimensions and electrostatic properties of the channel are consistent with a potential role in molecular exchange across the core wall. Together, our findings establish a structural framework for understanding the organization and potential function of the poxvirus portal complex.
