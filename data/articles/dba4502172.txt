Pope Leo XIV traveled from Peru's high Andes to the Amazon rainforest on Sunday.
During the final leg of his South American tour, the Roman pontiff warned that environmental destruction threatens both the region's fragile ecosystem and the communities that depend on it.
After visiting Cusco, the ancient Incan capital at an altitude of more than 3,400 meters (11,000 feet), the 71-year-old religious leader arrived in Pucallpa, a city on the Ucayali River in one of Peru's major deforestation hotspots.
"The cry of the earth and the cry of the poor are inseparable," he said, calling on Catholics to resist the exploitation of the Amazon "with perseverance."
Meeting missionaries and representatives of Indigenous communities, the pope also condemned the killings of Indigenous leaders defending their ancestral lands.
"The blood of these brothers cries out to God and demands justice," he said.
"The Church is with you," Leo told representatives of Indigenous communities and other peoples of the Amazon region.
Pope warns of threats to Amazon and its people
The pope warned that indiscriminate deforestation and water pollution were due to irresponsible exploitation of the Amazon's natural resources.
Peru lost 3.2 million hectares of forest between 2001 and 2024, according to government figures, an area roughly the size of Belgium.
The head of the Catholic Church, which has 1.4 billion followers worldwide, also voiced concern about the future of communities living in the Amazon.
"Protecting the Amazon rainforest cannot be separated from concern for the people who live there: Indeed, integral ecology, social justice and the spiritual life go hand in hand," he said.
Leo also pointed to large-scale drug trafficking as one of the issues undermining local communities' livelihoods.
"For us, the pope's arrival is a source of great joy, but it is also a great opportunity to make our demands, our grievances, and our problems heard," said Cecilio Soria, an Indigenous human rights activist.
Pope acknowledges suffering, contributions of Afro-Peruvians
Leo, originally from Chicago, served as the bishop of Peru's Chiclayo from 2015 until 2023, when he was called to the Vatican. He holds both US and Peruvian citizenship.
On Saturday, Leo held an open-air Mass in town of Santa Cruz de Succhabamba.
He spoke with pleasure of returning to the "steep hills, ravines and fertile valleys, in a land with Andean roots, where the Inca Empire flourished and which is the cradle of a profound Christian faith."
Leo also visited the city of Zana, some 45 kilometers (28 miles) southeast, which is a major center of Afro-Peruvian society.
He said Zana "preserves the memory of generations of Africans who were torn from their homes and subjected to slavery."
"Many endured great suffering and humiliation. Dear brothers and sisters of African descent, today I want to express my affection and closeness to you," he said.
"Thank you for all that you contribute to the life of this church."
Leo apologized for the Church's role in transatlantic slavery in May.
Pope laments instability, violence in Peru
For both Pope Leo and the city's faithful, his stop in Chiclayo marked an emotional homecoming.
Speaking from his former cathedral, he addressed growing unrest and political instability a country where the homicide rate has doubled over the past five years and far-right President Keiko Fujimori was elected after promising to tackle crime.
"We feel threatened by the uncertainty of widespread violence and by the unspeakable suffering that continues to afflict so many of our brothers and sisters," Leo said at the Cathedral of St. Mary in Chiclayo on Saturday.
"Yet, despite this darkness, which so often dims our hope and weakens our trust, God's gift continues to shine before our eyes."
Leo was scheduled to travel to the Peruvian capital, Lima, before heading back to Rome on Monday.
