The pro-longevity movement is only getting louder.
So declared Jill Villa, founder of GoldHaus Wellness, at The Catalysts in New York on Tuesday, where she delivered a crash course in peptides and predicted the rise of precision aesthetic technology in the coming years.
Villa began by highlighting the shift in beauty conversations, which have evolved from “How can we look younger?” to “How can we age more gracefully?”
At GoldHaus, this is explored through the examination of metabolic markers, biometrics and body composition, all of which form the basis of personalized plans that often include a cocktail of treatments.
“It’s not just about one treatment of one thing,” Villa said. “It’s about [taking] a holistic approach. How can we use radio frequency in combination with other things like peptides, NAD and IV infusions?”
GLP-1s, otherwise referred to as “glucagon-like peptide-1s,” have motivated this “layered” approach due to their effects on hair, skin and muscle mass, prompting users to address these issues through additional treatments.
Indeed, there are other peptides that are powering the pro-longevity boom in different ways. “Peptides are chains of amino acids that send signals to cells in our bodies,” Villa explained. “They can be topical, oral or injectable. They’re not a guarantee of results, but they are very exciting for the beauty and wellness industry.”
Villa works primarily with GHK-Cu, BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c and epitalon. According to her, GHK-Cu can support wound healing as well as collagen and elastin synthesis. BPC-157 and TB-500, on the other hand, are often referred to as “the wolverine stack” and can help with recovery. MOTS-c is used for mitochondrial repair and oxidative stress prevention.
“Epitalon is an exciting one,” Villa said. “If you remember from biology class, your chromosomes have these little telomeres on the end. They’re like little shoelaces, and as we get older, they get shorter and shorter as your chromosomes replicate, and when they get shorter, that’s how we get gray hair and wrinkles, and we need glasses. [Epitalon] prevents telomeres from shortening.”
As the convergence of beauty, health and science continues in the age of AI, Villa said, she believes precision aesthetic technology, advanced skin assessments and more will emerge.
“We’re definitely entering a new chapter with beauty, and it’s no longer a trend at this point,” she continued. “It’s a movement. Everyone is very curious and aware, and it’s exciting that we can use science to improve our health and our resilience and confidence. This is where I believe that the possibilities can emerge in the future.”
