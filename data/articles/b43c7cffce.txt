On October 18, there was a drone strike on a crowded transport hub near a market in Al-Dibab, a city in the West Kordofan State of Sudan. After ten minutes, a second drone struck the same place, and at least 139 people died in the attack. Eight days later, there was another drone strike in a market in Nyala, the capital city of South Darfur, killing over 100 people, according to the rights group, Emergency Lawyers.
These attacks point to the emergence of a concerning trend in the ongoing civil war in Sudan. What began as a fight for political supremacy between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in April 2023 is gradually evolving into a warfare campaign that heavily relies on unmanned aerial vehicles (UAV).
