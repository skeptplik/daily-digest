Workers at Grand Theft Auto maker Rockstar Games have voted against a union recognition deal.
1,544 staff members took part in the vote across the company's locations in Edinburgh, Leeds, Lincoln, Dundee and London, representing a 93.2% turnout. Despite this huge engagement, 75.3% voted against Independent Workers of Great Britain (IWGB) union recognition, while 24.7% voted in favour.
Had this vote gone the other way, IWGB Rockstar Game Workers Union would have been the biggest recognised union in the UK games industry, and Rockstar would have been obligated to engage with collective bargaining on pay and working hours.
"We are extraordinarily proud of our team, the culture we've built together and the launch of Grand Theft Auto 6," the company's head of publishing, Jennifer Kolbe, said in a statement.
"This result reflects our team's strong support for continuing the direct relationships, collaboration and open dialogue that have helped make Rockstar Games so innovative and strong. As we look ahead, we're excited to build on all we've accomplished together," said a company rep.
IWGB says that Rockstar staff will keep organising regardless of whether the union is formally recognised.
"For anyone at Rockstar who believes in a fairer workplace, a fairer sector and a fairer society – this isn’t a sign to retreat, it’s a sign to double down on what we stand for," the organisation said in its own statement. "Hundreds of our coworkers voted in favour of our union at Rockstar, yet sadly we recognise we have more work to do to build understanding and win the trust of more of our colleagues. We’re ready to put in the effort to make that happen.
"Whilst we haven’t emerged with a recognition deal, this ballot has put our union on the radar of every worker, and opened the door for new kinds of conversation to start happening. People entirely unfamiliar with unions were for the first time debating employment conditions, collective bargaining rights, and our contracts. We heard from many more colleagues about what mattered to them, and we hope people will continue to engage so that we can see where these new discussions can lead us.
"A recognition agreement would have marked a path towards greater workplace democracy. But it’s one route of many. At Rockstar we have already won, and will continue to win, tangible changes that improve our everyday lives. We’ll keep fighting for a say. For pay transparency, contractual overtime pay, and protections against AI and layoffs.
"The power to shape our working lives has always been, and will always be, in our hands."
Under UK law, the union may not re-apply for recognition for three years, although staff remain free to join the RGWU.
The question of forming a union at Rockstar Games followed allegations that 34 members of staff were let go from the Grand Theft Auto maker in a move that IWGB called union-busting. Rockstar parent company Take-Two has denied this claim, saying that staff were fired for "distributing and discussing confidential information".
Rockstar and IWGB are currently embroiled in an employment tribunal regarding these job cuts; closing arguments were issued on November 19. Judge Lucy Wiseman, who oversaw proceedings, said that there is no timeframe yet for the judgement, but said she would try her best to "get that judgement out as quickly as possible".
Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19th and made a record $3.79 billion in revenue in its first 24 hours on sale.
