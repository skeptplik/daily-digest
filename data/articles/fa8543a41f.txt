When Irish AFLW import Siofra O'Connell arrived in Australia, she wasn't entirely sure what to expect.
She had watched AFL games on TV, time zone permitting, and braced herself for the transition from limited-contact Gaelic football to the heavy contact of Australian Rules.
"I was like, 'This is chaos,'" O'Connell told ABC Sport, who signed for Carlton in December 2024. "It's been a learning curve."
But then all the quirks and traditions came along with the more than 130-year-old sport.
"I was like, 'Why are they selling pies at a game?' O'Connell laughed. "That was very eye-opening … The chicken and chips is a big one.
"And even the fact that there's alcohol on the ground. You're only allowed it outside the stadium (in Ireland). You can't bring it to your seat.
"So when I went to my first AFL game, and people were a bit tipsy beside me, I didn't know what was going on."
Just how much Melbourne is "an AFL city" also took O'Connell by surprise.
"You start getting used to that as well, in a way, because it's on the TV every night of the week almost, and from March to November," she said.
And playing at a big Victorian club like Carlton inevitably puts you in the headlines.
"I work one day a week in the club shop … and that opened my eyes to the supporters and the fan base, and how big the club is," she said.
"You can appreciate it a lot more when it's post-game, and you're engaging with the fans … I'm so not used to all the numbers and spectators. So, it's just amazing to hear a crowd."
There was a crowd of over 5,000 for the Blues' qualifying final against North Melbourne, and then about 4,000 for the semifinal against Hawthorn.
"You see the number on the board, and I never would have dreamt that there'd be 5,000 at a game before. (In Ireland), We might go to a game, and we have great support at home, but it's just not as big.
"And the platform isn't as vocal as it is (in Australia)."
That crowd made itself known for the Blues' win over the Hawks in inaugural player and fan-favourite Darcy Vescio's 100th game.
The two-time All Australian sealed the victory with their second goal of the night, prompting the stands to fill with signs celebrating the beloved player.
In the lead-up to the game, Carlton organised speeches honouring Vescio and had special shirts made to mark the occasion.
"Being able to celebrate Darcy today, and seeing that players are getting to 100 games, I just didn't realise how big an achievement it was coming in last season," O'Connell said.
"Because we don't celebrate these kinds of things at home. To see that it's so special, and it's for that person for the week … You're just so excited to go out and play with them, and it lifts you. It lifts your spirits."
