The number of offenders in cocaine-related cases handled by Tokyo’s Metropolitan Police Department is on pace to hit a record total this year.
In recent years, authorities have become increasingly concerned about the spread of cocaine among teenagers and people in their 20s. The MPD is boosting its measures against smuggling from overseas.
The number of cocaine-linked offenders detected by the MPD last year reached a record high of 415, about 10 times the level seen a decade earlier. The total since the beginning of this year topped 400 as of the end of September, making it likely that the annual sum will surpass last year’s figure.
Among illegal drug crimes, stimulant-related cases tend to involve people in their 40s and 50s. In cocaine cases, however, those age 29 or younger account for more than 70% of all offenders, raising concerns about the drug’s rising popularity among younger generations.
The growing prevalence is believed to reflect reduced resistance to cocaine use, as the drug is cheaper than stimulant drugs and can be used without special paraphernalia.
“It is spreading via social media and acquaintances, and the false perception that cocaine is safe and fashionable is taking hold,” a senior investigator at the MPD said, expressing serious concern.
Since virtually all cocaine is smuggled into Japan from overseas, the MPD has strengthened controls at gateways such as Tokyo’s Haneda Airport.
In August, the MPD arrested an Argentine woman for allegedly bringing a total of 1 kilogram of cocaine, with a street value of some ¥24.24 million, from Britain by swallowing 101 small packets of the drug.
In October, a Finnish woman was arrested for allegedly smuggling 3.2 kilograms of cocaine, worth about ¥76.8 million on the street, from France in a suitcase.
The MPD also arrested five Malaysian nationals who allegedly attempted to enter Japan with 5 kilograms of cocaine, valued at about ¥120 million, concealed in their shoes.
According to the U.N. Office on Drugs and Crime, global cocaine trafficking has been increasing.
The MPD is strengthening cooperation with Tokyo Customs, the Japan Coast Guard and the Kanto-Shinetsu Regional Bureau of Health and Welfare. “We are deeply concerned about the spread of cocaine,” Yoshio Kawachi, chief of the MPD’s drug and firearms control division, said. “We want to strengthen border controls.”
