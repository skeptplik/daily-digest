Millions of visitors flock to Tokyo every year to explore the organized chaos that is one of the world’s most populous cities. Whether it’s the tantalizing food scene, trend-bending fashion, or rich history, the Japanese capital entices every type of traveler. Quite frankly, you could spend months here and barely skim the surface. And choosing your hotel can be just as overwhelming as deciding what to cram into a short visit.
Let’s take it one step at a time and start with where to stay. To help you navigate the thousands of options, we’ve narrowed the list down to some of our favorites; they vary in neighborhood, from frenetic hubs to quieter residential enclaves, so the discerning traveler has a lot to pick from. Some are brand new, others are tried-and-true institutions. Many are from familiar international brands, but a few are the dreamy results of home-grown talent.
Vogue’s Favorite Hotels in Tokyo:
- For a modern-day ryokan: Hoshinoya Tokyo
- For a hip hangout: Trunk(Hotel) Cat Street
- For impeccable views: Bulgari Hotel Tokyo
- For minimalist design: Aman Tokyo
- For an urban sanctuary: Trunk(Hotel) Yoyogi Park
- For a spa getaway: Janu Tokyo
- For a private onsen: Fufu Tokyo Ginza
One thing to note for winter travelers: Keep in mind that New Year is Japan’s most important holiday. While temples and shrines remain open (and are at their busiest), most businesses and cultural sites are closed for several days—even in a city like Tokyo. If you’ll be in town at the start of the year, it’ll be more important than ever to pick the right hotel to keep you entertained.
With that in mind, here’s our guide to the best hotels in Tokyo.
Following a near-10-month closure for a transformative renovation under the guard of André Fu, the Four Seasons Hotel Tokyo at Marunouchi reemerged this past April with a new, contemporary look. Pops of color can be found in the airy lobby, which is constantly stocked with homemade cookies and fresh fruit if you’re feeling peckish. Accommodations feature floor-to-ceiling windows, teal blue and rust red accents, engraved wood, and spacious, spa-like bathrooms with deep soaking tubs. The spa itself has only two treatment rooms—but even so, you’ll still find a steam room and ofuro in the changing rooms. And with it being just steps away from Tokyo Station, many offer views of the railway tracks. (Train enthusiasts, take note: Splurge on the Corner Ginza Room for unobstructed vistas of the passing Shinkansen.)
With just 57 keys—making it the second most intimate property within the Four Seasons portfolio after Naviva in Punta Mita, Mexico—the brand’s exemplary service is brought to the next level here. From Sézanne, the hotel’s lauded French restaurant now under the helm of chef Stephen Lancaster, right down to the reception staff who greet guests by name, you’ll be well looked-after.
- Amenities: Complimentary Tokyo Station assistance, Dyson hairdryers, spa, fitness center, restaurants, bar
- Editor’s Tip: Whether you’re arriving or departing by way of Tokyo Station, the Four Seasons team can help guide the way. Whether you’re looking for a specific souvenir or some ekiben (railway bento meals), they’ve got an encyclopedic knowledge of what’s available in the massive station.
- Address: 1 Chome-11-1 Marunouchi, Chiyoda City, Tokyo 100-6277, Japan
In theory, a beautifully textured facade made from salvaged tile fragments should catch the attention of wide-eyed wanderers in a neighborhood that is otherwise modern; after all, we’re talking about Ginza. Here, where the goal is typically to shop until you drop, the last thing you’d expect to come across is a ryokan-inspired hotel hidden away on one of the area’s busiest streets. But that’s exactly what gives Fufu Tokyo Ginza that “if you know, you know” badge of honor.
The hospitality brand is known for its intimate onsen retreats in some of the country’s most popular countryside destinations; this is its first urban outpost. There are just 34 rooms, all of which feature furnished balconies dotted with vegetation and hot spring–fed tubs filled with water ushered in from Atami, the birthplace of Fufu Japan. (Either amenity is hard to come by in this city, but both is simply unheard of.) It only gets better when you head to the lush rooftop terrace, where semi-private banquettes hover above a foot bath. Like with any ryokan, half-board options are available and are worth booking. Dinner features either a sushi omakase or a seasonal kaiseki; and as for the breakfast, it’s easily one of the best in Tokyo.
- Amenities: Onsen, rooftop lounge, complimentary minibar, restaurants
- Editor’s Tip: Complimentary drinks and snacks are offered from 4 to 7 p.m. at the rooftop lounge. Soak away your tired hooves while enjoying a panoramic view of the city with a beverage in hand.
- Address: 1 Chome-7-10 Ginza, Chuo City, Tokyo 104-0061, Japan
For its inaugural debut in Japan, 1 Hotel set up shop in Tokyo’s Akasaka district, a central hub for business, entertainment, and dining just a stone’s throw away from other popular enclaves like Azabudai Hills and Roppongi. The brand new property opened this past March, with a whopping 211 accommodations that look out to either Tokyo Tower, the Imperial Palace gardens, or the city skyline. In keeping with the brand’s sustainable sensibilities, single-use plastic is non-existent, filtered water taps are installed in every room, and glassware is made from recycled wine bottles. There’s even a farmstand in the lobby featuring fresh fruit that’s deemed “too imperfect” by farmers to sell at local markets—though it looks fairly flawless by this plebeian’s standards—along with more than 1,500 live plants throughout the entire hotel, including the moss that lines the hallways.
Gin fans will delight in the gorgeous lobby bar, where you can choose from nearly 100 locally crafted varieties under a ceiling that’s inspired by karesansui (Zen gardens). At NiNi restaurant, vegetables are the star of the breakfast spread; the rest of the day calls for French Riviera cuisine with a Japanese twist, like a kabocha pumpkin soup de pistou. And if you have time for a dip, you’ll find an indoor pool with a beautiful outdoor terrace just next to the spa.
- Amenities: Indoor pool, Dyson hairdryers, spa, fitness center, restaurant, café, bar
- Editor’s Tip: A complimentary car service is available to book within a three-mile radius of the hotel. However, reservations are only available day-of and morning slots book up quickly—call as early as midnight if you’re keen on securing a ride.
- Address: 2 Chome-17-22 Akasaka, Minato City, Tokyo 107-0052, Japan
Every few years there’s a hotel that garners so much attention it becomes an icon before it even opens. In 2024, that title belonged to Janu Tokyo. The flagship of Aman’s long-awaited sister brand, Janu Tokyo trades in zen and serenity for a more energetic approach to hospitality, perhaps best exhibited by the property’s whopping eight food and beverage venues. The hotel also offers a robust wellness center that spans four floors, housing one of the city’s largest gyms, a 25-meter pool, and an extensive spa. Once you’re ready to retire, spacious rooms with largely monochromatic interiors welcome you with soaking tubs and balconies in most instances.
- Amenities: Spa, indoor pool, fitness center, restaurants, bar
- Editor’s tip: Two Spa Houses with their own double treatment rooms and hydrotherapy facilities serve as the pinnacle of a private wellness experience.
- Address: 1 Chome-2-2 Azabudai, Minato City, Tokyo 106-0041, Japan
Bookended by action with the heart of Shibuya on one side and Harajuku at the other, there’s a lot happening around Trunk(Hotel) Cat Street. In addition to its stellar location, this lifestyle boutique property has managed to capture the zeitgeist, turning it into a favored haunt for the neighborhood’s effortlessly cool inhabitants. The lobby lounge, with a café by day and bar by night, doubles as a co-working space and is quite the scene at nearly all hours. Upstairs are just 15 accommodations ranging from cozy rooms to residential-style suites, all accented with custom Japan-made adornments utilizing upcycled materials.
The hotel offers two on-site restaurants—one that serves Japanese-inflected international fare, the other a yakitori (skewered meats and vegetables) spot—and its own version of a convenience store with locally sourced bites and a curated selection of sustainable products.
- Amenities: Bar/café, restaurants
- Editor’s tip: The Living Suite has the most character, complete with a projector, record player, L-shaped bar, and a loft-style lounging space above the glass-encased bedroom.
- Address: 5 Chome-31 Jingumae, Shibuya City, Tokyo 150-0001, Japan
Remote destinations have been a part of Aman’s ethos since its inception—the opening of the Tokyo property in 2014 marked the brand’s first true city project. Resident architect Kerry Hill’s minimalist approach to the group’s luxury-defining properties arguably plays the most natural role in Japan, a country where less is more. The vaulted atrium in the lobby, which is a hot spot for afternoon tea, has become an icon of sorts within Aman’s portfolio, as has the stone-clad, 30-meter pool overlooking the city. Its bright and airy rooms are some of the largest in the city (the smallest coming in at 71 square meters), and are characterized by light wood and low-profile furnishings. Slide the shoji screen back to reveal a stone bathroom, complete with a window-side ofuro tub with uninterrupted views.
- Amenities: Complimentary breakfast, indoor pool, spa, fitness center, restaurants, bar
- Editor’s tip: Pop by the pool at sunset for dramatic photo opportunities.
- Address: The Otemachi Tower, 1 Chome-5-6 Otemachi, Chiyoda City, Tokyo 100-0004, Japan
Hotel Groove Shinjuku offers a more approachable price point than its sister property Bellustar Tokyo while still benefiting from the same shiny new tower and prime location. Entry-level rooms are cozy, but there are plenty of larger options (at relatively reasonable rates) like the Japanese Suite, which features a tatami-mat floor spacious enough for multiple futons, making it highly popular with families. Colorful carpets accent the otherwise neutral-hued accommodations while the artist-designed suites up the ante on personality. And if you’re here for a lengthier stay, the on-site coin-operated laundry is an unexpectedly wonderful touch.
- Amenities: Restaurant, bar, laundry room
- Editor’s tip: Direct limousine buses between Tokyu Kabukicho Tower and Narita or Haneda make getting to and from the airport extremely convenient.
- Address: 1 Chome-29-1 Kabukicho, Shinjuku City, Tokyo 160-0021, Japan
Top-floor occupancy of a high-rise is commonplace for luxury hotels in Tokyo; it’s no different at the Four Seasons’ second outpost in the city. When you exit the elevators on the 39th floor, you’re greeted by a tranquil water feature that draws inspiration from the Imperial Palace’s moat. In fact, the royal residence (and the sprawling metropolis’ skyline) can be seen from various points throughout the hotel, including the lobby lounge, where afternoon tea is served, and many of its 190 rooms. Speaking of accommodations, they’re spacious here, as to be expected from a Four Seasons, with floor-to-ceiling windows offering plenty of natural lighting and panoramic views. And if you’re looking to set your eyes on Tokyo Skytree, head down to the heated indoor pool for a couple of laps, then cool off in one of the mist chairs.
When hunger strikes there are a few on-site dining options to choose from, including the all-day Italian restaurant Pigneto (the hand-tossed Neapolitan pizzas are the highlight); but the crown jewels are Est and Virtù, both of which are worth visiting even if you’re not a staying guest. At the former you’ll find refined take on contemporary French cuisine with a commitment to sourcing nearly all ingredients from around Japan and a seasonal menu. Meanwhile, the French-Japanese love affair continues at the latter in the form of cocktails, where spirits from both destinations are the star. The Smoked Ume Fashioned is a prefect representation of the concept, which uses a homemade brandy umeshu with plums that have been aged for at least six months, as is the Yuzu Nagi, where yuzu gin meets cognac in a bright, citrus-forward tipple. (Make sure to ask for the seasonal cocktail menu, where you might just find a kakigori-inspired drink, and order the lobster okonomiyaki, an elevated take on the Japanese classic.)
- Amenities: Indoor pool, spa, fitness center, restaurants, bar
- Editor’s tip: Stay in one of the imperial garden rooms for vistas of the verdant grounds and Mt. Fuji on a clear day.
- Address: 1 Chome-2-1, Otemachi, Chiyoda City, Tokyo 100-0004, Japan
Back in 1962 when Japan was in the midst of an economic boom, the Okura was one of Tokyo’s preeminent hotels, playing host to a number of dignitaries during its tenure. So when it announced its closure in 2015 with a full demolition in tow—on par with the Japanese affinity for rebuilding rather than renovating, as exhibited with the country’s holiest shrine, Ise Jingu—the decision caused quite the stir amongst locals. Nevertheless, the Okura was reborn in 2019 with two glitzy new towers. Some of its history was preserved, like the original mid-century modern chairs that have since been reupholstered, the signature pentagon-faceted lanterns, and kumiko lattice woodwork seen throughout the property.
The main building, dubbed Prestige, is largely geared toward business travelers. The other (the Heritage Wing) appeals more to leisure travelers. All accommodations are suites with sumptuous, oversized bathrooms, complete with a jet tub and private sauna. Some even boast balconies—a rarity in the city. While you’re here make sure to check out Yamazato, a high-end Japanese restaurant with dedicated sushi and tempura counters, along with a visit to the spa for what is arguably one of the better massages in the city.
- Amenities: Complimentary minibar, Okura Museum of Art tickets, complimentary pressing service, spa, indoor pool, fitness center, restaurants, bar
- Editor’s tip: For those interested in the floral artistry of ikebana, the main lobby’s centerpiece is rearranged every Tuesday morning.
- Address: 2 Chome-10-4 Toranomon, Minato City, Tokyo 105-0001, Japan
In a city ruled by skyscrapers, finding some greenery in the middle of Tokyo is like a glass of ice water on a hot summer day. That’s what you get at Takanawa Hanakohro. Part of the larger Prince Hotels compound near Shinagawa Station, the ambiance is set by a classic garden complete with a koi pond, over 200 sakura trees, and the hotel’s very own temple. And while Takanawa Hanakohro occupies one of the complex’s three towers, what makes it particularly special is its 16 ryokan-style suites. Characterized by tatami floors, shoji screens, and large bathrooms with a tub, they’re about as traditional as you get, with courteous service by kimono-clad staff to match. A stay here comes with unfettered access to all of the four executive lounges, including Oh-Sai, which is reserved only for Hanakohro guests, along with a dedicated spa. And while breakfast is usually served at Oh-Sai, you can also request an in-room delivery; the best option, if you’re staying in one of the garden view rooms.
- Amenities: Complimentary breakfast, lounges with snacks and drinks, seasonal outdoor pool, spa, bathhouse, fitness center, restaurants
- Editor’s tip: While the hotel is in walking distance to Shinagawa Station, there’s also a complimentary shuttle bus for your convenience.
- Address: 3 Chome-13-1 Takanawa, Minato City, Tokyo 108-8612, Japan
The business district of Otemachi is perhaps one of the most unsuspecting locations for a ryokan, but that’s what makes a tranquil retreat like Hoshinoya Tokyo even more special. Upon entering, the cacophony of the bustling metropolis instantly dissipates and is replaced by a relaxing aura. You’re greeted by a diligent attendant who stores your shoes in one of the bamboo and chestnut cubbies lining the walls, welcoming you to walk barefoot on the tatami mat floors.
With the exception of the restaurants (which includes a recently added sushi bar), the hotel is strictly reserved for guests. And while there are 84 rooms in total, each floor only has six accommodations, creating a sense of intimacy as though every level is its own mini ryokan, along with a shared ochanoma lounge that’s stocked with complimentary snacks like onigiri and an array of seasonal teas. Enjoy spacious digs with low furnishings, deep soaking tubs, and traditional Japanese design elements like shoji screens, but while you’re here make sure to head up to the rooftop onsen and soak in the healing properties of the natural hot spring.
- Amenities: Onsen, lounge with snacks and refreshments, complimentary minibar (non-alcoholic beverages), spa, restaurant
- Editor’s tip: In addition to the snacks at the ochanoma lounges the hotel also offers complimentary soba in the evenings.
- Address: 1 Chome-9-1 Otemachi, Chiyoda City, Tokyo 100-0004, Japan
Hoshino Resorts has several brands under its umbrella—including the uber-luxe Hoshinoya—but at the opposite end of the spectrum is OMO, a more budget-conscious collection of hotels that feature compact but well-appointed rooms. At OMO5 Tokyo Gotanda the accommodations start at 200 square feet; ideal for solo travelers who want something more comfortable than a hostel without breaking the bank. Suites are just a little over 600 square feet. Most have sofas by the window so that you can enjoy the city views in comfort, and there is also a bunk-style room that’s great for families or a small group of friends.
But the real highlight here is being immersed in the lesser-explored Gotanda district, where you’ll find culinary gems like tonkatsu institution Tonki, famed reservation-only izakaya Shokudo Todaka, and stand-up udon bar Oniyanma (this particular store is arguably better than its other locations in the city).
- Amenities: Café and bar
- Editor’s tip: Cheers with a complimentary glass of non-alcoholic sparkling wine at the hotel’s nightly gathering and sample some sweet and savory treats from some of the neighborhood’s most popular establishments all in one place.
- Address: 8 Chome-4-13 Nishigotanda, Shinagawa City, Tokyo 141-0031, Japan
Ginza has no shortage of luxury hotels to complement its high-end stores. Just north of the city’s most glamorous shopping district is relative newcomer Bulgari Hotel Tokyo, which opened in 2023. Set directly across from Tokyo Station, the glitzy 98-key property blends its roots with its new environs through contemporary Italian design and time-honored Japanese craftsmanship. Bulgari fans will also appreciate overt nods to the brand in the form of framed sketches from the archives, the jeweler’s 1970s Mount Fuji brooch on display in the gallery, and the house’s signature eight-point star inlaid in the black granite floor of the reception area.
The mark of any stellar city hotel is when it’s enticing enough to keep you indoors despite an exciting metropolis waiting to be explored, and Bulgari Hotel Tokyo delivers. To start, the accommodations are incredibly spacious, starting from 51 square meters. And while there are plenty of luxe touches—throws crafted in Kyoto, hand-brushed gold ceilings, Bulgari-branded bathroom amenities—the automatic blinds are the winner, opening every time you step into the room to reveal panoramic vistas of the city and, in some cases, Mount Fuji on a clear day. Looking for even more dramatic views? Head up to the 45th-floor rooftop lounge, where you’ll be met with Venetian terrazzo flooring and a beautiful glass mosaic wall of birds and trees behind the sleek bar, flanked by lush terraces with plenty of seating looking out to a seemingly endless skyline.
