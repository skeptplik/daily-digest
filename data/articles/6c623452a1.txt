Bell Canada has confirmed that an "agentic" threat actor breached its systems on November 17, 2026, and stole enterprise customer data, including information from a system used to order and provision telecommunications circuits.
Bell is one of Canada's largest telecommunications providers, offering wireless, internet, television, and enterprise networking services.
BleepingComputer first learned of the incident after receiving a tip from a customer who shared a breach notification sent by Bell's Information Security Team.
According to the notification, an unauthorized party accessed a pre-production environment of Bell's Maximo system, which the company uses to order and provision telecommunications network connections.
Bell warned that information belonging to the recipient's organization may have been stolen, including business contact information such as names, job titles, corporate email addresses, phone numbers, and business addresses.
The potentially exposed data also includes information contained in ticket titles and some IP addresses associated with network elements or systems, along with limited contextual information.
However, Bell said the affected dataset did not contain credentials, highly sensitive personal information, PDFs, file attachments, or other supplementary information stored in Maximo.
When contacted by BleepingComputer, Bell confirmed the intrusion and said that an "agentic threat actor" had accessed multiple systems and stolen enterprise customer data.
"Bell is investigating a cybersecurity incident that occurred on November 17 in which an agentic threat actor accessed some of our systems, including a system used for ordering and provisioning circuits, and exfiltrated some enterprise customer data," Bell told BleepingComputer.
"Upon learning of the breach, we immediately contained the incident and secured our systems. There has been no impact to customer services."
The company's description of the attacker as "agentic" suggests that AI-powered tools were used during the breach.
Bell says it is sharing indicators of compromise (IOCs) and threat-hunting information with enterprise customers and the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security (CCCS).
However, Bell has not publicly released the indicators of compromise and has not responded to BleepingComputer's request to share them so other organizations can check their environments for related activity.
Bell told BleepingComputer that it has not yet identified the threat actor responsible for the attack.
"We currently do not know the attribution," Bell said.
Bell has not disclosed how many enterprise customers were impacted or the total amount of data stolen.
In its customer notification, Bell urged organizations to remain cautious of unsolicited requests and social engineering attempts by individuals impersonating the telecommunications provider.
