Supreme Court orders lamp lighting at Deepathoon, four days after festival
On Friday, the Supreme Court criticised the Tamil Nadu government and Madurai district officials for "wilful" defiance of the November 20 order and directed the lamp to be lit at Deepathoon on Saturday.
The Supreme Court suspended the Subramaniya Swamy temple's Board of Trustees and appointed a committee of two retired Madras High Court judges as receivers of the trust, who will oversee Saturday's ceremony.
The court also tweaked its earlier order and instead of a small team approved by the temple trust, has now permitted every devotee to light the lamp at Deepathoon on Saturday.
It has tasked the paramilitary CRPF with ensuring security at both the temple and the shrine ahead of the ceremony.
Modi posts Tamil devotional chant amid Deepathoon row
Meanwhile, Prime Minister Narendra Modi wrote a post on X on last night, invoking Lord Murugan, one of the gods associated with Karthigai Deepam.
Without making any mention of the row, the Hindu nationalist leader wrote the traditional Tamil devotional chant "Victory to Lord Murugan, who wields the spear!" thrice, in English, Tamil and Hindi.
Tamil Nadu: Lamp lighting at controversial Deepathoon site today amid security presence
Personnel of the Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) were deployed at the Thiruparankundram hillock near Tamil Nadu state's Madurai, a day after the Supreme Court ordered a Hindu ceremonial lamp lighting to take place at a controversial stone pillar on Saturday.
At the heart of the row, is the Deepathoon (stone pillar) situated atop Thiruparankundram, and about 50 metres from the Hazrat Sultan Sikandar Badusha Auliya Dargah, a shrine associated with Sufism — a mystical tradition within Islam.
What is Tamil Nadu's stone pillar controversy?
The Arulmigu Subramaniya Swamy temple, also located on Thiruparankundram, traditionally conducts the annual Tamil Hindu Karthigai Deepam (festival of lights) ceremony at the Deepa Mandapam site near the Uchi Pillaiyar temple, situated on the lower part of the hill.
But Hindu groups had sought permission to light the ceremonial lamp at Deepathoon.
On November 20, India's top court permitted the lighting at Deepathoon as a one-time arrangement, allowing a restricted team to conduct the ceremony there.
However, the lamp was lit at the usual Deepa Mandapam when the festival came on November 24 as the temple trustees decided to stick to what they assert is established practice.
The Tamil Nadu government, helmed by Chief Minister C Joseph Vijay, also maintains that the customary spot for the ritual is near the Uchi Pillaiyar temple and had filed a review petition in the Supreme Court challenging its November 20 order.
Tuesday's lamp lighting led to protests by Hindu groups and also workers of theHindu Nationalist Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), which accused the Vijay government of refusing to comply with the Supreme Court's directive.
