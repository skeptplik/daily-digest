Sat, 21 Nov, 1:05 pm, Final score, Kangaroos 83, Swans 51
Since the AFLW was made an 18-team competition, much speculation heading into the league's night of nights has been whether star Roo Jasmine Garner will earn the recognition she deserves.
Where in the past it has been players who have been around from the competition's inception who were the top vote-getters in the AFLW Best and Fairest Award — eight of the 10 winners to date were listed in the first two seasons — this year it is the top two draftees from the 2021 draft who are shaping up as the best chance at winning on Monday evening.
Garner is still a genuine chance at the medal, as is Sydney dynamo Chloe Molloy, but it is Charlie Rowbottom and Georgie Prespakis who are deemed the genuine frontrunners.
Ahead of that draft night in 2021, Rowbottom made waves by nominating the Queensland draft pool — obviously, before there was a truly national draft — and as a result, was taken with the first pick by Gold Coast. That left the other top pick contender, Prespakis, to fall to Geelong at number two.
Both have been vital cogs of their respective midfields across their seven seasons, and although some raw numbers do look very similar, the way they play their footy could not be more different.
What looks similar
As pure ball winners, both Prespakis and Rowbottom have been right at the top of the league throughout the home-and-away season.
They have each attended the most average centre stoppages for their respective sides, and therefore their average contested possession and clearance numbers also sit first and second across the league.
Each strong at the source, they are key starting points for their respective teams' attack. They have both played every game this season — for teams that reached finals for the first time since 2023, no less — and regularly starred along the way.
What is different
After that first phase of play, however, each player prioritises differently. Rowbottom tends to remain a tough, defensive option, bringing her physicality, while Prespakis is that sleek runner taking the ball forward.
The Gold Coast star is staunch in tight, choosing to release her running teammates like Niamh McLaughin, Ava Usher, and Lucy Single.
Her capacity to be the big body drawing attention that ultimately creates space for those on the outside was critical to the Suns' game plan this year, which averaged nearly four more goals per game in 2026 than it did in 2025.
So, for Rowbottom, the numbers that stand out in addition to those above are her average tackles (6.2) and marks (3.6), while she also pressed forward to kick a career-best five goals.
She is not a player who repeatedly gets involved in a transitional passage of play; rather, she is often the starting point and has developed the capacity to become the end point more reliably.
She finished with five or more tackles in seven of her games, and 10 or more clearances in four of those.
Prespakis is more of that player who remains involved in running handball chains to set up those inside her team's attacking 50. She was the number one score involvement player across the AFLW in the season, sits first for average loose ball gets (7.6) and sixth for average metres gained (428.2).
Her clearance game is more about her agility to evade danger and run the ball out, with her handball numbers more indicative of those back-and-forths as she and teammates dash up the field.
This resulted in six home and away games with eight or more score involvements, and seven recording 400 or more metres gained.
This style of play was emblematic of how the Cats played their footy this year. Fast, predictable to one another, and with every intention to take the game forward.
For an effective midfield to operate, each style of midfielder is necessary: the big-bodied contested beast, and the dashing metres-gained player. It is fair to say that — maybe except Garner herself — each of Prespakis and Rowbottom has been the best of their respective style of midfielder this year.
Should one of the pair claim the medal on Monday night, it would be a key signifier of this fresh era of the AFLW, leaning on those players who have enjoyed greater junior pathway opportunities, and greater support in their talent development at the elite level.
