Accra, Ghana – From the dusty, bustling markets of Accra to the busy streets of Hong Kong, the ubiquitous red-white-and-blue, checkered stripes of the “Ghana Must Go” bag have crossed borders and generations.
An everyday utility used to carry anything from clothes and household goods to food, the bag also carries painful memories of mass expulsion and displacement.
“Ghana Must Go is here, come and buy. It’s good, it’s good,” Agnes Ambomah called out in a rhythmic sales pitch from her roadside stall in the Ghanaian capital.
