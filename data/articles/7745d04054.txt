Politician, Paralympic gold medallist and Australian Para Ice Hockey player.
The unique description belongs to 57-year-old Liesl Tesch MP, who is balancing her role as the Gosford MP with the upcoming 2026 Para Ice Hockey Women's World Championships in Newcastle this month.
After seven Paralympic Games appearances across wheelchair basketball and sailing with a pair of gold medals, most would be more than content with their sporting careers, but Tesch wants more.
While sporting success has tended to follow Tesch wherever she has gone, getting used to it on the ice was not as immediate.
"The first day I felt like a turtle upside down," Tesch told ABC Sport.
"The next day, I was like, 'oh my God, how was I so bad,' [but] I have fallen in love with this sport.
"It just gives me such a rush, and it's [wild] and brutal and crazy."
A second chance
The 2026 Para Ice Hockey Women's World Championships will be the second of its kind, with the inaugural event held in Slovakia last year. Australia competed, finishing sixth, and losing all three of their matches in Dolný Kubín without scoring.
Lynda Holt, 54, is Ice Hockey Australia’s head of para ice hockey and also a Paralympian, and says this time will be different.
"We've all grown in the last year, we've all improved so much, and we have got some new players," Holt told ABC Sport.
"The team is great. I come from a shot put and discus background, a very individual sport on my own, and I'm just loving that we are this team that comes together."
Australia will be joined by Canada, the United States, Great Britain, Norway, and Team World, a combination of players from countries not represented.
The six teams will be split into two groups of three, with Australia in Group A alongside the United States and Great Britain.
The game is played on sleds, and like mainstream ice hockey, it is played six-a-side, with most rules carried over.
For Tesch, it is a much-needed opportunity for women to shine.
"It's a chance for women to get together and play against women because a lot of us, we are training and playing against the blokes," Tesch said.
Road to the Paralympics
Like several of her teammates, Lynda Holt knows the pressure of competing at a Paralympic Games, and being a part of landmark moments.
A shot put silver medallist at Sydney 2000, she recognises a chance now to be involved in another one, with the sport hoping for Paralympic inclusion as a full women's competition as soon as 2030.
She is not alone. Decorated swimmer Priya Cooper, who won nine gold medals across four Paralympic Games appearances, will also take to the ice, along with Susan Seipel, who won a silver medal as a Para canoeist at the Tokyo Games.
Tesch and fellow Paralympic silver medallist in wheelchair basketball, Lisa Edmonds, have both medalled on the court, and want to be part of a movement for women's para ice hockey players to have the same chance.
Para Ice Hockey has officially been a mixed gender event since the 2010 Paralympic Games, but Norway's Britt Mjaasund Øyen competed in 1994 as part of the team that won silver.
She was the first of only four women to compete at the level. Fellow Norwegian Lena Schrøder competed in 2018 and will captain Norway at this World Championships.
While the opportunity is there for women to compete alongside men, Holt is part of a wider community wanting a standalone women's tournament, something the event in Newcastle could help provide.
"Our whole goal, our whole movement we have got going on is [that] we have got to get there," Holt said.
"In order to qualify for the Paralympics, we needed to have two World Championships; this will be the second.
"We also need eight teams; we currently have five, and we have got three in development, so come next year, when they decide if we can be part of the Paralympics, we should be ready.
"This is history in the making for us."
The fight for time on ice
Ice hockey's growth in popularity around Australia has meant ice time has been harder to come by, regardless of what discipline you are attempting to play.
The Hunter Ice Skating Stadium, where the team have been training, is also home to several teams belonging to the hometown Newcastle Northstars, including its stand-up men's team, who are the Australian Ice Hockey League's most successful, having won its seventh title earlier this year.
Members of the public are also able to access the space, something Leisl Tesch knows all too well.
"Getting on here with the general public, I remember the first day I did it," she said.
"Everyone's standing up looking beautiful, and I am in this full body armour, but it is really good for speed and turn training with everyone else.
"It is also an inclusion conversation that people with disabilities can get on the ice too."
Holt and Edmonds are both based in Sydney, having to make the trip to Newcastle to train.
"There's not enough ice time for everyone, and it's such an expensive sport, six to seven hundred dollars an hour for the ice," Holt said.
"We're all fighting for it; we've all got goals, ours is the Paralympics."
The tournament starts on November 24, with Canada's clash against Team World. Australia will open its campaign the same day, against the United States.
