GTA 6 is a gorgeous-looking game, so naturally, you’ll feel the urge to pause regularly to take it all in. Thankfully, Rockstar knows this and has included a full-fledged photo mode at launch.
The developer is no stranger to such a concept, with GTA 5 including a whole movie director mode, and Red Dead Redemption 2 eventually gaining a photo mode alongside its PC launch. It’s a pleasant surprise, then, that it's here from the get-go with GTA 6.
It took 70 hours to complete my first playthrough of Jason and Lucia’s story, but I’d be lying if I said a couple of those hours weren’t spent messing around taking pictures when the mood struck me. Sometimes the light bounces perfectly off those waters off Ocean Beach, and you just can’t help but capture the moment.
The photo studio, found by pausing the game and hitting the square button on PS5/X button on Xbox, is impressively well-equipped, too. With aspect ratios, aperture, and depth of field all available to play with when it comes to the camera itself, a variety of different lighting from spotlights to floodlights to softboxes all available to insert into scenes, and a range of post-processing tools including a variety of filters, it’s a relatively impressive suite that I’ve enjoyed playing around with.
That being said, there are a few little things here and there that I’d love to see Rockstar introduce in future updates. Perhaps the primary one of those is the ability to reposition Jason and Lucia into new poses and change their facial expressions. Sometimes you just get that perfect background scenery, but unfortunately one or both of the couple just look bored by the thought of it, and I have to call the photo shoot off. Life’s hard sometimes.
I really am nitpicking, though. Much like the larger game around it, GTA 6’s photo mode is largely impressive, and I’m sure I’ll lose dozens more hours to capturing all of the weird and wonderful sights Vice City and its surrounding areas have to offer.
