This week on Dezeen, we revealed the transformation of London’s 1880s Smithfield General Market into the London Museum, completed by architecture studios Stanton Williams, Asif Khan and Julian Harrap Architects.
Ten years in the making, the renovated Victorian market building now contains two main gallery spaces – one an airy open space and the other a subterranean exhibition at Roman street level – displaying artefacts from London’s history.
Another major museum that opened this week is Kanal in Brussels, which at 40,000 square metres, claims to be “Europe’s largest museum development”.
Atelier Kanal renovated the 1930s Citroën Garage and added three volumes within its structure to form the modern art and architecture museum, which features exhibitions developed with Paris’s Centre Pompidou.
In design news, Swedish retailer IKEA announced a deep, reddish-purple hue as its Colour of the Year for 2027.
Named Wild Fig, IKEA said the colour “unexpectedly fits in”, whether used in furnishings, accessories, or painted across entire walls.
Following the announcement of this year’s Dezeen Awards winners, we rounded up key projects completed by the awards’ emerging designers of the year.
Notable projects by emerging architect of the year Kuma&Elsa include a house in Japan and a bathing pavilion designed for the Osaka World Expo 2025.
Projects by Dezeen Awards emerging designer of the year Simon Skinner include sculptural lighting made from secondhand glassware and afro combs.
Also this week, the Royal Incorporation of Architects in Scotland (RIAS) announced the Edinburgh Futures Institute as the winner of the Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland for 2026.
UK studio Bennetts Associates renovated the Category A-listed Victorian building, formerly an infirmary, and integrated new teaching and work spaces for the University of Edinburgh.
Popular projects this week include an art gallery in Doha adorned with kaleidoscopic patterns, a house in Maryland punctured with lightwells, and a renovated home in Yorkshire designed to feel like a wine bar.
