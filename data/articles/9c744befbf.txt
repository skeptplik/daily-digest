Uganda has announced plans to withdraw its troops from a town in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo within two weeks, as diplomatic tensions escalate between the two neighbours.
The troops will hand over security in Lubero, in North Kivu province, to Congolese forces after nearly two years of military operations, the Ugandan army said in a statement on Friday.
The announcement comes days after DR Congo summoned Uganda's top diplomat in Kinshasa over controversial social media posts by Uganda's military chief, Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba, who is the son of President Yoweri Museveni.
The Ugandan military did not give a reason for the withdrawal or say whether it was linked to the diplomatic row.
The Uganda People's Defence Forces (UPDF) were deployed to Lubero to pursue fighters from the Allied Democratic Forces (ADF) Islamist group fleeing joint military operations elsewhere in eastern DR Congo.
The ADF, which originated in Uganda in the 1990s, is affiliated to the Islamic State and has been blamed for numerous deadly attacks on civilians in both countries.
Uganda first deployed troops to eastern DR Congo under the current joint military campaign in November 2021, following a series of bomb attacks in the Ugandan capital, Kampala.
Ugandan army spokesperson Col Chris Magezi said that the joint military campaign, known as Operation Shujaa, would continue elsewhere in DR Congo.
The planned withdrawal applies only to Lubero town.
Five years into the joint offensive, the ADF remains DR Congo's deadliest armed group, despite a significant decline in its activities in Uganda, according to the conflict monitoring organisation Armed Conflict Location & Event Data (Acled).
John Ngono, Acled's DR Congo analyst, said the group was killing an average of four civilians a day and had expanded into previously unaffected parts of eastern DR Congo.
He said the findings suggested that "Operation Shujaa may have improved security in Uganda while shifting the geographical impact of violence within the DRC".
Acled says the group has not carried out an attack in Uganda since June 2025, but its growing interest in mineral resources in DR Congo could help finance its operations and sustain its ability to threaten both countries.
The planned withdrawal comes amid strained relations between Uganda and DR Congo, despite years of close military co-operation.
Earlier this week, Gen Kainerugaba appeared to endorse a rebel takeover of Walikale, a mineral-rich territory in eastern DR Congo, threatening that Ugandan troops would seize it themselves if the rebels failed to do so.
The Congolese foreign ministry described his remarks as "particularly serious" and demanded that Uganda clarify its official position and take steps to prevent similar incidents.
Gen Kainerugaba also defended Uganda's military presence in eastern DR Congo, insisting it was driven by security concerns.
The planned withdrawal comes amid renewed fighting between Congolese forces and rebels in the wider Lubero territory, raising fresh concerns about the safety of civilians.
Eastern DR Congo has endured decades of conflict involving dozens of armed groups, with millions of people forced to flee their homes.
