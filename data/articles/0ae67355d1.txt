Referencing David Hubel and Torsten Wiesel’s work on the development of the visual cortex in animals, I believe the answer lies in the dog’s upbringing.
Hubel and Wiesel worked extensively on kittens, finding that if certain visual cues were removed from a kitten’s environment in the period when the animal’s brain was wiring its visual pathways, it wouldn’t be able to see those things once they had completed their development. In experiments, kittens brought up in a box containing only horizontal striped lines couldn’t see chair legs or other vertical objects in a normal environment.
So, if a dog wasn’t brought up in an environment that included objects at different heights, then its brain would potentially never be able to recognise that there is an aircraft overhead. And with the cacophony of other motorised and industrial sounds in our world, it is likely that the aeroplane would blend into the background.
