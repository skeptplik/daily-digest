South Korean entrepreneur Mah Seungeun rarely talks herself out of buying something for her 10-year-old daughter. With no siblings to share the family’s attention — or its spending — each childhood milestone feels like a one-time opportunity.
“Because I have just one child, I always think: ‘this is the last year my kid will be five, this is the last year they’ll be six.’ So even if I hesitate over a purchase, I end up buying it,” said Mah, 38. “The grandparents buy a lot of gifts, and so do the aunts and uncles.”
That impulse is turning South Korea, home to one of the world’s lowest birth rates, into an unlikely hotspot for premium baby goods. Its dwindling number of children are becoming what Korean marketers dub VIBs — “Very Important Babies” — showered with pricey strollers, designer clothes and toys by families determined to spare little expense for their only progeny.
