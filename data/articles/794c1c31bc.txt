Sony has released three new models of its Walkman, with prices ranging from ¥215,000 ($1,350) to ¥655,000 for its flagship product — the most expensive version of the audio device so far.
The new Sony Walkman models — the first in over three years — allows users to stream music using Wi-Fi, has enhanced sound quality and are particularly large in size.
The flagship Walkman, which was launched as part of the WM1 series on Friday, is gold-colored and has a storage of 256 GB. The two other audio players come in black and have a lower storage capacity.
Sony’s flagship Walkman is described as combining “next-level” audio technology that maintains its “iconic headphone jack and side buttons which have been passed down since the first Walkman series,” according to the company’s website.
The two other products include the 128 GB Walkman WM1 (priced at ¥347,000) and the 64 GB Walkman ZX (priced at ¥215,000) which is part of the separate ZX series that sits below the high-end WM1 series.
Unlike the iconic early Walkman portable cassette tapes, Sony’s latest products also have a sizable HD touch screen of about 13 centimeters to 14 cm.
Racer Leung, 37, a software engineer visiting from Hong Kong, was one of the customers browsing Sony’s new Walkman series at its flagship store in the Ginza district of Tokyo on Friday.
Calling himself a “Sony fan,” Leung said, “I have been very lucky because I was going ’round Ginza today, and it happened to be the first day of the release of this (Walkman) model.”
“It’s nice to see Sony is not giving up on the hi-fi (advanced hardware) market even though it has many competitors, including Korean and other brands,” he said, adding that the choice offered by the product range, as well as the weak yen, made the price tag more attractive for him.
Lee Vardy, a 44-year-old tourist from Australia, said he was less impressed with the “high price point” of the new Walkman products while visiting the Sony store Friday with his family.
Vardy reflected that “we were going through the whole retro nostalgia as we came in because we saw the original Sony Walkmans there,” explaining that he grew up in the era of the early cassette tapes.
Compared to his 8-year-old son and partner, Vardy said, “I’m more of a music lover and tech geek than they are. (My son) is definitely a big gamer, but I don’t think he’s grown into music yet.”
