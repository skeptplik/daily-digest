The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake gives Link's first 3D adventure a new coat of paint, but some of its details have players second-guessing the reality they thought they once knew.
You don't have to be a Zelda expert to know what fairies look like in the original Ocarina of Time. Navi is an icon, which means most people who have ever even thought about touching one of the games in Nintendo's beloved fantasy franchise know that Link's little guide is little more than a glowing ball with wings – or so they thought.
The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time brought more than just a fancy new tunic for Link when it made the leap to Switch 2 last week. A few close-up shots early in the adventure make one shocking fact clear: The fairies of Kokiri Forest aren't orbs of light – they're fuzzy.
It's the kind of detail the original Nintendo 64 release could have never conveyed made real thanks to the artists at Nintendo and the power of the Switch 2. It's about as small as details come but also recontextualizes how many – myself included – have viewed Ocarina of Time's fairies for the last 28 years.
"It appears Fairies are just...Fluffy Balls that emit a light!" one Reddit user said. "It's neither a ball of pure light or a humanoid lil person hidden under a glow. Just...Fluff! Interesting :D And cute!"
"Answering the important lore questions like 'is that a sentient puffball or a tiny lady who's so shiny you can only see her aura,'" another fan commented.
Zelda lore is packed with details that fans have obsessed over for decades, so a revelation like this is kind of groundbreaking. Was it always Nintendo's intention for the fairies to be tiny talking puffballs? Probably not, and if you prefer the pure light orb fairies they'll always be there, too, but fans at least seem to have taken to the change after weekend one.

Fairies are far from the only characters making players question their childhood memories. One that more people have realized pre-remake has to do with the attire of Hyrule guards. Don't worry, if it looked to you like they were wearing some pretty standard fantasy knight armor, you'll be happy to know that is exactly what they're wearing in 2026.
Well, that and a pair of jorts.
"He's wearing f**king jorts?????" one player questioned.
"Yes and he looks great in it," another replied.

"I like to believe that all the guards are forced to wear jorts under penalty of death, purely because Zelda decrees it so," another Reddit user suggested. "It’s a really nice detail they added in the remake."
Unlike the fairies, it does seem like jorts were standard issue for Zelda's royal guard back in the day. It's just that it's easier to tell that now thanks to the Ocarina of Time remake's Switch 2 graphics. It's made for some funny reactions online, but not all of the new details are quite as charming as jort-wearing knights.
Boundary Break host Shesez, who offers out-of-bounds perspectives on video games, got up close and personal with The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time's creepiest enemies: the ReDeads. In the N64 game, these mummified zombie grapplers were scary enough thanks to their empty eye sockets. On Switch 2, they still look eyeless – at least at a first glance. Get close (but not too close) enough for a good look, zoom in, and you'll see the developers were nice enough to give ReDead's soulless eyeballs behind their masks. Thanks, Nintendo!
