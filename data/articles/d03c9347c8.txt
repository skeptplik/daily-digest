Residents of a remote South Korean island were ordered to take shelter on Tuesday morning after several Russian military aircraft came close to entering its airspace, prompting a pre-emptive alert from the military that anticipated a violation.
The defence ministry said it would summon the Russian defence attaché on Tuesday afternoon to lodge a strong protest.
The joint chiefs of staff (JCS) said the aircraft had entered the country’s air defence identification zone over the Sea of Japan – known in South Korea as the East Sea – and approached the island of Ulleungdo. It did not specify how many Russian aircraft were involved, but South Korean air force jets were scrambled before the aircraft left.
A JCS spokesperson, Jang Do-young, said the aircraft had approached “to the point just before violating Ulleungdo’s airspace” and that, because military aircraft move very fast, a violation was anticipated.
The alert was issued pre-emptively to protect residents, he said.
The interior ministry issued the emergency alert at 7.52am to residents of Ulleungdo, a volcanic island of about 8,700 people 120km off the east coast, telling them to take shelter. The English portion of the message read: “EVACUATION, AIR RAID”.
Television broadcasts were interrupted by on-screen warnings telling residents to “prepare to evacuate to a nearby underground shelter facility and listen to broadcasting”. The alert was lifted at 7.55am.
On the island, sirens sounded and residents reportedly heard the noise of fighter jets. Jeong Hae-seung, 41, who was working on a building site, told the Hankyoreh newspaper he ran out of his workplace the moment the text arrived.
“I went outside wondering what on earth was going on, and the civil defence broadcast was playing, but I couldn’t make out what it was saying,” he said.
Baek Un-bae, who lives on the island, told broadcaster YTN that an elderly resident had telephoned him asking whether war had broken out.
“Older people have no access to the media, so all they hear is the warning broadcast and they are too alarmed to know what to do,” he said.
Others said they did not hear the sirens at all, with windows shut against the cold and strong winds blowing.
A second message, sent by North Gyeongsang province, which administers Ulleungdo, said a flying object “presumed to be a North Korean aircraft” had left the danger zone.
The province said it had mistakenly taken the wording from its routine drill messages, changed only the time and sent it in error.
Jang described the alert as unusual but said he could not confirm whether it was the first of its kind.
Tuesday’s incident was the fourth known entry by Russian military aircraft into the zone this year and the second in a week.
An air defence identification zone is not sovereign airspace, but countries establish such zones beyond their own airspace to identify incoming military flights early. While submitting a flight plan in advance is international practice, entering an identification zone without prior notification does not in itself violate international law.
Russia argues that the Korean zone has no basis in international law and does not recognise Seoul’s authority over it, and has repeatedly flown into it without notice.
According to data submitted to parliament, about 60 Russian entries without prior notification were recorded in the five years to 2024, compared with about 430 by Chinese aircraft.
Seoul previously summoned Russia’s defence attaché in March 2025 after eight entries in 10 days. At the time, it was the first such summons since a Russian aircraft violated South Korean airspace in 2019 and South Korean jets fired warning shots.
