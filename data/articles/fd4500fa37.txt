The company was not named in the indictment but was described as a large, publicly traded technology and defence firm with offices across the US, including in Palo Alto, California.
A Russian national has been arrested in California on suspicion of spying on employees of a Silicon Valley technology and defence company that provided support to Ukraine, US officials said on Wednesday.
Igor D'iu faces charges of conspiring to act as an agent of a foreign government, operating as a foreign agent without notifying US authorities, stalking and fraud.
"Igor D'iu was allegedly recruited, directed, and funded by the Russian government to stalk employees of a major US technology company," Roman Rozhavsky, assistant director of the FBI's Counterintelligence and Espionage Division, said in a statement.
Rozhavsky said D'iu used "physical surveillance, electronic tracking devices, and fraudulent identities to gather intelligence."
US Attorney Craig Missakian said the company was targeted because of its "assistance to Ukraine in its war with Russia."
"We will not tolerate any efforts -- by foreign governments or otherwise -- to turn Silicon Valley into a base for foreign intelligence operations," Missakian said.
What does the indictment say?
According to the indictment, D'iu travelled from Turkey to Mexico in June 2026 before being smuggled into the United States, where he allegedly communicated with his Russia-based co-conspirators via Telegram.
They allegedly instructed him to gather information on company employees and visit a coffee shop near its Palo Alto office to "photograph their laptop screens while they worked."
FBI agents found $18,000 (€15,500) in cash, a fake US passport under the name "Hugo Landry Johns" and two Russian passports during an October raid on D'iu's home in East Palo Alto, according to the Justice Department.
If convicted, D'iu faces up to 10 years in prison for acting as an agent of a foreign government without notifying the US attorney general, five years for conspiracy, five years for stalking and one year for fraud.
He is scheduled to appear in federal court on 24 November.
