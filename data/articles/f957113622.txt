Cyberattack disrupts global news distribution platform at France’s AFP
French international news agency Agence France-Presse (AFP) was hit by a cyberattack Thursday, forcing it to take offline one of its main platforms for distributing news content to media organizations worldwide.
The attack targeted AFP Forum, an online platform that news outlets use to browse and download the agency’s stories, photographs and videos.
AFP confirmed the incident Thursday in an internal email to employees, which was quoted in the agency’s own reporting. The agency is investigating the attack with assistance from external cybersecurity experts.
“AFP was hit by a cyber attack overnight. The target was AFP Forum, one of the platforms our clients use to browse and download our content,” AFP management said in the email.
“As soon as the intrusion was detected, our teams moved immediately to contain the intrusion and took AFP Forum offline as a precautionary measure. AFP Forum remains unavailable until further notice.”
The agency is offering subscribers alternative ways to access its content while the platform remains offline, according to French media reports.
AFP Forum remained inaccessible as of Friday, displaying a maintenance notice in several languages. The agency has not identified who was behind the attack or provided a timeline for restoring the service.
It remains unclear whether the hackers gained access to sensitive information or whether other AFP systems were affected.
The agency did not respond to a request for comment.
AFP is one of the world’s three major international news agencies, alongside Reuters and the Associated Press. It traces its roots to Agence Havas, founded in 1835, and took its current name in 1944. It employs more than 1,800 journalists and has 210 bureaus worldwide.
This is not the first cyberattack to disrupt the agency’s content distribution services.
In September 2024, AFP detected a cyberattack on its IT systems that disrupted some of the services used to deliver news content to subscribers. The attack has not been publicly attributed to any hacking group.
