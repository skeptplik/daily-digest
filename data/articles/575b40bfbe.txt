Van Cleef & Arpels is bringing a dose of Parisian savoir-faire to Las Vegas, transforming Bar Centro at The Shops at Crystals into an immersive showcase for its Perlée collection, marking the first dedicated pop-up for the jewelry line in the Americas.
Running through Nov. 22, the 70-square-meter installation brings together contemporary creations, archival jewels and live demonstrations of craftsmanship within a playful environment conceived by French artist and longtime collaborator Arthur Hoffner.
The activation underscores how heritage jewelry houses are increasingly looking beyond traditional retail environments to engage clients, using immersive experiences to bring their design codes and craftsmanship into sharper focus. Las Vegas, with its international clientele and appetite for spectacle, offers a fitting backdrop.
“Las Vegas is a city defined by energy, imagination and celebration, which are qualities that resonate with the spirit of the Perlée collection,” said Helen King, president and chief executive officer of Van Cleef & Arpels Americas. “Bringing our very first Perlée pop-up in the Americas to Las Vegas allows us to enchant a vibrant and global audience in an atmosphere where creativity and wonder are openly celebrated.”
For the installation, Hoffner, who has collaborated with the maison since 2018, has created a whimsical landscape of geometric totems, moving spheres and animated showcases that play off the collection’s signature rounded forms. His sculptural vocabulary, informed by a background in metalworking and industrial design, finds a natural dialogue with Perlée’s graphic aesthetic.
The presentation also offers a closer look at the collection’s lineage.
Although Perlée was formally introduced in 2008, its defining golden bead has roots stretching back nearly a century. The motif appeared in the maison’s early creations before gaining prominence in the late 1940s with the Couscous collection. It subsequently punctuated figurative animal clips, the colorful Twist creations of the 1960s and, perhaps most recognizably, the contours of the Alhambra collection, introduced in 1968.
“At Van Cleef & Arpels, innovation and heritage have always nourished one another,” King said. “In 2008, with the launch of the Perlée collection, this signature detail stepped into the spotlight to become the hero of its own modern design language.”
That design language feels particularly relevant today. While Perlée’s rounded forms and polished surfaces are rooted in the maison’s history, their modularity speaks to a more individualistic approach to fine jewelry, one in which pieces are collected, layered and worn across occasions rather than reserved for formal dressing. The collection’s enduring appeal lies in its ability to feel both familiar and distinctly personal.
“With Perlée, we invite self-expression through its incredible versatility,” King said. “The collection lends itself beautifully to mix-and-match layering, stacking and personal combinations of golden hues, polished surfaces, hard stones and sparkling diamonds.”
That interplay between heritage and individual expression is also reflected in the pop-up’s presentation, where pieces from the maison’s patrimony collection sit alongside current designs. Intimate salons and a library offer visitors an opportunity to explore the creations, while master jewelers will demonstrate traditional techniques on select days.
For Van Cleef & Arpels, the experience is as much about illuminating the work behind the jewels as showcasing the finished pieces. “This experience allows clients to step into our universe and connect with the maison on a personal level,” King said.
“They discover not only what we create, but how we create it, turning a moment of discovery into a lasting relationship,” she said.
