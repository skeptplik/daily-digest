Xbox VP of Next Generation Jason Ronald has clarified that Project Helix is "designed to be a console that is also capable of playing games, not a PC that can also play console games."
In the Xbox 25th Anniversary Magazine, Ronald described Project Helix as "the ultimate gaming machine – one that removes all the barriers of play that have historically existed."
"The goal is making it easy, intuitive, and instant to access games. But if you're a hardcore gamer or a power user who wants to have that deeper level of customisability across settings and performance options, you can absolutely go do it."
Xbox CEO Asha Sharma confirmed Project Helix's console and PC compatibility in March, adding that the console will "lead in performance" for Xbox and PC games.
As Ronald previously discussed at GDC Festival of Gaming, Project Helix will utilise neural rendering, which uses AI models to enhance graphics. Ronald identified neural rendering as a particular focus for Project Helix, as it also addresses the ongoing component crisis.
"It's definitely a challenging time, but as a system and platform designer, it's one of the most exciting times, because we have to invent entirely new technology to continue to advance the state-of-the-art while also [making] things accessible and affordable."
"This is a big reason why we're really focused on advancements like neural rendering, because it's an entirely new way to generate a frame without requiring more computational horsepower [and] additional memory."
He concluded: "I think you're going to see more innovation over the next three to five years than you've seen in the last ten years."
Reports last month indicated that Project Helix is expected to launch during the 2028 holiday season, with a projected price between $1,200 and $1,800.
