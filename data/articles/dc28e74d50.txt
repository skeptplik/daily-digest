On Tuesday’s episode of the “Aspire With Emma Grede” podcast, the business mogul sat down with Amina Muaddi to talk about her career journey as a shoe designer and what it took for her to find success while building a brand that’s 100 percent independent.
In the episode, titled “How to Start Over and Get It Right the Second Time,” the fashion designer opened up about navigating the male-dominated footwear industry, having to fight to earn respect and how she reached a point where she no longer feels the need to prove herself.

After six years building her original luxury shoe brand Oscar Tiye, Muaddi left her role as cofounder and creative director in May 2017 after five years with the label. She shared that disagreements with her business partners led her to leave the company behind and start over, choosing her independence over retaining the brand.
Muaddi then launched her eponymous luxury label in 2018, and it was an instant success and has since received support from celebrity fans such as Rihanna, Beyoncé Knowles-Carter, Shakira and more. The designer has retained full ownership of the brand and says she has turned down offers from giants in the fashion world so that she could build the company at her own pace and on her own terms.
“I wanted to grow on my own, and I wanted to get to a certain point where I didn’t wanna rush it. I didn’t want to open a million stores right away,” she said. “The brand needs to grow, and people need to have awareness and know the brand. And I think there are necessary steps. I didn’t want the expansion to be immediate and fast.”
Grede then asked her if there was a set figure or condition that would make her consider selling her brand or joining forces with another fashion conglomerate. “It’s not a number. It’s really the human aspect of it. It’s the partnership and what would that partnership bring me,” Muaddi said.
“One of the big groups came to me, and I think I just said, ‘Not for now.’ That was my answer. I just wanted to grow more. I do want to have a higher valuation of my brand. I do want to be able to build it by myself and have the satisfaction that I built it by myself, and I don’t want to have a boss. Sometimes that’s the most important thing for me.”
Muaddi shared that she has witnessed people get derailed by their business partners who have taken their brands in directions they didn’t want to go in, adding that a friend told her that having investors feels like “having someone in your house every day.”
“They force them to do a category of product, or they do certain things that they don’t want to do,” she said. “Nobody tells me what to do when I wake up in the morning. I do what I want. If I feel a shoe is not great, I will stop selling it no matter what. I think that’s priceless for me.”
Muaddi also got personal about how spending years in survival mode from her childhood experiences shaped her ambition and resilience, and now that same ambition has allowed her to compete with other well-established shoe brands and luxury fashion houses. The conversation then led to them talking about her designs being duped, which she surprisingly said is an indication of her shoes being a success — unless it’s coming from a bigger brand than hers.
“In our industry, if you don’t get duped, this means you are not successful, or you don’t make a good product,” she said. “You know when I’m mad? When it’s big names. It’s a thing of cost. Basically, the dupes allow my product to get in the hands of an aspirational customer that cannot afford my brand.”
“What’s hurtful is when I see big houses or designers that I admire taking something that I do and doing something very similar. Because if a house that has hundreds of stores around the world does it, they might even take a product that I made and claim it because they have so much visibility that I don’t have.”
When it comes to mistakes that she has made in her career journey, Muaddi talked about being more on top of her finances and legally protecting herself and her designs. She also talked about sacrificing much of her personal life, including building a family, to prioritize her business, but said she’s now ready to make more room for dating and life outside work.
Muaddi won Designer of the Year at the 36th annual Footwear News Achievement Awards in 2022, marking her fourth major FNAA honor since launching her namesake brand in 2018. In 2020, she also won the FNAA Collaborator of the Year award alongside Rihanna for their work on the Fenty footwear collection.
