Rheinmetall 'grieving' for employees killed in Kuwait attack
German arms manufacturer Rheinmetall has confirmed that two of its employees had been killed in a drone attack in Kuwait.
"Two highly valued employees lost their lives while carrying out their professional duties, providing technical support for air-defense systems," the Düsseldorf-based company said in a statement.
"Protecting the people of that country was a matter of deep personal importance to them. Both the company's leadership and the entire workforce are devastated by the loss of our colleagues. We share in the grief of those they have left behind."
The two victims were Italian citizens who worked as technicians for subsidiary Rheinmetall Italia, Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani confirmed in Rome.
Tajani said the pair had been at an air surveillance station in Kuwait when it was attacked by two Iranian drones. Two other people, a Pakistani national and Kuwaiti army captain, were also injured.
The Iranian foreign ministry has denied any involvement in the deadly attack.
Rheinmetall secured a contract to supply the Kuwaiti navy with defense systems in July.
BSW: Left-wing populists seek path forward at annual party conference
Germany's left-wing populist BSW party is holding its annual conference on Friday and Saturday as it looks to remain relevant following a series of electoral defeats and internal splits.
The BSW was founded in 2024 by former Left Party politician Sahra Wagenknecht who leant her initials to the party's original name, the Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance or Bündnis Sahra Wagenknecht (BSW). The founder herself stepped down as party leader in 2025 and the party officially changed its name last month to "Alliance for Social Justice and Economic Reason" – also still abbreviated as BSW.
After initial successes in European and eastern German state elections in 2024, the BSW failed to reach the minimum 5% required to enter parliament in the 2025 federal election. In September, the BSW crept into the state parliament in Saxony-Anhalt with 5.3% of the vote but fell short in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and Berlin.
BSW split over stance on AfD
Ahead of its party conference in Dresden this weekend, the BSW has been split over its approach to the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) — which with it shares some economic and geopolitical policies, such as support for trading relations with Russia and opposition to military support for Ukraine.
Like most other German political parties, the BSW has ruled out entering into any coalition with the AfD but, in Saxony-Anhalt, has already voted with the far-right on certain policies and has held talks over support for certain issues which an AfD minority government would want to implement.
The BSW intends to abstain from the vote for the AfD's Ulrich Siegmund as state premier for Saxony-Anhalt on December 8 – but ultimately cannot force its five lawmakers to do so in what will be a secret ballot.
The softening of the BSW's position on the AfD has the backing of founder Wagenknecht and the party's lead candidate in Saxony-Anhalt, Claudia Wittig, who has slammed the policy of refusing to work with the AfD as "firewall idiocy."
"We're not cozying up to the AfD," she insisted, but said: "Let's not get tied up in knots over lies in the mainstream media."
Wagenknecht added: "Vibrant parties should have vibrant discussions about their future course, but we shouldn't accuse ourselves of things which aren't true. The BSW is not team AfD. Rather, we take voters seriously and want to implement political change to improve the lives of the majority."
But other founder members are resisting, writing in the Zeit newspaper this week that a "clear political distancing from the AfD" was a "foundational consensus" for the BSW.
"Just as it was right to reject the firewall, which only served the AfD anyway, so it is also wrong to make common cause with the AfD," they wrote, warning that the approach could morph into "political cooperation with AfD" which would "compromise the BSW and make it irrelevant in the long term."
They demanded: "No tolerance of an AfD government, no cooperation agreements, no abstentions from the vote for the state premier."
Left Party hosts international femicide summit
Germany's socialist Left Party (Die Linke) is on Friday hosting an international summit on femicide and violence against women.
Under the title "Voices Against Femicide," the summit in Berlin brings together activists, politicians and researchers from around the world, including Kenya, Nigeria, Kurdistan, India, Afghanistan, Syria, Mexico, Argentina, Spain and Germany.
Across three panel discussions, the speakers will highlight women's rights, feminist struggles and legal perspectives and debate political measures which are urgently needed in Germany to effectively combat femicides and patriarchal violence.
The summit comes just two days after Germany's parliament, the Bundestag, marked Orange Day, the United Nations' officially named day for the elimination of violence against women, with a minute's silence and a series of speeches.
Germany's Federal Criminal Police Office reported a total of 1,176 female victims of attempted and completed homicide in 2025, up from 859 in 2024.
The number of female victims of domestic violence in Germany in 2025 rose by 2.8% to 197,667.
German parliament to vote on €556 billion budget for 2027
German lawmakers will on Friday vote on a federal budget for 2027 which foresees €556 billion ($610 billion) in government spending, around €31 billion more than in 2026.
The largest chunk of expenditure (just over €200 billion) will go on federal contributions to the statutory pension system, while €110 billion is to be allocated to defense – a 32.7% increase.
The plans call for around €210 billion in fresh borrowing, a figure which is only expected to increase between now and 2030, while €2.7 billion are also expected to be shifted away from Germany's climate protection and transformation fund.
Chancellor Friedrich Merz's conservative Christian Democrats (CDU) and their Social Democrat (SPD) coalition partners have spent an unusually long time wrangling over the budget, with Merz finally clinching a deal last week after more than eight hours of talks at the Chancellery.
