In short:
Matildas midfielder Kyra Cooney-Cross is back in the squad for the first time since her mother's death in July.
She says it's bittersweet to be back playing at home.
What's next?
The Matildas will face Colombia in Canberra on Saturday, and Geelong on Wednesday.
When Kyra Cooney-Cross returned to the pitch for Arsenal in the Women's Super League in September, she shared a moving message from her mum.
Its essence is one that so many parents would have relayed to their kids in one manner or another in their sporting careers, whether they were as decorated as a Matildas' linchpin, or finding their feet for the first time in the under 5s.
"Kyra, try to remember what it felt like when you were little … when you just loved having the ball at your feet, playing with your mates, testing yourself against the boys or adults for the fun of it," the message read.
"Play because you love the ball at your feet, the rhythm of the game. Train because it feels good to move and challenge your body and feel strong.
"This is how you start doing it for you again — Mum."
Amongst the pain of losing her mother Jess to terminal bile duct cancer in July, Cooney-Cross has taken the message to heart, and three months after returning to club football, she's back for the Matildas.
The 24-year-old is in the squad for friendlies against Colombia in Canberra and Geelong, in a key test just seven months out from the Women's World Cup in Brazil.
"It's bittersweet. It's my first time back with the national team since everything that's happened with my mum and our family," she told ABC News Breakfast.
"But playing for your country, this is the highest level I can play at, and it's the highest level I can make my mum proud.
"So I'm really looking forward to it and being back at home in Geelong in front of my family and friends and just being back with the team."
Cooney-Cross's last international appearance was in March's Women's Asian Cup, and after her breakthrough tournament at the now fabled 2023 World Cup, she's eager to recreate those highs.
"It's definitely flown by. I don't think a lot of us players had even the time to process the World Cup and what we had done in this country and for women's football in Australia," she said.
"We're just going to work really hard. Stick together and focus on these next two games.
"The World Cup is obviously really important and we want to get really far."
McKenna making moves
Cooney-Cross will play a pivotal part in Brazil next year but others on the fringe, like midfielder Leticia McKenna, hope for their own breakout moment.
McKenna, 24, made her international debut in April, and starred for Melbourne City before securing a move to Nottingham Forest in the second tier of English women's football.
She was also recently named PFA Young Footballer of the Year.
"It's been very surreal. It's been a really big year, something I'm extremely proud of," she said.
McKenna's UK move made waves, setting an A-League Women's record with her transfer fee.
"It was a very, very hard decision to leave. It came up at quite short notice as well and I was very happy at the position I was in with Melbourne City and I was looking forward to the upcoming season in that regard," she said.
"But it was something I felt I was ready for. It was always in the back of my mind to make that step and move overseas."
Having made the most of her opportunities with the national side so far, and with only a handful of games left in the lead up, McKenna is eyeing off a berth in that final World Cup squad.
"You always gotta have a goal and aspirations and it's always been a dream," she said.
"Super grateful to be here and it's just making me even hungrier to keep dreaming."
