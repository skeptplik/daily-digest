“Human” has been selected by Cambridge Dictionary as its 2026 Word of the Year.
It's a surprisingly familiar word, but one which reflects growing fears regarding AI and concerns linked to identity, creativity and what makes us unique in an increasingly AI-driven world.
Cambridge Dictionary's 2026 pick reflects the backlash to the divisive technology, as well as the ongoing debate over how artificial intelligence continues to blur the lines between machines and people.
“AI is changing how we talk about being human,” said Colin McIntosh, the dictionary’s lexicographer. “Fears about what humans may lose when we outsource our thinking have made us reconsider the extent to which we rely on AI: from top mathematicians to teachers of young children.”
The word, derived from the Latin “humanus”, is defined by Cambridge as “having the qualities, faults and feelings that people have, as opposed to gods, animals or machines.”
The dictionary cited public interventions by intellectuals such as AI pioneer and Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton, who said: “We’re creating alien beings. I’m willing to be selfish and choose humans."
"AI brings us back to questions that we've asked for millennia - who are we? What does it mean to be human?" said Prof Rachel Adams, an expert on AI governance and human rights at the University of Cambridge. "AI is going to be a profound test for humanity. There's a real risk that it deepens existing inequalities, but it also has the potential to solve them."
Artificial intelligence has factored heavily in Word of the Year distinctions across several dictionaries in recent years.
“AI” itself was chosen by Collins Dictionary in 2023 — a year when Cambridge picked “hallucinate,” in reference to AI generating false or misleading information, and Merriam-Webster crowned "authentic".
Merriam-Webster last year named “slop” its Word of the Year for low-quality content produced by AI, while Collins went with “vibe coding” for the AI-assisted software development method.
Other word contenders for Cambridge Dictionary's 2026 Word of the Year included “agent,” for an AI system that can make decisions and operate without human control, and “analogue” for experiences or objects not involving the internet or digital technology. A non-tech contender this year was “wholesome”, a word to describe something which is likely to improve your life either physically, morally, or emotionally.
Earlier this month, “mogging” was named Collins' Word of the Year, while Dictionary.com went for "maxxing" - both rather depressing terms which are being reclaimed by Gen Z in modern slang and conversation.
