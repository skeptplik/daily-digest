So far, the Nintendo Switch 2 upgraded editions of games originally released on the Switch have been a bit of a mixed bag. While games like Kirby and the Forgotten Land received significant new content and noticeable performance upgrades, others such as Xenoblade Chronicles X weren’t quite as well received. According to many Pikmin fans, the recently released Switch 2 version of Pikmin 4 falls into the latter camp.
Pikmin 4: Nintendo Switch 2 Edition + Dandori Academy is an upgrade for the 2023 Switch game with added levels and higher-resolution graphics. It came out last Thursday and costs $10 to upgrade from the original Nintendo Switch version. Unfortunately, the new version of Pikmin 4 currently has a dismal 1.9 out of 10 user score on Metacritic, a massive drop from the original version’s 8.9 user rating.
Performance issues and lackluster additions
As for why it’s drawing so much ire, user reviews claim the Switch 2 version runs worse than it did on the previous system. Multiple players cite frame rate drops alongside longer load times. “This is essentially a paid downgrade and calling this a ‘lazy Switch 2 Edition’ or a ‘waste of money’ is an understatement for what is supposed to be an upgrade,” a Metacritic user review from PrestonandTom reads. They go on to claim that standard load times have gone from “six seconds to 30+ seconds” and that the frame rate has gone from a “consistent 30 FPS to an inconsistent 30 FPS.”
“Actively makes the game worse,” a review from Kevosan corroborates. “Longer load times than on NS1, frame drops, and glitches. The new content is NOT worth the trade-off. Standards are in the depths with this one.” Beyond the technical issues, some players also aren’t happy with the added Dandori Academy levels, which are apparently repetitive and reuse areas from the main story.
Still, there are a few upgrades that even the biggest detractors are fine with. The Switch 2 version of Pikmin 4 runs at a higher resolution, and some users commented that the voice support, which lets you give voice commands to the weird little dog-alien Oatchi, is a nice addition.
Many on the Pikmin subreddit weren’t quite as up in arms over the new version of Pikmin 4, with some saying that the negative reviews were being overdramatic. “0.6 is a score I’d only give to a game that is sub-functional,” one Redditor said about the review-bombed Metacritic page. Others argued that while the new Dandori Academy levels may reuse maps, the new enemy placements and other changes make these areas feel different.
Still, regardless of how fans feel about this upgrade, many Pikmin-heads are worried that the somewhat light changes compared to other Switch 2 editions mean that the series isn't being prioritized as highly as some of Nintendo's other tentpole releases. While Pikmin 5 hasn't been announced, at least the recently announced Pikmin shorts offer something to look forward to.
