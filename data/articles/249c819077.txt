Chinese tech giant Huawei has been found guilty of stealing trade secrets and bank fraud by a US jury but was acquitted of a more serious charge of racketeering, according to multiple reports.
The verdict, delivered on Tuesday, followed an 11-week trial at the US District Court for the Eastern District of New York.
Huawei has strenuously denied any wrongdoing.
In a statement, the company said the acquittal on the racketeering charge "confirms that these allegations lack factual basis and vindicates our consistent position over the past eight years."
"We believe the government’s remaining charges are equally without legal or factual merit," it added.
The US first announced the charges against Huawei in January 2019. The allegations were later expanded.
“The tone was set at the top by Huawei leaders and executives who themselves engaged in criminal conduct, like lying to Congress, lying to banks, violating US sanctions and trying to interfere with the very criminal investigation that led to this case,” prosecutor Meredith Arfa told the jury at the trial, the Associated Press reported.
Brian Heberlig, a lawyer for Huawei, said in his closing argument that prosecutors had leaned on "innuendo and facts ripped out of context" as well as testimony from ex-employees "with a bone to pick"
“Huawei didn’t steal its way to success,” Heberlig said, adding that much of the material the US alleges was stolen was already publicly available.
Heberlig said Huawei would appeal the convictions.
