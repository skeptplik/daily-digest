These are the stories making headlines in fashion on Wednesday.
U.S. shoe prices rose 2.1% in October, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, as higher oil prices tied to the war in Iran and tariffs on Asian-made products pushed up costs. Petroleum-based materials and freight have become more expensive, and executives at Crocs, Adidas, Steve Madden and Birkenstock have said those costs have squeezed their businesses. Gary Raines, chief economist of the Footwear Distributors and Retailers of America, told The Times he “expects record footwear prices for the full year.” Deckers raised the price of Ugg’s classic boot 2.6% to $195, and Hoka is expected to follow with increases this fall. {The New York Times/paywalled}
There are growing concerns among investors that an AI slowdown could trigger selloffs that deplete the stock market and curb spending by wealthy American consumers. Unfortunately, the U.S. is currently one of the only markets driving growth at big European luxury houses. Because of these fears, shares of Hermès fell more than 4%, while Kering, Burberry and LVMH dropped between 1.8% and 3.6%. Moncler fell 4.3% to its lowest level in four years. {Reuters/paywalled}
The European Commission has charged Temu with failing to do enough to stop sales of illegal products on its platform, a preliminary finding that exposes the Chinese e-commerce company to a second fine under the Digital Services Act. Regulators said Temu’s safeguards include “ineffective controls to check the compliance of products, a weak content moderation system and an ineffective sanction system.” {Reuters/paywalled}
Target’s third-quarter sales jumped 7.1% and the company raised its full-year outlook. While beauty sales grew more than 9%, apparel and accessories sales fell 3.8% to $3.7 billion. In a conference call, CMO Cara Sylvester named women’s apparel and shoes as the two problem categories, adding that new products are coming to remedy that in the new year. Target now expects full-year sales growth of about 6%, up from 5%. {Target}
Sources told WWD that investment bank Solomon Partners is pitching Spanx to potential buyers, months after Blackstone transferred ownership of the brand to its lender, the BlackRock-owned HPS. Spanx once pioneered the shapewear category, but has since lost ground to competitors like Kim Kardashian’s Skims. {WWD/paywalled}
