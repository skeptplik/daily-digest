Colombian security forces have freed around 20 shoppers and employees who had been held hostage for about 17 hours at a supermarket in the city of Cartagena.
Gunmen had stormed into the building at 08:00 (13:00 GMT) on Tuesday after what Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella said was a failed attempt at robbing an armoured car carrying money.
They held those inside at gunpoint while trying to negotiate their getaway.
With the situation becoming ever more intense as the day progressed, De la Espriella gave the green light for special forces and police to launch a daring operation to free the hostages, in which one gunman was killed.
Residents living near the supermarket reported hearing explosions as the security forces moved into the building.
Police later confirmed that officers had used stun grenades to overpower the hostage takers.
One was killed in the operation and four have been arrested.
De la Espriella described it as "an extraordinary joint operation", which he said had been carried out "flawlessly".
He flew into the northern city to oversee the operation.
In footage released since then, he can be seen, with a handgun strapped to his belt, flanked by the commanders of the operation.
Speaking after the successful rescue of the hostages, he said that the gunmen had been given the chance to hand themselves in, which they had refused.
Earlier in the day, the assailants had freed 10 of their hostages, among them elderly women.
But they continued to hold around 20, even as a police helicopter hovered above and special forces teams prepared to enter the building, which is located in a popular shopping mall.
Local media said that as well as asking for safe passage out of the supermarket, the gunmen also demanded nearly $2m (Â£1.5m) and armoured vehicles.
According to President De la Espriella, they had no political affiliations but had taken the hostages once their plan to rob an armoured car carrying money had been foiled.
"It's not that a terrorist group carried out a kidnapping - no, these are criminals who were caught off guard, had no way out, and then kidnapped a group of people," he said.
Photos taken during the rescue operation show armed soldiers inside the building as people are kneeling with their hands behind their heads.
It is not clear from the photos whether those kneeling are hostages or the hostage-takers.
Local media are reporting that the authorities feared that some of the gunmen may have tried to pass themselves off as hostages as the rescue mission unfolded.
The identities of the four arrested have not yet been released.
The hostages are being reunited with their families after being checked over by medical teams, according to the mayor of Cartagena.
Many anxious relatives had spent hours waiting for news outside the perimeter place around the supermarket by security forces.
A woman called Sandra told news site Caracol that her 76-year-old mother was one of those being held hostage.
Sandra's mother - among a group of elderly people released after six hours - was reunited with her daughters and grandchildren.
Footage recorded by local media showed bystanders cheering as some of those freed were escorted to ambulances waiting nearby.
But with around 20 people still inside the building, tension outside rose and police said they had to stop some of the relatives from breaching the perimeter.
At one point, flights into the city were halted, with planes diverted to Barranquilla.
Special forces, including units specialised in dealing with kidnappings, were deployed to Cartagena, a popular destination with foreign and Colombian tourists.
Cartagena's mayor, Dumek Turbay called for calm but the granddaughter of one of the hostages told Colombian newspaper El Tiempo that "this shows we're not safe anywhere".
President De la Espriella was elected earlier this year on a promise to crack down on Colombia's criminal groups.
On Tuesday, he had travelled to the central city of Villavicencio to inspect the damage caused by a car bomb which detonated outside the regional government's offices.
While De la Espriella blamed the leader of a dissident rebel group for that attack, he was adamant that the hostage takers were "simple bandits" without political affiliations.
