A man who almost died during liver surgery at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary (ARI) has told BBC Scotland News he believes mistakes were made during his operation.
Derek Appleton, from Stockport, was living in Orkney when he underwent surgery to remove 60% of his liver after being diagnosed with stage four cancer.
The 60-year-old lost 11 litres of blood and went into cardiac arrest, but says he has never been given a proper explanation for what happened.
It comes after a leaked review of 45 patients who died following liver and pancreatic surgery at the hospital found that 19 of those deaths could have been avoided.

Derek got in touch with BBC Scotland via Your Voice. He said: "I got told it was a life-changing operation. And call me naÃ¯ve, but I thought life-changing operation was going to be for the good."
He was told complications during his surgery meant he needed to have his chest and rib cage opened to allow medics to manually pump his heart after he went into cardiac arrest and that a surgeon had to put two fingers into his hepatic vein to stop the bleeding.
"Technically I died, not one single person in theatre thought I was going to come out alive," he said.
Derek lost 11 litres of blood on the operating table. The average human body holds 4.5 to 5.5 litres.
He was so sick that four days after the operation he was put into an induced coma, and his partner Mark was told to fly from Spain to say his goodbyes.
Derek recovered but added: "I am not the same person that went into the Aberdeen Royal."
The type of operation Derek received is no longer preformed in the NHS Grampian area, with patients instead being transferred to Edinburgh.
Derek says he believes mistakes were made during his care but he has never been told of any investigation or review into what went wrong.
When asked about Derek's case, a spokesperson for NHS Grampian said they could not comment on individual patients.
On Thursday, Dr Mohammed Habib, a consultant at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary (ARI) leaked an unpublished review from 2022 that found 19 avoidable deaths among 45 patients in liver and pancreatic surgery.
Among them were patients who bled to death on the operating table as a result of surgical errors.
NHS Grampian said it was aware of the issues raised and said an investigation had already taken place.
Scotland's Patient Safety Commissioner has said she is not confident that all the facts about concerns raised at NHS Grampian have yet come to light.
Karen Titchener told BBC Radio Scotland Breakfast programme she was seeking "independent assurance" that change was being implemented at NHS Grampian and added that it was "totally unacceptable" that the health secretary was not aware of the issues.
Asked if she was confident there was not more information to come on NHS Grampian, she said: "I'm not feeling confident about it, but the health secretary obviously knows much more than I do.
"This was 19 patients, the Royal College for Emergency Medicine said there was 800 avoidable deaths in A&Es in Scotland last year.
"This is bigger than NHS Grampian," she added.
Concerns 'ignored' - whistleblower
Health Secretary Angela Constance told the programme she was not previously aware of the reports but stood by her comment last week that patients at ARI and Dr Gray's Hospital in Elgin were safe.
"I want immediate action and improvements, but it also has to be sustained for the longer term," she said.
Habib, who is still a surgeon at the hospital, said he had been raising concerns for a decade, including with government ministers and police, but he had been ignored.
A 2022 review leaked by the whistle-blower looked at 45 patients in the care of liver and pancreatic services at ARI between 2015 and 2021.
It said 31 had died, and 19 of them were considered to have died of avoidable consequences of surgery or intervention.
The 2022 review found that mortality rates for liver and pancreas surgery at NHS Grampian was very high.
It said that between 2017 and 2020 the overall death rate for major liver resections was 12% after 30 days and 18% after 90 days, compared to an expected rate of 2.2%.
In a statement on Thursday Laura Skaife-Knight, the chief executive of NHS Grampian, said: "A thorough investigation has already taken place into these matters, with some elements of that work ongoing.
"Any new evidence or information will be carefully considered, and NHS Grampian will continue to respond through the appropriate clinical, governance and legal processes."
A Police Scotland spokesperson said an investigation was ongoing.
