Pierce Brosnan has welcomed Robert Pattinson into the exclusive 007 club by telling the newly-announced seventh Bond to "enjoy the ride".
Pattinson was revealed as the next James Bond on Monday, ending years of speculation about the future of the franchise post-Daniel Craig.
Brosnan was the fifth actor to portray Bond, starting with GoldenEye in 1995. He went on to star in three more films: Tomorrow Never Dies (1997), The World Is Not Enough (1999), and Die Another Day (2002).
In an Instagram post early today, Brosnan assured Pattinson he would make a “magnificent” Bond.
He wrote: “Dear Robert, Welcome to the world of Bond. You are going to be magnificent. I have fond memories of our time together making ‘Remember Me’. It has been a joy to watch you create a remarkable body of work and to go from strength to strength."
Brosnan and Pattinson starred alongside each other in the 2010 romantic-drama film Remember Me.
Brosnan concluded: “Enjoy the ride. Grá mor, Pierce.”
Actors including Jack Lowden, Josh O’Connor, Callum Turner and Nicholas Hoult were reported to be in contention for the role before Pattinson was unveiled as the man who follows in the footsteps of only six actors to have played James Bond on the big screen – Sean Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton, Brosnan and Craig.
Brosnan had previously given his backing to Turner, and earlier this week, Lazenby - the oldest living 007 actor – also welcomed Pattinson into the fold.
The 87-year-old, who played Bond once in 1969’s On Her Majesty’s Secret Service, posted a video message on Instagram.
“Robert, welcome to the most exclusive club in the world,” Lazenby said, before offering up advice: “But remember to enjoy yourself. Let’s see if you’ll have a ‘this never happened to the other fella’ moment.”
Lazenby concluded: “All the best mate, George.”
The 26th Bond film is in development, with Denis Villeneuve directing and Steven Knight on script duties.
Production is expected to start next year, but Pattinson will have to complete filming The Batman Part II – production on which was temporarily paused after director Matt Reeves was faced with a personal issue. Steven Soderbergh has stepped in to help shoot the final few weeks.
This means that the earliest audiences will get to see the next Bond adventure will be in the second half of 2028 - making it seven years since Daniel Craig's swansong No Time To Die, and the longest gap between Bond films since 1989's Licence To Kill and 1995's GoldenEye.
