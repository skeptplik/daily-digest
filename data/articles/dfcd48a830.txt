Russia-linked APT Star Blizzard hacked a US organization by impersonating an employee at another US organization it had previously compromised, Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) reports.
Also tracked as Callisto, Cold Relic, Cold River, UNC4057, and Gossamer Bear, and active for over a decade, Star Blizzard has been formally associated by the US with Russia’s intelligence service FSB.
The APT is known for mounting sophisticated phishing and malware campaigns to collect information relevant to the Russian government, and to use victims’ data in attacks on subsequent targets.
Star Blizzard “has been highly successful at compromising high-value targets (including government officials, defense contractors, and non-governmental organizations (NGOs) across Europe and North America) while maintaining a consistent operational tempo,” Google says.
In a recent attack against a US organization, Star Blizzard impersonated an employee of another organization it had breached months earlier. Google, which tracks the group as Cold Relic, believes the hackers likely used contact and relationship information obtained during the earlier intrusion to select and deceive their new target.
The first organization was hit in late 2025, using the compromised credentials of a super admin account. The APT leveraged a residential proxy network close to the victim and relied on multi-factor authentication (MFA) push notification fatigue to register a device to the victim’s tenant.
Next, the group downloaded the organization’s entire cloud storage drive, accessed the admin console to fingerprint defenses and remove evidence of the initial intrusion, exported full employee lists, and launched a domain-wide export.
According to Google, the group used passkeys, rogue MFA devices, and MFA backup codes to maintain persistent access to the victim organization for several months. It also exfiltrated emails and targeted cloud drive documents containing information of interest.
“When the threat actor was removed from the victim organization’s infrastructure, GTIG observed Cold Relic leveraging email accounts impersonating employees of the victim organization. These impersonated accounts were used to target both internal employees and associates within the same professional circles with renewed social engineering attempts,” Google says.
The APT impersonated an employee at the victim organization to establish communication with the second victim organization via a secure messaging application.
Ultimately, it shared phishing links designed to steal credentials and Entra ID authentication tokens, and used the secrets to register a new MFA device to the second organization’s SSO solution to retain access to the compromised account.
“By impersonating someone known in the same professional circles as the new target, Cold Relic increased their chance of phishing success. Ultimately, in this case, Cold Relic was able to obtain credentials to the organization and perform bulk download of files accessible in the compromised user’s cloud drive,” Google says.
To maintain persistence, the APT registered rogue MFA devices, passkeys, and MFA backup codes, conducted aggressive data collection and exfiltration, and searched for credentials, employee information, email archives, documents, and any other information it could use in subsequent spear-phishing attacks.
The threat actor also changed its profile name in the secure messaging application used in this intrusion, likely to reuse it in another attack.
“Cold Relic’s campaigns demonstrate the convergence of human-centric social engineering and cloud identity abuse. By obtaining access to strategic personnel rosters and operational documents, Russian intelligence services turn legitimate business communications into attack vectors against trusted partners,” Google notes.
