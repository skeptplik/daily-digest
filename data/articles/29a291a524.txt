Israeli strike kills 3 in vehicle distributing aid in Gaza, witnesses say
A witness said the vehicle appeared to be carrying food and papers
Israeli forces on Saturday hit a truck in Gaza, which witnesses said was carrying food and papers bearing the logo of the World Central Kitchen charity, but the organization said the vehicle was not affiliated with it.
The Gaza Health Ministry said three people were killed in the incident in Gaza City.
The Israeli military said several militant operatives were targeted in the area, without providing further details. It did not immediately respond to questions on why it had hit a vehicle that appeared to be distributing aid.
World Central Kitchen said in a statement that the truck was unaffiliated with the charity, which distributes food in Gaza. It said a driver of the vehicle had previously worked for the charity and that the two others in the car were not WCK workers.
The vehicle appeared to be carrying packaged foodstuffs, and papers inside carried the logo of World Central Kitchen, an aid organization that distributes food in Gaza, according to a Reuters witness.
"It was just about to stop to distribute the food and drink to children when it was hit by a missile," said Abu Zein Al-Daya, who also witnessed the strike.
Afterward, meals of cooked rice were scattered around the vehicle, which was partially covered in the blood of the victims of the attack.

The Israeli military previously killed seven World Central Kitchen workers in a 2024 strike, including citizens of Australia, Britain and Poland.
At the time, the military said there had been "serious errors and breaches of procedure," but earlier this year it said it had found no basis upon which to open criminal investigations into the incident.
Palestinian children inspect the site of the Israeli airstrike on a vehicle on Saturday. (Dawoud Abu Alkas/Reuters)
Israel briefly launched a ground operation in Gaza earlier this week and killed six people in a strike on a hospital that it said was targeting a Hamas commander.
An October 2025 U.S.-backed ceasefire halted major fighting in the shattered territory but has not stopped Israeli attacks on Gaza or secured the disarmament of Hamas.
At least 1,600 Palestinians and five Israeli soldiers have been killed in the time since, according to respective Palestinian and Israeli tallies.

Israeli troops now control about two-thirds of Gaza, up from more than half at the time of the ceasefire, with Hamas controlling a sliver of territory along Gaza's coast.
Israel's three-year-long assault on Gaza has killed more than 74,000 Palestinians, health officials say. The attacks have also caused a hunger crisis, internally displaced Gaza's entire population and led to assessments of genocide from scholars and a United Nations inquiry.
Israel calls its actions self-defence after an attack by Hamas-led militants on Oct. 7, 2023, killed 1,200 people and took 251 hostages, according to Israeli tallies.
