Republican lawmakers still want answers on 'China obtaining our most advanced chips'
While Donald Trump is taking credit for his successful effort on Sunday to convince Nvidia’s board to bring back ousted founder Jensen Huang, some Republican lawmakers broke with the lame-duck president on Monday to demand answers on whether Huang’s affair facilitated smuggling of advanced AI chips to China.
“I have warned the administration from the start about Communist China obtaining our most advanced chips through both smuggling and remote access,” Senator Tom Cotton, an Arkansas Republican, said in a statement. “The controversy with Nvidia and Megaspeed is just the tip of the iceberg, and it’s not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back. Congress has much to investigate and we will pass overwhelmingly bipartisan chip export-control bills, including my Chip Security Act, one way or the other.”
That sentiment was echoed by House Republicans on Monday afternoon, in a joint statement from John Moolenaar, Rick Crawford and Brian Mast, the Republican chairs of three House committees – the select committee on China, the intelligence committee and the foreign affairs committee:
The NVIDIA board’s brief internal investigation and reinstatement of Jensen Huang do not erase the national security threats and questions that must be addressed. Jensen Huang and Tim Teter have confirmed they will appear for their transcribed interviews on December 1 and December 2 as previously planned. We remain committed to getting answers on the mass smuggling of NVIDIA products, their diversion for Chinese military purposes, and their key role in advancing our foremost adversary’s AI capabilities.

Singapore investigated Alice Huang but found no basis for prosecution, prime minister says
Singapore’s prime minister, Lawrence Wong, just told reporters in Australia that his country had investigated Alice Huang, the suspected Chinese intelligence asset who had an affair with Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, “but we eventually released her because there was no basis for further prosecution”.
Wong, who is visiting Australia, said that authorities in Singapore are continuing to investigate Alice Huang’s former firm, Megaspeed, which is based in Singapore.
Megaspeed and subsidiaries in Thailand and Malaysia were added to a US commerce department blacklist on Monday over concerns that some of the advanced Nvidia AI chips the firm purchased for data centers in south-east Asia serving Chinese technology companies like Alibaba might have been smuggled to China, violating a US export ban.
“America has export controls on its chips. We do not enforce America’s export controls, but we do not want Singapore to be used and abused as a conduit by companies to circumvent America’s export controls,” Wong said. “So what we do is to facilitate checks by America to enforce its own laws.
“At the same time, we are also very clear we do not want companies to breach Singapore laws. So if there are companies that are caught breaching our laws, we will certainly take very firm enforcement actions,” he continued. So it’s on that basis that we were investigating Megaspeed, which is a Singapore-based entity. And we also were investigating Alice Huang herself. But in the course of our investigations, we did what we did; we were thorough with Alice Huang; we took her in, we investigated her, but we eventually released her because there was no basis for further prosecution. America understands what we have done.
“We did ask for some information; not all of it was forthcoming, but we did what we did with her and the investigations with Megaspeed are ongoing,” Wong added.
Trump likely made up to $15m on Monday as value of his Nvidia stock spiked after Jensen Huang’s return - report
Donald Trump likely made up to $15m on Monday as the value of his declared holdings of Nvidia stock spiked after he convinced the chip-maker’s board to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive, the Financial Times reports.
The US president’s most recent financial disclosures to the Office of Government Ethics, filed on 5 November, show that he owned as many as 370,000 shares in Nvidia as of 30 September, the FT notes.
While the shares dropped sharply in value after Huang was revealed to have had an affair with a suspected Chinese spy, they rebounded by more than 30% on Monday after Huang’s return was announced and Trump took credit for the decision.
“I spoke to them, let me put it that way,” Trump told CNBC on Monday. “And I convinced them … I made the deal.”
US commerce department adds Megaspeed to trade blacklist
The US commerce department has published an official notice on Monday adding the Singapore-based company Megaspeed, and its subsidiaries in Thailand and Malaysia, to a trade blacklist.
Megaspeed was formerly led by Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese intelligence asset who had an affair with Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang.
“These entities have been determined by the U.S. Government to be acting contrary to the national security or foreign policy interests of the United States,” the notice says. “These additions are being made because of the significant risk of these entities being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or end users.”

Newsom responds to Trump's attack on his commutations, noting his demand for wrongfully convicted Central Park Five to be executed
Shortly before Donald Trump attacked him for changing every California death sentence to a sentence of life in prison without parole, California’s governor, Gavin Newsom, released a scathing video statement in which he criticized Trump for his use of the pardon power and for demanding the execution of five wrongfully convicted young New Yorkers of color in 1989 for a brutal rape they did not commit.
“Today, Donald Trump is once again standing inside the Department of Justice next to his former personal defense lawyer who is now the attorney general giving a speech about protecting American families. So, let’s talk about protecting families,” Newsom said. “Donald, in 1989, you took out full-page ads screaming for the death penalty for five black and Latino teenagers before a trial, before a shred of evidence. They were innocent. DNA proved it. You never apologized.
“To the families standing with him today, your grief is real. That’s why every person, every person on California’s former death row will die in prison. No one walks out,” the governor continued.
“Donald Trump’s criminals, they walk out,” he went on. “A billionaire charged with bribery; his family hands Trump’s lawyer a $50m deal while she’s working in that very building; weeks later, pardoned. A man who stole $10m from nursing home workers’ paychecks; his mother buys a seat at a million-dollar Mar-a-Lago dinner; less than three weeks later, pardoned. He never served a day. Contractors convicted of killing 14 Iraqi civilians, including two children, pardoned. A man who attacked police on January 6th, pardoned. Then he pleaded guilty to soliciting what he thought was a 15-year-old girl for sex and walked free.”
Newsom also mentioned Trump’s pardoning of the former president of Honduras, Juan Orlando Hernández, and noted that he had “moved 400 tons of cocaine into American communities”.
“Donald Trump doesn’t oppose mercy; he sells it,” Newsom added. “If you’re poor, Black or Brown, you get the needle. If you’re rich and know Donald Trump, you get a pardon and a personal phone call.
“A justice department that prosecutes his enemies and pardons himself and his friends, that’s not law enforcement, that’s a protection racket,” Newsom also said of the president who criticized his clemency decisions. “We need foundational federal reform starting with this: no president can ever again turn the power of mercy into a business.”
Trump brings families of violent crime victims to Department of Justice to rail against Newsom's commutations
Donald Trump just finished speaking at the Department of Justice in Washington DC.
In his remarks, the president, whose first intervention in public policy was a 1989 open letter calling for five young men of color who were wrongly accused of the brutal rape of a white female jogger in New York City’s Central Park to be executed, devoted his remarks to railing against California governor Gavin Newsom’s decision to commute the death sentences of hundreds of California inmates this month.
As he did during all three of his campaigns for the presidency, Trump spoke in graphic terms about violent crime, dwelling on gruesome details of the murders carried out by the California inmates – who will remain in prison without the possibility of parole.
Trump also invited the families of some victims to share their outrage at what seemed more like a campaign event than a governing one, given that the federal government has no power to force states to carry out death sentences for state crimes.
Rather than announce some specific action that his administration planned to take in response to Newsom’s clemency decisions, Trump instead said that he would direct the Department of Justice to act more quickly to execute federal inmates.
“I will sign a presidential directive ordering the Department of Justice to seek the death penalty for child murderers, as well as rapists and human traffickers who kill their victims. My order also directs the attorney general to expedite the execution of federal death sentences. Once juries have spoken, capital punishment will be swift and certain,” Trump said.
The event seemed to underscore what a White House directed by Stephen Miller, the new chief of staff, is likely to look like for the next two years. Miller, who wrote many of Trump’s speeches during his campaigns, is, like his boss, intensely focused on the idea of using violent crime as a political issue, by suggesting that Democrats are soft on crime and will fail to keep Americans safe.
Trump says Newsom's commutations for death row inmates 'should not be allowed to stand'
Speaking at the US Department of Justice on Monday, Donald Trump just said that the commutations of death row inmates by California’s governor, Gavin Newsom, “should not be allowed to stand”.
As he has throughout his time in public life, Trump focused his remarks on the most graphic details of violent crimes.
“Just days ago, the governor of California, Gavin Newsom, granted clemency in mercy to 548 of the most evil monsters on the face of the Earth,” Trump said.
“Among the serial killers he spared is Charles Eng, who tortured, enslaved and slaughtered at least 11 people, including babies, in a torture chamber and a remote cabin, as well as the South Side Slayer, who killed 14 women and enjoyed every moment of it”, the president added.
“To every parent, son, daughter, sibling or spouse here today whose loved one was brutally killed by those monsters, we love you, we support you, and we are with you and your quest for us. Really the the ultimate of final justice. We have to do this. We can’t let this happen. What happened to you, what happened to all of us, should not be allowed to stand”, Trump said.
He then invited some of the victims’ family members to speak about the gruesome crimes that were committed against their loved ones and criticize Newsom for commuting the death sentences in their cases.
