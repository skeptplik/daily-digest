Massive Entertainment's general manager Christoph Hartmann wants the Ubisoft business unit to go beyond shooters and become the "A24 of gaming."
Speaking to Variety, Hartmann said he wants Massive Entertainment to "be a label which really looks after the core gamer."
Massive Entertainment was previously Ubisoft's Creative House 2 business unit, housing Tom Clancy's The Division, Ghost Recon, Splinter Cell, and new IP like March of the Giants.
"What I mean by that is that it's almost easier to describe what we're not going to do – I'm not going to do a racing game. I'm not going to do sports. I'm not going to do a casual kids title. But could we do an RPG one day? Could we do an MMO? Could I maybe do even a strategy game? Sure, it's all coming down to the right talent. If we have the talent internally or something happens, then we bring it on."
He continued: "Really, my dream would be to become like the A24 of video gaming, like a place I describe with winning Oscars and still being commercially successful, definitely having impact on what they're doing."
Hartmann noted that as a smaller business unit within Ubisoft, Massive Entertainment has fewer management layers, allowing direct communication with team members. "There's so much to learn – that's one thing I like, and I believe smaller organisations, especially in entertainment, are very good."
Regarding Massive Entertainment's role within Ubisoft, Hartmann said it provides the "opportunity to have a set of titles and IPs [that] you can fit together."
"I can build a new business, but don’t have to start from zero. It's a dream come true for me. [Ubisoft is] a company which cares about games; it breathes games every day."
Hartmann also discussed his decision to leave Amazon's games business earlier this year.
He spent seven years at Amazon and noted the company reduced its focus on games, cancelling the Lord of the Rings MMO and shutting down Glowmade's online multiplayer King of Meat after last year's cuts.
In August, Amazon further reduced its involvement in MMOs by transferring operations of Throne and Liberty and Lost Art to NC and Smilegate.
"Games just can't be their prime focus right now; there's so much going on. But for me, it's the prime focus. I'm a content person, I'm not a tech person. I love developing and publishing games. I did that for 30 years, and I'm passionate about it. I felt I needed to go to a place again where it's absolutely at the centre of everything. And Ubisoft definitely is one of those places. It's still fully focused on games."
Last week, Ubisoft reported H1 financial results, with net bookings down 16.5% year-on-year.
Also included in the results was the merger of Creative Houses 3 and 5, focused on live service and casual games, to "leverage synergies between Live experiences across platforms." Ubisoft stated its Creative House structure is now fully in place.
It also established Motherbrain, a subsidiary focused on AI-driven "gameplay innovation", as well as an unnamed dedicated business unit to "maximise the value of brands not included in a Creative House with an agile and investment-light model."
