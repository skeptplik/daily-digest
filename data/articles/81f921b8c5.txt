Sales of a baby sleeping bag have been halted in the UK by fashion retail chain Next, after a new mum raised the alarm over its safety.
Emma Hunter, 29 from Norwich, contacted BBC Your Voice to say she realised the neck hole was far too big - meaning her daughter Rosie could potentially wriggle down, burying her face and risking suffocation.
Emma recalls: "I woke up my husband and we both agreed there was no way we could put Rosie in it."
Next says its own-brand, 2.5 tog pink floral sleep bag with removable sleeves that comes in three sizes, has been removed from the website and stores and a product recall is under way.
Next will contact and advise customers who purchased the item from them.
A spokesperson for Next said: "We can confirm the item has been removed from our website and all stores. A product recall is in place, and we are contacting all customers who purchased the item directly to advise them of the appropriate next steps."
Emma says she started doing her own research that same night she realised the sleeping bag was unsuitable for Rosie.
Although the product had a label stating that it met British standards, Emma discovered that the maximum neck circumference for that size of bag should be 32cm. When she measured the bag, the neck hole was 36cm.
According to the British Retail Consortium (BRC), a significant hazard arises if a child slips through the neck opening of a sleep bag and the material covers the child's nose and mouth.
They state that "suffocation can occur and may lead to the brain becoming starved of oxygen, which may result in asphyxia and potential brain damage or fatality."
To limit this hazard, the EN 16781 standard specifies a maximum and minimum size for the neck opening circumference in relation to the child's height.
For example, a sleep bag designed for a child 50 cm to 65 cm tall should have a minimum neck circumference of 28 cm and a maximum of 32 cm.
Rosie, who is now three and a half months old, measured 59cm at the time the bag was bought.
After flagging these issues to the company, Emma says she was surprised by their initial reaction.
"They seemed quite laissez faire about it and just offered me a refund, but I said 'it's dangerous, surely you shouldn't be selling it any more and there should be a product recall?'"
Emma was told by Next that they were looking into it and that their product team was examining the bag and would feed back.
Check the gap around the neck
Emma's older brother died of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) as a baby so the risk to babies from suffocation is something close to her heart.
She told the BBC: "I am more aware of this because of what happened to my brother and my family, but it shouldn't take an infant mortality to see change.
"They didn't have any sense of urgency about it at all and I expected a big high street store like that to have a certain standard. You put your trust into these retailers.
"How can a product have a label on it saying it met the British standard, when it clearly didn't?"
Ruth Watts, who is a qualified health visitor in England, says that babies will differ in terms of size and height, despite their age.
But if a sleeping bag covers the chin or face in any way it should not be used.
"The best thing to do is check the gap around the neck. If you can fit one to two fingers around the neck then that's safe. If it's any tighter or looser than that, then you shouldn't use it," she recommends.
The Lullaby Trust is a UK charity that works to prevent sudden, unexpected infant deaths, including SIDS.
CEO Jenny Ward told BBC News that the charity could not comment on the safety of specific products, since it does not carry out independent product safety testing.
But she encourages families to check that any product they use has passed the necessary safety checks and meets British Safety Standards.
She said: "Remember to follow the manufacturer's instructions and if a family is worried about any product we would say to stop using it and contact the manufacturer.
"We support the Office for Product Safety and Standards' recent warning on certain baby sleep bags and would urge families to ensure a sleep bag fits well before they use it."
