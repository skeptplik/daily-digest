In short:
Australian star Cameron Smith is keen on a return to the PGA Tour after four years with LIV Golf.
LIV folded earlier this year and Smith has joined the DP World Tour on an invitational basis.
What's next?
Smith will play in the Australian PGA Championship in Sydney this week.
Moving quickly on after LIV Golf's demise, Cameron Smith has revealed a burning desire to return to the PGA Tour as his Australian peers welcome the end to the sport's bitter civil war.
With LIV filing for bankruptcy and being liquidated, Smith has joined the DP World Tour on an invite basis, but has no full-time status.
But for the first time since joining the Saudi-backed league a month after winning the 2022 British Open, Smith conceded he hoped to find a permanent home back on the PGA Tour.
"I guess that's the path. That's where I want to be now for sure," the one-time world number two said ahead of this week's Australian PGA Championship in Sydney.
"DP's given us a chance to kind of fulfil that. We'll see how that goes. There's plenty of good players out there and plenty of other guys that want to do the same thing, so it's going to be difficult.
"But for sure that's where I want to play."
Only the top 10 from the DP World Tour's season-long points race qualify for the PGA Tour.
But Smith can greatly enhance his chances of getting back to the US big time by at least gaining a full DP World Tour card by winning the PGA Championship, next week's Australian Open in Melbourne or topping the summer-long Australasian order of merit.
At the very least his intention to return to the PGA Tour is a major revelation.
In January, Smith flatly rejected an offer to come back under strict guidelines, insisting he had "no" temptation to follow Brooks Koepka and make a $5 million charity donation.
While he remains disappointed at how LIV collapsed, the 33-year-old ex-Ripper GC captain is relieved a resolution has finally come so he can open a new chapter in his decorated career.
"There were lots of rumours floating around throughout the year. There was a lot of uncertainty and then in a way it was good to just like get the noise over and done with," Smith said, maintaining he has no regrets about ever joining LIV.
"It was a big move to go LIV at the start for sure, and I think everyone that was close to me and all my friends saw why I was doing it and why I made the decision.
"So I didn't lose many friends over it — I didn't lose any friends over it, really. I still talk to the same people, I still have the same conversations. I've always tried to do the right thing."
Compatriots Min Woo Lee and fellow major champion Adam Scott said they'd love to see Smith back excelling in the States again.
"He's a good friend and we want to see a short-game [star] around the PGA Tour, so it'll be very exciting to see him play on the PGA Tour again," Lee said on Tuesday.
"Whenever we get Aussies on the tour, it's a good time. When we see each other, it doesn't seem like we've missed a day and it's nice.
"Hopefully the golfing world is coming back together. Even just yesterday I was in the locker room with Cam and it's just 'welcome back'.
"I don't pay attention too much to it — (the LIV rivalry) but, yeah, hopefully it all comes back together and we're all happy."
Scott dismissed the notion that Smith couldn't be a success again in America after losing his mojo on the so-called less competitive LIV league.
"Cam's incredibly talented and hell adjust to his new environment and he'll be just fine," Scott said.
"There's no doubt about it."
