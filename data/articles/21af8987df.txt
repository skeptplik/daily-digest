The trilateral meeting between U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, China, on November 19, 2026 is in focus for its implications on the geopolitics of the world.
The three leaders are meeting on the sidelines of the APEC Summit and amidst the looming shadow of the Russia-Ukraine war that has now waged for more than four years.
The Xi-Putin-Jinping meet is being compared with the Yalta Conference of 1945, which led to the establishment of four quarters of post-Nazi Germany and laid the foundations of the United Nations.
What was the 1945 Yalta Conference?
The Yalta Conference is the term given to a World War II meeting held in Yalta, Crimea from February 4 to February 11, 1945.
Former U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt, U.K. PM Winston Churchill, and Soviet Premier Joseph Stalin, the three leaders known as the “Big Three” met to discuss a plan to defeat Germany’s Nazi powers and organize the future of post-war Europe.
Statement by the Big Three
The Yalta Conference also decided the course of action to be taken against the Nazi Party in Germany.
A news report, published in The Hindu, from February 13, 1945, said that “the three leaders have agreed on a common policy of enforcing unconditional surrender terms which are to be imposed on Germany.”
“War criminals are to be brought to justice. The Nazi Party, its laws and institutions are to be wiped out,” said the report.
Heavy post war damages from Germany
The Yalta Conference pressed for large sums of post-war damages to be exacted from Germany, and it was decided that half of it would go to the Soviet Union.
However, the July 1945 Potsdam conference in Brandenburg, marked a conflict of interest. According to a 2015 analysis by The Hindu, Roosevelt agreed to the demands, but U.S. President Truman and his Secretary of State, James Byrnes, had other ideas. They wanted to avoid another situation like that of the Treaty of Versailles, where Germany’s damage payments post World War I led to the collapse of its economy and gave rise to the Nazis.
Foundation of the United Nations
The Yalta Conference of 1945 laid the foundation for a new world body, and a conference of the United Nations was held in San Francisco, beginning in April 1945.
The meeting envisaged the setting up of two bodies — a representative group on which all member-nations would have places, and an executive authority to be known as the Security Council. The latter was to consist of the U.K., France, the U.S., Soviet Russia, and China - as permanent members and six small countries which would take turns to be members from time to time.
The Security Council was to be the most powerful wing of the new world body. It was then natural for every country, interested in its own unimpaired sovereignty, to ensure that the Council could not apply sanctions against a nation against that nation’s desire. Russia argued that, having agreed not to take any action except on the basis of unanimity, the Allies, who were a party to the unanimity principle at Yalta, were estopped from even discussing this subject, on which clearly there was not only no unanimity, but serious differences.
Ukraine fears rehash of Yalta; Russia dismisses comparison
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said in October 2026 that “we really don’t want this to turn into a ‘Yalta 2,3,4” with a redistribution of influence, signalling the unease over the Xi-Putin-Trump summit and its implications on the Russia-Ukraine war.
The Kremlin has also dismissed comparisons between the trilateral and Yalta as “games of political scientists”.
Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov rejected any such notion, saying at least two participants, Russia and China, stood for a “multi-polar world order” with no decisions imposed on other nations.
“To say that someone or other intends to decide the fate of the world, this is definitely not Russia, definitely not China. We don’t want anyone to decide our fate and will never try to decide the fate of others,” Mr Peskov added.
