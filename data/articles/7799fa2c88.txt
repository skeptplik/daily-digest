The Bank of Japan’s indicator for more fundamental inflation accelerated to the highest level in three years, supporting the case for another rate hike.
Consumer prices excluding fresh food and special factors advanced 3.5% in October from a year earlier, the fastest pace since September 2023, after it quickened to 3.2% in the previous month, according to a BOJ report Wednesday.
The result is likely to strengthen already solid market expectations that interest rates will be raised again next month, given mounting evidence that increases in the cost of living are here to stay. Earlier Wednesday, data showed service prices among businesses rose by the most in 36 years.
The BOJ is widely expected to hike its benchmark rate when it concludes its next policy meeting on Dec. 18. Pricing in overnight index swaps indicate investors see around a 70% chance that an increase in borrowing costs will happen.
Last month Gov. Kazuo Ueda’s board said that underlying inflation has reached its 2% target and the job now is to keep it around the current level. The sharp acceleration in Wednesday’s data signals how difficult that task could become for the central bank.
The BOJ’s more fundamental data for inflation is clearly higher than the government’s inflation data because the latter doesn’t take out the impact from temporary government steps to ease the cost of living. The BOJ’s dataset excludes special factors including subsidies for gasoline and utilities.
Last week a report from the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications showed that consumer prices excluding fresh food increased 2.2% in October.
