U.K. Prime Minister Andy Burnham is expected to agree new terms for Britain’s relationship with the E.U. as he meets with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen in Brussels on November 20 at a Summit meeting. Mr Burnham has said in recent weeks that Brexit had caused “more harm than good”.
The two sides are expected to announce a scheme for 100,000 18-32 year olds to live and work across each other’s borders. In addition, they are working on closer ties in food and drink and linking the carbon trading schemes of the U.K. and the EU, as per reports.
“There’s the possibility of lower food prices, I can’t turn my back on that,” Mr Burnham said.
The summit is expected to announce the start of a dialogue on migration — including returns of irregular migrants to third countries.
The deal is a significant step to closer ties but is not expected to be the post-Brexit “reset” with Brussels that the Labour Party has sought . Mr Burnham, who is taking forward some of the discussions held with Brussels by his predecessor Keir Starmer, has committed to keeping to the red lines outlined in the Labour 2024 manifesto for the duration of this parliament (2029).
These red lines include not joining the bloc, nor its customs union and single market, and not allowing the “free movement” of people across UK-EU borders. However, Mr Burnham has said the U.K. would have to decide on its long-term options for its ties with the E.U. — including rejoining the E.U. The nature of the future relationship between the U.K. and E.U. is likely to be a campaign issue when Britain approaches its next general election.
“Business across the country have been clear, our current deal with the EU is not good enough,” said UK minister for EU relations, Hamish Falconer.
Mr Burnham had earlier said he would present legislation to the U.K. Parliament next week on this week’s agreements with Brussels. The U.K. will need harmonize some of its laws with the E.U.’s (i.e., follow them) to operationalize any deal on agriculture for instance.
