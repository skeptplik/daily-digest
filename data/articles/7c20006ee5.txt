This isn't a review.
Grand Theft Auto 6 is not on PC so I can't review it. But somehow I, a member of PC Gamer, got access to GTA 6 before it launched. For the past two weeks I've secretly sunk approximately 100 hours in it (on something called a PlayStation 5) and now, finally, I can talk about it. What a relief.
So this isn't a review, but I can give a verdict of sorts:
It's (mostly) great. But it will be (mostly) so much greater when it comes to PC.
The world is even bigger than I expected
I guess I shouldn't be surprised by just how massive and detailed the world is. It's Rockstar: huge open worlds is what they do, and they've had a decade to do it. But I was stunned when I started playing, and a hundred hours later, I'm honestly still pretty stunned. Rockstar said GTA 6's map is twice as big as GTA 5's map, but it feels twice as big as "twice as big" should feel. It's enormous.
I'm constantly impressed by the macro and micro of it all. I can step back and take in the sheer size and scale of Leonida: the archipelago representing the Florida keys to the south, the vast wetlands standing in for the Everglades, the dense neighborhoods and shining skyscrapers of Vice City, the miles of beaches and forests and industrial areas and state parks. And I can zoom in and admire the tiny details: the carefully placed clutter and photos and belongings in a stranger's apartment, the accurate mating rituals of two great blue herons in the wild, or the font choice on a printout pinned to the wall of an office cubicle instructing employees on breakroom etiquette.
I've lost hours doing nothing but wandering around the world, picking a point in the distance that looked interesting and getting distracted by a dozen other things along the way. Exploration is always rewarding: Sometimes, because I find one of hundreds of collectibles hidden throughout the map, but usually because I happen upon an interesting landmark, spot an animal I haven't seen before, stumble into a mysterious scene or sight (I have found many yet haven't unraveled any of them yet), or have a chance encounter with the seemingly inexhaustible supply of "Florida Man"-style weirdos that populate Leonida.

My only issue with the world is that present day Miami doesn't hold a lot of interest for me, pop-culture-wise. New York and Los Angeles are fascinating in any era, and I loved the neon-soaked '80s Vice City from Rockstar's game. But GTA 6's Vice City doesn't quite stand up against Liberty City or Los Santos. I'm not saying it's a bad place to spend time: it's an incredible facsimile of the real Miami, with an intense amount of detail and attention paid to the diversity of the city, like its recreation of Little Havana (called Little Cuba here). But it doesn't grab me the way the cities in the last two GTA games did.
Jason and Lucia are the best part
It's stressful being in a new relationship. What if my partner texts me and I don't respond right away? Am I giving them enough attention, or too much attention? If they want to hang out, can I say no if I'm busy doing something else? It's been quite a while since I dated IRL, but the early hours of getting to know Jason and Lucia gave me those early relationship jitters I remember so well.
There are lots of systems in GTA 6 for keeping Jason and Lucia's partnership strong: talking to each other, showing affection, doing activities together, and even committing crimes together will improve their bond. That makes it a little tense when one character wants to do something and you (as the other character) don't.
One day as I was playing as Jason, Lucia texted me because she wanted to talk about our financial future. But I was on a speedboat halfway across the map and I had just spotted what I suspect was one of Florida's many cryptids (which, after 100 hours, I still cannot confirm is actually in the game or not). Was I really supposed to drop everything I was doing?
I dropped everything I was doing. Instead of becoming a renowned cryptid investigator, Lucia and I went to the bank to set up a savings account. Was I happy about it? Hell no. I was annoyed. But that felt real to me—not the cryptid part, but the idea of being responsible to your partner. While playing them, both individually and together, I wanted to be a good partner, and sometimes that meant glumly turning around my stolen speedboat and having a frank talk about money. It's pretty cool that a game about robbing banks and blowing up police helicopters could make me want to be a good boyfriend.
The two of them are a satisfying couple to play in other respects, too. When knocking over a jewelry store or check-cashing place (cute date idea), your partner can perform crowd control, keeping security guards and employees docile, while you smash open display cases, empty cash drawers, and crack the safe in the back room. During story missions, the duo is often split up and you bounce between their perspectives, with Jason shooting his way through a crowd of goons in an office tower to Lucia fighting hand-to-hand several floors above.
But the best moments as a duo are when you're coming up with your own ways to play them as a couple. After trying to rip off narcotics from a gang stronghold, I got into a heavy gunfight in a narrow alleyway, and with Lucia on one side and Jason on the other, I was able to fight my way out. As Lucia I could cover Jason as he advanced, and as Jason I could get better angles on the thugs trying to bring Lucia down. It was fluid, fun, and not part of any main mission, just an organic bit of co-op play with me swapping between them as needed. This was the sort of thing that GTA 5 could only pull off in heavily-scripted story missions with its three characters, but here you really are a dynamic duo.
Even more fun? Just hanging out together as a couple. You can take them both to a store to shop for clothes, then compliment each other on their new looks. Or go on a date and play minigolf while gently trash-talking each other. (Tall Jason to short Lucia: "So, is this, like, regular-sized golf to you?") Hit the night clubs and do some dancing. Buy two ice cream cones, hand one to your partner, and enjoy them together. Even just lying in bed, side-by-side, and scrolling your phones is a warm and fuzzy activity amidst all the crime and bloodshed. It's good stuff. I had a really wonderful two weeks of ignoring my real life partner while spending quality time with these two.
I'm glad there's so much Red Dead DNA in it
Jason and Lucia are a good match for the more deliberate gameplay of GTA 6. Taking a page from Red Dead Redemption 2, your car is basically now your horse: not just a ride, but a gun locker. No longer can you keep a full arsenal in your weapon wheel or unholster a grenade launcher from your back pocket.
The pair can conceal two handguns in their pants, but "long" guns like shotties and rifles are visible on your back or in your hands. This matters: Even the locals of the gun-friendly Leonida will notice and sometimes panic at the sight of a heavily armed maniac, drawing outright alarm from security guards and cops.
So you'll have to stash those big guns in your car until you need them, along with a few changes of clothes because witnesses to your crimes will tell the cops what you're wearing. This makes even a quick, mindless smash-and-grab feel like a mission with phases and choices: scope it out, plan your moves and escape route, grab your gear, and then afterwards, stow your guns and change clothes once the cops are out of sight. When things go sideways, it can be a lot of fun to scramble your way out of the heat: dumping your big guns in an alleyway and running into a clothing shop to buy some new duds, a hat, and sunglasses, so you don't match the description the cops are looking for.
And while you can't play entirely non-lethally, you can at least play less-lethally. Using your focus meter (bullet time) will highlight your enemies' elbows, knees, hands, shoulders, and other non-fatal places to deposit a bullet, so you can incapacitate them instead of just headshotting them if that's how you want to roll. I did a lot of that in my playthrough, partly to keep Jason and Lucia feeling less like murdering psychopaths so that I could continue to like them as characters, but also because it added an extra challenge: aiming carefully at joints instead of just popping lead in every dome I see.
Playing less lethally is another way GTA 6 makes the near-constant violence a bit less mindless, though trust me, my body count was still pretty high by the end of the main story. It's hard to non-lethally shoot down a helicopter with a grenade launcher from the back of a speedboat.
There's a few things I didn't love
I don't love everything about GTA 6. The big action setpieces, while technically impressive, usually wind up feeling pretty samey: pull a big score, everything goes wrong, 100 guys show up, shoot all of them in the knees, then drive away, 100 cars show up, shoot all of them them in the tires (tires are a car's knees), escape. Rockstar missions are still Rockstar missions.
As with RDR2, these moments of front-page bombast contradict the fantasy of small-time outlaws struggling to get ahead, and GTA 6 goes full ridiculous with Fast and Furious-style mayhem. I'm not saying those sequences aren't entertaining, they just clash with the story of a couple of star-crossed crooks trying to save up enough money to get out of the game. And after you've shot your hundredth kneecap of the night, it gets a bit tiresome.
As for the story and characters? It's Rockstar, so some of it is genuinely good but a lot of it is exaggerated bluster. Lucia has some interactions with a family member that feel pretty real and there's a duo of rappers who are fun to spend time with, but I found the majority of the supporting characters either too grating or one-dimensional to care much about. Luckily, the writing and performances of Jason and Lucia make up for that. I enjoyed spending time with them. They had some raw moments that landed well. I felt feelings. I came to care about this couple.
If this is sounding too grown up, don't worry. There's a subplot about furries. A naked guy got into my car and took a sex toy out of his butt. There's a mission where you have to handle multiple dildos. The rideshare app's logo looks like a penis. All the actual penises also look like a penis, too. Rockstar is still Rockstar.
I'm ready for another 100 hours
Now that I'm done with the main story, the real game can begin: just having fun in that big, beautiful sandbox. And I've got lots left to do: while I mentioned minigolf, there are actual, entire golf courses, too (this is Florida, remember).
I've done a bit of scuba diving for treasure but still have dozens of shipwrecks to discover. I don't find hunting much fun—hitscan sniper rifles aren't satisfying to use—but I sure like spotting new animals to check off my list, and there are over 200 of them to find. I've only caught one fish. I haven't done any drone races or stunt flying or base jumping or been to a shooting range. There are several in-game videogames I still have to play and a bunch of in-game TV shows to check out. I've somehow hardly gotten anything done in the two weeks I've been playing.
And this is deeply embarrassing, but I barely own any cars! I'm still mainly driving the muscle car I got very early in the game. It's not that there's not a lot to choose from—there are hundreds of autos to theft—it's that the handling and acceleration and physics of every car is different. That's great, obviously, but I quickly got used to driving a few cars and didn't want to have to learn the quirks of more vehicles while fleeing from the cops. Now that the story is behind me, though, it's finally time to start collecting.
How it stacks up against Rockstar's other games
I'm still trying to decide where I'd place GTA 6 among Rockstar's other games. RDR2 remains my favorite, followed by Red Dead Redemption, but do I like GTA 6 more than GTA 5? Ultimately, yeah, I just decided while writing this: I do. I like Jason and Lucia more than the three crooks from GTA 5, and maybe even more than I liked Niko Bellic in GTA 4. It's not entirely down to their personalities and performances, but also the systems Rockstar created to let me play as a couple, that make me feel like I had a co-op partner along for the ride.
In a game like Skyrim where I can have followers, I almost always play solo. But in GTA 6, I found myself calling my partner to meet up almost every time I went out exploring. The game is better when they're together.
I wish we had some idea of when GTA 6 was coming to PC because after playing almost 100 hours on PS5, I'm desperate to get it on my desktop. I can't wait to see what modders can do with this thing (especially when they fix the weird cheat restrictions). As a PC gamer, I'm dying to aim at people's knees with my mouse instead of a thumbstick. And I'm especially curious to see what Rockstar has planned for an online mode (which they have said nothing about), and how FiveM, which Rockstar now owns, will tie into it. That big, beautiful world will only be better on PC where it belongs.
