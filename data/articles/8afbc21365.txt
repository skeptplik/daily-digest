Long before Dario Amodei butted heads with the Pentagon over whether the U.S. military could use Anthropic’s models for mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons, he was fighting another battle.
At the California Institute of Technology in 2003, Amodei called on his classmates to join him in protesting the U.S. invasion of Iraq, writing in an op-ed that citizen-scientists should not be “reduced to amoral technicians who run the machines of war.”
On Thursday, as 43-year-old Amodei led his startup to an initial public offering that raised a record $81 billion and valued it at about $1.5 trillion, the listing cemented his transformation from an idealistic physics student into one of the most powerful custodians of the artificial intelligence age.
