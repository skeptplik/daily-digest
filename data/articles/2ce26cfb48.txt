Hegseth, wife and aides spark scolding from guard at Tomb of the Unknown Soldier
Pentagon spokesperson said U.S. army sentinel 'correctly and respectfully' called for proper decorum
A soldier guarding the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at Arlington National Cemetery issued a verbal reprimand to a group of advisers and staffers with U.S. War Secretary Pete Hegseth following a Veterans Day ceremony.
Video shows Hegseth taking photos on the empty steps in front of the tomb with his wife, Jennifer, chief of staff Ricky Buria, deputy chief of staff Patrick Weaver and senior adviser Tami Radabaugh.
As the group broke from taking photos, a loud laugh could be heard, and the soldier guarding the tomb shouted that "all visitors will maintain an atmosphere of silence and respect at all times."
Sean Parnell, the Pentagon's top spokesperson, said that "while commemorating this solemn day, a soldier correctly and respectfully reminded everyone to maintain decorum."
"The Secretary and the entire Department have always honoured — and will always honour — the sacrifices our military men and women have made to keep America safe," he added.
Incident follows near impeachment of Hegseth
The tomb is considered to be one of the most solemn and revered war memorials in the U.S., being the final resting place of unidentified soldiers from the First and Second World Wars and the Korean War. It has been guarded around the clock — rain or shine — by a soldier from the Army's 3rd Infantry Regiment, also known as "The Old Guard," since 1948.
Hegseth and his staff were at the tomb as part of a larger ceremony commemorating Veterans Day that was also attended by U.S. President Donald Trump and Vice-President JD Vance.
The incident comes a day after Hegseth narrowly survived an effort to impeach him by lawmakers in Congress.
The effort was led by Republican Rep. Thomas Massie and backed by Democrats. However, while Hegseth's critics were not successful in Tuesday's vote, Republicans may have only delayed efforts to impeach him. With Democrats set to take control of the House of Representatives and Senate in January, those efforts are likely to be renewed.
Hegseth slammed the effort on Tuesday at his first news conference in months, arguing that he had committed none of the high crimes required in the U.S. Constitution for his removal.
A core part of his argument included the assertion that criticisms of him and his handling of the U.S. military were tantamount to an indictment against the nation's service members and their successes.
"On the eve of Veterans Day, these resolutions tell every one of them that their service was a crime," Hegseth said Tuesday.
"This is not patriotism," he said. "This is sabotage."
Following the reprimand at Arlington National Cemetery on Wednesday, the reaction from Democrats was swift.
Rep. Gwen Moore said Hegseth demonstrated "his utter lack of fitness for his office."
"One day after narrowly avoiding impeachment, he shows his contempt for our men and women in uniform by disrespecting the sanctity of the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier," she wrote in a social media post.
It was not the first time members of Trump's team have caused controversy at Arlington National Cemetery.
During his campaign for a second term, Trump attended a ceremony in 2024 to honour U.S. forces killed in the Iraq and Afghanistan wars. A campaign staffer reportedly shoved a cemetery employee trying to prevent two people from filming and photographing the event.
Trump, who had been invited by troops' families, later shared a video on TikTok showing him at the cemetery with a voiceover blaming the Biden administration for the "disaster" of the Afghanistan withdrawal.
U.S. law prohibits campaign or election-related activities within national military cemeteries.
