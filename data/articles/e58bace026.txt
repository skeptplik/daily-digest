Former Constitutional Democratic Party of Japan leader Kenta Izumi has proposed a two-bloc political system in the country to achieve a change of government amid growing fragmentation in the opposition camp.
Izumi’s proposal comes as Japan moves away from a two-party system due to the weakening of opposition parties.
In a recent interview, Izumi suggested that the existence of one large opposition party would make it easier to map out a vision for running a government than if there are many small, weak parties.
However, he noted that a change of government would be “quite possible if more lawmakers aim to form an alternative bloc (to the ruling Liberal Democratic Party-led coalition) that is capable of running a government.”
“Even if a two-party system is not feasible, I hope there will be a two-bloc system,” Izumi said. “I will keep moving forward without giving up.”
After Izumi stepped down as CDP chief in 2024 and Komeito left the LDP-led ruling bloc last year, then CDP and Komeito lawmakers in the House of Representatives formed the Centrist Reform Alliance ahead of the Lower House election in February.
“I was one of those who sympathized with the CRA’s ideals,” Izumi said. However, the new party decided to disband after suffering a crushing defeat in the Lower House election.
Following the collapse of the CRA, former CDP lawmakers in the Lower House launched the Democratic Reform Party, but Izumi did not join and instead formed a group called Shishi no Kai in the Lower House.
The group’s name “reflects our resolve to play a new role by maneuvering around the political world,” Izumi said. “We hope to increase our allies by repeatedly communicating our vision.”
Recognizing that past main opposition parties faltered when their one-time election strategies failed, Izumi emphasized the importance of continuing efforts to earn trust.
