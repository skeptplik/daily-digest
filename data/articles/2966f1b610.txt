Former Bank of Japan Gov. Haruhiko Kuroda flagged the possibility that persistent inflation risks might prompt authorities to raise the benchmark interest rate above the so-called neutral rate, where policy is deemed to be neither stimulative nor restrictive.
“It all depends on inflation,” Kuroda said in an interview on Monday. “If inflation becomes more serious, then rate hikes will naturally have to accelerate as well, and rates may even have to rise above the neutral rate.”
Kuroda pointed to inflationary pressure stemming from U.S. President Donald Trump’s war with Iran, which is raising costs for Japan because of its reliance on imports of oil, raw materials and food.
