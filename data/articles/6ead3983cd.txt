UK studio Waugh Thistleton Architects is set to transform the Edwardian Westbrook Pavilion in Margate, UK, into an arts centre for British artist Tracey Emin with a cafe, public sauna and changing rooms.
“The project will bring a much-loved seafront building back into use, combining its original purpose with new spaces for art, culture and the local community,” said Waugh Thistleton Architects.
The studio recently won planning permission from the local council to convert the abandoned building, which Emin purchased in 2023, into a facility run by the Tracey Emin Foundation.
The project aims to return the building to its former use as a changing room for swimmers and as a tea room, when it originally opened in 1914.
“Originally opened in 1914, the building provided public changing facilities for sea-bathing and a tea room,” said the studio.
“Following storm damage in the Great North Sea Storm of 1953, subsequent alterations detracted from its original Edwardian character,” it continued.
“The renovated pavilion will accommodate artist studios, an art gallery, clubroom, cafe, sea-bathing changing facilities, a new sauna and refurbished public toilets, restoring its original role as a place for bathing and socialising by the sea.”
Along with making the pavilion a building used by the public again, the studio aims to return the building’s core to its Edwardian look by removing later additions. It will also add glazed facades to the wings on either side of the central pavilion.
The building will be topped by a rooftop terrace, while a public toilet on the site will be renovated.
“The design draws on the proportions and character of the original building, removing later additions and reinstating lost features such as the historic belvederes which top the central tower structures,” said the studio.
“The beachfront facade will see a new glazing system and colonnade to each of the building’s wings, with a new sheltered archway forming a new seating terrace at the building’s heart,” it continued.
“The design seeks to reference the original architecture while providing contemporary needs of a public building. Where original features are references, these are distinguishable architecturally.”
Emin is one of the UK’s best-known living artists. We recently produced a video of a landmark exhibition of her work at Tate Modern.
