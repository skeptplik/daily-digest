It's a sign of the precarious position of Australian Rules Football in Western Sydney that Ethan Matthews' father, Cameron, took to bribing kids to join his son at the Ingleburn Magpies.
"Definitely got lots of players across from softball at times, in rugby league, a few soccer," Ethan told ABC Sport.
"When we were younger, not too much, five, 10 bucks, but then as we started to get a bit older, we started to demand a bit more, so I probably got up to $25, $50 at times, depending on how talented the athlete was."
Bribing kids to play Aussie Rules in rugby league heartland gives some indication about why the GWS Giants are so keen to bring their Academy player through their door in Thursday night's AFL draft.
Drafting a gifted Aussie Rules player is important not just for the Giants' playing list but also for the message that they are still trying to sell 16 years down the track: that the club can build a local Aussie Rules culture so that more kids like Ethan Matthews take up the game and follow the Giants.
The job of winning the hearts and minds of rugby league and soccer fans in western Sydney is a constant work in progress for the Giants.
While the Giants made the Grand Final in 2019 only to get thrashed by Richmond, the impact of COVID the following year put a halt to the team's momentum that they're still struggling to recover from.
So, Matthews is much more than the most talented player to come out of western Sydney in the club's short history; he's the poster child for the region and what local boys and girls can achieve.
The Giants are expected to take Matthews with their pick nine, but there's every chance that he could be bid on prior to that, which would mean GWS would have to match the bid.
The local boy from the village of Appin, south of Campbelltown, would be just the fifth western Sydney local drafted to the Giants since the team entered the AFL in 2012 and the highest draft pick of those by a country mile.
When he's picked, it will be the culmination of a dream for the politely spoken 18-year-old who'll be in Melbourne on draft night.
"I just expected it to be a great get-together with all my family and friends," Matthews said.
"Just a moment to celebrate the journey. It's been a great, not a few years; it's been 10 years now of hard work and effort put in."
Matthews knows that there's every chance another club could snaffle him, but the Giants have given him enough reassurance that he's heading to Sydney Olympic Park come Friday morning.
"They've certainly told me as much as they can; they would love to have me," he said.
"I'm not going to lie and say they aren't my preferred team to go to; [I've] been there since I was 12 and love the Giants very much."
The journey began even earlier at just four when his footy-loving dad and mad Collingwood supporter took him down to join the Ingleburn Magpies Auskick side.
From there, he progressed through to the Giants' development side in the under-12s to the Academy at 16 and then this year played a starring role for the Giants Talent League side and then the Allies in the National Under-18s Championships.
The highlight for the 186cm left-footer was a best-on-ground game against Vic Country, where he racked up plenty of disposals plus nine score involvements, six tackles and eight marks playing in the midfield.
The highlights of that game were a recruiter's dream as Matthews glided around the field dodging tackles and delivering pinpoint left-foot passes to leading forwards.
"I like to think I'm quite a fundamental and skilful footballer; I like to use my Footy IQ," he said.
Unfortunately, Matthews injured his foot later in the Championships, which brought his season to an end, but not before he picked up the Hunter Harrison medal as the best player from the northern states' academy programs.
And while his close footy mates were following his progress closely thanks to Cameron's bribes, that wasn't the case for his other school friends who were wondering where he'd disappeared to.
"Not too many people, I guess, especially at school, had too much of an idea about a national champs game, and no-one knows where you've been or what you've done, and I've kind of liked that," he said.
"But yeah, all my close friends and all my mates that play footy certainly do know, and I'm lucky enough with Dad bribing people to come across, most of my friends have at some stage played footy with me in younger years and are very interested."
Once at the Giants, Matthews is determined to work hard and break into the team, which is coming off a disappointing 2026 season highlighted by injuries to key players.
"It's going to be very hard to crack into the AFL team," Matthews said.
"So, first [I've] just got to get into the side, but once I do, I reckon I would start on a wing or a half back, and then ultimately in a few years' time I see myself as a midfielder."
Matthews also sees himself as a role model to help grow the game in Western Sydney.
"It's an honour and a privilege, especially to represent this area," he said.
"I'd love to be a role model for many kids, especially out in this area, to keep striving and play footy, and have a dream, I think, being a role model out here would be something very special to me."
Come Thursday night, Matthews will almost certainly be decked in orange and charcoal, and while that's been his destiny for six years, it will mean permanently abandoning his childhood team and first love, Collingwood.
"I definitely love the Giants just as much, but yeah, [I'm a] Collingwood fan," he said.
"Dad signed me up before birth, didn't have much choice there."
