With a client roster that includes Lauren Conrad, Halle Berry, Lucy Hale and Jenna Dewan, Kristin Ess has spent more than three decades establishing herself as one of Hollywood’s most sought-after hairstylists. She entered the beauty business as a founder in January 2017, when she launched her namesake hair-care line exclusively at Target. (Ess is no longer with the company, but more on that in a moment.) Now, Ess is bringing something new to the retailer with Otherwise, a hair fragrance brand.
“I have been obsessed with fragrance just as long as I’ve been obsessed with hair,” the hairstylist tells Fashionista. “And so it just felt really natural to merge these two together.” She wanted to approach the category in a “reimagined way,” rethinking traditional hair fragrances, many of which rely on alcohol-based formulas that can leave strands feeling dry.
“I wanted it to be fragrance-first, but I wanted to have hair-care benefits,” Ess says. She chose a water-based, micellar-inspired formula to disperse fragrance while offering a lightweight (and less drying) alternative to conventional hair perfumes. The dual-phase blend (water and fragrance oil) is meant to be shaken up before spritzing.
The 14 scents retail for $25 each and were created to be mixed and matched so you can “become the perfumer in your own way,” Ess says. She was inspired by her own fragrance routine, in which she layers Escentric Molecules Molecule 01 on top of a coconut body lotion. “That coconut lotion I use is maybe [fragrant] an hour or two hours, but then the Molecule [01] is there, and there’s this middle time where they’re blending together,” she explains. “And then the coconut’s gone and really what’s left is the Molecule and the Iso E Super [a synthetic fragrance molecule with a warm, woody scent].”
It’s the same idea with Otherwise fragrances, she says, which have a “very obvious top note and something that’s going to dry down and be very long-lasting.” What about the middle note, you ask? Well, it’s “imaginary.”
“My mind drifts to, what does this look like?” she says. “I don’t know why, but I can’t focus on anything except for what visual story I would tell or what visual comes to mind when I’m smelling it.” Examples include Blackberry Orbit‘s “The Idea of Purple” and Oolong Overnight‘s “Lost in Conversation.” In other words, it’s the vibe.
Otherwise is a venture under Ess’s new brand development group and holding company, Starseed Beauty Brands, and is entirely self-funded. “It’s very scary obviously to go at that yourself, but I’m a big believer in making things that I’m super excited about and things that I know my clients would be excited about,” she says.
Her “gut first, data second” philosophy drives the brand, as well as a sense of joy. “We want things to be a little more fun,” she says. “We want to feel something, and it’s just, why shouldn’t we play in this space?”
Ess’s brand-building journey hasn’t always seemed so joyful. Her first brand at Target, Kristin Ess Hair, was created in partnership with brand incubator Maesa. In November 2022, she sued the company over allegations of breach of contract and a lack of creative control. The parties settled in February 2023, though the terms of the agreement were not disclosed publicly.
“I got to spread my wings really wide on that brand,” Ess says of her time with Kristin Ess Hair. “We had over 100 SKUs and it was such a unique opportunity at a specific time and what a once-in-a-lifetime moment.” But she no longer wants to try to solve every problem with one line that has numerous categories. “I love a niche brand,” she continues. “I love something that is very focused and that serves a purpose.”
Of course, she still wants Kristin Ess Hair to succeed and is “proud” of what she built a decade ago. “I think that if you ultimately want things to follow your vision all the way through, you have to maintain control in some way. But you also have to be ready to maintain control because that’s not for everyone,” she admits. “There was no world in which I would’ve been able to do it this way back then.”
Under Starseed Beauty Brands, Ess is also working on a “very specialized hair-care line” that will still “resonate with a lot of people,” she says. “I’ll never leave the hair-care space. I can’t.”
