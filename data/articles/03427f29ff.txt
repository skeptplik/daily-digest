Paris police officers sentenced to prison over 2020 beating of Black man
A Paris court on Friday sentenced three policemen to serve time over the 2020 beating of Black music producer Michel Zecler, a rare such ruling. The assault, which was captured on camera, sparked nationwide outrage and renewed scrutiny of police violence and racism in France.
A French court on Friday sentenced three policemen to serve time over the brutal beating of a Black music producer in 2020, in a rare such ruling in a police brutality case.
The first two were sentenced to five years in jail, including three and three-and-a-half years suspended, for the assault on Michel Zecler, whom the officers had accused of not wearing a face mask during the Covid-19 pandemic.
The third was sentenced to five years, four of which were suspended, and was to serve the one year with an ankle tag instead of behind bars.
The assault on Zecler, now 47, triggered renewed outrage in France over police violence, which victims and researchers say is systemic but authorities and law enforcement unions claim is limited to isolated cases.
Campaigners and rights groups saw the case as further evidence of what they see as the French police's systemic racism, in a country where they say ethnic minorities receive far harsher treatment than white people.
Police brutality has also been thrust back into the spotlight by the student-led protests in recent weeks, including after a 14-year-old lost most of his teeth when a police officer appeared to fire a grenade at him at very close range.
Police stopped Zecler outside his Paris studio on November 21, 2020, accusing him of failing to wear a mandatory face mask and suspecting him of cannabis use.
They pushed him into the entrance of his office and violently beat him in an assault recorded by his studio's security cameras.
The medical report noted craniofacial trauma, a wound to the lip and scalp, and a complete rupture of his biceps tendon, leaving him unable to perform his job for 45 days.
In their arrest report, the officers claimed the producer had hit them and tried to seize a truncheon, a version of events refuted by the camera footage that French media website Loopsider released several days later.
Few cases of alleged police brutality make it to criminal court in France, as most are dealt with internally.
In another case to go before a judge, a French policeman is to be tried over shooting dead 17-year-old Nahel Merzouk at point-blank range in June 2023 outside Paris. Video footage also contradicted the police's version of events in that incident, sparking nationwide protests.
Guilty verdicts in alleged police brutality cases are rare.
In 2024, a judge gave suspended jail sentences to three officers who inflicted irreversible rectal injuries on a black man, Theo Luhaka, during a stop-and-search in 2017.
Young men "perceived as Arab, black or from North Africa" are four times more likely to be stopped than the rest of the population, according to a report last year by France's rights chief.
They are 12 times more likely to be subjected to more severe measures, including being frisked during checks, it said after a survey of more than 5,000 people.
