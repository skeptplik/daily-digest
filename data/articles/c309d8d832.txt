Fri, 30 Oct, 7:15 pm, Final score, Cats 88, Bulldogs 20
In short:
Melbourne is heading to its first AFLW grand final in four years after defeating Carlton 75-45.
North Melbourne has advanced to its fourth straight grand final after beating Sydney by 32 points at Princes Park.
What's next?
The Roos and the Demons will face off in the decider at Ikon Park this Friday.
Back in the AFLW grand final for the first time in four years, Melbourne are determined to embrace every bit of the lead-in to a blockbuster decider against two-time defending premiers North Melbourne.
After North beat a gritty Sydney by 32 points to reach a fourth straight decider, Melbourne overcame a stern challenge from Carlton to prevail 10.15 (75) to 6.9 (45) in front of 8,037 fans at Ikon Park on Saturday.
The preliminary final victory under first-year coach Tom Wilson sets up arguably the most hotly-anticipated grand final in years, next Friday night at the same venue.
The Demons and Kangaroos have never faced off in a decider but North only beat Melbourne, then playing under 2022 premiership coach Mick Stinear, by 10 points in last year's preliminary final.
North beat Melbourne by 31 in round seven but Melbourne will feel primed to deny Darren Crocker's Kangaroos an unprecedented three-peat after a high-pressure win in hot conditions.
"I couldn't be more positive and optimistic about what this week looks like,"
Wilson said.
"We're just going to enjoy every second of it because we worked extremely hard to get here and I don't want the girls to let that go by.
"I want them to sit in it and enjoy what comes with the week because they deserve to."
Midfield double act Olivia Purcell (31 disposals) and Tyla Hanks (20) were particularly influential.
"I'm extremely biased, but they're superstars in my eyes," Wilson said.
Defender Maeve Chaplin (27 disposals, 15 intercepts) was exceptional, skipper Kate Hore (20 disposals) and Megan Fitzsimon kicked two goals apiece while Tayla Harris (nine marks) made her presence felt.
"Really proud of the playing group. The way they went about it today was really professional," Wilson said.
"Difficult conditions, quite hot and Carlton threw a few punches.
"So for us to be able to absorb their throws … just really pleased for the girls and the playing group for the work that they've done."
Carlton skipper Abbie McKay (27 disposals) fought hard with support from Irish livewire Erone Fitzpatrick (two goals), Claudia Whitfort (17 disposals) and Lila Keck (two goals).
Down back, Harriet Cordner and the retiring Gab Pound never gave up as Mathew Buck's Blues suffered a second straight preliminary final exit.
"Disappointed as a group, of course," Buck said.
"But when we get time to reflect post this, it'll be a chance to reflect back on the whole season and be proud of another prelim that we've made it to.
"To fall short is disappointing, and we've got lots of stuff we still need to improve on, but achievement of itself to make it."
Melbourne led by six at quarter-time, then at the start of the second quarter, Carlton jagged two quick goals and took a seven-point lead.
But Melbourne wrested the contest back their way, peppering the goal until Shelley Heath-Barker drilled a terrific finish to restore the Demons' lead.
They wouldn't lose it again.
The Demons led by eight at half-time then seized control with a four-goal third quarter, with Maggie Mahony toe-poking home just before the siren to deliver a 26-point lead at the final change.
Poppy Scholz gave the Blues a lifeline at the start of the final quarter but the Demons romped to victory.
Buck conceded the Blues, who are set to again target GWS defender Cambridge McCormick, were currently five or six goals off the competition's best — North and Melbourne.
"We look forward to hitting the draft again this year and our trade period as well," Buck said.
"Any way we can do it (improve), we'll look to achieve it."
Roos survive Swans scare
North Melbourne is within touching distance of an AFLW premiership "three-peat" after fighting out a 32-point victory over a Chloe Molloy-inspired Sydney in their preliminary final.
Ash Riddell and veteran Emma Kearney were centre stage as the Kangaroos kicked six goals clear in the first half before holding off a brave Swans challenge in the 13.5 (83) to 8.3 (51) triumph at Princes Park on Saturday.
Darren Crocker's all-conquering side is into a fourth consecutive grand final, with the chance to complete the first flag hat-trick in the history of the women's competition.
But there is some concern over ruck Kim Rennie, who spent some time on the turf in the final quarter and eventually hobbled off with what appeared to be a right knee injury.
Riddell (29 disposals) and Eilish Sheerin (22) were outstanding, while superstar Jasmine Garner finished with three goals from 18 disposals despite attention from Swans tagger Tanya Kennedy.
Tahlia Randall and Blaithin Bogue kicked two each for the Kangaroos.
Kearney (15 disposals) was dependable as always on managed minutes in her return from a hamstring injury, and got under her opponents' skin in trademark fashion.
Molloy (three goals) did her best to lift Sydney, sparking a second-quarter fightback, but North had a greater number of contributors across the ground.
Laura Gardner (25 disposals), Zippy Fish (18) and Cynthia Hamilton (two goals) also fought hard for the gallant Swans.
A global Ticketmaster outage caused long queues for frustrated fans outside the ground, but those lucky enough to get in early witnessed a slice of history.
Hamilton opened the scoring for Sydney with the fastest AFLW goal, finishing well on the run just 21 seconds into the contest.
But Garner responded within a minute, and the opening term belonged to North, who led 4.2 to 2.1 at the first break.
Garner added her second early in the second quarter and it opened the floodgates, with the Kangaroos piling on another four consecutive goals.
Molloy breathed life back into the contest with three of Sydney's four goals in a nine-minute burst before half-time.
The Swans' superstar caught both Riddell and Kearney holding the ball, and kicked team-lifting goals against the tide.
The North duo demonstrably disputed the decisions with umpires but were not penalised further for dissent.
Kearney also sparked a melee when she riled her opponents by sarcastically joining in the Swans' celebrations.
Molloy's third major cut what was a 36-point margin back to 18 by half-time, with North leading 9.2 to 6.2 at the main break.
It was one goal apiece in a tight third quarter and the Swans got within a couple of kicks when Maddie Quinn (two goals) kicked the first major of the final term.
But North held firm and Garner kicked the sealer when she snapped her third major with less than four minutes left on the clock.
