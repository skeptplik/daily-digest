Update, November 26, 2026: Ubisoft has issued a response to reports that teams are reportedly being asked to explain why they would or wouldn't use generative AI when pitching projects.
"We encourage our teams to explore where AI can be useful and bring value to their work. This doesn't mean that projects are required to use generative AI," the firm said in a statement to Vertical Slice. "Teams need to determine whether, and how, these technologies make sense for the game they want to make and the experience they want to create for players."
Original story, November 25, 2026: Ubisoft teams pitching new projects are reportedly being asked to explain how generative AI would help them make games faster, and if they don't plan to use it, they have to give a detailed reason why.
According to multiple current Ubisoft employees who spoke anonymously to Vertical Slice, pitches that do not set this out in proposals will not be approved.
One source said that while Ubisoft leadership isn't outright mandating generative AI, it is all-in on the technology.
"The attitude shift among our leaders [means] we all feel cornered by generative AI all the time," they said.
As reported by Vertical Slice, Ubisoft workers have been "clamouring for the company to approve smaller projects with smaller teams instead of leaning on massive triple-A releases," and leaders reviewing new pitches are "purportedly touting generative AI as a means of supporting those ambitions."
"We were told that using AI will increase the speed at which pitches can be made by 'small lean agile teams,' [meaning] prototypes could be built faster," another source said. "We were told that as the goal was to move fast, iterate and discard projects to sift out the good stuff. AI was essential in maintaining a rapid pace. If you planned on not using it, you would have to have a reason."
The same source claimed Ubisoft believes generative AI is now "unavoidable," and that the company thinks most of its players don't mind it, with only a vocal few objecting.
The report follows last week's launch of Motherbrain, a dedicated generative AI subsidiary that houses the team behind Teammates, Ubisoft's first playable generative AI experience.
At the time, CEO Yves Guillemot said the unit would focus on "developing proprietary technologies for gameplay to empower creators and open up new possibilities for richer, more immersive and engaging player experiences."
It also comes amid ongoing cost-cutting. According to Ubisoft's latest fiscal report, its headcount fell by 5,200 over the past four years, from 20,700 in September 2022 to 15,500 in September 2026, and not all of those departures were down to layoffs and studio closures. Employees told Vertical Slice that morale is low.
"Ubisoft is in a top-down nightmare of AI psychosis. Yves [Guillemot] has hitched his wagon to the tech, demanding the rest of the company follow on," one said.
Another described the mood as "abysmal," pointing to cost-cutting and Ubisoft's five-day return-to-office mandate.
"Ubisoft still isn't replacing most people leaving the company, leaving some teams stretched unbearably thin. Going-away parties [are] happening every couple [of] weeks it feels," they said. "The headless-chicken-core approach to generative AI is just the cherry on top, and Motherbrain is the smaller cherry on top of the cherry."
Ubisoft had not responded to Vertical Slice's request for comment at the time of publication. GamesIndustry.biz has also reached out to Ubisoft for comment.
Shortly before its most recent earnings announcement, Ubisoft laid off 166 staff in its Montreal and Quebec studios, which followed the closure of studios in Winnipeg and Belgrade in June, alongside job losses worldwide including at its San Francisco office. There have also been layoffs at Ubisoft Barcelona in July.
