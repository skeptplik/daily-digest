Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez welcomed Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva to the Moncloa Palace on Monday, where the pair discussed the importance of international law and multilateralism at a time when both pillars of the global order are facing what the Spanish government described as the "threat of the far right."
"The head of the executive has highlighted the defence of international law and multilateralism, in a meeting in which the two leaders addressed the future of the United Nations and the importance of defending democratic institutions and social cohesion," the government said in a statement.
The two leaders are also said to have discussed their "bilateral agenda" and reaffirmed their intention to continue strengthening cooperation between Spain and Brazil.
After the meeting at the Moncloa Palace, the duo held a second conversation focusing on the state of the Western left and the progressive political vision they share. They highlighted a model centred on improving working conditions and fair taxation as ways to promote prosperity.
"I had a productive meeting with the Spanish prime minister," Lula wrote in a post on X. "We emphasised the importance of combating hate speech and disinformation from the far right and of promoting an agenda of social inclusion that strengthens democracy worldwide."
"We emphasised our stance in defence of multilateralism and the need for reform of the UN Security Council, which has become even more evident given the body’s ineffectiveness in the face of the escalating armed conflicts in Ukraine and the Middle East," he added.
Lula's visit to Spain comes after he skipped the 30th Ibero-American summit, held in Madrid at the beginning of November.
The summit exposed the ideological differences across the region, with the absence of the Brazilian leader and the president of Mexico, Claudia Sheinbaum, leaving Sánchez as the most prominent left-wing leader in attendance.
One of the summit's most talked-about moments was Argentine President Javier Milei's frosty encounter with Sánchez. The two shared a notably cold greeting upon their arrival at the Royal Collections Gallery. It was the first meeting between the two leaders since the Argentine branded Sánchez a "disgraceful dictator" in October.
Lula has also been involved in numerous clashes with Milei, including during the recent Brazilian election campaign, when he unexpectedly defeated Flávio Bolsonaro, one of the Argentine president's closest regional allies.
Sánchez, who is fighting to remain in power, called an early election in Spain after his government partners refused to back a series of measures proposed by his administration to tackle the country's housing crisis.
