Is Dylan Moran joking when he opens his show by telling Northampton’s audience “I didn’t write anything, or prepare”? By the end, you wouldn’t bet on it. Bleary directionlessness may be par for the course for the Irish comic, whose work has always combined poetic-philosophical dazzle with a certain wooziness. But after a promising start tonight, his new touring show, Looking for Trouble, conspicuously loses its way, as Moran frets about forgetting a significant chunk of material, before abandoning his encore and leaving a bewildered audience in his wake.
That’s a shame, because there are shards of trademark brilliance in the opening 20 minutes, which see the 55-year-old address himself, albeit obliquely, to the mysteries of being human in 2026, AKA “the burning ball of what you don’t understand” From Trump to AI, Moran surveys it all in wide angle not closeup, as symptoms of our confusion, atavism and fear of expulsion from the tribe. The quality of his thinking in this section, while cheerfully offering himself up as Exhibit A in this prosecution of humanity’s perplexity, is arresting. And there are zingers, too, including a word-picture of the genitals that, once heard, may never be forgotten.
But Moran turns his back on this material part-way into the hour-long set, asking instead: “What can we discuss that won’t depress us?” From which point the show becomes increasingly noodly, with our host drifting between artist’s easel – on which he paints portraits of favourite foodstuffs and audience members – and a bank of musical instruments, none of which he can play. That doesn’t stop him twanging strings and plonking keys along to an assortment of half-baked ditties that are seldom apropos of anything. “That song doesn’t go anywhere, or end,” he admits at one point. You don’t say.
Throughout this grab-bag of a show, Moran’s company remains enjoyable enough and, amid the pottering, there are droll routines on Irish accents, office life and the British Isles reimagined as travellers in a shared car. But – like Britain, you could say – the show hangs together awkwardly at best.
