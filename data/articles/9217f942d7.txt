An experimental mRNA vaccine developed by Moderna is being tested on 224 volunteers in Uganda as health authorities seek to combat a deadly Ebola outbreak that has claimed thousands of lives in neighbouring DRC.
Uganda has begun clinical trials of an experimental vaccine targeting the Ebola strain responsible for the ongoing outbreak in the neighbouring Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), the country's virus research institute announced on Friday.
The outbreak, caused by the less common Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, has killed more than 5,700 people, with over 11,000 cases recorded since May, according to the World Health Organization (WHO). No vaccines or treatments are currently approved for this strain.
Trials of two experimental vaccines are scheduled to begin on Monday in eastern DRC, the epicentre of the outbreak, targeting people who have been in contact with infected individuals and those considered at high risk of infection.
Uganda has already begun testing one of the two vaccines, developed by US pharmaceutical company Moderna using the mRNA technology widely used during the COVID-19 pandemic.
The Uganda Virus Research Institute said in a statement it had "launched Africa's first mRNA Phase I clinical trial... in collaboration with Moderna of an investigational vaccine against Bundibugyo ebolavirus".
The study will involve 224 healthy volunteers aged between one and 65 to assess the vaccine's safety and its ability to trigger an immune response.
Uganda, which shares a long and porous border with eastern DRC, managed to contain the spread of the virus after recording imported cases earlier this year.
The country was declared Ebola-free in August after recording 20 infections and two deaths.
Gavi secures 1.5 million doses of experimental Ebola vaccine
Meanwhile, Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, has signed a purchase commitment with India's Serum Institute for 1.5 million doses of the other experimental vaccine, developed in collaboration with the University of Oxford.
The alliance aims to have "hundreds of thousands" of doses ready for distribution if clinical trials prove successful and the vaccine is approved by the WHO and DRC government.
"We're investing in it, hoping that the signals will be positive, and if that is the case, we'll have gained months and months on the deployment," Emanuele Capobianco, Gavi's director of global health security, told AFP.
Capobianco said he hoped for trials to conclude "within the first half of 2027" and even "within the first quarter" in a more optimistic scenario.
Gavi also holds a stockpile of 500,000 doses of Ervebo, an existing vaccine against the Zaire strain of Ebola, which is being tested to determine whether it is also effective against Bundibugyo.
