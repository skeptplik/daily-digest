After a 13-year wait, Grand Theft Auto 6 has been met with universal acclaim. Despite a lead-up marred by delays and leaks the hype has only grown, with over 17 million pre-orders generating more than $1.6 billion in revenue, according to recent data.
Critics have spent weeks exploring Rockstar's Leonida, featuring the Bonnie-and-Clyde-inspired duo Jason and Lucia, leading to a Metacritic score of 99. Backed by over 100 reviews, the title is currently listed as the platform's best game of all time, above the original N64 release of The Legend of Zelda: The Ocarina of Time.
In the words of The Guardian's Keza MacDonald: "Rockstar has created the most extraordinarily, indulgently detailed virtual world that we have seen yet."
"Leonida is so lifelike that at times it beggars belief, so vast that it feels overwhelming [...] and contains so many potential experiences that it's hard to know where to start."
"Rockstar has created the most extraordinarily, indulgently detailed virtual world that we have seen yet"
Keza MacDonald, The Guardian
IGN's Luke Reilly emphasised that the wait for GTA 6 was reflected in the detail Rockstar has poured into its world. "This is a world that feels more lived-in and real than anything I've ever played, and it's one you can interact with at a level never seen before in the series," he said. "Rockstar has truly pushed the boat out.”
The game's level of detail raised concerns about performance, particularly its 30fps cap on all consoles, including the PlayStation 5 Pro. However, Reilly found that this limitation did not diminish the experience.
"What matters to me is that it looks absolutely incredible and runs smoothly and reliably on a standard PS5. I find all the brilliant visual flourishes far more important, from the concentric rings of ripples on water due to helicopter rotor downwash, to the almost imperceptible line of sweat that appears on Lucia's forehead after a brisk jog, or the buckling of the fabric on a shirt as torsos twist, to the temporary dents on a punching bag as you strike it."
"If a sufficiently stable and properly paced 30fps is the route to GTA 6 looking and functioning the way it does, bug-free, then I have no complaints."
Immersion
GTA 6's immersion extends to daily routines that improve stats like health and stamina, or managing car fuel levels. As Game Informer's Matt Miller observed, these activities "add pacing and counterpoint to the explosive march of major missions, narrative threads, and set-piece encounters that drive the game forward."
These everyday moments are initially character-specific, as The New York Times' Yussef Cole noted. "Jason can opt to fix up his run-down bachelor pad in preparation for Lucia's imminent release. There are numerous tasks around the house, like putting up drywall, painting and fixing the roof, that take up the player's time and the character's in-game resources, but they aim toward a productive and satisfying goal: a better relationship."
That's not to say GTA 6 isn't like past games, where violence is encouraged and chaos is one petty crime away. Miller emphasised that these moments do not detract from the "meticulously choreographed" set pieces, which "run the gamut from amusing shenanigans at a strip club to intense chases and escapes fueled by raging gunfights and explosions. Many of the best moments leverage an expert sense of musical accompaniment, weaving a slick original score alongside one of the most impressive and wide-ranging licensed soundtracks ever heard in a video game – often to surprisingly emotional effect."
Critics agreed that GTA 6 offers unexpected emotional depth, thanks to nuance in both the representation of the lead characters and the player's ability to control them.
Jason and Lucia
IGN's Reilly described Jason and Lucia as "unlike any leads that have come before them in the series," whose priorities are each other and escaping the criminal life they find themselves increasingly entrenched in.
"They're not angels, and it is invariably their recklessness as small-time crooks that puts them in the wrong place at the wrong time, but the overall sliminess of their far more powerful antagonists does make the couple easy to root for throughout their story,"he wrote.
The New York Times' Cole agreed, adding that the emphasis on the duo's relationship "reflects a maturation of a series that has generally only told stories about men solipsistically chasing after their own success."
He continued: "Choosing romance is the easy part, thanks to the chemistry and human characterisation of the two leads. Lucia and Jason's relationship builds on itself and becomes a constant, supportive presence. It gets reflected in the story missions too, as your characters make scripted choices that reflect how you behaved toward each other in the idle moments in between."
"[Jason and Lucia are], without a doubt, video gaming's most richly drawn and compelling romance"
Matt Miller, Game Informer
Game Informer's Miller described Jason and Lucia's relationship as "elevating the game to new heights, lending this instalment a heart, tension, and emotional core missing from prior entries."
"This is, without a doubt, video gaming's most richly drawn and compelling romance, featuring two leads who are immediately relatable and compelling to watch. Player actions dictate the strength of their bond, deepening the sense of engagement. Like the best crime fiction, you're left surprised by how much you cheer for two people who regularly commit terrible acts."
The Guardian's MacDonald "found it impossible not to care" for the couple over time. "They are our avatars in GTA 6's world, but they are not blank stand-ins for the player: you decide their actions but not their personalities. They feel like real people rather than puppets dancing to your whim [...] When the story gave me opportunities to decide what would happen next, what came to mind for me was what they would do, not what I would do."
MacDonald also praised Lucia's portrayal as the series' first female protagonist, noting it changed the game's appeal for her. "GTA has never been particularly kind to women (or men, for that fact, but in past games it has exclusively been the women who were sexualised). There is still a lot of sexism in GTA 6's world, but now we have Lucia to react to it. This is the first GTA in which I have felt the women are treated as people, not only Lucia but also the many other female characters in the story, from up-and-coming rappers to Lucia's mother, from a jaded strip-club worker to a badass middle-aged drug-runner whose custom purple carbine rifle is her prized possession. This is a game in which women can be as violent, visibly tough and deeply messed-up as the men."
Grand Theft Auto
At its core, GTA 6 remains focused on wanted stars, car theft, heists, and other criminal activities. However, this instalment introduces greater consequences for player actions.
Cops now act on information gathered about Jason and Lucia and their crimes, creating a more intuitive wanted system that requires players to change recognisable clothing or split up during chases.
"You're no longer just trying to choose the right streets to leave a hot zone, like Pac-Man trying to route away from the nearest ghost"
Luke Reilly, IGN
"You're no longer just trying to choose the right streets to leave a hot zone, like Pac-Man trying to route away from the nearest ghosts," said IGN's Reilly. "You're listening to the scanner for hints about what the cops know. You're eyeballing the traffic in the distance, trying to figure out if any of them are police cruisers. It's knowing when and where to stay out of view entirely, and when it's safe for you to hide in plain sight. That's a far bigger thrill."
"I think it's actually going to be a little frustrating to go back to the likes of GTA 5, where the police magically make a beeline towards you when your crimes cross a certain threshold – even if there are no witnesses, or any believable way for them to know which car you're driving or what you look like."
Cinematic setpieces remain, though Game Informer's Miller criticised them for being too "on rails regarding where to go and what to do at each step."
"At the same time, that directed approach keeps the pace relentless, and the action and outcomes thrilling. Over many hours, the game morphs and throws new wrenches into the mix at key junctures, teasing and then playing out criminal experiences from petty theft to group heists to illicit spec ops, and punctuates those distinct vibes with memorable and singular moments."
Overall consensus
Although critics had high expectations for GTA 6, many were surprised by what the game had to offer
"Exquisitely detailed, endlessly ripe for experimentation, and uncompromisingly engineered, GTA 6 has not stopped astonishing me since I started it," said IGN's Reilly. "I regularly find myself in love with video games, but it's extraordinarily rare that I find myself in complete awe of them. Put simply, Grand Theft Auto 6 is the single most impressive video game I have ever played."
Game Informer's Miller held similar views, calling it "a generational game that stands toe-to-toe with many of the great crime stories and satires across other media."
The Guardian's MacDonald and The New York Times' Cole were more taken by Jason and Lucia's relationship. Observing the nights out that can be explored in Vice City, MacDonald noted "that at the centre of it all, there are Jason and Lucia, dancing together, focused on each other in the midst of all this chaos and excess."
As for Cole, he felt that "where the dollars are most felt isn't in the intricate skylines or the typically bombastic missions [...] the game's artistry is felt most in its Bonnie and Clyde duo."
