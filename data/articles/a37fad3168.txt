Russian warship hit by suspected Ukrainian drone at Benghazi naval base, Libyan officials say
A Russian navy corvette docked at the Benghazi naval base in eastern Libya was hit on Sunday by a suspected Ukrainian sea drone, according to local officials, in what appeared to be the latest attack linked to the Russia-Ukraine war in the North African nation. Hours later, a cargo ship was also hit by a sea drone in a second attack off Benghazi on Sunday evening.

A suspected Ukrainian drone strike hit a Russian warship Sunday at a naval base in Libya, officials said, the latest attack in the North African nation linked to the Russia-Ukraine war.
The strike at the Benghazi Naval Base in eastern Libya severely damaged the Merkury, a Russian navy corvette, and ignited a fire at the facility, two Libyan military officials told the Associated Press.
They said that several Ukrainian sea drones were launched at the ship from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya, according to an initial investigation into the attack. At least four drones were intercepted and destroyed, while two hit the warship, they said.

The officials, affiliated with the self-styled Libyan National Army, which controls eastern and southern Libya, spoke to the AP on condition of anonymity because they weren't allowed to talk with reporters.
Libya has been divided since a NATO-backed uprising toppled longtime dictator Muammar Gaddafi, who was later killed, in 2011. A UN-supported government is based in Tripoli and a rival administration is located in the east. The oil-rich nation remains a focal point for a long-running rivalry between Russia and the West.
The eastern Libya-based government said in a statement that the strike on Sunday caused an explosion and fire at the base, which was later extinguished.
The vessel had docked at the base for refueling and supplies as part of a cooperation agreement between Russia and the eastern-based government in Libya, the statement said, without providing details on the vessel or its cargo.
A joint investigation was launched with Russia to “uncover the circumstances of the attack, its source, and the party behind it,” the statement said.
No group immediately claimed responsibility for the attack. There was no immediate comment from either Russia or Ukraine.
Second ship hit in drone attack
On Sunday evening, local media said that another sea drone hit a commercial vessel off the port of Benghazi in a separate attack.
Local officials said a "merchant vessel was targeted this evening by explosive-laden sea drones off the coast of Benghazi", but did not specify whether the ship had been Russian as well.
There was no immediate claim of responsibility for either strike, but they come as Ukrainian forces have stepped up long-range barrages against Russia far from the front lines.
Reports said the second ship was sailing under a Maltese flag.
The eastern Libyan administration said all crew members had been rescued and were receiving treatment for minor injuries.
Libya Alhadath television later reported that naval and air force units intercepted more than 12 "explosive-laden sea drones" off Benghazi.
No further details were given. The Almasar satellite television channel said that naval and air forces destroyed at least 12 other sea drones off the Mediterranean city.
'Dragging' Libyan ports 'into external conflicts'
The western Libya government expressed concerns about “dragging Libyan ports and civilian facilities into external conflicts,” in an apparent reference to the war in Ukraine.
“The government reaffirms its categorical rejection of turning Libya into an arena for international conflicts or a theater for external powers to settle scores,” it said in a statement.
In March, Ukrainian forces operating in western Libya under a covert deal struck a Russian tanker in the Mediterranean in a suspected sea drone attack near Maltese waters, Libyan officials said at the time.
The Ukrainian forces, most of them drone experts, operated mainly at an air base in the coastal city of Misrata, but also at other military facilities in the capital, Tripoli, and the coastal town of Zawiya, the officials told The Associated Press.
The United Nations called on Monday for an independent probe into drone attacks on two ships off Libya, including a Russian vessel.
There was no immediate claim of responsibility for either strike on Sunday off the port city of Benghazi, but they came as Ukrainian forces stepped up attacks against Russia far from the front lines.
The UN mission in Libya expressed "serious concern" over the attacks, calling for "an immediate, independent and transparent investigation".
"Libya cannot afford, and must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts," the UNSMIL wrote on X.
