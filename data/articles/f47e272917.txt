Police in India say they have uncovered an international cyberfraud and human trafficking operation on the outskirts of capital city Delhi and rescued 92 Indonesian and 72 Malaysian nationals.
They were allegedly lured to India with promises of lucrative IT jobs, but forced into cyber crime, according to police.
Police say the scheme was exposed after a Malaysian worker managed to escape and revealed the operation.
Dozens of foreign nationals have alleged that once they arrived in India, their passports were confiscated and they were held against their will in Noida - a Delhi suburb that is a major IT and business hub in northern India and home to several call centres.
Instead of the jobs and high salaries promised, police say, they were forced to pose as women online to lure unsuspecting victims into fake investment platforms.
So far one Malaysian national has been arrested in connection with the crime, and nearly 550 desktop computers, laptops and mobile phones have been confiscated, police say.
An investigation has now been launched into a potential wider global cyberfraud network.
Police and Indian banks have been trying to contend with an alarming rise in cyber fraud operations in recent years.
A paper, external by the Reserve Bank of India warned against rising digital fraud and "the manipulation of users through social engineering, coercion, or impersonation".
Figures from India's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal show 2.8 million cyber fraud complaints were recorded in 2025, with losses of about 22,495 crore rupees, external ($2.42bn; Â£1.85 bn). It's a dramatic rise in complaints by more than 4,000% since 2021.
The federal government said most cases involved fake investment and trading scams.
In fake investment scams, fraudsters approach victims through social media, messaging groups or phone calls, posing as financial experts and promising high returns.
They lure victims to fake trading platforms that show fictitious profits. Some are allowed to withdraw small amounts to build trust, but when they try to cash out larger sums, they are blocked or asked to pay more. The fraudsters eventually disappear with their money.
The Noida case is the latest in a series of international cyberfraud call centres busted in north India in recent months, with police raids in Lucknow and Delhi's Pitampura area.
In July, Lucknow Police arrested 119 people after raiding a fake international call centre targeting US citizens. Employees allegedly posed as representatives of major companies and US government agencies to defraud victims.
