Western Australian authorities say they are considering “all options” in their race to save a beloved jarrah tree they suspect was vandalised with a chainsaw in a national park south of Perth.
Police on Friday said they were treating the vandalism of “King Jarrah” as an alleged criminal damage incident, as they appealed to the public to help them find the person or people responsible.
The tree, in Wellington national park near Bunbury, was damaged on 21 or 22 November when someone attempted to cut it down, police said.
The WA environment minister, Matt Swinbourn, said an “absolute jerk” had damaged the “iconic” tree with a chainsaw, in an act he described as “appalling, senseless, and heartbreaking”.
“It’s hard to fathom how shocking it is that someone would do this to a tree believed to be about 500 years old, particularly in a national park,” he said in a statement on social media on Thursday night.
“Western Australians love this tree. Our community will be heartbroken, and they have every right to be angry.”
Experts, including an independent specialist arborist, were assessing the tree’s condition, but the immediate priorities were giving it every possible chance of survival and finding those responsible, Swinbourn said.
“Whoever did this has damaged something precious to all of us,” he said.
Swinbourn said surveillance cameras had been installed in the area which was now closed to the public.
“If you know anything, come forward,” he said. “Anyone capable of such a deliberate and callous act should be held accountable.”
The jarrah tree, Eucalyptus marginata, is a towering hardwood native found only in the forests of south-west WA, which plays a vital role in the local ecosystem.
Visitors travel to the state to see the jarrahs, known for their towering foliage and longevity, as well as their rich red timber and honey.
The state’s biodiversity, conservation and attractions department said a member of the public reported the damage to the parks and wildlife service on Tuesday.
A chainsaw had been used around the base of the tree, one of the tallest in the national park at 36 metres, which is the “focus and drawcard” of a small day-use recreation site, the spokesperson said.
They said a consultant arborist and relevant government staff travelled to the national park on Wednesday to assess the health and safety of the tree.
Formal technical advice from the consultant arborist on whether King Jarrah could be saved was expected next week, they said.
“All options are being considered to save this iconic tree,” the spokesperson said.
They said vandalism of this nature was a potential breach of several sections of the Conservation and Land Management Act 1984 and, if found guilty, court penalties for the person or people responsible could include a fine of up to $10,000 and one year’s imprisonment.
Police said a silver Jeep was seen in the area in the days after the incident and they believed whoever was in the car may be able to assist with their inquiries.
They encouraged anyone with information to contact them via crime stoppers.
