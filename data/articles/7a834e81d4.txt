King tides and huge waves have been battering both coasts of the US during a turbulent Thanksgiving period, with severe weather threatening millions across the country. Along the California coast, king tides caused considerable damage. In Pacifica, south of San Francisco, large waves battered the coastline on Wednesday, destroying part of a pier and sending debris into the ocean.
The National Weather Service issued coastal flood warnings covering parts of the Pacific coastline and shorelines around San Francisco Bay and San Pablo Bay. Several beachfront properties in Pacifica have already experienced flooding, while authorities in Marin County temporarily issued an evacuation warning for residents of beachfront homes at Stinson Beach.
The high tides also affected southern California. San Diego recorded a tide of 8ft 11in (2.7 metres) on Wednesday morning, while Los Angeles experienced tides reaching approximately 8.19ft, potentially surpassing a record set at the end of October.
King tides are unusually high natural tides that occur when the gravitational forces of the sun and moon combine around their alignment with Earth. Their impact is being heightened by unusually warm Pacific waters associated with the 2026 super El Niño.
A Nor’easter has also been bringing severe weather across the eastern US. The system produced heavy rain, damaging winds and coastal flooding, creating hazardous conditions for millions of Americans travelling to celebrate the holiday.
The severity of the forecast forced an unprecedented decision in New York: Macy’s postponed its 100th annual Thanksgiving Day parade, originally scheduled for Thursday, until Saturday. It was the first time in the event’s history that the parade had been postponed because of weather. The decision was taken to safeguard performers and spectators, particularly given the dangers that powerful gusts pose to the parade’s enormous character balloons.
Meanwhile, significant fresh snowfall during the past week has transformed conditions across several major ski regions in the Alps, providing an excellent early season foundation for the new season. French resorts have been among the biggest beneficiaries. Reported accumulated snow depths on Thursday included approximately 120cm in the Tignes-Val d’Isère area, while substantial snowfall was recorded around Les Deux Alpes, Alpe d’Huez and the Les Trois Vallées. Further snowfall is forecast across parts of France, Switzerland, Italy and Austria, with several resorts expecting additional accumulations over the coming days.
