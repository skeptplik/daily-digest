Kyiv – President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on Monday said Ukraine was behind strikes on two Russian vessels off Libya, adding that one of them sank in the latest spillover from Moscow’s war with Kyiv.
The attacks on Sunday near the Libyan port of Benghazi come at a time when Ukraine has stepped up its strikes against Russia far from the front lines.
But the United Nations condemned the strikes, warning against the prospect of a country still divided after the Libyan civil war becoming “a battleground for foreign conflicts.”
“It all took place off the coast of Libya. Our warriors deployed seven Ukrainian-made naval drones. Despite all the enemy’s efforts to counter the attack, the Russian corvette Merkury was sunk,” Zelenskyy wrote on X.
The Ukrainian leader claimed the Merkury was “one of their newest ships, commissioned in 2023, during the full-scale war.”
“Our second target was the Russian dry cargo vessel MV ANGARA, which the enemy used to transport weapons. Result: target hit,” he added.
A Ka-27 combat helicopter was also destroyed in the strike on the Merkury, Zelenskyy said.
Sunday’s strikes came after a drone hit a Russian cargo ship in the eastern Baltic Sea on Saturday, close to the waters of Finland, whose border guard said the strike was Ukrainian.
The U.N. mission in Libya expressed “serious concern” over the attacks, calling for “an immediate, independent and transparent investigation.”
“Libya cannot afford, and must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts,” the UNSMIL wrote on X.
In March, a Russian liquefied natural gas carrier sank off the Libyan coast following an attack, which Russian President Vladimir Putin blamed on Ukraine.
The vessel was part of what is known as Russia’s “shadow fleet” of tankers that carry its oil and gas around the world, skirting Western restrictions.
Ukraine has at times admitted to attacking Moscow’s shadow fleet, including in the Mediterranean last December.
The Tripoli government said it rejected any attempt to turn Libya into “an arena for international conflicts” and the use of its territory “for any military or logistical activities that could jeopardize” national security or sovereignty.
Libya has been beset by violence and instability since the overthrow of longtime ruler Moammar Gadhafi in 2011.
It remains divided between the government in Tripoli led by Prime Minister Abdulhamid Dbeibah and a rival administration in the east backed by military commander Khalifa Haftar.
