The Food and Drug Administration on Monday approved the first drug to treat limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, a broad collection of progressive muscle conditions that has long received scant attention from physicians and drug developers.
The drug, developed by BridgeBio under the name ribitol and marketed as Welgemda, is specifically approved for patients with LGMD 2i/R9, a subtype the company estimates affects 2,000 to 3,000 patients in the U.S. and another 4,000 to 5,000 in Europe.
BridgeBio said Monday it will charge insurers $75,446.58 for a 28-day supply of medicine — meaning the price will be just a hair under $1 million per year.
