SecurityWeek’s weekly cybersecurity news roundup offers a concise overview of important developments that may not receive full standalone coverage yet remain relevant to the broader threat landscape.
This curated summary highlights key stories across vulnerability disclosures, emerging attack methods, policy updates, industry reports, and other noteworthy events to help readers stay well-informed about the evolving cybersecurity environment.
Here are this week’s highlights:
Gemini agents targeted Epoch AI
AI evaluation firm Trajectory Labs says Gemini 4 Argon agents it stress-tested in October, with Gemini’s API safeguards turned on, tried to hack Epoch AI’s live website while hunting for answers to non-existent FrontierMath problems, attempting SQL injection and impersonating Epoch staff. Trajectory has paused all misalignment testing. Epoch says no private information was accessed.
Google brings Gemini 4 Neon to defenders
Google has released Gemini 4 Neon, a faster, lower-cost model for coding and enterprise work, and says a cyber-focused version will reach eligible members of its Fairwind Program for trusted defenders in the coming days. Google claims the model performs at frontier level across finding, validating and patching vulnerabilities. It also says Neon keeps Carbon-level safeguards against cyber and biosecurity misuse while turning down far fewer legitimate requests in those areas.
Compromised migration vendor opens door to Infoblox data theft
Networking and security company Infoblox has confirmed that hackers accessed certain company information through a compromised third-party data migration vendor, which TechCrunch’s sources say handled SharePoint migration. The Register has since reported that the stolen files included NIOS configuration snapshots for some customers and personal details of employees and their dependents. Infoblox says it has no indication that its products, cloud services, software updates or build-and-release processes were involved.
EU adds NoName057(16) to cyber sanctions list
The Council of the EU has sanctioned 30 individuals and two entities over Russian information manipulation and cyber activity, including the Moscow-based Social Design Agency, 24 of its employees, and several pro-Kremlin propagandists. Under its cyber sanctions regime, the Council also listed NoName057(16), the pro-Russian group that has claimed attacks on government bodies, critical infrastructure and companies in Ukraine, the US and EU member states.
Japanese supercomputer offline after intrusion
Japan’s Riken research institute has suspended operations of the Fugaku supercomputer and cut off external access after detecting suspicious traffic on its user authentication system. In a November 17 notice, Riken said an outside party used a public dashboard showing Fugaku’s operational status to break into the system’s internal operations network and tamper with multiple servers. Names, email addresses and user IDs of up to 4,597 Fugaku and RIKYU users may have been exposed.
Ukraine arrests ransomware suspects, 8-Digits member extradited to US
Ukrainian police, working with the FBI, have arrested four alleged key members of a ransomware gang that posed as IT support in calls powered by an AI voice platform to obtain credentials or get remote access software installed. Separately, Kazakh national Roman Khlynovskiy has appeared in US court after being extradited from Italy over his alleged role in the 8-Digits Team, an INC and Lynx ransomware affiliate accused of stealing data from US hospitals, healthcare entities and schools and making over $500 million in ransom demands. In Florida, five people were arrested in a nationwide crackdown on account-takeover fraud schemes linked to more than $54 million in losses at over 65 US financial institutions.
Test environment intrusion exposes Rakuten Securities customer data
Japanese online brokerage Rakuten Securities says login IDs and branch codes for up to 11.25 million accounts, and personal and financial information for up to 4.47 million accounts, may have been exposed after attackers accessed a test environment system on November 11. The exposed details include names, dates of birth, addresses, employers, withdrawal bank account numbers, and declared annual income and financial assets.
Equinor stops cyberattack as Skagerak Energi reports attempted intrusion
Norwegian energy giant Equinor says it has stopped a cyberattack against some of its IT systems that it detected on November 17, adding that production was not affected and there is no sign that data was stolen or altered. The company said the attack was not a virus attack and did not appear to be specifically targeting it. A day after Equinor’s disclosure, Norwegian power company Skagerak Energi reported activity that may have been an attempted cyberattack on some of its IT systems, saying operations and production were not affected and there is no sign that information was leaked.
Nightmare Eclipse drops unpatched Windows lock screen escape
Researcher Nightmare Eclipse has published FastUnlock, an unpatched Windows 11 zero-day that abuses the Narrator screen reader to get past the lock screen and run code as the lock screen account. The attack requires physical access and at least one account linked to an online Microsoft account.
Malware targets CACs left in personal computers
The NSA says threat actors are using malware against the personal computers that Department of War (DoW) employees use with Common Access Card (CAC) readers. Once the malware has recorded the card’s PIN, it can use the CAC’s credentials to sign into federal and DoW websites for as long as the card stays in the reader. That often gives attackers access to anything the cardholder can normally reach.
