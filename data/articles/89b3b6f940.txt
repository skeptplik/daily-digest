Taiwan's voters begun choosing mayors and county chiefs in local elections on Saturday that may gauge the mood ahead of the island's 2028 presidential elections.
Polls closed at 4 p.m. local time (0800 UTC/GMT) and results are expected by early evening. Winners will serve four-year terms.
Most candidates are from the ruling Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) or the Nationalist Party, also known as the Kuomintang or KMT. The latter has traditionally performed better in local elections and favors closer ties with China.
President Lai Ching-te has campaigned for candidates from his DPP.
Taipei mayor race closely watched in Beijing
China is paying close attention, despite not currently featuring as a key campaign issue.
Taiwan's government warned its people to be vigilant against fake news on social media, citing a Chinese disinformation campaign.
Taiwan's 22 cities and counties are currently focused on issues such as transport, childcare and the environment. While these issues do not directly impact the China policy, Beijing has repeatedly weighed in.
China denounced the governing DPP candidate for Taipei's mayoral race, Puma Shen, who is wanted by Chinese police for alleged "separatist activities."
He entered the mayoral race as an underdog but has managed to ride a political wave that drew huge crowds to his campaign rallies.
Earlier this month, Shen said Chinese police had never contacted him and that whoever wins, Taipei's election would be a victory for democracy in the face of pressure from Beijing.
If Shen wins, he would be the capital city's first DPP mayor in nearly 30 years. Taipei turning to a progressive candidate in what has traditionally been a KMT stronghold could be seen as "quite shameful" in Beijing, Austin Wang, political science professor at the University of Nevada, told AFP news agency.
China claims Taiwan as part of its territory under its "One China" principle and has recently increased pressure on Taiwan to accept its sovereignty claims, threatening force to seize the island.
Taipei's incumbent and KMT member, Mayor Chiang Wan-an has campaigned against Shen, also pulling in thousands of supporters to his rallies.
In the last election, KMT thrashed DPP, winning 14 of the 22 cities and counties while the DPP won only five. Chiang is also seen as a potential future presidential candidate.
What impact could the vote have on presidential elections?
Analysts have cautioned that the local elections are not a reliable barometer of how people might vote for the presidential elections in 2028. Still, Saturday's results could influence the selection process for the KMT presidential candidate.
The DPP is likely to rally behind President Lai for a reelection.
The most likely KMT candidates are Taichung's outgoing Mayor Lu Shiow-yen and parliamentary speaker Han Kuo-yu.
KMT Chairwoman Cheng Li-wun, who's pro-Beijing stance has even alienated her from many in the party, is also viewed as a potential challenger for the party's nomination. However, she could face pressure to step down if KMT performs poorly in the local elections.
