Aerin Glazer’s Tilt Beauty embodies the phrase “If you want something done right, do it yourself.”
Glazer — who was 13 years old when she was diagnosed with arthritis — quickly realized that her new disability would hinder her hobbies, chief among them playing with makeup.
“There was no cool, accessible beauty brand, and I didn’t feel seen,” Glazer said onstage at the 2026 Beauty Inc Catalysts. “That’s why I decided to make it myself.”
That was the genesis of Glazer founding Tilt Beauty and creating her own accessible line. “I loved makeup. It was a tool and an outlet for me, but I couldn’t grip onto the products. So I took it upon myself to modify my own,” she said, adding that she was “very embarrassed,” feeling that they weren’t “cool” to use around her friends.
In high school, Glazer started experimenting with different designs in her after-school ceramics class, making and pitching mini models of different beauty products to design firms. She endured rejection after rejection, but after comparing her handiwork to the Microsoft Adaptive Xbox, something “clicked” and Glazer “finally got that one ‘yes,’” she recalled.
“Inclusive design at Tilt Beauty starts before you even get to the product,” said Glazer, who creates for disabilities beyond her own. Accessible elements start with the packaging, which includes an easy-to-pull-off seal, along with bold colors and Braille for the visually impaired.
It was important to Glazer that the brand maintain an identity and aesthetic outside of offering serviceable products. “I really felt that Tilt Beauty did not belong in the ‘disability aisle.’ I wanted it to be as cool as anything else. I was really passionate that something created to fulfill an access need can be just as fun and fashionable,” she said.
In the same vein, Glazer didn’t want Tilt to only be for those with special needs. Take the products’ magnetic caps or matte finish, for instance. “If you have a limb difference, you can open this with one arm. Even if you don’t have a disability, if you’re older, this makes the process so much easier. If you’re a young person who likes trendy makeup on the go, this is also for you.”
According to Glazer, accommodating for people’s differences is a strength rather than an area for error.
“I was once asked, ‘Do you think that being in a niche such as catering for people with disabilities hurts your business?’” Glazer recalled. “I responded, ‘Absolutely not.’ I truly believe and prove it does the opposite. Tilt Beauty is as cool as any other product out there. When you work with this huge community, you can make amazing things happen for your business.”
The brand is the first within the beauty space to be certified for ease of use by the National Arthritis Foundation; it also partners with the National Psoriasis Foundation and the Braille Institute.
“One in seven people around the world live with a disability, and one in four Americans. The market is huge and wildly underserved,” said Glazer, calling accessibility a “massive driver for innovation.”
Currently featured in the Metropolitan Museum of Art’s Costume Institute, Tilt Beauty and Glazer have been on a “surreal” two-year journey since the brand’s launch in February 2025.
Glazer urged other beauty companies to look into accessible products. “I do not want to be the only person, the only brand, that designs for everybody,” Glazer said. “I really believe when you do design for everybody, everybody wins.”
