Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya ethnic minority, International Court of Justice rules
Court says 2017 campaign led to mass death, 'widespread acts of sexual violence' and ongoing refugee crisis
In a landmark judgment, the United Nations' top court ruled Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya minority in a brutal 2017 military operation.
International Court of Justice President Yuji Iwasawa said the court, in a 9-6 decision, found that Myanmar "has committed genocide against the Rohingya group in northern Rakhine state, in violation of its obligations under the Genocide Convention."
The court's 15-judge panel — which included Navi Pillay, the respected international jurist who won the Nobel Peace Prize last month — rejected Myanmar's defence that the 2017 campaign was a legitimate operation against insurgents.
Iwasawa said that "the court finds that Myanmar's security forces killed thousands of Rohingya civilians, perpetrated widespread acts of sexual violence, as well as acts of extreme violence against children, and attacked fleeing Rohingya as they sought to flee in large numbers."
The decision marks the first time the court has ruled that a state committed genocide.
"This is a great day for the Rohingya, and it is a great day for all oppressed people, everywhere, who have been told that their suffering does not count and that no one is listening," said Yasmin Ullah, a Rohingya rights activist who sat in the court's Great Hall of Justice for the three-hour reading of the ruling.
The intent to destroy a group in whole or in part is a key element in establishing whether crimes amount to genocide under international law.
The court said that evidence showed Myanmar forces killed about 10,000 Rohingya.
UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres called the ruling "a demonstration of international justice and a reaffirmation that impunity for such horrific acts must not stand."
Shortly after the ruling was read, British Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said his government was expelling Myanmar's defence attaché from the U.K. and "calling urgently for a UN Security Council session to discuss the judgment."
Over 1 million refugees
The Muslim-majority African nation of Gambia launched the proceedings at the International Court of Justice in 2019, asking it to rule that Myanmar has breached the 1948 Genocide Convention, order the nation to end any illegal activities targeting the Rohingya and ensure those responsible for genocide are punished.
In August 2017, Myanmar's military launched what it called a "clearance campaign" in Rakhine state in response to an attack by a Rohingya insurgent group. The campaign led to accusations that security forces committed mass rapes and killings and burned thousands of homes.
Rohingya children are seen wading through a flooded canal after heavy rain at the Kutupalong refugee camp in Bangladesh's Cox's Bazar on June 23. Thousands of Rohingya originally from Myanmar's Rakhine state live in squalid conditions at Cox's Bazar. (Munir Uz Zaman/AFP/Getty Images)
Under the 1948 convention, to establish that genocide occurred, the court will have to find that there was an intent to destroy the Rohingya "in whole or in part."
About 1.2 million Rohingya refugees now live in Bangladesh, primarily in crowded camps across Cox's Bazar district, where they endure harsh conditions marked by fire hazards, infectious diseases, monsoon landslides, inadequate sanitation and strict work restrictions.
"Genocides have similarities in how they are committed so this decision will have an impact on other cases," said Melanie O'Brien, a professor of international law at the University of Western Australia.

Gambia has asked for reparations for the Rohingya, including ordering Myanmar to grant them full citizenship and the right to return home.
Legally binding, but unenforceable
Yasmin Ullah, a Rohingya human rights activist who fled Myanmar as a young child with her mother in the 1990s, says the demands in the Myanmar case are rooted in her community's desire for recognition and dignity.
"Because so far, we've just been pushed around into this limbo state," she said. "It's been almost 10 years now that Rohingya have been living in very, very precarious conditions across the Asia-Pacific."

The court's rulings are final and legally binding, but it has no way to enforce it beyond reporting noncompliance to the UN Security Council.
In a previous judgment in 2007, the court said that Serbia "violated the obligation to prevent genocide" in the 1995 Srebrenica massacre, when Bosnian Serb forces murdered some 8,000 mostly Muslim men and boys in the Bosnian town.
Judges at the Hague-based court have also been asked to weigh in on other allegations of genocide.

South Africa has accused Israel of breaching the genocide convention during its offensive in Gaza, a claim Israel vehemently denies. That case is years from completion.
Ukraine also has a case pending against Russia. Shortly after Moscow's full-scale invasion in February 2022, Kyiv accused Russia of fabricating genocide claims against Ukraine to justify the attack.
