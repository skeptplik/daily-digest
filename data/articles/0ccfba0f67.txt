Fri, 30 Oct, 7:15 pm, Final score, Cats 88, Bulldogs 20
AFLW superstar Chloe Molloy has once again lifted Sydney to victory, with the Swans defeating the Western Bulldogs by 29 points in an elimination final.
League-leading goalkicker Molloy pulled off a surprise cameo down back to help the Swans claim a 7.4 (46) to 2.5 (17) win at a wet and windy Henson Park on Sunday.
Sydney will next play Geelong in an away semi-final. The Bulldogs' season is over.
Molloy's leadership in a wet-weather scrap proved the difference for the Swans, with the former Swans co-skipper finishing with one goal, 12 intercepts, and 14 contested possessions.
Tanya Kennedy was just as crucial for the Swans, locking down Bulldogs co-captain and gun midfielder Isabelle Pritchard.
Pritchard, on return from a knee injury, was kept to just 14 disposals and three clearances as Kennedy laid 17 tackles.
Fellow Bulldogs midfielder Ellie Blackburn, also returning after being rested in last week's loss to the Cats, led the visitors with 29 touches and a goal.
Swans captain Lucy McEvoy needed just 20 seconds to score the opener with a long bomb from outside the 50-metre arc thanks to the strong breeze.
A rushed behind gave the Bulldogs their sole score of the first quarter, as the hosts made the most of conditions to fire off their four unanswered goals and build a 24-point lead.
The Bulldogs could only manage one point with the breeze in the second, with Molloy helping limit the damage with five contested possessions and four intercepts as a defender.
Molloy's stint in defence forced Bulldogs coach Tam Hyett to bring defender Vaomua Laloifi into the forward line, and the pair's battle continued well into the second half.
Sunday's final turned into a slog after torrential rain came in the third quarter, with the Swans doing just enough to maintain a 25-point lead en route to victory in front of 4,325 dedicated fans.
Hawks break through for finals win over Suns
Hawthorn has broken through for their its AFLW finals victory in an 18-point defeat of Gold Coast.
A commanding third quarter paved the path for the Hawks to prevail 8.9 (57) to 6.3 (39) at People First Stadium on Sunday.
Hawthorn, who bombed out of the finals in straight sets in 2024 and 2025, will face Carlton in the first semi-final next Saturday night at Princes Park.
Eliza West (29 disposals, one goal) and Laura Stone (21, two) were outstanding for the Hawks, while Casey Sherriff, Hayley McLaughlin and Jasmine Fleming all picked up 20 touches.
Jenna Richardson was a wall in defence, with a game-high 11 marks while completely blanketing Suns forward Dekota Brown.
The Davies sisters — Darcie and Georja — impressed for Gold Coast, who were also chasing their maiden AFLW final success.
Charlie Rowbottom battled hard for her 20 disposals (18 of them handballs), a figure matched by Niamh McLoughlin and Daisy D'Arcy.
Adding injury to insult for the Suns, young gun Ava Usher suffered a serious-looking injury to her right knee in a heavy collision with teammate Maddy Brancatisano in the dying stages.
Hawthorn kicked two goals in the last 77 seconds of a high-octane opening term — through Poppy Stockwell and Jacqueline Dupuy — to lead by six points.
The Suns responded to take a three-point half-time lead, but Hawthorn raised its pressure in the third quarter and Gold Coast couldn't go with it.
The Hawks' backline repelled 11 consecutive entries from the Suns, while their midfield trampled Gold Coast at clearance and on the outside.
Hawthorn had 33 more disposals for the term, 13 more tackles and pulled ahead by 15 points at three-quarter time.
Mikayla Nurse goaled early in the fourth quarter to give Gold Coast a sniff, but the Hawks continued to pile on the pressure and exorcise their own demons.
