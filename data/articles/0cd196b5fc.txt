The bigger Netflix gets, the easier it is to miss stuff. This is true of all digital platforms, of course, but it’s especially true of Netflix of late, as the streaming service’s lineup now also includes a growing library of reality shows, live-programming, and even games. It makes it easy for some of the best shows to zoom right past you, unnoticed — which almost happened to me with Midnight Diner.
Netflix’s coziest show has made a very quiet return
A new season of Midnight Diner: Tokyo Stories is streaming now, though it’s easy to miss.
If you haven’t seen it, Midnight Diner is probably the most comforting thing available on Netflix. The show follows the patrons of a cozy little restaurant tucked away in the backstreets of Shinjuku, which is only open during the unusual hours of midnight to 7:00AM. This, in turn, attracts a unique blend of customers, who help each other out under the watchful eye of the shop’s owner (Kaoru Kobayashi), known only as “master.” The show aired for three seasons on Japanese TV, before being picked up by Netflix for a handful more under the subtitle Tokyo Stories.
What’s especially notable about the show’s return is the huge gap: season 2 of the Netflix iteration streamed in 2019, and it took seven years for it to come back. The break was so long that season 3’s first episode, which premiered in October, is about the restaurant’s struggles during the pandemic. There are a bunch of cute scenes of the diner’s regulars sneaking outside to check if the restaurant is open yet, and wondering if it will ever be able to. If nothing else, the break was a chance for the reliable master to finally take a vacation.
But once Midnight Diner’s new season gets going, it’s right back to everything that made it beloved. Each episode uses a different dish as a way of exploring a particular story. An overworked manga artist imagines the life he missed out on over plates of spicy konjac, while a pair of old friends reconnect while munching on pork cutlet sandwiches. What’s remarkable about the show is the way it balances being a charming, comforting watch with exploring truly difficult and heartbreaking topics. It’s the combination of sweetness and sadness that makes it work so well. It’s as likely to make you laugh as it is to make you cry.
And unlike most Netflix series, the whole season isn’t streaming all at once. Instead, new episodes come out every Tuesday; so far six of a planned 10 episodes are available. It’s a welcome way to spend an evening, as the show’s slow pace and warm sense of humanity can feel like all-too rare traits in the current streaming landscape.
But the main reason I’m even writing about Midnight Diner’s return is that it has been so easy to miss. There’s been next to no promotion for it — all I could find was a trailer on Netflix’s Japanese YouTube channel — and I only happened upon it accidentally after the initial premiere while browsing aimlessly through Netflix’s new releases. This, despite being someone who has watched and rewatched the previous seasons multiple times. You’d think I’d get a notification or something. Netflix has always been full of hidden gems, but now that it’s busier than ever, those gems are all the more hidden.
