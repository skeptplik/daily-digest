Holidave, the stalwart leader of SeaWorld’s jump-roping toy soldiers troupe, is known for his ability to jump rope while sitting down, “a skill only shared by 0.1 percent of Earth’s population.”
He’s the most “laid-back” of all the toy soldiers, allowing fellow toy soldier Jackary Jingleson to take center stage. And “experts” and “recent polling data,” “fans believe it is Holidave who holds the most whimsy” out of all the SeaWorld toy soldiers.
This is all according to AI slop videos posted by @wejumprope_memes, a fan page devoted to the group of masked professional jump ropers, who perform an annual holiday show at SeaWorld Orlando. Per toy soldier lore, in addition to Jackary Jingleson there’s also Sugarsnookie, Soulja Toy, Big Chestnut, and Frosty McSleigh. And all of the edits are the same: an AI-generated narrator recites stats about the toy soldiers, such as their net worth and their vertical leap, as if they were elite athletes and not niche theme park performers.
For the past two weeks, fan page videos featuring the jump-roping toy soldiers have been going viral, with @wejumprope_memes’ most popular video garnering 1.2 million views. In the comments sections, fans debate the merits and deficits of each toy soldier, declaring their allegiance to their favorite as if they were a ’90s boy band: “Jackery Jingleson is having a banner year! His speed is incomparable, his style undeniable, and his technique is utterly flawless,” reads one comment. Another disputes this, writing “Soulja Toy the underrated gem.”
Mostly, people seem confused as to why these videos keep showing up in their algorithm and why they keep watching them. “I have a PhD and lecture at a university. And yet here I am,” one commenter writes on a video about whether Major Bounce, a new toy soldier, will show up for the 2026 season. (In a November 20 Reel announcing “major news for the jump-roping toy soldier community,” he finally did.)
As it turns out, there’s a simple explanation for the virality. @wejumprope_memes is run by Devin Meek, an Orlando-based professional entertainer who works for WeFlip, a production company that produces acrobatics shows. But he’s now perhaps best known as his alter ego—Holidave.
What started just a few weeks ago as him making memes to make his coworkers laugh has metamorphosed into an internet sensation, with the toy soldiers amassing a genuine, and ardent, fandom. “I really just made these videos for my friends in the jump-roping community,” Meek tells WIRED. “But I guess they resonated with more people than I thought.”
The videos are piggybacking on a larger—and increasingly absurd—trend of seasonal theme park employees going viral. In September, as WIRED previously reported, SeaWorld Orlando enjoyed a bump in ticket sales thanks to Just One More Chapter, a BookTok-themed Halloween scare zone at SeaWorld Orlando that featured swaggering bad boys from the romantasy genre flirting with guests. And last month, women on TikTok swooned over Archer and Fryboy, two performers at Hersheypark Halloween known for sliding around in kneepads wearing tactical streetwear and clown makeup, a practice known as “sliding.”
Meek, who has been jumping rope for the past 20 years, was inspired to create the videos featuring Sugarsnookie, Jackary, and the like after seeing the success of Archer and Fryboy, who have a combined more than 3.1 million followers on TikTok. “It was such an interesting phenomenon, because I’m somebody who is in a very similar position where I work a few months out of the year as a holiday-themed performer,” he says. “So I got a kick out of it.”
Meek already had an account, @wejumprope_memes, where he posted jump-roping fails and compilations. He posted one video on November 13 featuring old footage from SeaWorld Orlando, where he’s been performing with the other toy soldiers for the past six years. (The group got started during the height of the Covid pandemic—hence, why they wear masks during their shows.) “Fryboy who? Move over, Archer,” it begins, before introducing the lore of Jackery Jingleson, the most “light-hearted, nonchalant” of the toy soldiers.
To Meek’s surprise, the video received almost a million views and 588 comments, most of which were not from fellow members of the jumping community but from total strangers. So Meek posted more toy soldier lore on his meme page, introducing Big Chestnut (“a powerful jumper”), Soulja Toy (the developer of the “Christmas Ice Storm 261”), Sugarsnookie (“the girl’s girl of the group,” who is dating Big Chestnut) and Frosty McSleigh (“he has the fastest salute time of all the toy soldiers.”)
Though the lore is fictional, it’s also based on the jumpers’ actual skills and personalities: the jumpers who play Big Chestnut and Sugarsnookie are actually in a long-term relationship, and Jackery Jingleson, perhaps the most popular of the group, once held the world record for the fastest jump roper in the United States. “Honestly, that makes [the popularity of the memes] even funnier to us,” he says.
Though some fans have speculated in the comments that the toy soldier memes are a marketing campaign for SeaWorld Orlando, Meek denies that’s the case: He doesn’t tag SeaWorld in the comments, nor has he been in contact with the theme park about his page. (SeaWorld Orlando did not respond to WIRED’s request for comment.)
“This is something that is so targeted to people who are internet-brained and have this ironic sense of humor,” he says. “And that’s exactly the type of person who’ll tune out immediately if they feel like they’re being sold something on the internet.”
Others have even speculated that there is an actual feud brewing between the SeaWorld Orlando “jumpers” and the Southern California–based “sliders,” though Meek says the rivalry is playful, and he has even reached out to collab with them. (The sliders may view it differently: So far, he says, they “don’t seem super interested” in joining forces.)
What has surprised Meek the most, however, is that very few commenters suspect that the toy soldiers themselves are behind the memes. In fact, he’s been shocked by how many organic meme fan pages have popped up within the past week, with people drawing their own fan art of Holidave saluting or Jackery Jingleson jumping. WeFlip has also been deluged with business inquiries: “People are like, how can we get Jackery Jingleson out here?”
Essentially, what started as an inside joke within an extremely niche community has evolved into an inside joke for the internet at large. “I think people like the feeling of being in on a joke and being a part of it and building it up,” he says.
So what’s next for the SeaWorld Orlando jump-roping toy soldiers? The rotating cast typically performs at the park on the weekends, ramping up appearances as it gets closer to the holiday season. Meek also shares that the toy soldiers will “likely cross state lines” in the coming months, including a stint at the Christmas market in Cullman, Alabama. Frosty McSleigh, renowned for his 0.08-second salute, plus a slew of new, yet-to-be-introduced toy soldiers will also make an appearance.
Though it may come as a disappointment to some that the roots of the toy soldier phenomenon were inorganic, Meek isn’t concerned. “The people are real. The athleticism is real. The lore is real,” he says. “For all of these people, jump rope is their life, and we’re super-dedicated to teaching it and sharing it. And I think people can feel that.”
