The US Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Crime and Counterterrorism held a hearing this week to examine child safety on Roblox and consider a bipartisan bill that would allow families and victims to bypass forced arbitration.
As Bloomberg reports, the No Forced Arbitration for Child Sexual Exploitation and Cyber Injuries Act, introduced by Senators Josh Hawley and Dick Durbin, would allow those affected to pursue legal action in public court, even if they previously agreed to private arbitration in a company's terms of use.
In response, Roblox global head of public policy Nicky Jackson Colaço told Wired: "Safety has been central to Roblox since we launched. We require age checks for chat, place users under 16 in age-based accounts with protective defaults, and block images and video in chat."
"A legal position about arbitration is not a measure of a company's safety record, and a hearing built around it fails to advance safety. Protecting children online takes stronger protections, better tools, and consistent standards that apply everywhere kids spend time online."
During the hearing, Senator Hawley criticised Roblox's child safety record, stating it failed to address issues because doing so would "threaten their user count, and therefore their revenues."
"If your children are on Roblox, they are being exposed in all likelihood to sexual predators," he added.
Roblox CEO David Baszucki declined to testify in court. In a letter obtained by Bloomberg, he stated that "Roblox continues to welcome the discussion of how online platforms protect young people online."
"Online safety cannot be solved by any one company. Bad actors move between platforms, and a child's online life spans many apps, games, and services."
The US Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Crime and Counterterrorism began its investigation into Roblox in August, requesting records related to child safety, sexual or physical abuse, bullying, and financial exploitation.
In a letter to Baszucki, Hawley and Durbin said the "alleged criminal activity on Roblox warrants congressional scrutiny."
"You trumpet Roblox as a safe digital playground for children as young as five," the senators wrote. "But the public record raises serious questions about this promise of safety."
"Over the past decade, police have arrested numerous individuals for committing serious acts of violence and harm against children they met via Roblox, including grooming, sexual abuse, kidnapping, rape, and even murder [...] children on your platform are hurting. Congress will not look the other way."
Roblox has implemented several new safety tools in recent years, such as restricting users under 13 from direct messaging, introducing global facial recognition checks, adding a "sensitive issues" content tag for experiences, and strengthening moderation policies and actions.
