Texas-based antique and design show Marburger Farm has unveiled an exhibition building made partly from salvaged materials sourced directly from across the 43-acre property, as shown in this video produced by Dezeen.
Called Gage Hall, the building’s facade is made from reclaimed timber sourced from around the farm and is topped with a traditional Texas metal roof and contemporary skylight elements that bring natural light into the hall.
The hall brings an additional 7,500 square feet of shopping space to Marburger Farm, bringing the fair’s total space to 100,000 square feet. Designed to honour the site’s dairy farm heritage, the hall also showcases its vision for the future.
The antique and design show has become a destination for international designers looking to source unique interior objects including furniture, lighting and vintage textiles.
The 43-acre show is located in Round Top, Texas, and has attracted designers such as Kelly Wearstler and Leanne Ford.
The fair offers visitors access to objects from over 200 independent dealers and has become one of the largest antique shows in the United States.
The site also includes a Texas-style town square, 10 restored historic buildings, four tents and a 150-year-old saloon, which has been restored to its original use as a social space and bar.
Founded in 1997, the show has grown to attract over 30,000 people annually.
The antique show takes place in January, March and October each year.
Marburger Farm is owned by Brook Partners, a Dallas-based investment company focusing on culture and commerce.
The company also runs the upcoming Dallas Art Fair, which is held from 15 to 18 April 2027 in Dallas, Texas. To learn more, visit the Marburger Farm website.
