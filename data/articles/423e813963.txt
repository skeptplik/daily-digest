Does the EU need to invest more in data centres? To become a key player in artificial intelligence, it needs a lot more computing infrastructure to get there.
The European Union’s latest move is the AI Gigafactories initiative, which could mobilise over €30 billion in public and private investment to build large AI computing facilities.
The need is growing. The bloc had around 2,269 data centres in 2025, less than half the US total of 5,427. It also had around 12.4 gigawatts of data-centre capacity, almost 3 GW below estimated demand.
Data centres are buildings filled with servers that store and process information behind everyday services, from online banking and hospitals to public transport and streaming. Having them closer to users can make digital services faster and more reliable, while making it easier to comply with European privacy laws.
Data centres also attract major investment in Europe's digital infrastructure and create short- and medium-term jobs.
But there is a catch. Data centres consume huge amounts of electricity, water and land. In Ireland, they accounted for 23% of metered electricity consumption in 2025, up from 5% in 2015. Some regions now restrict new connections because their grids cannot keep up. Cooling servers also requires water, particularly problematic in drought-prone areas.
Then there is the cost. In 2025, average industrial electricity prices in the EU were roughly twice those in the US, making European facilities more expensive to operate. Building them also requires land, power lines, fibre-optic cables and expensive cooling systems.
Unless electricity comes from low-carbon sources, expanding data centres can increase emissions. The challenge is finding enough energy, space and money to support growth without putting too much pressure on local communities and the environment.
