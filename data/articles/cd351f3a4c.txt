Teletubbies, Numberblocks and Hey Duggee are among a host of the BBC’s preschool shows to be made available on Netflix, as the broadcaster battles to arrest a generational shift away from its programming.
In an unprecedented six-month trial, a dedicated CBeebies hub will be set up on the world’s biggest streaming service, with a substantial selection of 24 shows to choose from. It will include some of the BBC’s best-known titles and characters.
BBC executives said the deal was a “win-win”, allowing the BBC to reach more families and handing Netflix a windfall of quality children’s programming. It marks the BBC’s latest deal with an “off-platform” provider, having already announced plans to make content designed for YouTube. The shows will be available on Netflix globally apart from in China, New Zealand and South Africa.
While parents and children still report appreciating the BBC’s children’s content, a huge shift to global streaming and digital platforms means gen alpha – people born between 2010 and 2025 – are simply not as reliant on the broadcaster as previous generations.
Linear broadcast television reached just a third of children aged four to 15 in an average week last year, down from 54% in 2022.
Meanwhile, the BBC’s iPlayer has been falling behind. More than 90% of children aged three to 16 say they like the services offered by YouTube, Netflix and Disney. Only 65% of child users say they like using the iPlayer.
The CBeebies deal also helps Netflix, which is itself under pressure from its rival Disney, which has a younger audience, and YouTube. Children in the UK aged four to 15 watch the most YouTube of any age group at home across all devices, averaging 61 minutes a day.
Children are now heading to YouTube from the moment they turn on the television. Netflix is close behind, though BBC One remains in the top five of first TV destinations.
The new Netflix deal will also strengthen the argument that anyone watching a streaming service should pay the BBC’s licence fee. That proposal is expected to be announced by the government later this year, following growing concerns that the BBC’s current licence fee model will soon mean the broadcaster is unsustainable without radically reducing its output and remit.
The CBeebies hub will be trialled on Netflix for six months from the start of December. Some CBeebies shows have previously featured on the streamer, but the trial marks an unprecedented expansion.
Patricia Hidalgo, the director of BBC children and education, who has previously raised concerns about the lack of culturally relevant content reaching young children, said it was an opportunity to increase “the amount of content that is homegrown and telling British stories to British children”.
The BBC is now engaging in more deals with streamers and digital services, as it seeks to increase commercial income and remain relevant as audiences fragment.
Hidalgo said the trial would provide a “taster” to Netflix subscribers and would be assessed after six months to see if it was helping the BBC reach new audiences and whether they understood it was BBC content.
Anne Mensah, the head of content for Netflix in the UK, said CBeebies held a “special and unrivalled place in the hearts of UK families and we are delighted to bring that love to all our Netflix homes in the UK and beyond”.
