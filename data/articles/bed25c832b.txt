The government conducted a crisis management drill assuming the first domestic Ebola case on Tuesday, in light of the World Health Organization’s international emergency declaration regarding the ongoing outbreak in Congo.
The government has held an annual infectious disease response drill since 2023, and this year’s drill was the first to address the highly lethal disease.
Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, related Cabinet members and the head of the Japan Institute for Health Security participated in the drill.
During the drill, participants checked initial responses following an imaginary report that an Ebola patient had been confirmed in the southwestern prefecture of Fukuoka, which has an international airport.
The prime minister instructed officials to make every effort to prevent the virus from spreading, provide information based on scientific knowledge and ensure the safety and security of people.
Ebola has a fatality rate of 25% to 90% and can be transmitted through contact with a patient’s body fluids. In Japan, the disease is classified as the most dangerous category under the infectious disease control law.
In Congo, more than 5,500 people have died amid the Ebola outbreak since May.
