SAP has confirmed to BleepingComputer that it took urgent action to protect its customers after detecting suspicious activity in its IT environment.
The German software giant said its evidence so far does not show downstream compromise of customer systems or data.
Based in Walldorf, Germany, SAP is one of the world’s largest enterprise software vendors, with 110,000 employees in 157 countries, 300 million subscribers, and over $40 billion in annual revenue.
Der Spiegel reported earlier today, based on an internal SAP memo it saw, that the software firm was hit by a cyberattack it tracks under the code name “Project Granite.”
The software company reportedly asked all employees to keep the project details confidential.
According to the German outlet, sources within the company said the attackers breached SAP’s GitHub environment, possibly with help from AI tools.
Insider sources say that the attacker managed to steal authentication tokens from SAP employees and leveraged them to copy and also modify SAP source code on GitHub.
BleepingComputer has contacted SAP for a statement on the reported breach, and the company acknowledged the incident and said it had taken steps to protect its customers.
“SAP has identified suspicious activity and promptly implemented the necessary measures to protect its customers and preserve the security of its services,” a company representative told BleepingComputer.
“Based on our investigation to date, we have found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data.”
“Our customers trust us to protect their data, and that remains our highest priority. In a highly dynamic threat environment, we will continue to strengthen our security to provide the secure and resilient environment our customers depend on.”
SAP did not specify the environments where the suspicious activity was observed, or whether customers downloading code from its GitHub repositories or performing updates that are directly fetched or indirectly fed by those repositories should take any action.
BleepingComputer has asked SAP about this, but a spokesperson declined to share any further information.
Meanwhile, Germany’s Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) issued a public warning yesterday about agentic AI tools that empower cyberattacks, such as ARTEX, CAIRN, and PentAGI.
BSI cautioned organizations in the country to focus on responding to security incidents and ensure they follow all recommended security-strengthening practices.
This warning comes shortly after the national cybersecurity agencies in Switzerland and Italy reported the first detections of large-scale AI-driven cyberattacks targeting organizations in the critical infrastructure sector.
