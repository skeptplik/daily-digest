In FocusHong Kong helps mainland Chinese firms go global – but can they really win now?
From workplace customs to legal hurdles, mainland firms are adapting abroad as Hong Kong offers advice, connections and financial services
When Shenzhen-listed digital infrastructure provider Runjian Corporation expanded into the Middle East and Southeast Asia through Hong Kong, adapting meant more than translating software interfaces or navigating local data privacy laws.
It also meant changing the company’s working practices and abandoning the expectation, carried over from its domestic operations, that one mainland Chinese employee could match the output of three local hires.
“When expanding overseas, companies must do more than just take their products and technology abroad. We also need to respect local culture and truly integrate into the local market,” said Athena Zhao Hua, Runjian’s vice-president and president of its overseas business group. “When in Rome, you have to align with the local rhythm.”
