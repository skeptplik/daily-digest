N-able has patched a critical server-side request forgery (SSRF) vulnerability discovered during its investigation into a cyberattack targeting its Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud services last week.
On November 16, the company disabled access to the portal and API and suspended related services, including logging, mailbox synchronization, the management portal, and quarantine, noting that email delivery was not affected.
Two days later, it confirmed that the services were targeted in a cyberattack and that the hackers hit a web server and likely accessed certain credentials stored there.
The company immediately initiated response and remediation actions, contained the incident, changed credentials, and revoked API tokens and OAuth tokens.
On November 21, N-able restored access to the Mail Assure and SpamExperts dashboard and APIs, while continuing to monitor the environments. The company says it has found no evidence of exploitation beyond the previously identified N-able-hosted server.
On Sunday, N-able revealed that it has patched an SSRF vulnerability identified during its investigation. Tracked as CVE-2026-134624 (CVSS score of 9.3), the flaw exists because the vulnerable service “did not restrict the destination of the LDAP synchronization connection check performed when a relay is configured”.
The flaw could allow an API user with sufficient privileges to make the vulnerable server connect to arbitrary internal hosts and ports, identify those that are reachable, and send crafted data to internal services.
“N-able has remediated the recent security incident affecting our Mail Assure and SpamExperts environments and restored the impacted functionality. Our investigation to date indicates that exploitation was confined to a single N-able hosted server and continued monitoring since remediation has found no further malicious activity,” the company told SecurityWeek.
N-able also noted that its investigation into the incident continues, adding that it would provide further details on the incident in a formal root cause analysis within the next two weeks.
Mail Assure/SpamExperts version 20261121.1943 contains patches for the security defect.
