The district of Katsushika seems typical of Tokyo’s unglamorous east side at first glance: a jumble of old and new shopping streets, narrow alleyways, a few gardens and temples. Aging workshops and factories show the area’s industrial roots. The Tokyo Detention House, which houses high-security inmates and execution facilities, looms along the Arakawa river.
It’s now the unlikely epicenter of a historic property boom that’s reshaping the Japanese capital.
Rents in Katsushika, one of Tokyo’s 23 wards, have surged 23.9% in five years, the most of anywhere in the city, according to a Bloomberg analysis of exclusive data from property portal estie. That comes after decades where prices barely budged throughout Japan’s long battle with deflation, and a renter could expect their housing costs to never rise.
