If you've played the first 20 minutes or so of Grand Theft Auto 6, you'll know there's a hugely significant plot revelation there - one Rockstar somehow managed to keep entirely secret until the game's launch.
It's somewhat miraculous they did, given the many leaks the studio has suffered until now. And while, frankly, the embargo restrictions around discussing it in our GTA 6 review made a deeper analysis of Jason and Lucia's story more than a little awkward, I'm also mostly glad it remained a surprise. It's one of the great twists delivered in a game of this kind.
Speaking to Eurogamer in an interview shortly before GTA 6 was released, Rockstar Games' head of development Rob Nelson discussed the secret and, notably, the fact the team also discussed going another way entirely.
Spoilers for GTA 6's opening half hour - and further backstory around that moment from later in the game - below.
The big twist of course is that Jason and Lucia have a young child, Tomás, who we see Lucia abandoning in order to rob a store at gunpoint during the game's prologue.
It's an astonishing moment, both in the complexity it immediately lends Lucia, who is at once painted in a more negative light as a result, and in the stakes it affords her and Jason. This is the first time GTA's protagonists have genuine, human stakes that underpin their actions during the game's story, and at the same time Rockstar, in doing so, opens a route for itself to offering a far more nuanced take on modern crime.
But it was also something of a risk – and a hotly debated one, as Nelson told me during our conversation – as giving the game's protagonists that kind of narrative weight ran somewhat counter to the crime movie, straight-to-the-action thrills of previous GTAs.
"I'm so grateful that we managed to keep that under wraps," Nelson said. The subversion of expectations was intentional. "You feel like you maybe know these characters, or what the game or the story is going to be about, and then in the first 10, 15 minutes, it takes on a completely different context."It was an idea that came about organically. "But the idea of the character wanting something that maybe is at odds with what you thought you wanted as a player coming in, which is amassing as much material wealth as possible, and as many cars, and as many things," he said, was something Rockstar found particularly compelling.
The next point of the studio's focus then became the justification for Lucia's decision, Nelson explained. "The story was always, broadly, going to go through some of the major beats that it did," once Rockstar decided on the key points of the protagonists being a couple and them having a child. "But the details of the relationship, the narrative details of it, we dialled in a lot - because we're doing a lot there. She's leaving a kid early on – that's not a very sympathetic move to make."
That led to the decision to show, later in the story, more about why Lucia opted to leave Tomás in that moment. "Many chapters later, where [Jason] is like: 'What? Why did you do that?' And she has a flashback to sort of being at her wit's end," Nelson said, which then flows into the question of why she left Jason in the first place. "Cal asks Jason, 'Why did she leave?' And so, well, she didn't know she was pregnant at the time. 'Okay, but why didn't she call you?' 'Well, because I might've told her I didn't want [to be a father].' So you have to just make sure that you can understand both of their perspectives."
The tension from the fact they had a child, and that the two of them were away from their son as you play the game, was significant enough to become a point of discussion within the studio.
"Multiple times throughout development, we had this child, and people are like, 'Can it not be, like, theirs?'" Nelson said. "'Could it be like her little sister? Could it be– because I really feel bad having a good time in Vice City…' and the questions are valid," he continued. "But it wouldn't be powerful if not. So it was up to us to figure out how [you resolve that tension]."
Like with Lucia's justification for leaving Tomás - revealed in a striking dream-slash-flashback later in the game, that mixes typically exaggerated Rockstar satire with genuine social commentary - this then led to a cascade of other character moments. "How about he's in a better position than he would ever be with them, and so he's with her aunt, who's way more stable, and has a loving husband and other little kids, and he's always having a good time?" Nelson said.
He points to a conversation between Jason and Lucia in the game's first chapter as another example. "Very early on, in chapter one, they have this chat where he's like, 'Let's FaceTime - FruitFace is the thing we use - and she's like, 'No, I don't want to confuse him. He's better off until we get our shit together.' And he's like 'Okay' and she's like, 'It's not a cop-out', and he's like, 'No, no, I met my father on the other side of a glass divider, so I'm kind of trying to break that cycle.' And that's right in chapter one, and so it's a thread all the way through."
The result, then, is that the decision to add Tomás ultimately shaped many of the key parts of Jason and Lucia's characters, from their relationships with their own parents – Jason's being in jail, and Lucia's mother too narcissistic to help – to what is, really, the entire narrative arc of the story. And rightly so; a child is a big deal, and one of many reasons why GTA 6 is able to tap into something deeper than any of the previous games in the series before.
