Kazakh ransomware hacking suspect extradited from Italy
An affiliate of the INC and Lynx ransomware strains appeared in a Tennessee court on Monday after being extradited from Italy.
Roman Khlynovskiy, a 43-year-old from Kazakhstan, is accused of launching ransomware attacks against U.S. schools, hospitals and companies.
Khlynovskiy is facing a maximum penalty of 27 years in prison on computer fraud, money laundering, wire fraud, extortion and identity theft charges.
Prosecutors accused him of being a member of a cybercriminal group called the “8-Digits Team” that launched ransomware attacks on behalf of the larger INC and Lynx ransomware gangs.
The team’s cyber intrusions led to $500 million in ransom demands and they claimed to have stolen 50 terabytes of data consisting of more than 70 million confidential health records.
FBI director Kash Patel said U.S. agencies worked with Italian law enforcement to extradite Khlynovskiy.
“The defendant is charged with participating in one of the largest healthcare data breaches in decades,” said Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva. “The indictment alleges a conspiracy that targeted hospitals and healthcare providers, stole vast quantities of sensitive patient information, and then sought to profit by threatening some of the most vulnerable members of our society. These alleged crimes exploited the deeply personal medical information of millions of people.”
Khlynovskiy made his first federal court appearance in Knoxville, Tennessee, on Monday.
Members of the 8-Digits Team attacked companies in at least six states, often targeting schools and hospitals. The hackers breached networks, stole data and extorted victims, demanding payments in exchange for not publishing the data.
The INC ransomware gang took credit for a 2024 ransomware attack on prominent Michigan healthcare system McLaren Health Care — which was forced to cancel dozens of surgeries and operate without critical digital systems for days.
The group also caused chaos last year with an attack on the Dutch conglomerate behind several major U.S. supermarket brands.
The INC gang emerged in 2023 and attacked the State Bar of Texas, Yamaha Motor and printer manufacturer Xerox.
According to Palo Alto Networks and Group-IB, INC either rebranded to Lynx or Lynx actors purchased the group’s source code.
