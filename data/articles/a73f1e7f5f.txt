Magic: The Gathering has never been shy about crossing over with other beloved franchises, and its latest Universes Beyond set takes players into the final frontier. The Star Trek set pulls together characters, spacecraft, alien civilizations, and iconic moments from across the franchise's decades-long history, translating them into Magic’s mechanics. It's an ambitious crossover that gives longtime Trekkies plenty to get excited about, even if UB sets haven't always been embraced by Magic's more traditional player base.
One of the most interesting aspects of the set is how it translates Star Trek's science fiction into Magic's established gameplay. Newer mechanics like Station, first introduced in Edge of Eternities, allow players to build up spacecraft before sending them into battle, while Beam Me Up offers a clever way to bring cards back from the graveyard. Between legendary captains, familiar alien species, and Sagas inspired by some of the franchise's most memorable episodes, there's a surprising level of variety throughout the collection — and you don't even have to chase the biggest mythic rares to find some genuinely clever designs.
The following 10 uncommon cards from the new Star Trek set might not be game changers or instant bans, but they definitely deserve a closer look given their surprising strength.
10 70,000 Light-Years from Home
Star Trek: Voyager builds its entire premise around one of the franchise's most daunting challenges: an impossible journey home. Stranded in the Delta Quadrant, Captain Janeway and her crew must navigate uncharted space, forge uneasy alliances with unfamiliar civilizations, and survive countless dangers as they face a decades-long journey back to the Alpha Quadrant.
Capturing this moment is Magic’s own 70,000 Light-Years from Home, a two-mana white sorcery that lets you exile a target creature or spacecraft at the cost of letting the target’s owner search their library for a basic land and put it into their hand. It’s a generous trade-off for getting rid of a troublesome permanent, but the ability to remove virtually any creature or spacecraft for just two mana makes this an excellent addition to almost any white deck. It’s also a lovely tribute to one of the franchise's most enduring series.
9 Beckett Mariner, Impetuous Ensign
Star Trek: Lower Decks' Beckett Mariner has never been interested in following Starfleet protocol, and her Magic card attempts to capture her rebellious streak through an interesting tap and damage ability. As a 2/1 creature with first strike, Beckett Mariner, Impetuous Ensign gets a promotion counter every time she taps, whether that be for attacking or activating another ability — it doesn’t matter. Once she accumulates three promotion counters, she deals two damage to every opponent.
As a seasoned, notoriously insubordinate Ensign aboard the U.S.S. Cerritos (and the secret daughter of Captain Carol Freeman), Mariner spent five seasons turning mundane second-contact missions into chaotic adventures before finally earning a promotion to Lieutenant Junior Grade. Her promotion counters and damage cycle cleverly mirrors her perpetual habit of getting promoted just high enough to cause trouble before inevitably getting knocked back down the ranks. By turning routine tap decisions into a steady source of table-wide damage, this uncommon proves a genuine multiplayer threat that embodies the exact comedic energy of Mike McMahan's animated series.
8 V'Ger, the Intruder
One of the most fearsome antagonists in the original Star Trek film series, V'Ger, the Intruder descends into Magic as a 5/7 legendary artifact construct creature, replete with flying and an enter or attack trigger. Whichever option you choose allows you to either take a peek at an opponent's hand and draw a card or give all creatures your opponents control -1/-0 until your next turn. Both options are relatively useful, most especially when you're trying to get additional value out of an expensive creature.
V'Ger isn’t exactly the cheapest card to play, but it certainly makes an impression. Its 5/7 stats make it a formidable threat once it gets going, but seven mana drives a hard bargain unless you’re running tons of ramp. Fortunately, it isn’t classified as a spacecraft, so you don’t have to worry about any charge counters to make it a creature, and if its hefty mana cost feels like a major burden, you could always get it out for free with a Dr. Eggman on the field.
7 Rutherford, Upgraded Ensign
A surprisingly delightful addition to the Star Trek set is Rutherford, Upgrade Ensign, the same lovable engineer from Star Trek: Lower Decks. The three-mana white legendary creature comes with a useful enters-the-battlefield (ETB) trigger that lets you look at the top six cards of your library and put an artifact into your hand. That’s a free card for essentially playing a creature, an ability you can abuse with blink-focused cards like Flickerwisp and Ephemerate.
True to his engineering smarts, Rutherford also lets you drop a +1/+1 on a target creature whenever you cast a Spacecraft. This makes it particularly useful in artifact decks built around the set's new Station mechanic, allowing you to develop your board while preparing your ships for battle, like the U.S.S. Enterprise-D, Galaxy-Class; Queen's Diamond, and Deep Space 9. It's a fitting design for a character whose engineering expertise is often the difference between a successful mission and a complete disaster, even if it’s not the strongest ability on the battlefield.
6 Impossible Choice
Some of the best Star Trek stories revolve around impossible moral decisions, which the aptly named Impossible Choice puts directly into players' hands. This three-mana black instant offers you one of only two options: Surgical Separation destroys a target creature, but its controller receives two 1/1 red Officer tokens in return, while Remain Fused lets you draw two cards while all players lose two life.
No matter which option you choose, you’ll always be faced with a consequence, which makes the card's design feel fittingly Star Trek. It’s also an awesome nod to the infamous Voyager episode “Tuvix,” which involves Lieutenant Tuvok and Neelix getting fused in a transporter accident. The card's mechanics, capturing the same no-win scenario from the episode, force you to either execute a surgical separation that leaves behind two officers or live with the draining consequences of keeping the fusion intact.
5 U.S.S. Excelsior, Cutting Edge
A well-timed Starfleet arrival can turn the tide of even the most desperate battles, and the U.S.S. Excelsior, Cutting Edge brings that same feeling to Magic’s battlefield as a four-drop legendary white spacecraft. Upon entering, Cutting Edge immediately destroys an opponent’s tapped creature and even becomes a flying 4/4 once you get its station ability up to 8 charge counters. It’s certainly not the most powerful artifact from Star Trek, but it is definitely one of the more interesting uncommons, especially when thinking in terms of Panharmonicon, Strionic Resonator, and Elesh Norn, Mother of Machines.
It's a fun representation of the franchise's iconic vessel, and a particularly satisfying example of how Magic translates Star Trek's spacecraft into playable cards. The set is filled with a variety of awesome spaceships, from the similarly ETB-focused Deep Space 9 to the free-drawing U.S.S. Enterprise-D, Galaxy-Class.
4 Locutus, the One Who Speaks
Resistance is futile, and Locutus is here to make sure your opponents understand exactly why. As one of the most recognizable figures from The Next Generation, Locutus represents Jean-Luc Picard after his assimilation by the Borg during the iconic "Best of Both Worlds" storyline. Magic represents the character as a modest two-mana 1/1 white and black legendary artifact creature, and its presence gives every other artifact creature you control a +1/+1 bonus.
Locutus, the One Who Speaks also has an activated ability that lets you discard the card to deal two damage to a target creature while gaining two life, a slightly more vampiric ability that sits nicely within the color scheme. The card is a neat representation of the Borg collective, turning one seemingly insignificant unit into a powerful part of a much larger machine, like the Borg Cube. Just don't expect your opponents to surrender without a fight.
3 In the Pale Moonlight
Benjamin Sisko's moral descent in "In the Pale Moonlight" remains one of Deep Space Nine's finest storytelling achievements. Desperate to bring the Romulans into the Dominion War, Sisko becomes increasingly comfortable with deception, manipulation, and ultimately becoming an accessory to murder. By the episode's end, Sisko's chilling confession that he can live with the blood on his hands, followed by his decision to erase the entire personal log, cements his transformation into a wartime pragmatist willing to sacrifice his own principles to protect the Alpha Quadrant.
Magic attempts to capture Sisko's complicated story through a five-mana black saga enchantment named after the episode, In the Pale Moonlight. The Saga plays out across three chapters: The first makes each opponent sacrifice a nontoken creature of their choice, the second lets you investigate, and the third lets you sacrifice an artifact or creature to create two 2/2 black Romulan tokens.
It's a solid mechanical interpretation of the episode, particularly in its final chapter, which asks you to sacrifice something valuable to secure the Romulans' allegiance. Sometimes, the ends really do justify the means, at least when the fate of an entire quadrant hangs in the balance and you're willing to live with the consequences of getting your hands dirty.
2 Khaaaaaaaaaaaannn!
One of the set's most entertaining red instants, Khaaaaaaaaaaaannn! turns an iconic Star Trek moment into a surprisingly useful removal spell. For X plus one red mana, it deals twice X damage to a target creature, exiling it instead if it would die during the turn. It’s a lot like Red Sun’s Zenith and Burn from Within, offering a flexible effect that helps you deal with everything from early-game nuisances to far larger threats later on.
The card's name and flavor text are also absolutely perfect, referencing one of the most recognizable moments in cinematic science fiction. Captain Kirk's furious scream of Khan's name in Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan has become a pop culture staple, later echoed in Star Trek: Into Darkness, with Magic capturing the sheer absurdity and intensity of the original scene in its art.
1 The Measure of a Man
Is Data a person, or is he simply a machine designed to imitate one? That's the central question behind "The Measure of a Man," one of the most celebrated episodes of Star Trek: The Next Generation. After Starfleet attempts to claim ownership of Data and authorize his dismantling, Captain Picard takes the case to court to defend his android officer's right to self-determination.
Magic turns this landmark Star Trek episode into a three-chapter white Saga that temporarily exiles a target nontoken artifact or creature, then creates a Clue token, and eventually returns the exiled permanent to the battlefield while gaining you two life. It's a wonderfully thoughtful adaptation of the episode's central conflict, and the decision to make the card an artifact-focused Saga feels especially appropriate given Data's nature. Paired with cards like Padeem, Consul of Innovation; The Reality Chip; and Deep Space 9, The Measure of a Man can slot neatly into artifact-focused decks that reward you for recycling valuable permanents and generating additional value.
