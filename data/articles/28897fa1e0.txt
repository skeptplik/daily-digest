Cameron Bancroft claims the entire Australian cricket team knew about their 2018 sandpaper plot, in an explosive article that will cast another dark cloud over the Test squad.
Bancroft, who was banned with Steve Smith and David Warner over the tactic, said the entire team knew about the plan despite recent claims from current Test skipper Pat Cummins to the contrary.
"It was not just the three of us — Steve Smith, David Warner and I — who knew about me using sandpaper on the ball in South Africa. It was the whole team," Bancroft wrote in an article that originally appeared in the West Australian before being sent out to all media.
"I withheld important details, including when the use of sandpaper began, how long it went on for, and the involvement and awareness of others.
"The final straw for me to write this account was Australian cricket captain Pat Cummins's press conference."
"It made me feel angry, betrayed and alone," Bancroft wrote of Cummins's response.
"What I saw, experienced and lived with — not only him, but everyone else on that tour in South Africa — is not the public account he gave in his press conference and in the years previously when he has spoken on this time."
In a statement to the ABC, Pat Cummins' agent said:
"Whilst we feel for Cam and everything he's been through there's nothing in the statement that hasn't been answered by Pat in his press conference."
Bancroft said he recalled having several conversations with the bowlers on that tour about how the ball was moving.
"I recall conversations with all the fast bowlers Pat Cummins, Mitchell Starc, Josh Hazlewood and Mitchell Marsh about whether it was reverse swinging enough," he wrote.
"I also spoke with Tim Paine, who was in a key position behind the stumps to observe how the ball was moving. I wanted and needed feedback.

"How else am I to know if what I'm doing is effective or not if I don't have these conversations with the bowlers bowling with it and the wicketkeeper?
"All the bowlers gave me feedback if my work on the ball was working. It was helpful."
Bancroft also said he had received "wraps and encouragement" from players and coaches regarding his work on the ball, naming former bowling coach David Saker, while also suggesting coach Darren Lehmann was aware of what was happening.
"This event is crystal clear in my mind. Not only for the praise I received. But I also remember a very different reaction," he wrote.
"Our media manager, Kate Hutchison, expressed her objection to what was happening.
"I recall her making it clear that she believed it was wrong, that she wanted nothing to do with it, and walking away from the discussion.
"I will never forget that. Somebody had spoken up."
Bancroft said 12th man Peter Handscomb had rushed out to him to tell him to put the sandpaper away, on direction from Lehmann.
"He [Handscomb] said to me: 'I think it's time I take that off you now'. And Pete was doing his job after Boof shouted to him on the walkie talkie 'get the f****** sandpaper off Bangers now'," he wrote.
"In my opinion, if he didn't know, he wouldn't have said that.
"And if it wasn't clear enough before, then it was obvious now. Once again. WE ALL KNEW."
Speaking on ABC Sport's coverage of Australia's ODI clash with England on Wednesday night, Lehmann said he would take time to read Bancroft's article and form a response.
"It's a lot for everyone I presume, not just me," Lehmann said.
"There's no point rushing into anything that's said. I just want to make sure I'm really clear which way I'm going."
However, Warner and Khawaja immediately criticised the forum, with all conversations set to remain private.
"Are we going to get down to what the public want, [which] is the truth?" Warner said.
"Where do we actually end here, because it is upon the board to review what they want to be published. Yes that's a safe space, but is everyone able to talk?
"I would have liked an open public inquiry and the people that were there at the time can come forward and speak."
Cricket Australia released its own statement on Wednesday night addressing Bancroft's claims.
"We respect that Cameron Bancroft has told his story, and we will take time to consider his comments," the statement read.
"We expect that there will be many dimensions to the events of 2018, which is why we have established the forum for individuals to give their version of events.
"We are finalising the terms of reference and will commence the process as soon as possible."

Veteran cricket journalist Gideon Haigh said the Bancroft column puts captain Cummins in an uncomfortable position ahead of next month's home Test series against New Zealand.
"I think it's mortifying for him," Haigh told 730.
"We should keep in mind that he was the junior member of that pace ensemble. He maybe didn't feel confident about going against the flow, in the same way that Cameron Bancroft didn't feel confident about going against the flow.
"Bancroft talks about a meeting taking place afterwards where the use of sandpaper was freely discussed and what the team would say, and no-one speaking up, and no-one saying afterwards, 'I didn't know about this'."
"So Pat Cummins, presumably, remains silent in that meeting, and I guess now he's got to give an account about why."
Haigh said the Bancroft column came from a man who felt he "had carried a great burden" for over eight years.
"It's a statement from a cricketer who really has no reason to lie, in fact every good reason to keep his own counsel about this," Haigh said.
"He feels like a man who has carried a great burden for the past eight and a half years and perhaps there's some relief involved in sharing his story with a wider audience."
Haigh said then-coach Lehmann may have questions to answer given Bancroft's claims in the column that the former Test batsman was aware of the process.
"He even had a story at the end about [coach] Darren Lehmann sending Peter Handscomb onto the field at Cape Town and telling him to get the sandpaper off the field," Haigh said.
"Darren Lehmann denied specifically any knowledge of the [sandpaper] plans to CA's investigators in 2018, so it will be interesting to see if he has any response."
