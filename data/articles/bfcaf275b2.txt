The United States has confirmed it is investigating computing company Megaspeed, suspecting it of violating US export controls by helping to ship tech giant Nvidia's chips to China.
In a joint statement, the US Department of Justice and Department of Commerce said they had been investigating Singapore-based firm Megaspeed since 2024.
It comes after questions were raised about the possibility high-end AI chips may have ended up in China despite US export restrictions.
Concerns emerged in recent weeks after Nvidia founder Jensen Huang revealed he had an extramarital affair, allegedly with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang. US authorities suspect her of spying for Beijing, which China denies.
Jensen Huang resigned from his role as chief executive last week following revelations he had two affairs.
Megaspeed is the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in South East Asia and is suspected of helping divert advanced chips to China. The Singapore-based company said on Sunday it had launched an internal investigation into its business.
The justice and commerce departments said on Monday that "it is in the public interest to provide the facts and address inaccurate information" regarding the investigation.
Laying out a timeline, it said it began investigating Megaspeed in 2024 "for potential export control violations".
In May 2025, Department of Commerce investigators requested records from Nvidia "concerning direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed". In June 2025, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its 'no sell' list.
Agents from the Bureau of Industry and Security, in conjunction with Singaporean law enforcement officials, then conducted an on-site check at Megaspeed that month and interviewed Alice Huang, who was chief executive at the time.
Following this enforcement action, the Department of Justice took the lead role in the criminal investigation of Megaspeed.
The Nvidia scandal has created tensions between the US Treasury and Commerce departments over shipments of high-end chips to Megaspeed.
On Friday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed the US Commerce Department, led by Howard Lutnick, for failing to properly oversee the export of the chips.
Lutnick has been called to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month over his department's role in allowing Nvidia to export its advanced chips.
While Singapore does not prohibit the transfer of advanced AI chips to companies abroad, such moves can breach US export controls.
Huang has agreed to testify in Congress, including before the House Select Committee on China to discuss whether US national security was compromised by his affair.
The White House said on Monday it would further restrict China's access to AI chips from firms like Nvidia.
The reported affairs by Jensen Huang created a crisis for one of the world's most valuable firms - a major company in the AI industry and central to the US as it competes with China for dominance.
In the statement, the US government said it would "bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred".
They added: "Sensitive technologies like semiconductors are among the most valuable and strategic assets of the US economy, and the Department of Justice and the Department of Commerce will remain vigilant in pursuing justice for those who want to compromise America's technological edge."
