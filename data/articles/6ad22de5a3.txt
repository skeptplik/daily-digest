A pair of waterspouts were recorded recently in the San Francisco Bay area, much to the delight and shock of several observers, as storms pummelled the region.
Two waterspouts – vortexes of air and water mist, essentially tornadoes – cut a sheer streak into the sky, as they spiraled above the bay, according to social media footage capturing the weather phenomenon.
While prevalent in the Great Lakes and the south-east Floridian coast, waterspout sightings are not common for the Bay area, according to federal storm data.
The National Weather Service (NWS) confirmed the waterspout occurrence in a special marine warning Monday. Some waterspouts, dubbed “fair weather”, are weak, while others, typically tornadic waterspouts can pose a risk to boaters and coastal areas if they travel on shore.
The inclement weather is set to continue for a few more days. According to an NWS forecast, the Bay area is predicted to be drenched in rain on Tuesday with potential thunderstorms on Wednesday and Thursday.
A high wind advisory is in place until early Wednesday morning and NWS has issued a coastal flood advisory from early Wednesday until Thursday morning. The two-day storm will bring 1.5in to 3in of rain to most of the Bay area with the northern counties getting up to 4in and the Santa Cruz mountains predicted at 5in.
Elevated tide levels raise the risk of coastal flooding through at least Thursday, according to the agency.
Powerful wind is also forecasted with East Bay Hills expected to see gusts reaching up to 70mph (112 km/h).
After Thursday evening, the showers may subside with some rain lingering over the Pacific and its adjacent inland communities.
The storm is expected to drop 4ft of snow over the Sierra Nevadas this week with NWS warning that mountain travel may become impossible on Wednesday and Thursday.
Owing to the Super El Niño, California is facing a wetter winter than typical, which is poised to bring storms and flooding across the state.
