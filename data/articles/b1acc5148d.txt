Extra paramedic shifts are to be funded to relieve pressure on ambulances after the suspension of maternity services at Newry's Daisy Hill Hospital, MLAs have heard.
On Monday, the Southern Health Trust moved all of its hospital-based births to Craigavon Area Hospital because of a shortage of consultant obstretricians.
The chief executive of the Northern Ireland Ambulance Service (NIAS) has since expressed concern that the move will put "additional pressure" on an area that already has a "poor ambulance performance".
Jenny Keane said NIAS has been offered extra resources to address the issue, but added the money would only cover extra paramedic shifts, not more ambulances.
Keane, who took charge of NIAS in July, made her remarks on Thursday as she briefed Stormont's health committee on the challenges the service is facing.
At the outset, she apologised over NIAS's inability to meet emergency targets across Northern Ireland as a whole, acknowledging that some patients "have died while waiting" on an ambulance to arrive.
'Unacceptable and intolerable'
"Our average ambulance response time to our patients in Northern Ireland are the slowest in the United Kingdom and are consistently failing to get to patients within the standard we aim for," she said.
"This is unacceptable and intolerable."
Keane said the average response times for "category one" 999 calls so far this year has been 11 minutes, compared to a standard target of eight minutes.
She added that NIAS's average response to category two calls was 64 minutes, where the target is 18 minutes.
A category one call is defined as situation where a patient's life is at immediate risk.
A category two call is still a 999 emergency - it is defined as a "potentially serious incident" but can include patients suffering a suspected stroke or cardiac chest pain.
Keane acknowledged that delayed ambulance response times puts patients at risk.
"This is not the level of service that we want to provide and I want to apologise to our service users and all those who have experienced or been affected by a poor ambulance response," she said.

Keane replied she did not have that information to hand.
"What I can say that, with absolute certainty, is we know that most days in Northern Ireland there are about 100 patients waiting more than an hour for a category two ambulance response, which none of here find acceptable," she added.
'Additional pressure'
Committee member Diane Dodds, from the Democratic Unionist Party (DUP), questioned Keane about the impact of the Daisy Hill maternity move on NIAS.
Dodds pointed out that in a potential emergency, some women were now facing journeys of up to 90 minutes to give birth.
Keane replied there had been "significant engagement" on the issue with the Southern Trust and the Strategic Planning and Performance Group (SPGG), which oversees the delivery of health and social care services in Northern Ireland.
"We have only this week received a positive response from SPPG with regards to resourcing for additional support to deploy within the southern division to help to address that," Keane said.
When Dodds asked what that extra support would consist of, she was told it would be used to fund more paramedic shifts.
"What it doesn't do for me is provide me with additional ambulances â the actual capital allocation for additional trucks," Keane admitted.
"My concern in that area is the underlying poor ambulance performance in the area and that adding the additional pressure of a transfer â we're not doing it on a baseline where we have good performance."
The Department of Health said the extra funding for the NIAS was being provided on "an interim basis" and would be "kept under review".
Pregnant woman taking legal action
The Southern Trust, which runs both Daisy Hill and Craigavon Area Hospital, currently faces legal action over its decision to suspend maternity service in Newry.
A pregnant woman from the Newry area is challenge the move via a judicial review and her case is due to be heard in the High Court next month.
According to her solicitors, she is also challenging the trust for not holding a public consultation before it suspended the service.
The trust has repeatedly said senior staff shortages meant it had little choice but to consolidate all hospital-based births at one site in order to protect patient safety.
At a meeting of the trust's board on Thursday, members were told the transfer of maternity services has run smoothly since the change was implemented on Monday.
Its Assistant Director for Maternity, Gynaecology and Women's Health Services, Michelle Harrison, said there was a "positive atmosphere" in the Craigavon unit.
"There is a strong feeling from staff that they want this to work," she said.
"There are some aspects where there is minor refinement required, but the staff are very much engaged and involved in this and they want to make this the safest service possible."
Meanwhile, the trust is continuing its efforts to recruit to reopen the Newry unit.
On Friday, job advertisements appeared in newspapers seeking a consultant obstetrician and gynaecologist for Daisy Hill and another consultant obstetrician for Craigavon Area Hospital.
The advert stated that the roles may include "a greater focus on one site or a role across both sites".
