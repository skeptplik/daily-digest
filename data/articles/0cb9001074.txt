Suzuki Motor plans to launch the domestic production of lithium iron phosphate, or LFP, batteries, which do not rely on rare metals, for electric vehicles.
Production will start in October 2029, the company said Thursday.
By making rare metal-free batteries, Suzuki aims to curb procurement risks for materials for which Japan relies on imports. “We will build supply chains that do not depend on specific countries,” a company official said.
LFP batteries use iron and phosphorus, and they are thought to be cheaper and safer than what are known as ternary batteries, which depend on rare metals such as nickel, manganese and cobalt.
Suzuki will produce LFP batteries in Japan for the first time. They may also be used as storage batteries in houses.
The company will work with Eliiy Power, a Tokyo-based battery manufacturer, to develop technology for energy-efficient, safe LFP batteries.
Suzuki plans to set up an LFP battery production base in the city of Kosai, Shizuoka Prefecture, for which it will receive up to ¥18.7 billion ($117.3 million) in subsidies from the government.
Japanese automakers mainly use ternary batteries for their EVs.
Ternary batteries feature a high energy density and a long range. But as many of the materials used for the batteries are imported from a limited number of countries, such as China, procurement would be difficult if such countries introduce measures to restrict exports.
