Wales' largest health board is still failing, with "fundamental weaknesses" in governance and management, a new report says.
Betsi Cadwaladr University Health Board has been in special measures for nine years in total, with the latest period starting in 2023.
A review of its leadership published on Tuesday found, in some parts, targets and money "have a greater priority for senior management than the quality and safety of care".
Committed staff and ability to deliver some strong local services were highlighted.
The review did not suggest splitting up the health board, but Wales' health minister said the findings were "unacceptable" and BCUHB "does not function as a single coherent organisation".
The review was commissioned by the Welsh government in the summer, after it gave Betsi Cadwaladr University Health Board (BCUHB) a "final chance" to improve.
It concluded that senior leadership was "immature and ineffective", with weak senior management putting the quality of patient care at risk.
The report said: "Many areas reported a culture where targets and financial matters have a greater priority for senior management than the quality and safety of care.
"Fear of speaking up was described often... staff did not believe that raising a concern would result in corrective action.
"Despite this, the panel witnessed many teams striving to deliver the very best care they could for patients."
The reviewers also found a "toxic" culture at the health board where staff were afraid to speak out, and worried that no-one would listen if they did.
'Betsi spaghetti'
The health board's chief executive, Carol Shillabeer, previously announced she would be leaving in the new year to take up an NHS role in England.
Her departure follows a string of other senior leaders who have left the organisation in recent months, including the chief operating officer and executive director of finance.
The report also said that bureaucracy and slow decision-making led to a "loss of local ownership" and staff feeling frustrated.
The reviewers wrote: "Staff and leaders described decisions moving through multiple layers of approval, authority becoming concentrated at senior levels and operational leaders lacking sufficient autonomy to act.
"A phrase used in a focus group characterised the resulting complexity as 'Betsi spaghetti'."
However, the review concluded the health board could still be improved without being broken up into smaller organisations - an option previously discussed by ministers.
It said staff were "loyal and committed", and there were "strong local services" and "significant clinical expertise across north Wales".
Health Minister Mabon ap Gwynfor, said "these failings are utterly unacceptable".
"I also share the understandable frustrations of staff and patients alike in north Wales at the lack of progress in delivering and embedding improvements to services and standards of care," he said.
"The report identifies that the health board does not currently demonstrate the organisational conditions required to deliver and sustain reliable improvement.
"It does not function as a single coherent organisation and board governance is immature, impacting on the health board's capability to discharge its accountabilities adequately."
Ap Gwynfor is due to make a statement in the Senedd addressing the report on Tuesday afternoon.
'People are dying'
Benjamin Hodge Mckenna MS, Reform UK Wales's health spokesperson, said BCUHB had been "plagued by mismanagement, poor performance and repeated failures".
"Senior leaders and government ministers cannot continue to preside over failure without facing consequences," he said.
Darren Millar MS, leader of the Welsh Conservatives, said: "These damning findings are sadly nothing new.
"Putting the health board in special measures was supposed to improve performance, yet things have got worse and people are dying as a result."
