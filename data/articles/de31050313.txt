Kate Middleton is sharing a rare insight into her healing journey and cancer recovery. In a new interview published on November 11 in Country Life magazine, whose latest issue was guest-edited by her husband, Prince William, the Princess of Wales spoke to writer Robert Macfarlane about nature, mental health, healing, and more.
“I was told in hospital that my recovery would not be linear,” Catherine said in the interview, which focused on the role of the natural world in the future queen’s life. “There will be good days and bad days, that it will take time. This is the same in nature. It shows us that periods of rest, reflection and renewal are equally as important as moments of growth.”
Catherine has been in remission since January 2025, and has used her royal platform to speak about the ways in which time in the great outdoors has helped her recover. For her 44th birthday in January 2026, she shared a poignant final installment of “Mother Nature,” her video series that explored the relationship between wild spaces, mental resilience, and physical healing. Recently, in October, she visited Dose of Nature, a charity that focuses on nature-based mental health care.
On Wednesday, November 11, Catherine made an appearance at The Chase Community Hospital in Bordon, Hampshire, as part of her work with the Royal Foundation Centre for Early Childhood. She wore what looks to be a new suit by London it-girl label Bella Freud, pairing it with pops of red in various shades, including a cranberry turtleneck, a burgundy Smythson bag, burgundy Gianvito Rossi heels, and a remembrance poppy in observance of Armistice Day.
At the hospital, the princess met with doctors and health workers who use the Alarm Distress Baby Scale (ADBB), a training that helps identify early signs of social withdrawal in infants in order to support them. This is the latest step that Catherine has taken with her early childhood healthcare initiative, which she launched in 2021.
In her Country Life interview, Catherine also spoke about her interest in childhood development, including how it impacts her own parenting style as a mother to Prince George, Princess Charlotte, and Prince Louis. “We are often so busy we simply forget to pause, slow down and notice the detail in life. It is something I remind myself to do with my own children; to give them freedom to explore, rather than constantly telling them what to see or what to think,” she said.
