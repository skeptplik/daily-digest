Activision has filed a patent to train NPCs from in-game voice chat using a neural network and large language models (LLMs).
Eurogamer reports that the patent was filed in July and published in November. It is titled 'Methods and Systems for Applying Machine Learning to Generate Gameplay Audio.'
As the abstract reads, the patent is for a "system and method to generate audio reactions for a virtual game character that closely resembles reactions of a human player in a multiplayer video game environment."
The method involves capturing human player reactions and correlating them with specific game events, such as kills and deaths, that triggered those responses.
"The correlated information is used to train a neural network that subsequently aids in generating audio reactions for non-player characters during a similar game event."
As Eurogamer notes, the goal of this technology is for NPCs to "speak naturally" instead of relying on prerecorded audio. The technology could also be used outside of in-game voice chat on other social apps including Discord.
As with any patent, filing does not guarantee the technology will be implemented in-game.
The use of generative AI in the games industry is surrounded by signfiicant debate, especially regarding its use in voice acting.
Recently, six European game workers' unions published a manifesto examining generative AI's impact on the industry, while the Japan Actors Union announced new guidelines addressing unauthorised use of the technology to replicate voice performances.
