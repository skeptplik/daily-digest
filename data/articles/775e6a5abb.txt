Private diaries of Pope Francis reveal spiritual crisis and visions of the devil
Diaries provide insight into the faith and struggles of late pope
The late Pope Francis suffered a two-year spiritual crisis starting in 2021 when he felt tempted by the devil and struggled with how to wield power over the Catholic Church, a new collection of his private diaries published on Tuesday revealed.
Francis, who led the Church for 12 years until his death in April 2025, experienced visions of the devil and hell and battled against not wanting to be someone "who uses others like puppets," according to the diaries and accompanying commentary.
The volume does not specify whom the late pope might have treated like a puppet.
"It was all mixed with governance issues, pressure regarding decisions to make, and aridity and desolation," said a December 2022 entry from Francis describing his crisis.

Considered a reformer who tried to open the staid Church to the world, Francis encountered unusually strong resistance to his papacy from conservative cardinals, who worried he would water down the Church's firm teachings.
Cardinal Victor Fernández, a close Francis collaborator, collected the diaries — the first by a pope to be released to the public since the 1960s — and arranged them into a volume with his own accompanying commentary.
The late pope "ultimately recognized that it was the worst trial that had tormented him ... and that he felt that he was in a continual battle not to fall into this demonic seduction," wrote Fernández.
Believed he had seen the devil
The Private Diary of Pope Francis collects diary entries that Francis, an Argentine and the first pope from the Americas, made in a small planning notebook from 1992.
The diaries provide a rare and striking insight into the faith and mental struggles of the late pope. Most recent popes have directed that their papers be burned after their deaths, although some papers of John Paul II, pope from 1978 to 2005, were later made public. The last pope to have diaries published posthumously was John XXIII, who died in 1963.
Fernández, a fellow Argentine whom Francis appointed in 2023 to lead the Vatican's powerful doctrinal office, does not present the full diary but gives excerpts, arranged by theme.

Totalling 160 pages, the diaries offer few direct comments on the events of Francis's papacy or about Vatican officials. They focus instead on the late pope's spiritual life, including his belief that he had seen the devil and the entrance to hell and that God spoke to him in dreams.
An entry from January 2021, at the beginning of the late pope's self-described spiritual crisis, lists three times throughout Francis's life when he thought he had seen the devil, including once as pope in a meeting with Vatican officials.
"I can't forget the powerful raspiness of his voice," Francis wrote in the entry about the later vision. "The Lord granted me an opportunity to leave that meeting, and he thus freed me."
The diary entries begin in December 1992, when the future Francis, then known as Jorge Mario Bergoglio, was an auxiliary bishop of Buenos Aires. They run until August 2024, about eight months before his death aged 88.
Addressing Francis's spiritual crisis, Fernández recounts being in a meeting with the pope in 2023, when another bishop asked Francis why he did not chastise an unnamed Vatican official who had been speaking badly about Francis in public.
Vision of hell
"[Francis] responded by explaining that he felt a strong sensation in his heart that God did not want him to become a puppeteer who uses and manipulates people according to his own convenience," Fernández states.
Francis recounted seeing a vision of hell in a June 2018 entry. "Last night I saw the entrance to Hell," he wrote. "I peeked in to look a little, and it terrified me."
"I grabbed the Rosary as a weapon," Francis wrote, referring to Catholic prayer beads. "I thank the Lord for this help."
The diary does refer directly to a few events of Francis's papacy, including his contested 2018 visit to Chile, when the late pope was sharply criticized for dismissing allegations of sexual abuse and cover-up against a bishop there.
During Pope Francis's 2018 trip to Chile, he was criticized for dismissing allegations of sexual abuse and cover-up against a bishop there. (Alessandro Bianchi/Reuters)
Francis later apologized and summoned the country's bishops to Rome, where they all offered to resign in an unprecedented move.
"Devastated because of the situation in Chile," Francis wrote in a 2021 entry. "It was hard for me to admit what happened in Chile; I didn't think it was so serious."

Fernández also recounts Francis commenting on some senior officials, including U.S. Cardinal Robert Prevost, who in May 2025 was elected Francis's successor and became Pope Leo. Francis called Prevost "a great pastor, a generous missionary, and an irreproachable man," Fernández states.
Francis also offered kind thoughts about Guinean Cardinal Robert Sarah and German Cardinal Gerhard Müller, two of his most vocal critics. He said Sarah had "a refined spiritual sensitivity" and that Müller "seems aggressive, but he's a good man," according to Fernández.
As for his own election as pope, Francis made one simple entry in July 2013, more than four months after he became pontiff. "They elected me pope on March 13," he wrote.
