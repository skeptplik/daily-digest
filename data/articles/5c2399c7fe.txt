Geelong ruck Piper Dunlop has been stretchered from the field on a motorised cart in concerning scenes during her side's AFLW semifinal clash with Sydney, before the Swans went on to win by three points.
Dunlop went down after a head clash with Sydney's Tanya Kennedy during the third quarter at Kardinia Park.
She initially returned to her feet before becoming unsteady and falling on the edge of the centre circle, caught by Sydney's Lexi Hamilton before trainers sprinted onto the ground to attend to her.
The game was stopped for nine minutes as medical staff gathered on the field, Dunlop eventually leaving the field on the motorised cart.
Geelong has said Dunlop has been taken to hospital for "further precautionary assessment".

Forward Chloe Molloy was the star for the Swans in a pulsating semifinal, kicking three goals and taking a key late mark to see her side through to its first-ever AFLW preliminary final.
The 8.2 (50) to 7.5 (47) victory means Sydney will face two-time defending premiers North Melbourne next weekend.
Geelong, meanwhile, bows out of the finals series in straight sets after just one loss in the regular season.
The Swans led by 16 points early in the final quarter, but goals to Chloe Scheer and Jacqueline Perry cut the deficit to four points minutes later.
Scheer proceeded to hit the post, with her behind proving the final score of the game.
Geelong experienced a torrid afternoon on the injury front.
In addition to Dunlop's concerning head knock, the Cats lost Amy McDonald to a hamstring injury in the shadows of half-time, Rachel Kearns was absent for most of the second half, and star midfielder Georgie Prespakis spent time off the field after a heavy collision.
All three of Molloy's goals came in the first half, including one spectacular effort in which she gathered and snapped from an acute angle on the right.
