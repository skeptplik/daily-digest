At least 24 people were injured after a car bomb exploded in Villavicencio in central Colombia on Monday, authorities said.
The blast damaged a regional government headquarters as well as neighbouring buildings.
Video footage circulating on social media shows piles of rubble alongside wrecked vehicles and a large crater in the road.
The attack, which has so far not been claimed by any group, comes amid a wave of violence in the South American nation.
Alexander Baquero, the mayor of Villavicencio, has since imposed a temporary ban on motorcycle traffic along several key roads in the city, which is the capital of the Meta Department.
The Colombian army's Seventh Brigade has also been deployed to the area.
Both the mayor's office and the regional government are offering up to 150 million Colombian pesos (around €38,000) for information leading to those responsible.
Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella arrived in Villavicencio at around 9 am local time on Tuesday as he prepared to hold a security meeting and visit the scene of the blast.
"Narcoterrorism is desperate because we are confronting it resolutely," de la Espriella wrote in a post on social media. "It seeks to intimidate us and force us to back down in order to pursue another false peace process."
In a later update, he added that he would "not back down or negotiate with criminals."
"I will confront them with absolute determination and use every tool granted to me by the Constitution and the law to restore security," he wrote on X.
