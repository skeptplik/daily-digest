I am haunted still by an interaction I had at my stepson’s ninth birthday party in 2014, where one of the kids asked if I could put on Grand Theft Auto V for them all. “I have it at home!”, he said, giving me the puppy-dog eyes. Mentally replaying that game in my head, flashing back to the many scenes involving such things as smoking meth, over-the-top gratuitous violence and grubby sex in one character’s trailer, I said “no way”. Later I gently mentioned to the kid’s mother that GTA V was an 18-rated game and really wasn’t suitable for kids. “Oh, but he’s really good at these games,” she told me. She was genuinely shocked to learn that an 18-rating on a video game was a flag for adult content, rather than an indication of its difficulty.
Grand Theft Auto VI is released this week. It is an incredible creative and technical feat, conjuring a realistic-looking and exhaustively detailed virtual Florida filled with every kind of filth and criminality you could imagine. It is brilliant, entertainingly observed and even sometimes unexpectedly touching, largely thanks to a central love story between the player couple, Jason and Lucia. It is lewd, violent and frequently hilarious. It is also absolutely full of stuff that I really would not want my 10 and seven-year-old boys to see or hear. I have never played a game that has more thoroughly earned its 18 rating.
Everyone knows that Grand Theft Auto is a violent game. It’s about crime and criminals, and VI’s story is about two fugitives in Vice City and the absolute dirtbags they meet along the way. You steal cars, run drugs, shoot cops and rival criminals and hold up gas stations; you kill hundreds of people over the course of the game. There are executions, brutal police raids and depictions of torture. None of that is particularly new, but it’s still pretty hardcore for, say, a nine-year-old. Plenty of millennials played Grand Theft Auto when we were underage, but video games are an awful lot more realistic now than they were 20 years ago; graphic violence on a PlayStation 2 from the 00s looks very different from graphic violence on a PlayStation 5 in 2026.
Let’s leave the violence aside for a minute, though: there is also a lot more explicit sexual content in GTA VI than any previous GTA game, or any other video game period. 2004’s Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas was briefly pulled from sale because it contained a sex minigame that you couldn’t even access without hacking the game’s code. GTA VI, meanwhile, contains a LOT of sex that is in no way hidden, including between the two main characters. On another character’s laptop, there in a browsable in-game version of OnlyFans that pulls exactly no punches. There is full-frontal nudity, male and female. One mission takes place during a porn shoot, several others in strip clubs and bars, one in a hotel where you can burst into the wrong room to find an ongoing orgy or a bunch of people eating sushi off a naked body. I don’t personally fancy explaining any of this to my kids!
Then there’s the drugs. You run drugs, you steal drugs, you sell drugs and you take drugs. Pretty much everyone in Vice City really enjoys their coke; there are copious party scenes where powder is everywhere, and the player characters can partake if you want. Chapter 3 of the game is literally titled “Tits Ass Drug Money”. You might read all of this and think, my teenager has seen worse. That’s perfectly fair – but this is not stuff a 10- or 12-year-old can really contextualise, in my opinion (and the opinion of the ratings boards). If you think your child is too young to watch Breaking Bad, they are definitely too young for Grand Theft Auto VI.
Even the in-game TV and radio is full of vulgar humour. On a phone-in show, I heard a caller and the host vamp extensively about her boyfriend’s penis’s resemblance, in size and smell, to a chicken nugget. A psychic medium considers whether people can still indulge their foot fetishes in the afterlife, in incorporeal form. One of the in-game podcasts stars two female hosts talking in explicit detail about how attracted they are to various famous murderers. Walking past random pedestrians on the street you will frequently hear people talking at length about their dicks and what they have recently done with them. This is not what you might call mature humour, but it is adult.
To be clear: I am not here to start a moral panic about adult video game content. All of the above is fair game in media for adults and, truthfully, I found a lot of it extremely funny. But believe me, as both a parent and a person who has spent over 70 hours playing this game, GTA VI is not for kids. For some reason there remains an immense knowledge gap between children and parents when it comes to the content of video games, and I think it’s in everyone’s interests for parents to make informed decisions.
