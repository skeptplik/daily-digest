What Is MagSafe?
MagSafe is Apple’s magnetic technology built into the iPhone 12, iPhone 13, iPhone 14, iPhone 15, iPhone 16 (excluding the iPhone 16e), iPhone 17, iPhone 18 Pro, and iPhone Duo. It features a hidden ring of magnets on the back of the phone and was developed to create a faster, more precise wireless charging system. Those magnets also connect to accessories such as external batteries, car mounts, and wallets.
If you use a case on your iPhone, make sure it’s also MagSafe (the case will have a ring of magnets inside). This will ensure a proper connection with MagSafe accessories. If you use a non-MagSafe case, the connection will be very weak, and MagSafe products may just slide off your iPhone. We have lots of case recommendations in our guides:
When buying a wallet, make sure it supports MagSafe or Qi2 connections. The product description should indicate this prominently. Qi2 is the wireless charging standard, and all MagSafe iPhones support Qi2. Android phones now also support Qi2, and some have magnets inside, like the Google Pixel 10 and Pixel 11 series (Google calls it Pixelsnap).
MagSafe wallets use the same ring of magnets that lets them snap directly onto the back of the iPhone or a MagSafe- or supported Qi2 case. This gives the wallet a strong and secure hold on your device. The wallets we’ve included in this guide all have the MagSafe seal of approval.

Do MagSafe Wallets Work With Android Phones?
To use a MagSafe wallet with an Android phone (or the iPhone 16e), you’ll need to use a MagSafe case. Several case manufacturers have started adding a ring of magnets to Android phone cases to bring the same magnetic experience native to iPhones.
Will a MagSafe Wallet Demagnetize My Credit Card?
No, MagSafe will not demagnetize your credit cards. Credit cards have magnetic stripe encoding strong enough to resist demagnetization, and the MagSafe wallets in this guide include a built-in shield designed to keep your cards safe. However, you may run into issues with hotel key cards and some gift cards. Keep those away as a precaution.
How We Test MagSafe Wallets
I use a MagSafe wallet every day as my primary wallet. I check that it snaps firmly onto the iPhone or MagSafe case—and that it doesn’t slide off when I take my phone out of my pocket, bag, or jacket. I also make sure cards fit well in the sleeve and don’t fall out. I only recommend wallets that feel durable, are easy to use, and can hold the standard amount of cards (at least two to three). I occasionally call on my fellow WIRED reviewers for long-term testing or to check out new models.
Other Good MagSafe Wallets
ESR Geo Wallet Stand for $47: Apple’s Find My app lets you track your devices if they’re lost, and ESR’s Geo wallet has it built right into the wallet itself. This is the wallet I’ve been using for the past few weeks, and outside of its Find My capabilities, I love that it strikes a great balance between size and usability. ESR says it can hold up to three cards, but I’ve been using mine with four—three cards plus my ID—and I can get them all out easily. Although having a wallet with magnetic charging pins is weird—the Find My feature requires a battery that lasts three months—you can turn it off if you don’t care for it (but then, maybe just buy a different wallet). It does kind of suck that you need a proprietary charger to recharge your wallet, though. The magnets adhere well to the back of the phone, and you can pop open the wallet and use it as a kickstand. ESR even includes a strap on the back that works as a phone grip.
OtterBox Symmetry Series Cactus Leather MagSafe Wallet for $45: This wallet is nice and simple, thin, lightweight, has a strong hold on my phone case, and offers a dedicated fabric-covered slot at the bottom to push the cards out (I was able to fit three without much trouble). This OtterBox wallet is made from cactus-based leather, which feels nearly as luxurious as real leather. Just know that cactus leather isn’t as eco-friendly as it’s made out to be—these cases are still infused with layers of plastic.
Spigen MagSafe Classic LS Wallet for $40: This cool-looking wallet incorporates design inspiration from the classic Macintosh computers. The gray might seem a little bit drab, but any Apple enthusiast will likely enjoy the nod to the computers of yesteryear. It’s otherwise pretty standard fare, with space for up to three cards. I like the thumb notch, which makes it easy to slide your cards out.
Avoid These Wallets
Pelican Protector MagSafe Wallet: This MagSafe wallet is simply too bulky. It’s difficult to slip into a pocket, and we don’t think the protective hardshell is worth the trade-off since it can only hold two or three cards.
Kate Spade New York Morgan MagSafe Wallet: This wallet looks nice, but the finish starts to degrade with regular use. It also opened on its own once or twice, which left me worried about a card slipping out.
Ohsnap! Snap Grip Wallet: I had high hopes for this one, mainly because of its build quality. The wallet is aluminum, can hold up to eight cards (depending on whether they have raised lettering or not), has MagSafe support, and comes with a grip that doubles as a kickstand. Unfortunately, the grip is made of plastic, and it broke after a short time (it won’t fold properly back into place). The magnetic hold isn’t as strong as other MagSafe wallets, even with a MagSafe-approved case on my phone. None of that is great, especially at this high price.
Apple FineWoven MagSafe Case: Any time this wallet is separated from your iPhone, you’ll get an alert and can track it in Apple’s Find My app. It has a single slot that fits up to three cards, but to remove them, you have to take it off your iPhone and push the cards up through the slot on the back. Unfortunately, Apple’s MagSafe wallets use only the company’s proprietary FineWoven material (made from recycled materials). It’s a commendable effort to reduce reliance on leather production, but several WIRED reviewers have said the material doesn’t hold up well over time.
Journey Loc8 Universal MagSafe Slim Wallet: My cards would slip out of this wallet if I didn’t have it stuffed full, which makes it an obvious “Do Not Recommend” choice when so many other comparable wallets don’t have this problem.
