A German-Ukrainian company said on Monday that its SPYS interceptor UAV had shot down a Russian jet-powered drone in Ukraine.
Quantum WIY Industries announced that it had successfully intercepted a Russian Geran-5 K5-K attack drone during combat testing in Ukraine, according to a press release shared by Quantum Systems, a German defence firm that launched the joint venture alongside Ukraine's WIY Drones.
“We need to keep developing and scaling interceptors to protect Ukrainian cities and critical infrastructure. Russia keeps adapting its attack systems, so we work closely with Ukrainian brigades to adjust our systems accordingly," Sven Kruck, co-CEO of Quantum Systems, said.
"You can never stand still," he added.
According to WIY Drones, the SPYS system is a high-speed interceptor UAV that can be deployed to defend against aerial targets.
It is designed to counter both attack and reconnaissance drones and to protect military positions, mobile units and critical infrastructure, the company says on its website.
The SPYS interceptor can reach speeds of up to around 450 kph and has a flight ceiling of 4 km, according to WIY.
The GUR, Ukraine's military intelligence agency, said in early November that Russian forces had begun using the K5-K variant of the Geran-5 in attacks on Ukrainian cities at the beginning of autumn.
The agency said that "based on its aerodynamic scheme and tactical-technical characteristics," the K5-K "can already be classified as a compact cruise missile."
It added that the drone can hit speeds of more than 450 kph and can reach an altitude of 6 km.
Unlike conventional attack drones such as the Geran-2, which use propeller engines, jet-powered drones can hit significantly higher speeds, making them harder for Ukrainian air defences to deal with.
