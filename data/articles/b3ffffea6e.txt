Finding the perfect gift for someone who only likes tech stuff can be a challenge. Partially because great tech gifts tend to be inherently expensive. If you're buying something for a tech-savvy person this year, it has the potential to be the only gift that truly gets them excited while also blowing a massive hole in your budget.
There are exceptions to this, of course. Plenty of high-quality tech and gaming accessories are surprisingly affordable and practical. If you're looking for ideas of what to get the tech nerd in your life for Christmas, we've rounded up some of our favorite items, in no particular order, to help you get started.
IGN's Recommended Tech Gifts for 2026
If you enjoy listening to music while you run or work out, then this headset should be on your radar. Instead of the traditional method of sending sound vibrations to your eardrums, the OpenRun Pro uses bone conduction technology to send vibrations through your cheekbones directly to your inner ear. As such, the headphones are actually placed in front of your ear instead of on your ear. The biggest advantage is that you can fully hear your surroundings; if you like running on streets, for example, you'll be aware of everything that's going on around you while your music is playing.
“I have a pair of AirPods Pro that I use regularly, but I always set them aside for the OpenRun Pros when I run. The Transparency mode on the AirPods Pro does work well for most situations, but they still can't compare to the awareness you get without having any buds plugging up your ears. In my experience these headphones stay in place extremely well and the sound quality is great compared to other sport headphones I've tried.” - Eric Song
Nintendo Switch 2 + Mario Kart World Bundle
Between this year’s studio closures and Sony’s decision to kill game discs, Sony and Microsoft are having rough years. The only console maker that’s thriving this generation is Nintendo. Not only has the Mario company managed to release a steady stream of excellent first-party games for the Switch 2 since launch, but it’s also the only company to say it’s not planning to increase its hardware price, at least for the foreseeable future. If you want a game console with plenty of good years ahead of it, you can’t go wrong with a Switch 2. - Chris Reed
Asus ROG Xbox Ally
The RAM crisis hasn’t been kind to hardware costs this year, and if you’re trying to get your hands on a handheld gaming PC that won’t break the bank, the Asus ROG Xbox Ally is your best bet without trying your luck on the used market. It’s powered by a chip that’s roughly on the same level as the Steam Deck, and should have no problem playing most PC games, as long as you’re willing to tweak with the settings. It doesn’t hurt that it’s incredibly comfortable to hold, as well. - Jackie Thomas

Apple AirPods Pro 3
This isn’t the most exciting pick, and it’s certainly not the newest gadget on this list. But I use my AirPods Pro 3 headphones every single day. Whether they’re being used for video calls, phone calls, listening to music while walking the dog, or working out, they are constantly in my ears, and they check all the boxes: they sound great (shout-out to the excellent bass for such tiny in-ear headphones!), have excellent battery life, are comfortable in my ears, and have proven quite durable. It’s not that there aren’t plenty of other great headphones out there, but the AirPods Pro 3 have been refined to near-perfection, and they’re on the very short list of my favorite tech products in my life. - Ryan McCaffrey
Peakdesign Beacon Wallet Tracker Card
If you know anything about Peakdesign you know that their name is synonymous with quality and the new Wallet Tracker card continues the trend. One of the thinnest and longest lasting tracker cards around this wirelessly charging credit-card sized device works unfortunately exclusively with Apple to track whatever belongings this gets forgotten in. I put it in my wallet when I go on trips and I put it in my pokemon binder for added security at conventions. It’s a great alternative to Apple’s Airtags, which are sometimes too bulky but still provides peace of mind when needing to FindMy devices. It makes a surprisingly audible noise when searched for from something so compact. Makes for a great stocking stuffer for anyone who’s synonymous with the very fun game “Has Anyone Seen My Wallet?” - Amir Rakib
Twelve South - AirFly Pro 2
The AirFly Pro 2 is my go-to travel essential that lets you pair your own noise-canceling headphones or wireless earbuds with airplane seatback entertainment systems —complete with dedicated dual-pairing buttons for sharing in-flight movies with a seatmate, on-device volume controls, and 25+ hours of battery life. Because it delivers low-latency Bluetooth audio from any 3.5mm jack, it also doubles as a handy adapter for gaming handhelds or a PS5 DualSense controller during layovers and hotel stays, making it a compact, high-utility stocking stuffer. And you can connect it to all of the Bluetooth headphones suggestions in this guide! - Libby Pei
ReMarkable: eink Digital Notebook
The ReMarkable digital notebook is the perfect companion for avid notetakers and digital readers. It integrates seamlessly with my current lifestyle and travels almost everywhere with me. Besides the metallic build of the body and the really satisfying matted e-ink screen to write on, the tablet is a pleasure to write on, draw with, and work off of. It integrates with google drive, PDFs and epubs and syncs wirelessly with all my devices. It has auto handwriting recognition to digitize notes, but also lets you screenshot directly any doodles. It's a highly recommended alternative to tablets for any analog tech lovers and artists to embrace. I have loved mine so much, I bought their portable ‘move’ version for on-the-go and on-site reading and note taking. - Amir Rakib
Sennheiser HD 490 Pro Open Back Headphones
It may seem outdated in this wireless era, but there’s something to be said for a good pair of wired, over-ear headphones. The Sennheiser HD 490 Pros hit the sweet spot for me. They sound great, with a more well-rounded tuning than the more famous HD 600/650/6xx series (they’re also easier to power than those sets). And they’re also fantastic for work purposes, be that video calls or editing audio. I use these all day every day, and since picking them up I’ve never felt the pull to upgrade to anything better. These handle everything I could ask of them. - Jesse Schedeen
SteelSeries Aeon Pro Controller
A lot of people might look at the SteelSeries Aeon Pro’s price tag and immediately run the other way, but hear me out: it’s the best controller I’ve ever used. The controller is just heavy enough that it feels remarkably solid in your hands, kind of like the Xbox Elite Controllers. But unlike Microsoft’s high-end gamepads, the Aeon Pro has a stand that not only charges the controller, but houses a second battery, so you can easily swap between them whenever your battery runs low, all without a cable. This controller also has the best back paddles I’ve ever used in a controller that feels incredibly clicky. And thanks to hall effect sticks and triggers, this is a gamepad you buy once, and then never have to even think about buying another one. - Jackie Thomas
Amazon Kindle
If someone on your list wants to read more, get them a Kindle. It’s a thin, lightweight device that’s easy to throw into a bag when you’re leaving the house. It can hold hundreds of books without adding any weight. It syncs your position in books to other devices, so you can dip back in on your phone when you’re waiting in line somewhere without your Kindle. And thanks to the Libby app, you can check out ebooks using your library card, and load them onto your kindle for free. - Chris Reed
XTEINK X3 3.7" Pocket E-Ink eBook Reader
Since purchasing this handheld phone-sized magnetic e-reader I have not touched my Kindle. For the size and price, it makes for a nice little stocking stuffer for anyone looking to have reading be more accessible. XTEink makes two sizes, the X3 which perfectly fits on the back of my iphone 15 or the X4 which fits larger models. I don’t need to pitch the clarity and convenience of an e-ink screen to those in the know but for those skeptical of its size the battery lasts over 3 weeks and the font size is adjustable to assuage any concerns over eye-strain. I had my concerns about ease of reading on the smaller screen size, and convenience of use for the custom firmware but after reading multiple books exclusively on this device I can proudly report no issues on either end. Wireless transfer is easy from any browser (mobile or otherwise) and epubs, pdfs, or images look and read beautifully. I was happy to see reading stats like speed, percentage, and performance come standard and I genuinely don’t leave on any trip without it. If you’re ready to put down another amazon-locked product, I can’t recommend the X3 and X4 enough! - Amir Rakib
VITURE Beast XR/AR Glasses
I’ve used dozens of these AR display glasses over the last year or so, and the one that I actually come back to use is the Viture Beast. For the uninitiated, these are glasses that you can plug into the USB-C port of a gaming handheld like the Steam Deck, and have it project a display in front of your face. That seems pointless at first glance, but these glasses are a godsend if you frequently travel and you don’t want to hold your device up in your hands when you’re on a flight. They are a bit expensive, coming in at around $489, but they’re worth the cash if the idea of having a giant display that you can carry around in your bag is at all appealing. - Jackie Thomas
