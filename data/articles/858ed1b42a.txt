A gun control advocate is grappling with a grueling recovery after being shot in the face during the recent mass shooting at a Washington DC condominium building – an ordeal that she began with a handwritten note from her hospital bed reading: “Gun control is a public health crisis”.
Susan Thurston Hamerski had traveled to the nation’s capital from her home in Minnesota’s Twin Cities for her daughter’s wedding when she was shot by a gunman who killed one person, injured five others and evidently died by suicide on 12 November at a condo building in the Logan Circle neighborhood near the White House, according to her friends and family.
Hamerski had stayed back to take care of her daughter’s dog while the newlywed bride, Madeleine, and her husband went on their honeymoon, family friend and spokesperson Tina Wilcox said. She was walking the dog, Wrigley, when the mass shooting erupted in and around the condo building, and the attacker – who was locked into an ongoing legal dispute with the complex’s management – shot Hamerski.
She survived after being brought to a hospital in critical condition. But she was left needing to undergo multiple surgeries to restore her jaw and tongue’s function as well as to repair her face. The procedures require months of rehabilitative therapy, Madeleine and her family wrote in a statement online after the daughter cut her honeymoon short to be at her mother’s side.
As Wilcox put it, there is a dark irony to the plight confronting Hamerski, who spent six years voluntarily managing the social media in her community for the non-profit group Moms Demand Action for Gun Sense in America.
Susan Hamerski’s role with Moms Demand Action saw her share news and research about gun safety in the US, where as of Tuesday there had been more than 415 mass shootings reported nationwide. Mass shootings are colloquially defined as ones in which four or more victims are either wounded or killed – and the high numbers of such cases reported perennially in the US have prompted many, including those aligned with Moms Demand Action, to call for more substantial federal gun control.
But Congress in Washington DC over the years has not implemented such measures, creating an environment in the US where sometimes it seems “you can’t go anywhere [outside your home] and feel safe”, Wilcox said.
It feels as if “it can happen to any of us”, Wilcox said. “And it’s happening so frequently that more and more innocent people are losing their lives or suffering massive injury.”
After the mass shooting victimizing Hamerski drove that reality home as closely as possible for her and her family, she made clear that her advocacy remained as top of mind for her as her looming recovery journey.
Hamerski did that by writing a note from her hospital bed that said, “Gun control is a public health crisis” – words that she couldn’t speak because of her injury.
Her son, Sam Hamerski, shared a picture of the note with the Minnesota news station KARE.
“It’s ironic that … the people [who] do the most and care the most are often the people that are affected,” Sam Hamerski told the outlet.
In a social media post, Moms Demand Action said Susan Hamerski’s handwritten words are a reminder that gun violence doesn’t just change lives – it “reverberates throughout families and communities, leaving survivors and their loved ones to navigate unimaginable physical, emotional, and financial hardships”.
“We are in awe of Susan who, through her pain and suffering, found a way to make her voice heard,” Moms Demand Action’s post added. “We hold Susan and her loved ones in our hearts as they come to terms with their new reality as survivors of gun violence. We will keep fighting in her honor, working toward a future where no family has to endure this kind of horror and devastation.”
As word about Hamerski’s shooting has circulated, there has been substantial public support for the novelist and poet who has worked as a Minnesota state public health department communications specialist – and has served as a governing board member of the Illusion Theater in Minneapolis, too.
An online campaign meant to help Hamerski cover her medical expenses as of Tuesday had raised more than $210,000 from about 2,600 individual donations.
The campaign’s page noted that Wrigley was back home and safe. The page also said that the money raised would be used to pay costs described as “enormous”, including getting Hamerski back to Minnesota from Washington DC, ongoing medical care and financially supporting her while she is convalescent and unable to work.
Wilcox said that has certainly been a boon for Hamerski and her loved ones as she musters all the strength she can to navigate her recovery.
She also remarked: “Susan is the type of person who you say, ‘If it had to happen to anyone.’
“Because she’s doing the best she can, and she’s a really strong individual – and she’s going to need to be to survive this.”
