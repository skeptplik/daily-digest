An analysis of protein-coding DNA from more than 1.4 million people has identified hundreds of genes that influence a person’s height. Researchers identified 207 genes that were linked to a height difference of two centimetres, on average. They also identified 17 rare variants that prevent proteins from functioning, which have more striking effects. These include one variant associated with a 17-centimetre reduction in height and another with an 11-centimetre increase. The findings might help with the development of treatments that mitigate height changes caused by some medical conditions.
Major prizes in economics, including the Nobel, are being awarded to researchers affiliated to an increasingly concentrated set of US universities. Just eight institutions dominate these top economics awards: Harvard University; the University of Chicago; Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT); Stanford University; Princeton University; Yale University; the University of California, Berkeley; and Columbia University. On the other hand, prizes in the natural sciences, engineering and social sciences outside economics have become less concentrated to certain institutions over the past 70 years.
Reference: National Bureau of Economic Research working paper (not peer reviewed)
Four years after NASA’s DART Earth-defence test mission smashed into an asteroid and succeeded in altering the space rock’s movement, a follow-up mission has confirmed it on camera. The European Space Agency has released the first images from its Hera mission, which show detailed views of the asteroid’s new crater. “Hera is a sort of Inspector Columbo going back to the crash scene to investigate what is the outcome of this impact,” says Patrick Michel, principal investigator of the Hera mission.
Where I work
Wildlife conservationist Reshu Bashyal works with people in Nepal to protect the more than 500 species of orchids that grow there. Prized for their ornamental value and uses in traditional medicine, hundreds of these flowering plants are illegally harvested and sold each year. But blanket bans on the orchid trade are unlikely to be effective, Bashyal says, so more innovative strategies are required. One of these is an ‘anti-poaching taskforce’ that can document illegal activity, conduct patrols and spread information about the importance of conservation. (Nature | Leisurely scroll)

A biomedical innovation model that utilizes scientists in industry with government as their main funder could accelerate the development of therapeutics for common diseases that industry considers too risky to invest in, argue physician Victor Dzau, health-policy advisor Emily Shambaugh and healthcare entrepreneur Will Ellaissi. This government funding could operate at a national level, or several countries could form a consortium. Such a model “moves beyond the market-determined trajectory of research translation and ensures the public’s role in shaping the future of health,” the authors write.
Commensality — the act of sitting down to eat a meal with other people — has myriad health and social benefits, such as protecting against cognitive decline and helping us build trust with others. The presence of other people might also increase the enjoyment we get from eating. In an increasingly digital world, it might seem that opportunities to dine with others are dwindling. But research suggests that people are much more open and willing to accept invitations than we think, and simply putting ourselves out there might be an easy way to a dinner date.
