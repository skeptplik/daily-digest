Global stock markets have slumped as Anthropic’s record-breaking IPO failed to stem a sell-off in AI-linked companies.
Asian stock markets were on track for their worst week since 2020, as shares in big chip companies fell on Friday. In Taiwan, stocks dropped 6.7% to hit their lowest point since mid-April, with the chip maker TSMC down about 6%.
In Japan, the Nikkei index fell 5%, while South Korea’s tech-heavy Kospi index lost 5.5%. Shares in the tech investor SoftBank had their worst day in more than two years, down 14.5% in Tokyo, amid worries around its aggressive bets on AI.
European stock markets were heading for their worst week since March, with the pan-European Stoxx 600 dropping 1.3% in the first 90 minutes.
The sell-off followed losses on Wall Street on Thursday, where the main indices all fell by more than 2% on a day dominated by Anthropic’s IPO.
Anthropic’s shares jumped by 17% on Thursday, after briefly surging by almost 40% when trading in the frontier AI firm began. But they were heading for losses on Friday, down 6% at $91.30, falling back towards the IPO price of $83.
Anthropic’s flotation has failed to ease worries around the AI trade, as investors increasingly question whether companies can deliver the returns necessary to justify the billions of dollars being poured into the AI build out.
Daniela Hathorn, a senior market analyst at Capital.com, said: “The IPO demonstrated that demand for AI exposure remains strong, but it has not been enough to overcome concerns about elevated valuations, profitability and the sustainability of investment spending.
“The broader market mood has shifted from buying the dip towards questioning whether the correction has further to run. The weakness in equal-weighted indices suggests that selling pressure is no longer confined to a handful of technology giants, while Europe’s recent outperformance has also faded.”
Britain’s FTSE 100 share index hit a five-month low on Friday morning. It has dropped by 4.8% this week, and is heading for its biggest weekly fall since the start of the Iran war.
