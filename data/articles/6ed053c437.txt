Scotland beat Wallabies 15-12 in dour Nations Championship clash at Murrayfield
The Wallabies have delivered another woeful display on their spring tour, falling to a 15-12 defeat against Scotland at Murrayfield.
The defeat was the Wallabies' fourth in a row and piles the pressure on new coach Les Kiss after another display of limited attacking rugby.
A dour contest never really ignited was always destined to be close, with both teams struggling to get their attacks working on a freezing Edinburgh afternoon.
"I thought there were some really nice attacking moments from us," Les Kiss said post-match.
"Our defence definitely turned up.
"Our physicality overall was right up, which was pleasing.
"A couple of moments, that try in the corner with the maul, probably a couple of discipline moments that gave them easy yards just put us on the back foot.
"Full of fight, that's for sure. But you want to win those ones."
In the sort of dank, drizzly conditions that a Scottish autumn is famous for, the match descended into a gruelling arm-wrestle.
With the pre-match fireworks still hovering above the playing field, Matt Fagerson opened the scoring for the hosts inside the opening 10 minute, finishing off a spell of dominant possession in the corner.
The Wallabies hit back straight away, Harry Potter contorting himself to reach over and finish a rare flowing move sparked by the returning Carter Gordon in midfield.
That was the end of any real attacking impetus for the Wallabies for almost an hour though as the team fell into the habit of one-out hit ups and box kicks.
As the two sides grappled with close-contact battles, former Wallaby Jack Dempsey burrowed over to score, the last points before a 45-minute scoreless spell that stretched deep into the second half.
The Wallabies felt they had hit back just before half time when Ryan Canham dived over from a rolling maul, but it was bought back by the TMO for obstruction by Jeremy Williams.
"It was a tough one," Wilson said.
"They're really trying to crack down on that at the moment and that was probably a really big moment in the end, because 14-all at half time we might have had them there.
"We've got to be better at icing those big moments."
Neither side could get going in the second half, the Wallabies defence standing tall despite Billy Pollard sin binning just after the break.
A Russell penalty looked to have sealed the game for the Scots, before Max Jorgensen scored a brilliant try in the final minute, an attacking raid of such class it raised questions as to why there had been no go-forward before then.
The Wallabies even had one chance to win the game late when Joseph-Aukuso Suaalii made a line break and Tom Wright was freed down the right wing.
However, he was bundled into touch and the Scots held on.
The Wallabies next head to Cardiff to take on a Wales team buoyed by beating the All Blacks for the first time in 73 years.
