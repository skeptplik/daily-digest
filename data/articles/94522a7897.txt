A late barrage of runs has put Australia in the box seat of the second T20I against England on the Gold Coast.
Ben Dwarshuis hit three massive sixes off the final three balls as Australia plundered 50 runs off the final three overs as Australia made 8-184 at Carrara.
Nikhil Chaudhary scored 45 off 35 and Joel Davis 39 off 25 for the hosts, who won the first T20I in Melbourne on Saturday night.
Aside from that though, it was boom or bust for the Aussies at the top of the order, as England controlled the powerplay.
Mitch Marsh (15) smashed two sixes and then hit a return catch to Henry Crocombe, Jake Fraser-McGurk (27) bashed three before holing out to Harry Brook off Sam Curran's first ball.
Chaudhary faced three dot balls before roasting Sam Harper (3) with an ill-judged run to the diving debutant James Coles at point.
Then, Tim David (6) skied a Jamie Overton delivery to Coles to give England four wickets in the powerplay, and Australia 4-58.
Middle-over maestro Adil Rashid bowled three overs unchanged for a miserly 1-13, working in tandem with left-arm spinner Liam Dawson to stymie the flow of runs.
That spell accounted for Lachlan Shaw, who laboured to a 17-ball 10 before sweeping the Rashid straight down the throat of Phil Salt.
Davies and Chaudhary built their 49-run partnership steadily, although it could have been over far earlier when Davies was dropped on 6 by Aneurin Donald at deep backward square, the Welshman using a combination of both hands and his face to save a boundary.
Davies then survived a LBW review after Curran hit him square in the box.
Those near-misses looked to cost England as the 23-year-old belted Rashid for 10 in his final two balls.
Curran's ability to vary his pace was paying dividends, with his first over only going for a single run, while his second garnered just five more.
He then had Chaudhary caught at deep square leg for 45 (35), Curran holding a clenched fist towards the Delhi-born batter, a low-key addendum to their heated MCG discussion on Saturday night.
The Australians countered beautifully at the close; hitting 16 runs off Overton in the 18th over, 14 off the expensive Crocombe in the 19th, and 20 off the otherwise excellent Curran in the last.
Jack Edwards did fall, caught by Brook for an entertaining 18 off Crocombe, while Curran effected the runout of Davies (39) off his own bowling when Davis and Dwarshuis attempted a suicidal second run.
However, Dwarshuis made up for that by crushing the Englishman for three-straight sixes to end the innings and set England a more than acceptable 185 to win the match.
