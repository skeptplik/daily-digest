A federal gas reservation scheme requiring Australia’s largest exporters to keep up to 20% of their product for domestic use will go ahead with the Coalition’s support.
The opposition waved through the government’s plans in the in a late night Senate sitting on Thursday night, giving Labor the numbers it needed. A short sitting of the lower house rubber stamped changes, seeing the legislation pass parliament.
The scheme will begin in January 2028.
On Friday, the resources minister, Madeleine King, said it would deliver cheaper gas prices for Australians but could not say by how much.
“We don’t want to set a figure at this time because the experience with price caps when you name a figure is that also becomes the floor, the lower price,” she said.
“And we want to make sure the market’s able to operate to drive prices down as far as they can.”
Speaking in the upper house on Thursday, the shadow resources minister, Susan McDonald, said the Coalition would not oppose the scheme after weeks of hard negotiations between the two major parties.
“The Coalition will act in the national interest,” she said.
“We will allow the government’s proposal to move forward because we do believe in a domestic gas reservation and more gas being made available to Australia.”
Labor granted Western Australia a permanent carve-out after lobbying from the federal Coalition and the state’s premier, Roger Cook.
On Friday, King said: “WA is really the inspiration for this national scheme because they’ve had a scheme since 2008, which has been really important for gas prices and supply in WA.”
WA has an existing scheme that requires exporters to reserve 15% of liquified natural gas (LNG) for the domestic market over the life of a development.
That target has not yet been met. Cook said in August the proportion of LNG delivered to the domestic market was 8.7% in 2021 and rose to 12.5% by 2025.
Business groups and gas companies pressured the federal Coalition to do a deal with Labor to keep the Greens out of negotiations.
The industry minister, Tim Ayres, said the changes would deliver “fundamental economic reform that will make Australia stronger”.
“Australia’s gas is a national asset, and that national asset should be managed in the national interest,” he said.
“This is not an export ban, nor is it an export cap. It is a predictable and proportional contribution to domestic supply.”
Ayres said Australia would remain a reliable supplier of gas for its key partners.
The key sticking point between the parties was the government’s requirement for gas companies to oversupply the market by 10%, which is designed to drive down prices for households and industry. This will remain in place.
The Coalition originally argued this would lower prices to a level that would dry up future gas investment.
They have secured a statutory review to assess the impact of a reservation policy on investment after five years.
The Greens demanded a 25% tax on gas exports in return for their support and voted against the scheme in its current form.
The minor party’s leader, David Shoebridge, condemned the scheme as a “stitch-up” against the Australian public.
“This week, parliament ends with Labor and the Coalition cutting their deal for the gas industry, delivering for the 1% of their corporate donors,” he said before the vote in the Senate.
The lower house is set to sit for a short period on Friday morning, a day later than expected, in order to pass legislation amended in the Senate.
