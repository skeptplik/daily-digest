A consumption tax cut on groceries, which has been proposed and is expected to start in April, will likely complicate pricing strategies for food and beverage businesses.
”In Japan, the consumption tax is something that people tend to pay close attention to, so consumers are very sensitive to price changes,” said Keiji Kanda, chief economist at Daiwa Institute of Research.
In an effort to take the edge off inflation, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi has proposed lowering the consumption tax on food and beverage items purchased at stores from 8% to 1% for two years starting in April. Japan’s consumption tax is 10% for most products, but is set at 8% on groceries.
