A serious cyber incident that forced ministers to take down online benefits and pension services was “probably an attack”, the UK’s former chief technology officer has said.
The Department for Work and Pensions took down several systems that appeared to still be offline on Saturday morning. David Knott, who led government technology until December 2025, said much infrastructure was outdated, and underinvestment in defence and increasing sophistication of attacks meant it was only getting more vulnerable.
“The government has been very sensible in using quite precise language, and being very evidence-based in the way they’ve reported it,” he told BBC Newsnight on Friday evening. “At the same time, some of that language does tell me it’s probably an attack.
“They’ve used the word ‘cyber’, which normally means it’s an attack. They’ve taken down some of the citizen-facing services, which implies to me that there’s some kind of attack could be mounted through the front door. So my strong suspicion is this is an attack.”
Downing Street said there was no evidence a “malicious actor” had targeted other departments. The government took its benefits portal down late on Thursday after detecting “unusual activity” – meaning people were unable to check their state pension, make a disability benefits application or claim universal credit.
The DWP makes about 2.5m regular payments a day, and it is understood that reported delays are not related to the cyber incident. The department urged people to be alert to any scams related to the outage and stressed that the government would never ask people to confirm bank details or click a link.
DWP insiders said payments for Friday would already have been sent earlier in the week, but there could be a backlog of manual payments running into tens of thousands due early next week to be processed over the weekend. Internal DWP systems were restored on Friday, allowing officials to begin working again, but the public-facing portal remained offline.
It also said people should contact their benefit helpline if they were having urgent difficulties and confirmed that no one would face a sanction for not meeting conditions while the system was offline.
Knott told Newsnight government systems were under attack every second of every day, saying: “There’s a statistic that, if you put an unprotected PC on the internet, it will get attacked within 27 seconds. And that’s a PC with no interesting assets on it. If you imagine, if you were holding the country’s tax records or health records or pension records, then you’re under attack all the time.”
He added: “The challenges in defending against cyber-attacks has been an asymmetric conflict for quite a long time; it’s really hard to defend, and it’s getting easier and easier and easier to attack. A lot of people are using AI tools to mount those attacks, which just makes it easier and easier to do. At the same time, if you’ve got systems that haven’t been upgraded for several years, and they’ve got known security vulnerabilities in them, you don’t need an advanced AI model to figure out how to attack them.”
Knott said the government measured how much of its infrastructure would be considered out of date. “I think in 2023, it was 26%. In 2024, it was 28%. So the numbers were going in the wrong direction.”
A DWP spokesperson said: “We have taken some DWP services offline as a precaution following a cyber incident. We are working closely with the National Cyber Security Centre and Government Cyber Coordination Centre to investigate and to bring services back online as quickly as possible to minimise any disruption.
“Regular benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal. Anyone experiencing delays or other issues with payments should call our helplines in the usual way. As we work to safely get our services back online, customers directly affected by this incident will not face sanctions.”
No 10 said work was being carried out “as quickly as possible” to restore services. Internal DWP systems were restored on Friday, allowing officials to begin working again, but the public-facing portal remained offline.
GCHQ’s National Cyber Security Centre and government experts are investigating the suspected hack. The UK’s data protection watchdog said it is in contact with the DWP about the incident.
