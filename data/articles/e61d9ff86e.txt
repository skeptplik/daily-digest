The health ministry is considering raising nursing care insurance premiums for some older people with an annual income of ¥1.2 million ($7,500) or more after public pension and other deductions, effective from fiscal 2027, which begins in April, sources said Thursday.
The ministry also plans to implement measures to reduce the premium burden on low-income earners, according to its premium review plans presented at the day’s meeting of the Social Security Council.
The changes come as the nursing care insurance system faces funding and other challenges amid growing demand for nursing care services in Japan’s graying society.
The system collects premiums from people who are 65 and older. Municipal governments set standard premium amounts, and the nationwide average is ¥6,225 per month for the period between fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2026. Standard premium amounts are reviewed once every three years.
