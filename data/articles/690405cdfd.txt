Between matches at the 2026 League of Legends World Championship, Riot Games teased fans with a long-anticipated announcement: The next era of League of Legends is upon us.
Revealed in an array of cinematics and gameplay, the “all-encompassing, large-scale update” coming to the 17-year-old game in 2027 is titled New Dawn. If the cheers that bellowed through the Barclays Center in Brooklyn are any indication, diehard fans of the franchise are ready.
Riot left most details of this major overhaul to the imagination. The cinematic trailer for New Dawn revealed stunning renders of several fan-favorite champions, including Blitzcrank, Lux, Leona, Sett, Poppy, Volibear, Riven, Jhin, and Ahri. Snippets of gameplay saw Master Yi and Morgana in action. But what is at the heart of the update? The studio says it will have more to reveal in January.
What the announcement did reveal is that League will get a brand-new game client in 2027; Riot last overhauled the architecture in 2016. The sizzle reel also promised a refreshed version of the Summoner’s Rift map, a defining feature of the long-running MOBA. For now, fans can only speculate what Riot’s description of “evolved gameplay, and new ways to experience players’ favorite champions” really means.
Whatever the case, there’s a clear ambition on Riot’s part to grow the game not only for longtime players but a potentially new audience that may only know League from Arcane or the Riftbound TCG. At a pre-Finals press event, League executive producer Paul Bellezza made it very clear that Riot sees the game as “multi-generational” and would love for it to run “100 years.” Doing that requires strategy. At a time when major live-service franchises are also gunning for expanded and renewed attention (see: World of Warcraft: Forever), it’s only natural that Riot would follow suit with League.
But the company isn’t ready to share their real game plan just yet. Instead, it bundled announcements of a new vision for gameplay with a reminder that League remains a major esports endeavor. Next year’s calendar will see some bigger changes too: The 2027 season kicks off in China with an expanded First Stand tournament, followed by the Mid-Season Invitational in Valencia, Spain. Worlds 2027 will span Japan and South Korea, with Nagoya hosting Japan’s first-ever World Championship matches before the tournament concludes in Seoul. Riot is also considering replacing the traditional Worlds Play-In Stage with the Demacia Cup, potentially opening the door for more teams to compete.
