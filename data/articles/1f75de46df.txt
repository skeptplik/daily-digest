Law enforcement authorities from 11 countries have disrupted 57 illegal streaming services that operated hundreds of websites and drew over 7.3 million visits each year.
Such illegal IPTV services offer access to television channels, live sports, and movies without permission from the copyright owners for a small monthly fee.
According to the European Audiovisual Anti-Piracy Alliance (AAPA) trade association, legitimate service providers lose around €4.83 billion annually as roughly 60 million people in Europe use illegal platforms for streaming content.
"The operation resulted in the disruption of 57 illegal IPTV services and the identification of 321 websites illegally offering copyrighted content, with a combined annual traffic of more than 7.3 million visits. Investigators have so far traced over EUR 26 million in cryptocurrency transactions, with further analysis ongoing," Europol said in a Tuesday press release.
"The operation targeted a criminal market worth billions of euros annually, in which illegal streaming providers sell unauthorised access to premium television channels, live sports and films, often collecting subscription payments through cryptocurrency."
As part of this joint action, which also involved Europol, the European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO), INTERPOL, and AAPA, the investigators also partnered with crypto industry experts from Binance, Coinbase, and Chainalysis to trace cryptocurrency payments and identify the financial networks powering these illegal IPTV services.
They did this by looking for links between the illegal IPTV services, crypto wallets, financial transactions, and the individuals who control these services, who used the wallets to receive and transfer payments, making it easier to move money between countries and hide the origin of their profits.
Europol and the European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO) joined forces in 2016 to create the Intellectual Property Crime Coordinated Coalition (IPC3), which operates within Europol and targets counterfeiting and online and offline piracy.
In June, another major international operation coordinated by Bulgaria with Europol's support, dubbed "Operation KRATOS 2," resulted in the removal of more than 27,000 illegal streaming URLs, the shutdown of nine organized crime groups, and 29 arrests.
That followed Operation KRATOS, another international anti-piracy operation carried out in summer 2024, which shut down a massive illegal streaming network with over 22 million users worldwide.
Earlier this year, in January, Europol, Eurojust, and Interpol also coordinated Operation Switch Off, a global law enforcement action that led to the seizure of three industrial-scale illegal IPTV services.
More recently, in May, Italian authorities dismantled the CINEMAGOAL piracy platform, which provided illegal access to many online streaming platforms, including Netflix, Spotify, and Disney+.
