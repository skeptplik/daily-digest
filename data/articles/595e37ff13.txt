Swiss watchmaker Mondaine has expanded its Doppio collection with the Solar Skeleton, a timepiece that brings solar technology into the foreground of its design.
Rather than concealing the solar plates beneath the dial, the watch reveals them through a precisely cut circular opening, turning a functional component into a defining visual feature.
The design builds on the distinctive visual language of Mondaine‘s Doppio collection, which is characterised by double-line hands and indexes, a concave dial and a double-domed sapphire crystal.
Mondaine explained that these elements establish a sense of repetition, rhythm and structural clarity, while the exposed solar plates introduce an additional layer of depth and visual interest.
While solar technology is typically hidden from view, Mondaine has taken a different approach with the Doppio Solar Skeleton.
A carefully positioned circular opening around the centre of the dial offers a controlled view of the solar movement, while the outer section retains the indexes to ensure clear readability.
By making the technology visible, Mondaine aimed to bridge the watch’s technical function with its aesthetic identity, without compromising the “clarity and legibility” that define the collection.
Powered by a Swiss Ronda solar movement, the watch converts natural and artificial light into electrical energy, which is stored in a rechargeable battery.
According to Mondaine, a few minutes of sunlight can reactivate a fully discharged movement, while a full charge provides a power reserve of up to eight months in complete darkness.
The brand explained that this technology eliminates the need for regular battery replacements, offering a more resource-conscious approach to timekeeping.
The Doppio Solar Skeleton is available in two versions, each offering a different interpretation of its distinctive design.
The first combines a polished silver 316L stainless steel case with a vegan grape leather strap, bringing together traditional watchmaking materials and a contemporary material alternative.
The second features a polished black stainless steel case paired with a matching mesh bracelet, creating a more monochromatic appearance that emphasises the watch’s clean lines and geometric detailing.
Both versions feature a 41-millimetre case, a discreet date window and a precisely laser-etched Mondaine logo and solar markings.
A double-domed sapphire crystal and water resistance of up to 5 ATM complete the design, “combining refined aesthetics with everyday practicality”.
“With the Doppio Solar Skeleton, we wanted to make solar technology part of the design itself,” said Mondaine.
“By revealing the cells beneath the dial, we have created a watch where functionality becomes a defining aesthetic feature, while staying true to Mondaine’s principles of clarity, precision and Swiss design.”
The Doppio Solar Skeleton reflects Mondaine’s commitment to iconic Swiss minimalist design, where form and function work together.
By integrating visible solar technology into the collection’s established design language, the watch intends to demonstrate how a functional element can become an integral part of a timepiece’s aesthetic identity.
