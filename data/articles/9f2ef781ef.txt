Liz Daly Byrne, a model and philanthropist who was married to PMC vice chairman Gerry Byrne for more than 55 years, has died at age 81.
	Liz Daly Byrne faced a battle with cancer three years ago, and recently learned that her illness had returned. She had faced recurring complications from severe injuries suffered in a bicycle accident in 2000. Byrne died Nov. 17 after a period of home hospice care in New York.
Byrne was active as a philanthropist and social advocate through her work with the United Nations’ Operation Smile campaign, traveling internationally with the organization on medical missions. She had long been involved in causes to support military families and veterans. Byrne was remembered by her family, including her adult children, digital entrepreneur Gavin Byrne and actor Megan Byrne, as a woman who embodied “elegance, strength, wit, faith, generosity and compassion.”
Liz Daly Byrne was also extremely fast on her feet, as she demonstrated in 1997 during the Cannes Film Festival. During a post-screening party for Warner Bros./New Regency’s buzzy period drama “L.A. Confidential” at a vineyard, a tipsy guest fell into the pool. As Variety reported, when Byrne saw what happened, she “threw off her jacket and shoes and dove into the water for a dramatic rescue, after which she calmly took her seat for the sit-down dinner and fireworks show.”
Born in 1945, the-then Liz Daly grew up in the shadow of Yankee Stadium in the Bronx, N.Y. She met her future husband Gerry, who served as publisher of Variety from 1989 to 2000 and at present is vice chair of WWD parent company PMC, while he was attending Fordham College in the borough. The two were neighbors, but in a wonderfully Bronx love story touch, they met one evening during a ride on the D train.
	Early on, Byrne was drawn to New York’s fashion world. She signed with top modeling agencies Mannequin and Ellen Harth and launched her career as the primary designer’s model for retailers Saks Fifth Avenue and Bonwit Teller. In addition to appearing in TV commercials, Byrne spent years as a runway model with Anne Klein, Donna Karan, Oscar de la Renta and Bill Tice. She then spent another decade working with designers Bob Mackie and Ray Aghayan.
The couple married in 1969 at the chapel at St. Patrick’s Cathedral Chapel, after Gerry had completed a three-year tour with the U.S. Marine Corps in Vietnam. In tribute to her husband’s service, Byrne successfully launched a POW bracelet program to honor the sacrifices made by some families. The effort took root after being publicized nationally through coverage in The New York Times.
	Byrne was a dedicated marathon runner who participated in multiple New York City marathons as well as runs hosted by the Marines in Washington, D.C. She was active in numerous nonprofits and in entertainment and media industry charitable efforts.
Byrne will be memorialized with a Mass of Christian Burial to be held at the Church of St. Ignatius Loyola in New York on Dec. 1 at 11 a.m.
