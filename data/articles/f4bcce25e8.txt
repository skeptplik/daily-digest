Alan Jones, the 1980 Formula 1 world champion, has died aged 80.
He won 12 grands prix during his F1 career and joined Sir Jack Brabham as the only Australian to be crowned world champion.
Jones turned 80 on Monday.
A statement released on behalf of Jones's wife, Amanda Butler-Davis, said the legendary racer died following a short illness.
"We are extremely saddened to announce the passing of our beloved Alan, who passed away on 5 November following a short illness," Butler-Davis said.
"He will be dearly missed and fondly remembered by those closest to him, as well as the many fans whose support he was always grateful for.
"Alan dedicated his life to motorsport, building a legacy that will live on through the countless people he touched along the way.
"Through his achievements, passion and unmistakable character, both on and off the track, Alan left an enduring mark on the sport both nationally and across the globe.
"As a family, we would like to take this opportunity to thank everyone who was part of Alan's journey for the many years of friendship, love and support. He was incredibly fortunate to have so many wonderful people around him."
Jones was born in Melbourne on November 2, 1946.
His father, Stan, was a racing driver who won the 1959 Australian Grand Prix.
Jones wanted to follow in his father's footsteps and left for Europe in the late 60s, but could not afford to kick-start his racing career overseas.
He returned to Australia, but determined not to give up his dream, Jones moved to London in 1970.
He got his break in 1973 when he got a sponsored Formula 3 gig with GRD. He took his first win at Silverstone that year and went on to finish second in the British F3 championship.
He made his F1 debut in 1975 for Hesketh at the Spanish Grand Prix, a race remembered in infamy for a crash that caused the death of five spectators.
The team went out of business before the season finished, but Jones moved to Graham Hill's team, where a fifth-place finish at the Nürburgring was the highlight.
An ill-fated season with Surtees in 1976 left his F1 career in limbo, before the tragic death of driver Tom Pryce at the 1977 South African Grand Prix led to Jones being signed by the Shadow team.
Jones claimed his maiden win in Austria, which led to Ferrari offering and then reneging on a deal for the Australian.
Ferrari instead signed Gilles Villeneuve, while Jones ultimately signed for Williams.
Jones won five grands prix in 1980 — Argentina, France, Britain, Canada and the USA — to claim the world title and become just the second Australian to achieve that honour.
He first retired from F1 at the end of the 1981 season, after winning the season finale at Caesars Palace.
He made a few brief returns before his final grand prix in 1986.
Jones continued racing internationally for many years, while also entering media. He was a commentator with Channel Nine as part of the F1 coverage in Australia from 1987 to 2002, and was also on Network 10 when it had the F1 races in the early 2010s.
He is survived by his wife and his six children, Christian, Amber, Zara, Jack, Camilla and Emma.
