India presents its case to IOC for hosting 2036 Olympics
India, which has tossed its hat in the ring in the bid to stage the 2036 Olympics, presented its plans to the International Olympic Committee's (IOC) Future Host Commission on Thursday and appeared confident of advancing to the next phase.
India is pitching for Ahmedabad, located in Prime Minister Narendra Modi's home state of Gujarat and a major business hub, to be the host city.
"I think we presented a good case and we are very, very hopeful that we will move to the next ‌stage (of the bidding process)," Indian Olympic Association (IOA) chief executive Raghuram Iyer told the media, after the virtual presentation.
India is competing with six other nations to host one of the world’s biggest sporting events — Germany, Hungary, Qatar, South Africa, South Korea and Turkey.
Iyer said that a final pick for the 2036 games is not expected until after the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, or in early 2029.
Meanwhile, Ahmedabad will host the 2030 Commonwealth Games and is also bidding for the 2038 Asian Games.
India minister says US trade deal nearing completion
India's Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal on Friday said that a trade deal with the United States was "close to culmination."
"The process and pathway, which was always something which held back finalising the deal, is now taking some shape," Goyal told the India Debt Capital Market Summit in a virtual address on Friday.
Goyal's statement comes after the office of the United States Representative (USTR) on Wednesday proposed a 25% tariff on Indian imports after conducting a probe into 16 major US trading partners over alleged excess capacity.
The US proposal came with a provision to slash the levies on India to 17% if a bilateral trade deal, negotiations for which have experienced road bumps over several months, is reached.
Special parliament sitting: What is on Modi government's agenda?
Union Minister Kiren Rijiju and the communications from the two houses do not elaborate on why Modi's government has called the sitting.
But political and Indian media circles are abuzz with guesswork that a bill to expand assemblies, contentiously linked to the implementation of a 33% quota for women in the national Parliament and state assemblies, could be back on the table after failing to get a two-thirds passing majority when it was proposed by the government in April.
While the women's reservation broadly has bipartisan support, it is tied to a separate Delimitation Bill that seeks to redraw constituency boundaries based on population — which is the bone of contention.
The opposition is concerned that a population-based redistribution of seats would swing the balance in BJP's favor, as the party has a large support base in the densely populated northern states.
Meanwhile, leaders of southern states, where birthrates have declined, fear that when it comes to political representation the population-based delimitation would favor the northern states, where population growth has been higher.
How has india's opposition reacted to the reconvening of parliament?
India's main opposition Congress party has deemed the move by Modi's government to call for the brief sitting of the parliament "an assault on the Constitution of India" which Congress General Secretary in-charge Communications and MP Jairam Ramesh said "must and will be resisted with all might."
In a post on X, Ramesh accused the government of "moving to manufacture a fraudulent two-thirds majority in the Lok Sabha"
Ramesh also listed out a three-point Congress stance on delimitation and women's quota, which includes:
- Maintaining the current strength of the Lok Sabha for another 25 years.
- Maintaining the current state-wise distribution of seats in the Lok Sabha for another 25 years
- Implementation of one-third reservation for women and reservation within reservation for women belonging to Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes from the next general elections, slated for 2029.
A meeting of the Congress Parliamentary Strategy Group is also expected to be held on Saturday to discuss the gameplan for the upcoming parliament sitting, news agency ANI reported.
Indian parliament to hold special sitting after a gap of more than 100 days
A political storm could be on the horizon in India as both houses of India's bicameral parliament are set to reconvene next week for a special sitting at the request of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government.
The Lok Sabha (lower house) is slated to sit on November 30 while the Rajya Sabha (upper house) will meet on December 2, official memos said, adding that the session may conclude on December 3.
"The Government has requested Hon'ble Speaker, Lok Sabha and Hon'ble Chairman, Rajya Sabha to reconvene Lok Sabha on Monday, 30th November, 2026 and Rajya Sabha on Wednesday, 2nd December, 2026 respectively to transact essential Government Business," Union Minister of Parliamentary Affairs Kiren Rijiju wrote on X, announcing the government's request on Thursday evening.
The resumption of parliament comes after the monsoon session, which is the second regular session of India's legislative body, saw both the houses being adjourned sine die (indefinitely) on August 13.
The session never ended formally and has remained unprorogued for a record over 100 days, while a winter session that typically starts early December looks delayed.
The monsoon session was turbulent as it began amid a massive youth-led protest called by India's "Cockroach" movement that sought, and later managed to get, the resignation of Modi's then-education minister Dharmendra Pradhan over exam irregularities.
The parliament, itself, became a protest ground as both Modi's ruling coalition, led by his Bharatiya Janata Party, and the opposition INDIA bloc, by Congress, held demonstrations within its premises.
The political deadlock was unrelenting and the parliament struggled to get much business underway, culminating in the adjournment.
