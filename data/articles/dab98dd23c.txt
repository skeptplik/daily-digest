Skyscraper in Riyadh damaged as Saudi Arabia reports surge in new Houthi attacks
A skyscraper in Saudi Arabia's capital Riyadh was damaged on Thursday after the Gulf country reported new attacks by Yemen's Houthis. Witnesses said the city's Public Investment Fund Tower, which showcases Saudi Arabia's economic ambitions, was "damaged across several floors, with windows blown out".
A skyscraper in Riyadh housing the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund was damaged on Thursday, witnesses said, as the kingdom reported new attacks by Yemen's Houthis.
Saudi Arabia has been battling a surge in attacks since fighting reignited over the summer in neighbouring Yemen's civil war, where Riyadh backs the internationally recognised government against the Houthis.
In the early hours of Thursday, AFP journalists in the Saudi capital reported a powerful blast had been heard.
Around the same time, civil defence authorities issued a warning for "potential danger" but said minutes later that the danger had passed.
The Saudi-led military coalition fighting the Houthis said it had "addressed a missile threat launched by the terrorist Houthi militia towards Riyadh".
"Initial assessment indicates the impact of debris, with material damage currently being surveyed," said coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki.
Witnesses later told AFP that Riyadh's Public Investment Fund Tower, which at 80 storeys, is the tallest in the city's financial district, had been damaged.
"The facade is damaged across several floors, with windows blown out," said one source who requested anonymity. Two other sources confirmed the damage.
Standing 385 metres high, the tower dominates King Abdullah Financial District, which acts as a showcase for Saudi Arabia's economic ambitions.
With imposing skyscrapers, luxury shops and green spaces, the district also hosts the headquarters of the Saudi stock exchange along with multinationals such as Goldman Sachs.
Chaired by Saudi Arabia's de facto ruler, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, the sovereign wealth fund is one of the largest in the world with more than $900 billion in assets under management.
It is also one of the main instruments for diversifying the kingdom's economy away from oil, plans which have been threatened by the latest fighting.
Deadly airport attack
Malki also said Thursday that the coalition had intercepted five ballistic missiles launched towards Saudi Arabia's southern region, which borders Yemen.
The Iran-backed Houthis have stepped up their attacks against Saudi Arabia in recent weeks, striking targets including airports, energy infrastructure, military sites and Riyadh's financial district.
In October, a deadly attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport killed 14 people and injured hundreds, forcing the suspension of flights for two weeks.
In response, the Saudi coalition has been striking Houthi targets in areas of Yemen held by the group.
On Thursday, Houthi media outlets reported that Saudi Arabia had launched another attack on Yemen's capital Sanaa.
The Almasirah television channel said Saudi forces have "once again targeted the shoe and textile factory of the Alyateem Development Foundation", citing its correspondent in Sanaa.
The Saudi coalition said it had "destroyed a ballistic missile launch platform in Dhamar", a city about 100 kilometres (60 miles) south of Sanaa that was used by the Houthis to strike Riyadh.
