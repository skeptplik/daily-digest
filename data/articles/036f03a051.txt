The government plans to build a network to share natural disaster-related information across Asia, as well as knowledge accumulated in Southeast Asia, government sources said Monday.
The government hopes to use the network to encourage countries across Asia to introduce early-warning systems that analyze meteorological data and issue alerts before natural disasters strike.
The plan will be announced during the 31st Conference of the Parties to the U.N. Framework Convention on Climate Change, or COP31, which is being held in Turkey.
Early warning systems analyze data collected by satellites and radar to alert those at risk of sustaining damage from disasters. Examples in Japan include the government’s heatstroke alert system and “Kikikuru” service visualizing the risk of landslides and flooding on a map.
Japan started a public-private project in 2023 to spread services using disaster early warning systems in the Asia-Pacific region. It has conducted research on market needs and feasibility in member states of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations and supported companies hoping to start related businesses in such countries.
In Thailand, where flash flooding caused by sudden weather changes is a major problem, Japan’s Weathernews provides weather information such as forecasts and warnings of sudden localized downpours.
Through the envisioned network, the Japanese government aims to share knowledge and examples from six ASEAN countries and facilitate the introduction of disaster early warning systems throughout Asia to help minimize the loss and damage from climate change.
