In short:
The Socceroos have been beaten 2-0 by Jordan in their international friendly in Doha.
Jordan scored in each half, missed a penalty and had another goal chalked off for offside.
What's next?
Tony Popovic is set for contract talks with FA in the coming months.
Tony Popovic's Socceroos have been deservedly beaten 2-0 in Qatar by a Jordanian side that had not previously won in 2026.
Jordan scored once in each half, had a penalty saved by Patrick Beach and what looked a good goal ruled out for offside.
Popovic described the first period as "terrible", and while Australia did improve in the second, any hope of a comeback disappeared when substitute Daniel Arzani was sent off for two bookings inside six minutes. He was on the pitch for 15 minutes.
"We got what we deserved," Popovic said.
"A real contrast from the other night against Qatar. They came with a lot of energy, they were fresh, they were focused, they were more aggressive than us and it showed."
The one positive for Popovic and Football Australia, as they prepare to negotiate over a new contract, is that the Socceroos were at their best when they were at their youngest.
When Arzani was dismissed in the 80th minute, he was, at 27, the team's second-oldest player after Jason Geria (33). The other nine were aged 19 to 23.
But after 26 matches in 26 months (12 wins, seven losses), the 53-year-old's future is in limbo. Talks on a new contract, he says, should take place "in the next couple of months", but he admitted beforehand this could be his final game.
"We have to learn, all of us; me included," he said.
"There are some big lessons tonight. This has been a reality check for all of us. There's a lot of work to do."
Popovic had chosen the same XI that started Friday's impressive 3-0 win over Qatar, but it soon became clear this would be a very different match.
Rennes winger Mousa Al-Taamari was particularly influential as Jordan poured forward, and it was no great surprise when he won a 31st-minute penalty.
Al-Taamari took it himself, but it was poor — yet still a good save with his feet by Beach.
But two minutes later, the Australian keeper was beaten. Lucas Herrington lost possession and Al-Taamari was released. Running away from Paul Okon-Engstler, he turned Souttar inside-out, then tucked his shot inside the near post.
Souttar was replaced at half-time by Herrington's older brother Diesel, who made his debut against Qatar.
He was part of an improved performance as Australia stemmed the flow of Jordanian attacks, albeit without mounting much threat of their own.
Popovic made further changes soon after the hour and subs Kasey Bos and Marcus Younis twice almost combined for an equaliser.
But Arzani, having been booked for hauling down Al-Taamari, was late on Abdallah Nasib to receive a second card.
A man down and pushing forward, Australia left gaps. The assistant referee's flag saved them when Odeh Fakhouri went through to score in the 93rd minute, but there was no escape a minute later.
Herrington and Alessandro Circati failed to deal with a long ball and Ghaith Al-Madadha found Ali Olwan, who produced a composed finish.
