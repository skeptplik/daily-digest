India's Dommaraju Gukesh and Javokhir Sindarov of Uzbekistan, two of the world's youngest chess grandmasters are meeting in Geneva to battle it out for the game's biggest title and celebrity bragging rights.
Two titans of Chess go head-to-head in Geneva on Tuesday in a match that will be the most youthful world championship contest in the competition's 140-year history.
Defending the crown is India's Dommaraju Gukesh, who entered the record books in 2024, when he took the title at 18-years old.
He's up against Javokhir Sindarov of Uzbekistan, who's also 20, and for some is favourite to win based on form, having won the World Cup and Candidates Tournament. He's currently fourth in the global rankings compared to Gukesh, who's dropped to 26th.
Sindarov also goes into the battle with a psychological advantage with two victories and one draw from their three previous encounters.
But, at the press conference on Sunday at the United Nations’ European headquarters where both players presented themselves to the world, Gukesh rejected suggestions that he faced a more difficult task this time round to protect his title.
“I don’t think I’m here to defend anything,” he said. “I’m here to win it all over again. That’s the mindset I’m in.”
The tournament is taking place at the Fondation Martin Bodmer at Cologny, one of the most important private libraries in the world, as it includes some of the oldest surviving copies of the New Testament and Shakespeare's First Folio from 1623.
The match will consist of 14 classical games. The first player to score 7.5 points or more will be crowned World Champion and claim the larger share of the total prize fund of $2.5 million. If the score is equal after 14 games, the winner will be decided by a tiebreak which will be played on 12 December.
