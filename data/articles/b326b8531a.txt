Ukrainian authorities, working with the FBI, have arrested four suspected members of an international ransomware organization linked to more than $40.5 million in ransom payments from at least 28 companies worldwide.
The arrests come after a two-year investigation into a cybercrime organization comprising at least 27 individuals from Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Russia, and other countries.
Investigators say the organization was connected to a larger cybercrime network of more than 60 members operating across eight jurisdictions, including former members of the notorious Conti ransomware gang.
According to Ukraine's National Police, the group targeted organizations in the oil and gas, pharmaceutical, manufacturing, logistics, and financial sectors, as well as critical infrastructure.
The attackers researched their victims' financial standing, assets, employees, and IT vulnerabilities before launching attacks designed to steal corporate data, encrypt systems, and extort cryptocurrency payments.
Speaking in an exclusive interview with Ukrainian news outlet Censor.NET, acting National Police chief Maksym Tsutskiridze said investigators had identified at least 28 companies that paid more than $40.5 million in ransoms.
"We have identified at least 28 companies that transferred more than $40.5 million in ransom payments to the hackers, equivalent to approximately UAH 1.8 billion. This is money that has already been paid. We are still establishing the full extent of the damage," Tsutskiridze said.
In one attack, the hackers stole 1.4 terabytes of data from a U.S. company and expanded their attack to other companies within the same corporate group.
The attackers initially demanded $10 million but ultimately received $5 million after negotiations.
As first reported by French cybersecurity news outlet LeMagIT, Ukrainian court records identify several victims of the criminal organization, including Warren Oil Company, Maxxis International-USA, Newpark Resources, and Quantum Health.
The court documents identify Warren Oil Company as the victim of an August 2024 attack in which hackers copied 1.4 TB of data before receiving an 84.5 BTC ransom payment, equivalent to approximately $5 million at the time.
The records also allege that Newpark Resources paid $1.5 million in December 2024, while Quantum Health paid $5 million in June 2026 following an initial $15 million ransom demand.
LeMagIT also noted that the BlackSuit ransomware gang claimed Maxxis International as a victim in November 2024, while Ukrainian court records identify Chaos as the ransomware used by the criminal organization.
The Conti ransomware operation shut down in May 2022, with its members dispersing into smaller ransomware gangs and other cybercrime operations.
One of the groups linked to former Conti members was BlackSuit, which initially operated as Quantum ransomware in January 2022 before becoming Royal.
In June 2023, Royal began operating under the BlackSuit name after testing a new encryptor and carrying out high-profile attacks, including one against the City of Dallas, Texas.
However, Ukrainian authorities have not identified the four arrested suspects as former Conti members or confirmed their individual affiliations with BlackSuit or Chaos.
AI-powered IT support impersonation
To breach corporate networks, the cybercriminals impersonated IT support personnel and used a custom-developed platform with AI-generated voice messages to automate fraudulent phone calls.
During these calls, they tricked employees into disclosing login credentials or installing remote-access software, allowing the attackers to access internal corporate systems.
In one documented incident, an attacker kept an employee on the phone while another attacker accessed the company's systems, with the call ending only after the attackers confirmed the intrusion was successful.
"They created their own platform featuring voice messages generated by artificial intelligence. They called company employees, posing as IT support specialists. Under the pretext of providing technical assistance, they persuaded employees to disclose their login credentials or install remote access software. This allowed them to gain access to corporate networks," Tsutskiridze explained.
The Ukrainian court records provide additional insight into these attacks, describing how the hackers used phishing pages impersonating corporate VPN services, spoofed phone numbers, and remote-access tools such as TeamViewer and AnyDesk.
During the Warren Oil Company attack, the records allege that the attackers tricked an employee into entering credentials on a phishing page, then obtained a two-factor authentication code that granted them remote access to the corporate network.
Once inside the network, the attackers stole sensitive information and encrypted files using ransomware, disrupting the victim's operations.
The gang then demanded cryptocurrency payments in exchange for restoring access to encrypted systems while threatening to publicly release stolen corporate data.
"They would deprive a company of access to its own data and then demand millions of dollars in exchange for decrypting it. At the same time, they threatened to publish the stolen information. Victims were put under pressure from both sides, forcing them to pay the ransom in cryptocurrency," Tsutskiridze said.
Ukrainian authorities conducted 66 simultaneous searches across Kyiv and 11 regions of Ukraine as part of the joint operation with U.S. law enforcement. Four suspected key members of the organization were detained, although authorities did not disclose their exact arrest locations.
During these searches, investigators seized computers, mobile phones, bank cards, nearly $370,000 in cash, and €20,000.
Four alleged key members of the organization were formally notified of suspicion and are currently in custody.
The suspects face charges related to participating in a criminal organization and unauthorized interference with information systems under Ukrainian law. If convicted, they face up to 15 years in prison.
Ukrainian authorities say the investigation is ongoing as law enforcement works to identify additional members of the international network and determine their involvement in other cyberattacks.
The National Police also released a video showing footage from the investigation, including law enforcement raids, seized cash, interviews with investigators, and a suspect being questioned.
The video also contains audio from an alleged ransomware extortion call demanding $15 million after claiming to have stolen more than 1.5 TB of corporate data.
