Komatsu, Ishikawa Pref. – Usually, nicknames like “the god of sake” are platitudes. But for brewer Naohiko Noguchi, it’s now as close to a literal description as can be.
On Oct. 16, Japan’s Agency for Cultural Affairs made an announcement without precedent: ginjō-zukuri — the exacting techniques used to brew aromatic ginjō sake from highly polished rice at low temperatures — be designated an Important Intangible Cultural Property, with 93-year-old master brewer Noguchi recognized as its holder.
Once formally approved at a ceremony scheduled for Nov. 16 in Kyoto, Noguchi will become the first-ever tōji (chief brewer) to be named a ningen kokuhō (living national treasure) — a recognition with significance far beyond the world of sake.
