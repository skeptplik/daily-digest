Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva has appointed private banking executive Luiz Carlos Trabuco as Finance Minister for his next term, the presidency said on Thursday (November 26, 2026).
Mr. Lula’s shock election win in October sent stock markets tumbling while the national currency, the real, weakened significantly.
The appointment of Mr. Trabuco, chairman of the board of Bradesco bank, aims to reassure the business community in Latin America’s largest economy.
“Luiz Carlos Trabuco has accepted President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva’s invitation to take the helm of the finance ministry,” the presidency stated, specifying that the banker agreed to take the job during an afternoon meeting with the Brazilian leader.
Mr. Lula will begin his fourth non-consecutive term in office in January and has so far only named one minister for that mandate.
Bradesco is one of Brazil’s largest private banks.
Ahead of the official announcement, rumors circulating about Mr. Trabuco’s new job appeared to restore market confidence.
The Sao Paulo Stock Exchange was up 6% at closing on Thursday (November 26, 2026).
The national currency meanwhile regained strength after briefly crossing the symbolic threshold of six reais to the dollar on Monday.
