People visiting a large illuminated sculpture of the Moon have said it is an "amazing" sight.
ArtHouse Jersey has brought Luke Jerram's artwork to the St Helier Methodist Centre until Sunday 29 November.
The 6m (20ft) wide display, which has been shown around the world, features detailed imagery of the lunar surface from photographs by Nasa.
Arthouse CEO Tom Dingle said: "Something that makes the most of a public space, that makes us think about the world around us, about ourselves, but also something that we can all share together all at once, is really exciting."
The Moon follows a similar public artwork display in Jersey in 2023, when more than 31,000 people visited the Floating Earth on Queen's Valley Reservoir.
The installation features light and sound displays, which ArtHouse said would allow people "to encounter our closest celestial neighbour in an entirely new way".
Nine-year-old Ava thought the moon was "cool" and "like a big spot in the middle of the sky".
Primrose Keenan also enjoyed the exhibit, saying it was "amazing".
"We saw the Earth on the lake as well a few couple of years ago and that was amazing too," she added.
Toby Balderson said: "It does make a difference being able to see everything in such detail.
"We always just see little snapshots of it."
Youngsters Aquila and Starla, who are sisters, said it looked "really good" and "very big".
Dingle added: "Sometimes to stop, to take a pause and to reflect on the wider things, to think about the world out there, is actually really healthy and therapeutic."
He said the exhibit also showed the importance of free public artwork: "It enlivens our public realm, it gives us something to do together.
"It actually can change the pace for a day. It can give somebody a moment to reflect and it can mean something different to everybody - and yet we have the same universal experience at the same time."
