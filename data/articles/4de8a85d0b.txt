The 73-year-old told French broadcaster BFM TV she would take legal action against the publisher and author of the essay.
Pelicot made headlines across the world when she waived her right to anonymity in the trial of her then-husband Dominique, who was jailed for 20 years for drugging and raping her, as well as allowing other men to rape her while she was unconscious.
The publisher apologised, saying it would no longer print or promote the work after "an unfortunate and inappropriate communication".
Pelicot told BFM: "I am deeply shocked that images from the most intimate part of my private life are being displayed and exploited for commercial purposes."
"Today I find myself once again humiliated, exposed and hurt, as if the ordeal of the trial had not been enough," she said.
The essay was written by Sabine Prokhoris, a philosopher and psychoanalyst, who has previously criticised the Me Too movement.
French media reported that Tinbad, a small Paris publishing house, emailed nude photos of Pelicot taken by her ex-husband to some media outlets in order to promote the essay.
In a statement on its website, Tinbad said that no further print runs of the book would be produced beyond the 280 copies already in circulation.
"We offer our sincerest apologies to Ms GisÃ¨le Pelicot," it said. The company also apologised to the author.
Tinbad said "no images of rape or pornographic videos" were sent to the media.
French news agency AFP quoted the blurb of the book as saying coercive control was "a cornerstone of #MeToo orthodoxy".
"These psychological situations, fundamentally ambiguous in nature, involve each of the parties in an equally responsible, though not symmetrical, way," it said.
Pelicot's lawyer Christophe Bigot told BFM the book "calls into question her status as a victim".
Prokhoris told AFP that she placed "full responsibility" for the "inappropriate" promotional campaign on her publisher.
Fifty men were convicted alongside Pelicot's ex-husband in 2024, in a case that made her a global feminist icon.
After her husband was arrested for secretly filming underneath women's skirts in a supermarket, she discovered she had been repeatedly raped by dozens of men, including her husband, while unconscious, over a period of nearly a decade.
Pelicot said she decided to waive her right to anonymity to show how "shame has to change sides" from victims to perpetrators.
