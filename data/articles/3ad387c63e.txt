Ride-hailing company Uber is notifying nearly 150,000 drivers that attackers stole their data after breaching AI infrastructure provider Fireworks in early October.
In breach notification letters sent to affected individuals on November 10, Uber said threat actors accessed their personal information after breaching an Uber account with a third-party service provider.
While Uber didn't share the total number of affected drivers in these letters, it revealed in a filing with Maine's Office of the Attorney General that the data breach has exposed the information of 149,346 drivers, including their names, driver’s license numbers, dates of birth, and addresses.
After being informed of the breach, Uber launched an investigation and severed data connections with the service provider to block further malicious access to its data.
"On October 9, 2026, a service provider notified Uber that an unauthorized third party had compromised the service provider's internal credentials and used them to access Uber's account with the service provider," Uber said. "After being notified of this incident, Uber immediately severed data connections with the service provider and launched an investigation. On October 13, 2026, our investigation determined that the unauthorized party had used the compromised credentials to access Uber data connected to the service provider."
Although the company didn't directly name the hacked service provider, TechCrunch first reported that the Uber data breach stems from an October 24 security incident at AI training provider Fireworks, which was hacked after a third-party cloud provider hack.
Fireworks has attributed the attack to a financially motivated cybercriminal threat actor group linked to a series of large language model hijacking (LLMjacking) attacks.
The Fireworks breach also led to security incidents at AI coding platform Cursor and digital workspace platform Notion, which also reported that the attackers stole data from a limited number of customers and (in Cursor's case) infected users' devices with backdoor malware.
An Uber spokesperson was not immediately available for comment when BleepingComputer reached out for more information about the data breach.
The company disclosed another data breach in December 2022 after a threat actor leaked IT asset information, employees' email addresses, and corporate reports stolen from third-party service provider Teqtivity.
Uber previously confirmed that hackers who breached some of its systems in October 2016 stole data from 50 million users and 7 million drivers, and that the Lapsus$ extortion group also hacked it in September 2022 in an MFA fatigue attack using the stolen credentials of an Uber EXT contractor.
