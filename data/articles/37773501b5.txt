Abstract
New catalytic manifolds that challenge existing boundaries for synthesis and sustainable manufacturing are notoriously difficult to implement1, particularly for catalytic paradigms based on metals which lack access to the fundamental processes that underpin many established d-block mechanistic pathways (e.g. oxidative addition and reductive elimination). The widespread exploitation of systems based on main-group elements therefore requires alternative elementary steps to effect turnover. Herein, we demonstrate a new catalytic platform for nucleophilic fluorination, which exploits a complex based on calcium (the fifth most Earth-abundant element) as the fluoride delivery vehicle. The key intermediate is a calcium-fluoride complex featuring a terminal Ca–F interaction, a unique structural geometry enabled by a highly sterically encumbered dianionic ligand-scaffold. This intermediate links reactants and products through F– capture and release steps, and thereby unlocks catalytic F– shuttling. Late Ca–F bond breaking in the rate-determining fluoride-delivery transition-state means that electronic and steric tuning of F– by calcium is chemically significant; proof-of-concept results show a hitherto unprecedented level of selectivity for substitution over elimination brought about by Ca-modulation. In a broader context, this work demonstrates how control over catalyst speciation and reagent binding affinity are key factors for the future development of sustainable non-transition metal paradigms in catalysis.
