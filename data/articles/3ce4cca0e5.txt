Where does one even begin when narrowing down the best perfumes for women? It’s a daunting task, to say the least. With hundreds of thousands of women’s fragrances flooding the market (and that’s putting it mildly), finding your signature scent or building out a fragrance wardrobe can quickly lead to sensory overload.
First, there’s the matter of personality and preference: Everyone’s idea of “the best” is deeply personal and sometimes even polarizing. Are you drawn to hyper-femme blooms, or do you lean toward moody, spice-forward aromas? What about delicious gourmands, warm solar scents, or—conversely—earthy notes grounded in fresh greens and wood? Perhaps you are a no-fuss minimalist who sticks to subtle skin scents. Are you interested in something long-lasting or a light and airy eau de toilette? Maybe you’d rather layer a few different perfumes to create your own unique combination. The choices are quite literally endless.

Whether you prefer standout blends from legacy houses or niche scents from innovative alchemists—there’s something for everyone, with picks from experts and Vogue editors alike. Ahead, our definitive guide to the best perfumes for women, along with expert tips on finding the perfect scent that feels uniquely yours. Prepare for a sensory journey that balances tradition and innovation.
Best White Floral: Parfums de Marly Valaya Eau de Parfum
- Why We Love It: While Parfums de Marly’s Delina often steals the spotlight, there’s an unsung hero in the brand’s lineup that deserves its moment: Valaya. Described by fragrance expert Eudora Nwasike as “heaven on earth in a bottle,” this scent is anything but ordinary. Nwasike calls it a joyful, signature-worthy fragrance that opens with a radiant blend of peach, aldehydes, and bergamot. As it settles, soft white florals and a veil of musk create a clean, almost satin-like finish on the skin—elegant, versatile, and quietly luminous.
- Editor’s Experience: I was a certified floral fragrance hater because I had an extremely narrow idea of what they were. I consider myself much edgier than the girly-pop commercial florals the category is typically associated with. Valaya changed my mind, showing me that white florals in particular can sometimes spark intrigue. This scent is angelic, indeed, and while it wasn’t my usual scent profile, it is probably my most consistently complimented perfume and compels folks to ask what I’m wearing. —Conçetta Ciarlo, beauty shopping writer
- Key Notes: Bergamot, mandarin, white peach, orange flower, nymphaea, vetiver, musk.
- This Smells Like: An utterly ethereal garden.
Best Amber Floral: Chanel Coco Noir Eau de Parfum
- Why We Love It: With Coco Noir, Chanel proves that the brand has range, offering warm and spicy scents too. Launched in 2012 by Jacques Polge and Christopher Sheldrake, the perfumers were inspired by Coco’s midnight Venice adventures. No doubt, this is a richer, dare we say darker scent that features notes of bitter grapefruit, Indonesian patchouli, and frankincense. Meanwhile, Venezuelan tonka bean adds a touch of sweetness.
- Notes: Grapefruit, Calabrian bergamot, rose, narcissus, rose geranium leaf, jasmine, tonka bean, sandalwood, vanilla, patchouli, white musk, frankincense.
- This Smells Like: The Odile to Coco Mademoiselle’s Odette.
Best Solar Scent: Merit New Wave L’Extrait de Parfum
- Why We Love It: Perfumers Dominique Ropion and Meabh McCurtin described their scent creation as “unconventionally constructed.” New Wave, Merit’s latest launch, is a particularly complex scent—with 21 notes, to be exact—which evolve on its wearer over time. At the top, it blends incense, woods, tobacco, vanilla, tonka, musks, and spice for a layered, intriguing base. “In this case, solar is less about a specific ingredient than a sensation. It is a feeling of warmth, radiance, and light on skin,” they explained.
- Editor’s Experience: I’m not sure there’s ever been a fragrance that’s lasted longer on me—I’m talking 12 hours later. New Wave seems to smell a bit different to everyone. My interpretation, though, is that frankincense is its star, and one which I’m particularly fond of. To me, this smells seductive and mysterious, like burning incense. —C.C.
- Notes: Pink pepper, angelica seed, ylang-ylang, patchouli heart, frankincense, musk, cypriol, vetiver, tobacco accord
- This Smells Like: Freshly lit incense in a swanky hotel.
Best Tart & Earthy: Régime des Fleurs Wildly Fig eau de parfum
- Why We Love It: Not your average fig fragrance, Régime des Fleurs founder, perfumer, and artistic director describes the scent profile as “undomesticated, effervescent.” Here, a blend of crisp greens, fresh lemon, and spicy cardamom forms the top notes, slowly evolving into a tart fig and nutty pistachio base. Rather than a resicous fig jam, the scent reads as balanced, earthy, and fresh from the garden.
- Key Notes: Lemon, cardamom, bergamot, fig leaf, orange blossom, mandarin, laurel, pistachio, vetiver
- This Smells Like: Figs picked fresh from the garden on a crisp morning.
Best Boozy: Byredo Havana Tonk extrait de parfum
- Why We Love It: Decadent and sophisticated describes Byredo’s latest launch, and iteration of their Night Shades collection. “The crème brûlée and roasted tonka bring a beautiful creamy richness, while the tobacco, suede, and patchouli introduce a darker, more textured side,” head perfumer Jerome Epinette previously said. As complex as fragrances go, calling this a gourmand would be far too simplistic—while its delectable notes might be the initial olfactory stars, its animalic notes linger on the skin.
- Key Notes: Bergamot essential oil, rum, pink peppercorn essential oil, crème brûlée, tobacco leaves, violet, cashmere woods, white suede, skin musk, roasted tonka bean absolute, patchouli essential oil, benzoin resinoid
- This Smells Like: A pugg of a freshly rolled cigar after dessert.
Best Soft and Cozy: Lore Lil’ Softie eau de parfum
- Why We Love It: Lore may be new to the fragrance scene, but it’s already made a lasting impact. Lil’ Softie, the brand’s latest launch, is a cozy ode to tiny kittens—why do their fuzzy selves smell powdery and inviting when all they do is groom with their sandpaper tongues? Here, lactonic notes (cereal milk, to be specific), paired with cotton, pink peppercorn, and cashmeran, create a skin scent with subtle sweetness you’ll undoubtedly want to cuddle up with.
- Notes: Cereal milk, cotton, musk, ambroxan, cashmeran
- This Smells Like: A fuzzy little fluffy kitten—purrfeclty cashmerean, slightly sweet, and a little lactonic.
Best Unique: Dries Van Noten Soie Malaquais Refillable Eau de Parfum
- Why We Love It: When Dries Van Noten burst onto the beauty scene in 2022, there was much anticipation. Luckily, the fragrance line’s charm extended well beyond elegant shelf appeal. Aside from art-worthy bottles, each scent is 88% naturally derived. For Soie Malaquais, a current editor favorite, master perfumer Marie Salamagne drew inspiration from the fashion house’s silk dresses, bringing the ingredient here as a key note. From there, nutty chestnut, rich cocoa, and tart black currant add contrasting moody elements, while rose and bergamot add a sense of subtle freshness and flowering.
- Notes: Bergamot, black currant, rose, silk, chestnut, cocoa.
- This Smells Like: Equal parts sweet, subtly sour, and silky.
Best Modern Lavender: Matiere Premiere Metal Lavender Eau de Parfum
- Why We Love It: Lavender is quite literally one of the world’s most ancient fragrance ingredients. No, really—4,000-year-old perfumes were discovered in Greece in 2007, proving as much. Matiere Premiere’s Metal Lavender, however, is totally modern. Cofounder and seventh-generation perfumer Aurélien Guichard was inspired by the massive metal vats that create lavender oil at Provençe distillation factories. The two forms of lavender that make up this perfume are sourced from the brand’s own French flower farms, while metal and cashmere add a contrasting urban twist.
- Editor’s Experience: Soft yet severe. Simple yet cool. Metal Lavender is like a ’90s It girl—cool, edgy, and minimalist in a nonboring way. It’s impossible to explain the soothing nature that lavender oil contrasts with metallic notes, but it leaves a complimentary question mark whenever sniffed. And yes, I already drained my travel-size version and need a full bottle ASAP. —C.C.
- Notes: Lavandin oil grasse, metal, lavender absolute, musk, cashmeran.
- This Smells Like: A lavender perfume for Brutalist enthusiasts.
Best Ozonic: Guerlain L’Heure Bleue Eau de Parfum
- Why We Love It: “I wanted to express a contrast between warmth and freshness, between the sensuality of fragrance on skin and the cool air that creates a shiver,” Guerlain’s creative director and perfumer, Delphine Jelk, previously told Vogue. Here, ozonic, crisp air notes contrast with warm florals, while iris, marshmallow, and orange blossom lie at the heart of the fragrance. Together, the scent creates a unique, mature olfactory composition.
- Key Notes: Iris, cascalone, marshmallow, orange blossom, pear, vanilla, ambroxan
- This Smells Like: A breath of fresh air post-sunset.
Best Lactonic: Discothèque Next to Me or Nothing Eau de Parfum
- Why We Love It: At Discothèque, every perfume is inspired by particular iconic nightlife spots from around the globe and is accompanied by custom playlists and mood boards. The latest launch, inspired by a record-playing bar in Kyoto called Jazz, has been selling out constantly. The scent’s top note, milk, has a creamy, airy quality. At the same time, star jasmine adds undertones of intoxicating honey. Meanwhile, sandalwood and white musk linger, hugging the skin like a sheer knit top. Another key note that makes this unlike any other? Cloudy sake. Cheers!
- Editor’s Experience: This is my new favorite perfume full stop. I was already a fan of Discothèque for its impeccable storytelling alone, but the fragrances hold up too. Lola at Coatcheck is a crowd-pleaser, but this new fragrance is signature-scent-worthy for me. Vanilla haters needn’t worry because this is not a gourmand. Sake, flowering plants, and fresh wood notes make sure of that. Call it a cool-kid skin scent, but it’s so much more. —C.C.
- Notes: Cold milk, star jasmine, nigori, Iso E Super, sandalwood, tonka bean, white musk.
- This Smells Like: Crisp and creamy with a kick.
Best Moody Rose: Frédéric Malle Portrait of a Lady
- Why We Love It: Isabel Archer—the spirited heroine of the Henry James novel this scent is named after—was a passionate free thinker, and it’s easy to imagine her spritzing on a bit of Malle before a night out in the Eternal City. The floral note here is complex: It can lean sweet or spicy, sultry or refined. In this case, Turkish rose skews moodier—more cool-girl edge than overtly sweet. “One of my personal favorite floral fragrances, this scent announces my presence the moment I enter a room,” Nwasike tells Vogue, describing it as bold, sensual, and impossible to ignore. And did we mention it’s a favorite of Madonna’s?
- Editor’s Experience: Though this fragrance has become a classic in its own right, it feels fresh each time I spritz it on—or smell it on someone else. Portrait of a Lady is the first rose perfume I ever loved; the kind of sophisticated floral that is warm and sensual, yet light and feminine. I’ll wear it in any season. —Kiana Murden, beauty editor, shopping
- Notes: Turkish rose, patchouli, sandalwood, frankincense, black currant, raspberry.
- This Smells Like: A rose fragrance for the brooding.
Best Seductive: Tom Ford Figue Érotique Eau de Parfum
- Why We Love It: Figue Érotique lives up to its provocative name. The fragrance opens with ripe fig and bergamot, while brown sugar, licorice, and vetiver lend a dark, seductive edge. It’s pretty much a dead ringer for Tom Ford’s velvety, silky, and sexy ’90s collections at Gucci, donned by the likes of Kate Moss. Yes, it’s a fruity fragrance—but one that’s far more decadent and deep than you might expect. Plus this scent is super long-lasting.
- Editor’s Experience: Dare I say, this is my new favorite Tom Ford perfume. I reach for it when I can’t decide on a scent profile. I like that it leads with fresh and fruity notes without being overly sweet or earthy. Instead, the muscovado comes to the fore for a cozy touch. —K.M.
- Key Notes: Kadota fig accord, vert de bergamote, muscovado accord, fig leaf accord, ylang-ylang absolute, vetiver essence.
- This Smells Like: The olfactory embodiment of ’90s-era Gucci with Tom Ford at the helm.
Best Jungle-Inspired: Costa Brazil Aroma eau de parfum
- Why We Love It: Sarah Brown, executive director of Violet Lab, describes Violet Grey’s best-selling fragrances as modern classics in the making. Inspired by the Amazon rainforest at dawn, this gender-neutral perfume layers white jungle florals, Brazilian orange oil, pink pepper, and grapefruit zest over grounding notes of bourbon, vetiver, musk, and patchouli. It’s a favorite among Vogue editors: Murden calls it one of her most complimented scents, while senior beauty and wellness editor Margaux Anbouba has been a devoted fan since it was available only as a body oil.
- Editor’s Experience: When I want to smell warm and aromatic, I reach for Costa Brazil’s Aroma. I really appreciate its spiciness—nutmeg, pink pepper, and grapefruit zest. It’s lively and head-turning; there’s nothing quite like it. —K.M.
- Notes: White jungle flora, Brazil orange oil, pink pepper, grapefruit zest, bourbon, vetiver, musk, patchouli.
- This Smells Like: An earthy zest.
Best Spicy Vanilla: Diptyque Eau Duelle
- Why We Love It: When most people think of a vanilla fragrance, they immediately assume a gourmand. But Diptyque’s Eau Duelle leans into a smoky spiciness—pairing Madagascar-derived bourbon vanilla with calamus, cypriol, and pink pepper. It’s an alluring, cozy kind of perfume that, like a warm latte, is both sweet and spicy; it gets better the longer you wear it. Vogue’s Molly Barstein considers the fragrance her go-to during the colder months.
- Notes: Vanilla, pink peppercorn, cypriol, calamus.
- This Smells Like: Bourbon, spice, and everything nice.
Best Juicy and Herbal: Amouage Love Hibiscus Eau de Parfum
- Why We Love It: Nordstrom’s beauty director Autumne West previously called Amouage’s Love Hibiscus an elevated, unexpected fruity floral. Juicy passion fruit contrasts with frankincense, sandalwood, and cypriol, while the scent settles into decadent salted caramel. That said, it’s far from being a strictly gourmand scent—but rather, a tart and slightly sweet licorice or steaming herbal tea.
- Editor’s Experience: I’ve been obsessed with hibiscus for as long as I can remember, so when I heard Amouage had a scent built around it, I had to try it. Master perfumer Jérôme Epinette made this flower feel tart and a little tropical, sweet without tipping into sugary. Sandalwood, frankincense, and cypriol keep it grounded, giving it a warmth that lingers on your skin. It’s delicious and sensual, all at the same time. —Valeriya Chupinina, contributor
- Notes: Salted caramel, passion fruit, bergamot, hibiscus, orris, frankincense, sandalwood, cypriol, vanilla.
- This Smells Like: A super-juicy hibiscus herbal tea.
Best Delicious: Liis Choux Choux eau de parfum
- Why We Love It: If you’re aiming to smell downright delectable, look no further than Choux Choux. Adored by Vogue’s associate shopping director, Talia Abbas, it has fans in beauty writers Kiana Murden and Conçetta Ciarla too. “This is decadent, like a fluffy pastry or freshly flambéd baked Alaska,” says Ciarlo. Fair warning: Complete strangers will stop you just to say you smell incredible. Rather than this skewing more as a juvenile gourmand, it reads playful and whimsical.
- Notes: Citrus peel, fresh cream, fleur de sel caramel, sandalwood, cocoa, mocha.
- This Smells Like: Utterly yummy—not to be confused with an adolescent, sickeningly sweet gourmand.
Best Complex Floral: U Beauty Proem Eau de Parfum
- Why We Love It: Yep—luxe skin-care brand U Beauty does fragrance too. Having only launched last year, this complex scent can’t quite be boxed in as a floral fragrance alone. In fact, its a concoction of 12 notes, created by master perfumer and fragrance expert Jérôme Epinette, capturing a range of blossoming flowers, jammy fruits, earthy woods, and more. “This fragrance is a journey and not a boring one,” Epinette previously told Vogue, noting that the scent is meant to capture the beauty of a day.
- Editor’s Experience: I never expected to love Proem as much as I do, but as a longtime fan of U Beauty’s cosmetics, I should’ve known Tina Chen Craig’s vision would compel me. This was one of the first fruity scents that captured my attention, though I wouldn’t really call it fruity. Its sweetness and floral notes create a vibrancy that defies categorization entirely. —K.M.
- Key Notes: Chinese magnolia, centifolia rose, jasmine, lychee, rhubarb, pistachio, vetiver, peppercorn, cedarwood.
- This Smells Like: A multihyphenate that’s clean yet complex.
Best Ethereal: Witch New York Fleur Abstraite eau de parfum
- Why We Love It: The Gabbriette type in your friend group requires something a bit more niche and nuanced than a crowd-pleasing perfume from any ole’ beauty retailer—take it from me. 
Having launched only two years ago, Witch recently expanded into fine fragrance. Of the collection, Fleur Abstraite is downright ethereal—where fresh green apple, jasmine, and rose oil contrast with pink pepper, black tea, and musk. Though the scent is something of a shape-shifter, the latter two notes evolve slightly differently on each wearer.
- Editor’s Experience: I only recently discovered Witch about two months ago; because the aesthetics are up my alley, I admittedly was interested in it from the jump. But beyond its shelf appeal and small-batch quality, each scent is inspired by a curiosity about the natural world. Jasmine and black tea are two of my favorite fragrance notes, so I knew I’d like Fleur Abstraite. The fragrance projection is like an inviting whisper, but it lingers on my skin and clothes for hours. —C.C
- Key Notes: Jasmine, pink pepper, green apple, rose otto, black tea, ambroxan, white musk
- This Smells Like: If a Cocteau Twins song were a fragrance (I’m thinking “Pepper-Tree.”).
Best Flirty: Jo Malone London Peony & Blush Suede Cologne
- Why We Love It: There’s no denying the ubiquitous allure of Jo Malone London blends—with a handful of perfumes across six fragrance families, there’s certainly an aroma for everyone. Of the most sought-after fragrances for women, the British label delivers Peony & Blush Suede. Though the name might conjure powdery elixirs popular decades ago, this perfume is invigoratingly sweet—and dare we say flirty? All in all, crafted to embody the essence of charm.
- Editor’s Experience: Still one of my most complimented scents two years later, this Jo Malone perfume is feminine as can be. I think the peony, rose, and honey notes are what draw me too it. Mingling to create a mid-summer freshness with a hint of sweet. —K.M.
- Notes: Red apple, peony, suede, jasmine, rose, gillyflower.
- This Smells Like: A flirty floral.
