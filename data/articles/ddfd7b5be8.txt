‘Evidence-based’: Legco to require research from lawmakers raising non-binding motions
The requirement, effective next year, will help the public understand motions’ intent and follow debates more easily, Legco President Starry Lee says
Hong Kong’s legislature will require lawmakers to submit relevant research findings and recommendations when they raise motions that have no legislative effect, a move its president has said is aimed at helping the public follow debates more easily and fostering “evidence-based” discussion.
“This will allow the government and the public to better understand the intent and rationale of the motions, and also help the public better follow the debates in the chamber,” Starry Lee Wai-king said.
“I hope the government, the chief executive and all officials will take on board more views from lawmakers and step up exchanges. I hope [the requirement] will make our discussions on public policy more evidence-based.”
