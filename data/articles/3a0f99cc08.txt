As GTA 6 launches around the world, businesses are bracing for a wave of so-called "GTA flu" as staff call in sick to play Rockstar's hugely-anticipated video game.
The U.K.'s National Health Service has even issued guidance on how to stay healthy over the coming days and weeks, as millions begin long gaming sessions to explore Vice City. "Planning on dedicating time to gaming later this week?" reads a post by the NHS on Instagram.
"Try and take regular breaks, obviously. And be mindful of RSI (repetitive strain injury). That’s a term for pain that can be caused by repeated movement of part of the body, such as hands and fingers. Search online for 'NHS RSI' to get some advice on how to avoid getting any injuries."
Whether you're risking calling in sick to work or taking an unplanned day off from school, concern over the impact of GTA flu is spreading, particularly among teachers and parents. A school in Norwich, England has issued a warning about pupils declaring they have "GTA flu", Norfolk's Evening News reports, following a spate of similar messages to parents set out by schools across the U.K. and Ireland earlier this week. One school is even offering gift cards as an incentive for students to actually show up.
In Australia, requests for annual leave have reportedly jumped 29% around launch day compared to the same time period last year, News24 noted, with men aged 18 to 44 making up 75% of those vacation requests. According to The Sydney Morning Herald, meanwhile, GTA 6's launch is expected to cost the Australian enconomy at least $70 million, as it's expected that at least 1 in every 100 workers will call in sick.
U.K. tabloid The Daily Star claimed that "massive workplace carnage" would take place on both sides of the Atlantic, with 1.5 million US staff and 2.1 million U.K. employees expected to be absent, albeit via booking the day off. This could, the paper reported, drain U.S. national output "by 2% per cent in a single day, costing the American economy a staggering $1 billion in lost productivity."
While the flurry of excitement around GTA 6 may seem extreme to some, others would argue that this is a moment in gaming like no other — the launch of a fresh entry in an industry-defining franchise for the first time in over a decade, and after multiple high-profile delays. Indeed, even Strauss Zelnick, head of GTA 6 publisher Take-Two Interactive, previously said he expected "a whole lot of people calling in sick on November 19."
