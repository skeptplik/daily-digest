Naha – On Monday, visitors returned to the seiden (main hall) of Okinawa’s Shuri Castle as the reconstructed building opened to the public for the first time since 2019, when a devastating fire destroyed much of the historic complex.
The reopening marks a major milestone in the restoration of one of Okinawa’s most recognizable landmarks, with visitors now able to enter the three-story wooden building and see its restored royal throne rooms, intricate decorative features and extensive new fire prevention systems.
Once the political and ceremonial center of the Ryukyu Kingdom, the seiden was among nine structures destroyed or damaged in the Oct. 31, 2019 fire.
