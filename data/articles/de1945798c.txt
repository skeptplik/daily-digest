Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang allegedly threatened employees with violence and "humiliated" them for decades.
According to sources speaking to The New York Times, Huang — who stepped down as Nvidia's CEO amid a scandal involving infidelity and alleged espionage — has a history of using threatening language against employees.
Several alleged quotes from Huang are cited in the report, including an incident where Huang reportedly told an employee he wanted to rip their head off and "pee on [their] brain."
In another incident, he is quoted as saying, "You have to do this, or I will rip your f***ing heart out and stomp on it." Others described an incident in which he allegedly went around a room and said he would kill managers, and allegedly said that everyone hoped an employee in their 70s would die soon.
He is also alleged to have told a member of staff: "I’d rather torture you into greatness, because I believe in you." It is further claimed that he described receiving verbal humiliation like this as a "rite of passage" at Nvidia.
This language was described as "foundational" to Huang's style of leadership, and is said to date back to the 2000s. The New York Times said some employees who complained about Huang's behaviour received settlements, and that HR was aware of his alleged behaviour.
One settlement was made with the former senior vice president of HR, Michael Winston, it was claimed. Winston was said to have described Huang's behaviour as "verbally abusive, threatening and intimidating." It is reported that his settlement totaled more than $1 million.
Huang denied Winston’s allegations in a statement issued to The New York Times. Nvidia declined to comment.
Earlier this week, Huang said he was ready to return to the company he co-founded, while apologizing for the problems he'd caused both Nvidia and his family. In a statement, Huang insisted he would be willing to return as CEO if Nvidia's Board of Directors decided it was in the best interest in the company.
Some Nvidia employees and other tech CEOs have rallied to Huang's defense and called for him to be reinstated as CEO. The New York Times report alluded to this, acknowledging that Huang does have a following within Nvidia. The attitudes towards Huang are described as "cultish" in the report, with the former CEO alternating between praise and ridicule. It is alleged that he "ruled through fear."
Huang is reportedly due to appear before the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and China Select Committees on December 1. "The American people need answers on Mr. Huang and Nvidia’s role in allowing dangerous PRC actors to access our most advanced chips, as well as the role of a potential Chinese intelligence asset in this scandal,” Reps. Brian Mast, R-Fla., John Moolenaar, R-Mich., and Rick Crawford, R-Ark., the chairs of the Foreign Affairs, China Select and Intelligence Committees, said in a statement reported by CNBC.
