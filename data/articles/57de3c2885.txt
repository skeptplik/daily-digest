Naha – The restored Seiden main hall of Shuri Castle in Naha opened to the public Monday, seven years after it was destroyed by fire.
Visitors to the bright vermilion castle, which reflects the splendor of the Ryukyu Kingdom, admired its beauty and expressed their excitement, saying, “I’ve been waiting for this” and “I’m happy it’s back.”
The Hoshinmon gate leading to the Una square in front of the main hall opened at noon, and many people entered with cameras and smartphones in hand. Advance reservations are required to tour the inside of the main hall.
“I was looking forward to this day,” a homemaker in her 70s from the Shuri district of Naha said with a smile. “The colors of the interior are beautiful, and I felt the atmosphere of the kingdom era.”
Satoshi Tachioka, a 57-year-old company employee from Osaka Prefecture, said he is a fan of Shuri Castle and often visited it with his wife before it was heavily damaged by the 2019 fire and during its restoration work. “I can feel the romance of history,” he said.
The main hall, where the Ryukyu king conducted political affairs and ceremonies, is a three-story wooden building.
The first and second floors each have the Usasuka throne at the center. The second floor features elaborate decorations, including golden dragon pillars.
Nonoka Sakita, 9, was visiting from Urasoe, Okinawa, with her family. She said she was studying Shuri Castle at school.
“I’m glad it’s back. The place where the king sat was especially beautiful,” she said.
Before the public opening, an event hosted by the Okinawa Prefectural Government took place in the square. High school students performed court dances, creating a celebratory atmosphere.
The Seiden was destroyed by fire in the early hours of Oct. 31, 2019, along with the Hokuden north hall, the Nanden south hall and other structures.
Incorporating findings from research conducted since the 1992 restoration, the hall has been reconstructed to more faithfully reflect its original appearance. Fire safety measures have also been strengthened.
The cause of the fire has not been identified. A third-party committee established by the Okinawa Prefectural Government has pointed to the possibility of a problem with the electrical system.
