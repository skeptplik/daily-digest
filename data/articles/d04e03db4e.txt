US midterms: what happened while Asia slept, and when to expect results
Apart from tech issues at some polling stations, voting went smoothly despite Trump’s claims of fraud and an ICE warning to illegal voters
The first polls closed at 6pm Eastern Time (7am Hong Kong time), with most states following over the next several hours. The last state to close polls will be Alaska, at 1am Eastern Time (2pm Hong Kong time). Some results could come within hours, but others may take days.
Here is what happened while Asia slept.
1. Democrats go in as favourites
Prediction markets on Tuesday afternoon implied a 94 per cent chance that Democrats would take the House and a 74 per cent chance they would win the Senate, according to analytics firm BetInt. Senate races to watch include Maine, Texas, Georgia, Michigan, North Carolina and Iowa.
2. Floods and glitches, but few serious problems
Voting went relatively smoothly, according to the Associated Press. Flooding in Florida forced officials to move at least 19 polling stations, while heavy rain hampered voters in four southwestern states.
