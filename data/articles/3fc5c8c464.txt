More than three-quarters of Russian regions have banned or plan to ban the sale of vaping products starting next year, according to state media.
Regions gained the authority to impose five-year vaping sales bans from March 1, 2027, through March 1, 2032, after President Vladimir Putin signed a decree in June.
Prior to that, bans had already been in effect in the Perm region, the republic of North Ossetia, as well as the Nizhny Novgorod and Penza regions.
According to the state-run TASS news agency, at least 69 Russian regions, including annexed Ukrainian territories, had either passed or announced plans to enact retail bans on vaping products and liquids.
The Federal Service for Alcohol and Tobacco Market Regulation (Rosalkogoltabakkontrol) told RIA Novosti that 25 regions have adopted bans, saying its tally accounts for regional heads signing legislative approvals into law.
The business news outlet Expert put the figure at “nearly 40” after at least five regional legislatures and governors passed restrictions on Tuesday.
Most of the measures are scheduled to take effect on March 1, with the republic of Buryatia eyeing a Sept. 1 rollout.
That comes as some activist groups call for a comprehensive federal ban on the manufacture, import and sale of vaping products and liquids, as well as a ban on websites that advertise these products.
Supporters of the crackdowns cite school surveys showing e-cigarette use among 27% of boys and 23.7% of girls aged 15-17 in 2023-24. They say daily use has risen eightfold among boys and more than tenfold among girls since 2019, arguing that vaping is a gateway drug and poses a threat to national security.
