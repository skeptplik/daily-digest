A Chinese official has been sentenced to death for spying for a foreign country, a government-linked publisher has said.
Gold Wall Press, a publishing company associated with China’s national administration of state secrets protection, said the convicted person was surnamed Ma and was approached and recruited by an unnamed foreign intelligence service while working abroad.
Chinese courts typically do not publish information about state security cases, but in recent years the powerful ministry of state security and Gold Wall Press have increasingly publicised arrests, as well as using social media, movies and comics to promote warnings about espionage.
The statement on Monday said Ma had signed a cooperation agreement containing clauses on “confidentiality” and “loyalty” and received fixed monthly payments. In total, it said, he provided 35 pieces of information that were classified as national secrets and received payments worth more than 1m yuan ($150,000).
An intelligence operative identified as Kailin “frequently arranged to meet with Ma, hoping to engage with him more”, citing an interest in learning Chinese, the statement said. Kailin later introduced him to a colleague who threatened to report his earlier disclosures to Chinese authorities if he refused to cooperate.
After returning to China, Ma continued to gather intelligence for the foreign agency, which provided him with a laptop, the statement said. He used his position to collect material from classified documents during the day and transmitted it overseas at night via an espionage website.
The statement did not identify the countries involved, or say when or where he was sentenced or whether he had already been executed.
The case comes as US intelligence has been publicising its own efforts to recruit sources in China. The five most-viewed videos on the CIA’s YouTube channel are recruiting advertisements in Mandarin.
“This shocking case serves as yet another wake-up call,” the Gold Wall Press said, warning that foreign intelligence agencies were “cunning” and used emotional ties, financial interests and threats to recruit people in key positions.
The statement also urged officials to remain vigilant, strengthen their loyalty to the Communist party, protect state secrets and guard against foreign recruitment.
In 2023 Beijing broadened its anti-espionage law, expanding the definition of espionage and strengthening the investigative powers of state security authorities. The following year it revised its state secrets law to extend restrictions on sensitive information to so-called “work secrets”.
