Sonos R&D has been busy in 2026 with a bevy of new releases, both revolutionary and evolutionary. On the software front, Sonos released its new Sonos 27 home audio operating system earlier in September. It merges all of Sonos' speakers into one centralized control system with AI integration. On the hardware front, Sonos released the Era 100 SL speaker, Sonos Play portable speaker, Beam Ultra soundbar, and Ace Ultra headphones. Of those products, the latter two deserve the spotlight as we head into the holiday season.
New for 2026: Sonos Beam Ultra Soundbar
The Sonos Beam Ultra is more than just an incremental upgrade over its Gen 2 predecessor. Like the Arc Ultra compared to the Arc, this is practically an entirely different soundbar. Although the size profile isn't much different (29 inches long vs 25 inches), the Beam Ultra now houses a total of nine total speakers instead of five. This improves the Beam's surround sound capabilities two-fold: (1) it is now technically a 7.1.2ch system instead of 5.1.2ch and (2) it has two physical upfiring speakers for true Dolby Atmos support whereas the previous Beam Gen 2 used virtual height channels for a simulated Atmos experience. Other upgrades include AI speech enhanced to isolate and amplify dialogue, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.3, and USB-C line-in. It also has a microphone for voice control.
New for 2026: Sonos Ace Ultra Headphones
The Sonos Ace Ultra has also undergone major renovation, which isn't as surprising since the original Ace was Sonos' first foray into the world of over-ear headphones. The Ace Ultra is better sounding than ever before thanks to a combination of second generation custom engineered 40mm drivers and adaptive EQ that tunes the sound in real time based on how the headphones fit. Noise cancelation has also improved up to 2X with the presence of ten microphones instead of eight.
An interesting feature unique to the Sonos Ace Ultra is the replaceable battery. All lithium batteries lose maximum charge over time. And most of the time, the only solution is to replace the headphones. Sonos, however, allows you to buy a replacement battery kit that you can install yourself. It's not hard, Sonos provides instructions, and it doesn't void your warranty. although it's not completely toolless.
