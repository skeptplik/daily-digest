MILAN — Brett Johnson, the American but Milan-based luxury menswear brand, is debuting womenswear.
“We really established the menswear business exponentially in the past two years and we felt like it was the right time to incorporate the women’s,” Johnson told WWD, speaking at the brand’s showroom in Milan where the first women’s collection was on display.
“We really wanted to create something that resonated with the man’s actual companion versus an extension of just the brand… She’ll be her own. Her own identity, her own independent thing,” he said.
Amid a multiyear slump for the luxury sector that has yet to reverse its course, the move is seen as laying the grounds for organic growth, the brand’s chief executive officer Marco Fiandesio said in a joint interview with the designer.
“Growing a brand means managing costs while finding new ways to drive sales. For us, that meant building on menswear — our core business — through wholesale, while adding a new category. We had reached the limits of what we could offer in menswear, so womenswear was the natural next step. It gives us room to grow with existing wholesale partners and bring new ones on board. Women also tend to be more open to exploring different brands,” said Fiandesio, who joined the brand 13 months ago.
Debuting for fall 2027, with the sales campaign kicking off Tuesday in Milan, the womenswear line will be fully unveiled next February during Milan Fashion Week.
“It’s all about quiet confidence. She’s an independent woman, [who] can be somewhat sensual, but in her own dynamic way,” Johnson said. “The collection can be for a more mature woman, but we have girls here on the team in their 20s, and it also resonates with them.”
Hinged on a streamlined palette of blacks and whites, the lineup shares a penchant for understated design and precious natural fibers already core to the men’s offering.
Cashmere is plied into double-faced shawled overcoats with leather piping, grainy deerskin is turned into cool oversize bomber jackets and pony hair is worked into five-pocket pants. Tailored blazers with strong shoulders are cinched at the waist, while knitwear pieces are cropped and shrunken, making for ideal underpinnings. The range is completed by footwear and a few bags.
As the designer prepares to expand the brand’s universe, he is also opening its first European retail outpost in Geneva, Switzerland.
Bowing in early December, the 1,076-square-foot store is located on Place de Longemalle within the architectural complex of the historic Hôtel de la Cigogne overlooking Lake Geneva.
Decked in oak, travertine and brass conjuring a soothing palette of warm neutrals, the store is equipped with a modular display system allowing flexibility. It will eventually also carry the women’s line.
“We really wanted to reach our customers where they are, rather than chase bigger markets like New York, Paris or Milan,” Johnson said, explaining the rationale for the location. “It also speaks to the brand and who we are, being a little bit more niche. If you know, you know.”
The company’s CEO characterized Geneva as a crossroads where many of the brand’s customers — perhaps grown tired of bigger “quiet luxury” stalwarts — gravitate toward.
The opening is part of a comprehensive retail and omnichannel strategy set to keep the brand busy for the next few years.
Declining to provide exact figures, Fiandesio said Brett Johnson generates about 40 percent of its revenues in the DACH (Germany, Austria and Switzerland) and Northern Europe regions, followed by Eastern Europe, which accounts for 30 percent of sales, and the Middle East, representing 10 percent of revenues.
The latter region was experiencing healthy growth until the war dented consumer confidence there. Brett Johnson is nonetheless banking on the market’s resilience and hoping to find the right location to open a second flagship in 2027 in Dubai.
In 2022, the brand set up shop in the United Arab Emirates with a store at The Dubai Mall opened with a local partner, with which it later cut ties. It now plans to operate its retail in the region directly.
“The Middle East remains a strong prospect region for us,” Fiandesio said.
Although the designer launched the brand in the U.S., it has little distribution Stateside, a market that Fiandesio is hoping to zero in on starting next year, leveraging a recently signed partnership with the multibrand showroom and distributor Stylistico.
The label currently boasts a corner at Saks at Bal Harbour Shops in addition to being carried by a few independent specialty stores in the U.S.
Brett Johnson has tested retail waters with a pop-up strategy elsewhere, for example opening units at Harrods in 2024, Galeries Lafayette in 2025 and now debuting at KaDeWe in Berlin through the end of November.
The brand also boasts a steady online business, currently accounting for 4 percent of revenues. That’s the channel it has leveraged to raise awareness, and boost sales, in Asia, via a partnership with JD.com and Tmall.
Johnson, who grew up in Washington, D.C., launched his namesake label in 2014 in the U.S. and opened a showroom in Milan in 2019, relocating some of its operations to the Italian city. The 3,229-square-foot space is home to the sales department and part of the design team and has allowed Johnson to keep track of manufacturing, which is based in Italy’s Tuscany and Umbria regions.
The brand is available at Gio’ Moretti in Milan; Amicis in Vienna; Bungalow in Stuttgart, Germany; and the Rubaiyat department store in Jeddah and Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. It has also linked with Discovery Land Company, a golf club operator, to distribute its collection at the latter’s outposts CostaTerra in Comporta, Portugal; Los Angeles, and Tuscany.
