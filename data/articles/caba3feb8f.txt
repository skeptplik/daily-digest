Death of Nahel: French police officer who shot dead a teenage boy to stand trial for murder
A French police officer who shot and killed 17-year-old Nahel Merzouk during a traffic stop in June 2023 will stand trial for murder following a ruling by the Versailles appeals court. Merzouk's death at the hands of the police sparked intense riots in the sprawling suburbs surrounding Paris.
A French appeals court on Tuesday ruled that the police officer who fatally shot a teenage boy of North African descent in June 2023 during a traffic stop should be tried for murder. The police killing of 17-year-old Nahel Merzouk set off days of riots in Paris’s poorer suburbs.
The Versailles court of appeals found that there was “sufficient evidence” against the defendant and rejected the “mitigating circumstance raised by the defence”, it explained. The decision may be subject to further appeals.
Mobile phone footage of police officer Florian M. shooting Nahel point-blank inside a car during a traffic stop on a busy street went viral following the boy’s death, sparking days of protests.
The police initially alleged that Nahel had driven his car at the officer – a story contradicted by the video evidence, which showed two officers standing by a stationary car, one of them pointing his gun at its driver.
Tuesday’s ruling is only the latest in a series of competing judicial rulings over a case that has for many become a symbol of police brutality in France.
The police officer, who spent five months in pre-trial detention before being released in November 2023, had initially been set to be tried for murder before appealing the decision.
The appeals court in March ruled that he should instead be tried for the lesser charge of violence leading to death in a court without a jury.
That decision was then overruled by France’s highest court in June, opening the path for the police officer to stand trial for murder.
(FRANCE 24 with AFP)
