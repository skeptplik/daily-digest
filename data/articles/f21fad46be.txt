The United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres has reconstituted a 10-member group that works on promoting science, technology and innovation toward achieving its Sustainable Development Goals. The lone Indian in this group is former biologist and former Council of Scientific and Industrial Research CSIR Director General, Shekhar Mande, who is also currently President, Indian National Science Academy.
The group has been constituted even as the international community ramps up efforts to achieve the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, while preparing for the 2027 SDG Summit.
“The group comprises distinguished experts from diverse geographic and disciplinary backgrounds from both developing and developed countries. Each member, recognised for their international excellence in science or technology, brings a wealth of experience and insight from civil society, the private sector, and the scientific community, underscoring the inclusive and comprehensive approach the UN is taking towards sustainable development,” an accompanying press statement noted.
The other members of the group are Marian Dorcas Quain (Ghana), Deputy Director-General of CSIR Ghana; Giorgio Rossi (Italy), Vice-President of Elettra-Sincrotrone Trieste; Magnus Andersson (Sweden), Associate Professor at Malmö University; Mônica Tejo Cavalcanti (Brazil), Professor at the Federal University of Campina Grande; Evgeniya Dmitrieva (Russia), Member of the Civic Chamber of the Russian Federation; Motoko Kotani (Japan), Executive Director of Science at RIKEN; Jin Li (China), President of Fudan University; Madin Maseeh (Maldives), President of the Maldives Space Research Organisation; and Rita Orji (Nigeria), Canada Research Chair in Persuasive Technology at Dalhousie University.
Professor Mande is also President of Vijnana Bharti, which, along with the Ministry of Science and Technology is gearing up to host the India International Science Festival in Pune in December.
Announcing this on Wednesday (November 18, 2026), Union Minister of Science Jitendra Singh said at a press briefing that the government had taken a conscious decision to replace “scattered, repetitive gatherings”, including the erstwhile Indian Science Congress, with two flagship national events carrying distinct identities.
While the Emerging Science, Technology and Innovation Conclave in October saw 13 Ministries and Nobel Laureates, and was an academic-driven conclave exploring frontier themes, the India International Science Festival (IISF) brought a “festive, public-facing character with international resonance”. By rotating the venue to different regions, after Chandigarh, Hyderabad, Goa and Delhi, bringing IISF for the first time to Pune leverages the western hub’s vibrant academic, research, and industrial ecosystem.
