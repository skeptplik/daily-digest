Jennifer Lopez says being Latina limited her early acting roles. Not everyone agrees
Some users criticized actor's comments, pointing to her extensive career
Jennifer Lopez says she received fewer acting offers in Hollywood than many people assume — and believes being Latina played a role.
In a Nov. 20 interview with the New York Times’ Popcast podcast, Lopez, who is Puerto Rican, said, "Even from the beginning of my career, I got offered things, but I was only offered a few things.
"I had to kind of create things for myself."
She said she did not fit the mold of "a white actress," adding that although casting has become more diverse over the years, performers may still have to create opportunities of their own.

Her comments drew criticism on social media, with some users pointing to her high-profile career and extensive filmography as evidence that her account does not add up.
Adrianne Curry-Rhode, a former America’s Next Top Model contestant, called Lopez's comments "next-level delusion" and criticized her acting. Other users similarly questioned her acting ability, arguing that her performances — not her ethnicity — could explain why she was not offered certain roles.
In an email to CBC News, a representative for Lopez said the remarks should be understood as referring to the 1990s and early 2000s, and said that other Latino actors had described similar constraints, citing performers John Leguizamo, Michael Peña and Eva Longoria.
Indeed, other racialized actors have made similar claims about getting major roles. But Toronto-based pop culture critic Rachel Ho, who has followed Lopez’s career since the late 1990s, says that although she is a hard-working performer whose accomplishments are sometimes dismissed, Lopez's visibility and range of roles may make it difficult for audiences to understand the limits she describes.
"I do think she must have faced a great deal of discrimination that we have no idea about, and I do think she has worked very hard to get to where she’s at," Ho told CBC News.
"But on the other hand, I do think she needs to have a little bit more awareness of her privileges, even as an ethnic woman, in this Hollywood space."
A career spanning film and music
Lopez’s breakthrough role came with Selena (1997), in which she portrayed Tejano singer Selena Quintanilla. She went on to star in Steven Soderbergh’s Out of Sight (1998) with George Clooney, as well as in romantic comedies like The Wedding Planner (2001) with Matthew McConaughey and Maid in Manhattan (2002) with Ralph Fiennes.
She has also pursued a major music career, releasing On the 6 in 1999 and J.Lo in 2001.
In her breakthrough role, Jennifer Lopez plays the titular character Selena in the 1997 biopic about the Tejano singer. (Ricco Torres/AFP/Getty Images)
Ho said Lopez’s success was largely shaped by her ability to work across acting and music at a time when several Latino performers were gaining mainstream attention. She described the period as a "Latin explosion," naming Lopez alongside Marc Anthony, Ricky Martin and Enrique Iglesias.
She also noted that Lopez has played characters whose ethnicity was not central to the story, and said this can make casting a Latino performer feel less complicated to studios than casting actors whose cultural background is treated as integral to a role. Furthermore, she pointed to Lopez's appearance, saying she could be cast in roles not explicitly written as Latina.
Other actors describe barriers
Actors of other ethnicities have also questioned whether success guarantees access to meaningful work. Mahershala Ali, a two-time Oscar winner, discussed the issue on the podcast 7PM in Brooklyn with Carmelo Anthony, where he said a Black actor winning an Oscar doesn't guarantee a steady supply of high-quality roles.
"There’s more good actors than there is good material," he said.
Actor Mahershala Ali poses for photographers at an event in Beverly Hills in 2025. (Frazer Harrison/Getty Images)
Actor Halle Berry also recently spoke about the issue of race and casting. Berry became the first Black woman to win the Oscar for best actress in 2002, for her role in Monster's Ball. In a recent interview with the Cut, Berry said she had expected a surge of scripts after her win, but recalled directors questioning what casting a Black woman would mean for a film’s story and whether such a movie would sell overseas.
Ho said Lopez’s comments can sound tone-deaf when viewed against her fame and the number of roles she has secured. She recalled Lopez’s prominence in the early 2000s and said she would not describe the actor as struggling in the ordinary sense. Still, she cautioned against dismissing the possibility that Lopez faced discrimination or had to work around limited choices.
"I think she’s a fascinating celebrity," Ho said. "I genuinely love her, but … at her point in her career, and maybe it’s because she is in a bubble and she doesn’t really see the wider world around her."
