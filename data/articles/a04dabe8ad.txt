Inaugural AFLW North Melbourne captain and eight-time All Australian Emma Kearney has announced her retirement following the Kangaroos' third straight premiership victory.
Kearney joined North Melbourne in 2019 after two years at the Western Bulldogs, where she also won a flag.
She led the Kangaroos for six seasons before handing over the captaincy to Jasmine Garner before the 2025 campaign, having steered the club to its maiden AFLW premiership in 2024.
Her teammate, three-time premiership player, inaugural player and long-time partner, Kate Shierlaw, also announced her retirement.
The pair told teammates today they would both be hanging up their boots.
“This club means the world to me, and I couldn’t be prouder of everything we’ve built and achieved together” Kearney said.
"Getting the opportunity to join North Melbourne ahead of our inaugural season in 2019 was so special, and watching this program grow into what it is today has been the greatest privilege of my career.
“The thing I’ll miss the most is simply being around this group every single day. Walking away from that daily environment is the hardest part.
“I’m so grateful to Crock (coach Darren Crocker) for the belief, guidance and care he has shown me and the entire group.
“Finally, a massive thank you to the North Melbourne fans. Your passion and loyalty through all the highs and lows has meant everything to us, and sharing these moments with you is something I’ll cherish forever.”
Following the premiership win over Melbourne last night at Princes Park, Crocker said Kearney had "left a legacy for our football club".
"She has really set the standards, when a lot of players actually didn't understand what it looked like," he told reporters post win.
Garner, who asked Kearney on stage to lift the cup beside her, said she was "an amazing player but also an amazing person".
"I looked up to her. A lot of us didn't really know what it looked like to have really high standards and work hard and be a team-first player," Garner said.
"I feel like she helped guide us through that.
"Not sure what she's going to do, but it was just really nice to have her up there (on stage)."
Shierlaw, an inaugural player for Carlton and co-captain for St Kilda before joining North, told ABC Sport the third flag was the most emotional win of the lot.
"I'm very overwhelmed with emotions. Relief, excited," Shierlaw said.
"It's just like a lot of hard work over four or five years … just Kearns and I having a great time out here, living our dreams. Three in a row."
