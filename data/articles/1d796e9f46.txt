Grand Theft Auto VI generated $3.79bn in retail sales on its first day, publisher Rockstar Games has announced, calling it the biggest launch for any entertainment product in history.
The worldwide figure was based on company estimates through the end of the day Thursday, the day the game launched. Rockstar said also it had sold 32.5m units of the game on prerelease, setting a record across platforms.
“We are incredibly humbled by the response from players around the world and we couldn’t be more excited to share this moment with you,” Sam Houser, Rockstar’s president and founder, said in a statement.
The company said the sales set “a new record for entertainment across any medium … becoming the biggest entertainment launch in history.”
The figure is nearly five times the roughly $800m that predecessor Grand Theft Auto V attracted during its first 24 hours in 2013.
It also exceeds the entire box office run of Avengers: Endgame, which became the highest-grossing film of all time last month at about $2.9bn worldwide after a rerelease pushed it past Avatar.
The game went on sale Thursday on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, launching first in New Zealand and rolling out market by market as each reached midnight local time.
The release quickly became a global phenomenon, generating more social media posts than this year’s World Cup, according to monitoring platform Visibrain.
Critics praised the game, which has a score of 99 on review aggregator Metacritic.
Rockstar said that made it the highest-rated game ever.
Like its predecessors, “GTA VI” is an open-world action game in which players roam freely through a sprawling city, stealing cars, pulling off heists and carrying out missions for criminal gangs.
Its busy streets, neon-lit nightlife and beaches are rendered in striking detail, and non-player characters go about their lives whether or not the player is involved.
While critics have raved about the game, it has also prompted real-world warnings from authorities.
With the game’s release on Thursday, police departments across the US reminded people that the car thefts, reckless driving, violence and other criminal mayhem depicted in the blockbuster title should not be re-enacted on the country’s streets.
There is insufficient evidence supporting a scientific causal link between violent video games and violent behaviour, according to the American Psycyhological Association. Nonetheless, several police departments took to the internet with warnings – and even jokes – aimed at keeping GTA VI’s lawlessness firmly in the virtual world.
The Atlanta police department delivered one of the clearest messages in a social media post.
“GTA is not a real place, but Atlanta is!” the department warned. “Don’t confuse virtual entertainment with real-life decisions.”
