A ruling by the United Nations' highest court that found Myanmar's security forces conducted State-sponsored genocide against the country's Rohingya Muslim minority was unfair, a Government official said on Friday (November 20, 2026).
The decision was not unanimous and Myanmar had been unjustly accused, said Khaing Khaing Soe, a Government spokesperson.
The ruling by the International Court of Justice (ICJ) was the first time the court has held a State responsible for genocide. It came seven years after the case was filed by predominantly Muslim Gambia.
It was the fourth time since the Nazi Holocaust that an international court ruled that the legal threshold to prove genocide had been met.
"Our country was unjustly accused," Mr. Khaing Soe told a press conference in the capital Naypyitaw.
"I want to say that this decision is not a unanimous decision by everyone, it is not fair."
Judges found that Myanmar's military carried out mass executions of men and boys, committed widespread rape and sexual violence against Rohingya women and burned or razed hundreds of villages during so-called clearance operations.
