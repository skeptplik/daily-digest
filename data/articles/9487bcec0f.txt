In a closely watched trial seen as one of this year’s most impactful immunology drug readouts, Kymera Therapeutics announced on Monday that its experimental therapy effectively and safely treated eczema patients’ itchy, inflamed skin.
In the Phase 2 trial, moderate-to-severe eczema patients randomly assigned to receive the biotech’s pill, KT-621, showed a statistically significant reduction in disease severity compared with those given a placebo. This was true across all three doses tested after 16 weeks of treatment, with a peak placebo-adjusted improvement of 45% among those given a moderate dose. Patients on this dose were also more likely to see an improvement of 75% or more in disease severity, with 62% of treated participants clearing that bar compared with 22% of the placebo group.
The BROADEN2 trial succeeded across all primary and secondary endpoints, including itch reduction and the fraction of patients who achieved clear or almost-clear skin. KT-621 was also generally safe and well tolerated. The only side effect that was more common among patients on the drug was conjunctivitis (i.e. pink eye), which appeared in 20%, 13%, and 11% of patients on a low, middle, and high dose, respectively. All of these cases were mild to moderate, and none caused participants to stop treatment.
