In short:
Western Force winger James Ramm will make his Wallabies debut against Ireland at 28.
Ramm never played First XV at school and only took up rugby once a promising gymnastics career ended.
Ramm's rapid improvement after moving from the Waratahs to Northampton Saints in England put him on the Test radar.
James Ramm, the late-blooming, one-time gymnast who has finally made the international rugby grade at the age of 28, wants his long, battling journey to the top to inspire a new generation of wannabe Wallabies.
When winger Ramm takes the field at Twickenham against Ireland for his Australia debut on Friday night (Saturday AEDT), it will be the culmination of a road beset by injuries and false starts, one on which he switched sports and had to move to the other side of the world to reboot his career.
But now, when he thinks how the one-time gangly kid who couldn't even break into his first-team at Sydney's St Joseph's College is going to be one of the modern-day Wallabies' oldest wing debutants after years of perseverance, he declares: "I'm really proud of my journey."
"And all I hope from it is that other people who are in similar situations, playing seconds or thirds at school who want to pursue rugby, realise that school's not the be-all and end-all," he said.
"As soon as you leave school, you understand that, but when you're in it, it's quite hard to see. A couple of years ago, I went back to Joey's actually to talk to the second XV players at the school and explain my story and show that not everyone's journey is the same.
"If you really want it, you can get it from whatever position you're in at school."
So it was that Ramm's rugby adventure really only began after he'd given up on his promising gymnastics career, which had once dealt him dreams of being an Olympian.
Yet he couldn't crack the rugby hierarchy at "Joey's", unlike his schoolmate Tom Wright, with whom he'll now be working in the back three at Twickenham.
"Wrighty was sort of a superstar at school, and I'd love it if he ever talked to me," said Ramm, as he sat next to a chuckling Wright at the Wallabies' team hotel in London on Thursday.
Ramm did finally break through for Randwick and represented the Junior Wallabies.
He went on to a brief spell in Dave Rennie's Australia squad while with the Waratahs, but injury woes and lack of progress at home led him to head for England, his mother's home country.
There, he really blossomed with Northampton, scoring 32 tries and helping them win two English titles and reach the final of last year's Europe's Champions Cup Final, before also going on to impress while propelling Northland to the NPC final in New Zealand.
Now firmly back in the Australian fold having signed a two-year deal with Western Force, he's suddenly burst back into the picture under coach Les Kiss, admitting he was "overwhelmed" when he got the call to play this week after not even being in the 23 the previous weeks on the Nations Championship northern tour.
He's going to make his debut at the home ground of the country that even sniffed around trying to sign him up as an England player.
Having not even settled back at his new home in Perth before being jetting back out to the UK with his Wallabies teammates, Ramm said: "I didn't think I'd sort of be touching down in Sydney and then a couple of days later be back over here in this beautiful weather as quickly as I have."
That "beautiful weather" will be a bone-chilling night at HQ, but it will all be worth it for Ramm, who calls Twickenham "a sick stadium", having got used to the ambience in big finals with Northampton. He reckons he's ready for anything.
"Age is definitely just a number. My rugby career started a bit later than probably what's usual, so I'm hoping that I can bank those years at the back end of my career," Ramm said.
"And for me, this validates every time you wanted to take an easy option, or thought 'I'm gonna have a day off or skip this' but didn't. It validates the fact that all that hard work pays off.
"It's tough sometimes, and you're in the dumps, but it really shows that if you persist with it and you keep on that line, it's all worth it."
