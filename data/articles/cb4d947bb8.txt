WASHINGTON — Even as Republicans pick up the pieces following devastating midterm election losses, spirits are high at the Department of Health and Human Services, where recent changes in the administration — namely the appointment of Stephen Miller as White House chief of staff — have put wind under the wings of health secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.
“The Senate has no leverage over me anymore,” Kennedy told a crowd of Make America Healthy Again supporters at an event called “A Toast to a Healthier America,” held less than a week after Republicans lost control of the House and Senate to Democrats.
Stefanie Spear, Kennedy’s ever-present principal deputy chief of staff, has been telling staff that Miller’s effusive support of MAHA means it’s go time, according to one person familiar with the department, who spoke with STAT on the condition of anonymity because they are not authorized to speak publicly.
