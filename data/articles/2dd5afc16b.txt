Deaths linked to heat pass a record 5,000 in England this year, UKHSA says
More than 5,000 deaths were linked to heat in England this year, the highest since current records began in 2016, the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) has said.
An estimated 5,258 deaths were associated with hot weather, comfortably beating the previous high of 2,985 set in 2022.
The UKHSA said this was “the highest annual total of heat-associated deaths recorded since monitoring began in 2016, with the greatest number associated with the June heat episode”.

Stephen Timms says Pip review being published today will ‘rehumanise’ benefit
Good morning. Stephen Timms, the minister for social security and disability, will today publish the review he has been leading on the future of Pip (personal independence payment), Britain’s main disability benefit. He has been working on this for more than a year and he was appointed as Keir Starmer’s plans to cut welfare spending by £5bn (mostly by making it harder to claim Pip) were being wrecked by a Labour backbench revolt. The review was launched to allow the government to salvage some sort of reform from the wreckage, and to repair relations with the disability sector.
This was never intended to be a cost-cutting exercise; the terms of reference said that the review would “operate within the Office for Budget Responsibility’s (OBR) projections for future Pip expenditure” – ie, it would assume planned spending on Pip remained the same – and it has been “co-produced” with disabled people. Claiming Pip can be fiendishly complicated, and the review was about making the benefit operate more effectively.
A few months after the Timms review was launched, Alan Milburn was appointed to lead his review of youth unemployment, or Neets (young people not in education, employment or training). Some Neets are on Pip and there has been an overlap. We have already had the Milburn report and it will form the basis for the government’s main welfare reform project for the rest of this parliament. Timms has been overshadowed; some of his recommendations will clash with Milburn’s (the Timms report is not expected to back the Milburn proposal for under-25s to lose Pip), ministers will have to blend the two sets of recommendations when they produce legislation for next year, but Milburn’s ideas are likely to take priority.
But the Pip proposals still matter. Around 4m people in England and Wales receive Pip, and around 90% of them are over 25. The plans being announced today could have a huge impact on how they live.
We know quite a lot about what to expect because Timms published an interim report in the summer. And in the Commons yesterday, during DWP questions, he gave some clues as to what is coming today.
Timms said:
When the review is published, the review will call for a fundamental overhaul to rehumanise Pip … There are a number of difficulties with the current system. Pip is a highly valued benefit but the current assessment is not fit for purpose, and tomorrow we will set out proposals to change it.
As well as saying the review would propose changing the way Pip assessments are carried out, Timms also said it would open the door to some Pip claimants getting part of their benefit in the form of services, not cash. He explained:
We will set out the view that in addition to a cash benefit from Pip, which we are recommending should remain a cash-first benefit, there may well be services that would be helpful to people as part of their Pip award.
The Motability scheme already enables many disabled people to enjoy the mobility that they would not otherwise be able to enjoy, and they choose to forgo part of their cash benefit in return for access to the scheme. We think that principle could be applied more widely with the Pip scheme.
