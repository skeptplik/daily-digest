A Senate committee chaired by Ted Cruz (R-Texas) voted today to approve a Federal Communications Commission nominee, despite accusations that the nominee gave false testimony about her work as a legal advisor for FCC Chairman Brendan Carr. Democrats attempted to prevent a quorum by not showing up, but Cruz proceeded with a Republican-only vote.
All 15 Republicans on the Senate Commerce Committee voted to approve Trump nominee Danielle Thumann, while the committee’s 13 Democrats were absent. “We’re going to be doing our job today, even if the Democrats refuse to show up to do theirs,” Cruz said at today’s hearing.
Nominees are vetted in committees first, and then seek require majority approval from the Senate. Republicans are hoping to get Thumann confirmed before Democrats take Senate control in January.
Although the Senate Commerce Committee generally requires one minority member to be present for nomination votes, a spokesperson for Cruz said Senate rules allow the nomination to be advanced for a vote in the entire chamber. A spokesperson for Sen. Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.), the top Democrat on the committee, said in response that “since 2005, Commerce Committee rules required that a quorum include one member of the minority for official action on bill, resolution, or nomination.”
Technically, the committee has operated with no rules during Cruz’s chairmanship because Cruz “failed to pass committee rules” for the current session of Congress, Cantwell’s spokesperson said. “Nevertheless, in practice it has operated in line with rules from the last Congress (and this provision, specifically, since 2005), including that a quorum with a member of the minority party must be present for official action on a nomination or legislation. Until today.”
False testimony allegation
At a hearing last week, Thumann said she never worked with Carr on media-related topics, leading to accusations that she provided false testimony. “I have not worked with the chairman on any media-related issues,” Thumann said after Sen. Ben Ray Luján (D-N.M.) asked, “Do you support Chairman Carr’s illegal campaign to remove Jimmy Kimmel from the nation’s airwaves?”
