Donald Trump has headlined his third cryptocurrency gala at his country club in Virginia, rewarding top holders of his own meme coin, as Democrats accuse the president of improperly profiting from his crypto ventures.
The dinner, which took place at the Trump National golf club in Sterling, Virginia, included meet-and-greets with Trump supporters including the boxing champion Floyd Mayweather, the Shark Tank investor Kevin O’Leary, the NBA champion Tristan Thompson and the magician Lior Suchard, according to the event’s website.
Organisers indicated that 185 crypto wallets met the criteria for Sunday’s dinner, although the leaderboard on the $Trump coin club website listed just 130 wallets when the contest concluded on 16 November.
Together, those listed wallets held $8.6m – a shadow of the $148m listed for the dinner in May 2025, which was attended by 220 crypto investors.
A Trump fragrance, a poster, a commemorative ticket, and a watch were reserved for 120 attenders. The top 29 investors were invited to a VIP reception, and the top four received a “Trump 18K gold watch”.
Trump said his motive for supporting $Trump was to boost the economic competitiveness of the US. “I’m doing it for the United States … If we don’t do it, China’s going to do it,” he told attenders.
Participants stood in the rain as they waited to enter the venue. Attenders described the scene inside as subdued. It was a very different atmosphere to that of the first gala, when tuxedo-clad contest winners pulled up to the club in luxury sports cars, and protesters chanted and stomped outside the club as the presidential Marine One helicopter descended.
Trump has hosted two similar galas since he took office last year, bringing a flurry of criticism as Democrats accuse the president and his family of monetising his brand through an array of equities and crypto ventures while in office.
After sweeping wins in the midterms, Democrats have vowed to investigate conflicts of interest involving Trump and his personal wealth during his presidency. They also say they plan to push for legislation that would ban activities such as the meme coin gala dinners.
A letter sent by Adam Schiff and eight other US senators last month said: “This dinner and scoring mechanism appear carefully constructed to increase investment in $Trump, allowing the president to abuse the power of his office to personally enrich himself, as he has throughout this term.”
Since returning to the White House, Trump has made more than $1bn from his crypto businesses, according to financial disclosures released in June. Trump received more than $500m from his World Liberty Financial business selling new crypto products, and raked another $600m from sales of the $Trump meme coins launched days before his inauguration.
The White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment. The White House spokesperson Anna Kelly told Reuters earlier this year: “Neither the president nor his family has ever engaged – or will ever engage – in conflicts of interest. President Trump proudly made the United States the crypto capital of the world through executive actions.”
More than half of the digital wallets publicly listed on the contest leaderboard for Sunday’s dinner were tied directly to crypto exchanges that bar US-based users, indicating they are linked to foreign nationals, according to analysis of blockchain data prepared for Reuters by the nonpartisan, non-profit consumer advocacy group Public Citizen.
The Trump family’s other crypto-connected equities and ventures have also fallen in value to record lows. Together, outside investors in their five main crypto and crypto-linked projects have lost more than $5bn, according to a Reuters analysis.
