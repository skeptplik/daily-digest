A charity set up by the family of the pandemic fundraiser Captain Sir Tom Moore has applied to be struck off.
The charity is now known as the 1189808 Foundation, having dropped Moore’s name at the request of his family last year.
An application to have the organisation struck off appeared on the Companies House register on Friday.
The dropping of the captain’s name came after the charities watchdog found there had been “repeated” misconduct and mismanagement on the part of the second world war veteran’s daughter and her husband in their running of the foundation.
The Charity Commission’s report published in 2024 found a “repeated pattern of behaviour” through which Hannah Ingram-Moore and her husband, Colin, made private gains and which the regulator said would have left people feeling “misled”.
The report found that a £1.4m book deal and an £18,000 awards ceremony appearance fee were among the financial benefits the Ingram-Moores enjoyed through their family links to the Captain Tom Foundation, which was launched in May 2020.
The Ingram-Moores have already been banned from being charity trustees, and Hannah Ingram-Moore was prohibited from being a trustee or holding a senior management role in any charity in England and Wales for 10 years, and Colin Ingram-Moore for eight years.
They both resigned from the foundation.
Moore became a household name during the first Covid-19 lockdown, raising £39.4m for NHS charities by walking laps of the garden at his home in Marston Moretaine, Bedfordshire. He was honoured with a knighthood from Queen Elizabeth II.
The millions raised and donated to NHS Charities Together for its Covid-19 Urgent Appeal before the foundation was formed were not part of the commission’s inquiry.
Moore, a father of two who was born in Keighley, West Yorkshire, died aged 100 in February 2021 after testing positive for coronavirus.
