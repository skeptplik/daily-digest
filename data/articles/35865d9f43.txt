Sony's standard DualSense controllers—the mainstay for controller for the PS5—don't have the hall effect sticks and triggers we might hope for, let alone TMR tech. But they do have Sony's proprietary adaptive trigger tech, which provide some delightful tactility in games that support it.

I spent years using a DualShock 4 for my controller-based PC gaming, back when it was more likely that you'd have to rely on community-made input mappers that would inevitably decide to arm wrestle with Steam Input for controller dominance. It eventually got to be enough of a headache that I bit the bullet, switching over to Xbox controllers and 8bitdo alternatives.
I've been borrowing a friend's PS5 for the launch of GTA 6, and after getting reacquainted with PlayStation controller hardware, I've been realizing how much I've been missing by not giving the DualSense its fair shake. Like our Morgan Park, I've been enamored with GTA 6's use of haptic feedback. I'm an adaptive trigger convert now, and thanks to Black Friday deals, I can grab a DualSense for $54 at Walmart and revel in the dynamic trigger technology I've been sorely missing.

It's time to go back to the old me. But like, a little newer. The newer old me. Do you know what I'm saying?
While it'll be nice to have a DualSense on hand for GTA 6's eventual PC launch—whenever that ends up being—I've been operating under the assumption that it's otherwise a bit of a wasteland in terms of adaptive trigger support on PC. Thanks to this handy list of games that support DualSense adaptive triggers from PCGamingWiki, however, that misconception has been dispelled. A lot of games I love are on here! I could've been enjoying Ace Combat 8 with the controller triggers doing things other than vibrating slightly! I've been a fool.
Better late than never, I guess. And better a $54 controller than a $75 one.
