U.S. stocks slumped on Thursday (November 19, 2026) after Anthropic’s super-sized IPO failed to revitalise faded enthusiasm over artificial intelligence, as an early spike in its share price ebbed.
All three major U.S. stock indexes ended the session sharply lower, with the benchmark S&P 500 logging its fifth straight session of losses.
The S&P 500 was on course to post its steepest weekly loss since April 2025.
Anthropic’s debut, the largest IPO on record, was priced on late Wednesday (November 18, 2026) at $83 per share and was widely viewed as the next high bar to clear for the AI trade. The stock opened at $111.50 shortly before noon. After the company’s market valuation briefly surpassed $2 trillion, its momentum began to lose steam.
“The Anthropic IPO is attracting gobs of attention,” said Jed Ellerbroek, portfolio manager at Argent Capital in St. Louis, Missouri.
Even so, Ellerbroek’s enthusiasm for the AI trade has waned.
“My beliefs about the growth in data centre capex spending have come down a little bit over the last month or two,” he added. “Nvidia’s crisis situation is a big deal. The midterm elections have had an impact. Data centres are going to be more expensive to build and they’re going to be built more slowly.”
Advanced Micro Devices shares tumbled after investigative outlet Hunterbrook said its affiliated fund, Hunterbrook Capital, had taken a short position in the company.
Most chipmakers were lower, including Micron and Broadcom, among others.
Nvidia marked its sixth straight session of losses after the chipmaker’s upbeat revenue forecast did little to lift sentiment.
The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index closed lower. Neocloud firms CoreWeave and Nebius sustained steep losses.
In other stocks, Palo Alto Networks shares jumped after the cybersecurity firm raised its 2027 revenue guidance. Walmart dipped after the retail giant’s annual sales forecast disappointed investors.
According to preliminary data, the S&P 500 lost 148.39 points, or 2.03%, to end at 7,175.24 points, while the Nasdaq Composite lost 544.00 points, or 2.23%, to 23,885.88. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1,102.71 points, or 2.20%, to 49,095.46.
Focus turns to the FED
Following Wednesday’s release of minutes from the U.S. Federal Reserve’s October policy meeting, investors are recalibrating their expectations pertaining to upcoming rate decisions.
The minutes revealed a desire to rein in inflation even prior to the recent release of consumer and producer price indexes, which showed price pressures heating up.
Financial markets are currently pricing in about a 94% likelihood of a 25-basis-point rate hike at the central bank’s December meeting, with a small but growing chance of a super-sized 50-basis-point increase, according to CME’s FedWatch tool.
Next week, the Commerce Department is expected to release its personal consumption expenditures (PCE) report, which includes the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, the PCE price index. Analysts expect the report to show year-on-year core inflation of 3.2%, well above the central bank’s 2% annual target.
