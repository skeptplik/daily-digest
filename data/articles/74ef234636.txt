A new exhibition at London's Saatchi Gallery is inviting visitors to put down their phones and talk with strangers over ping pong. Created by British-French artist Charlotte Colbert, 'Conversations with Strangers' features sculptural tables designed to encourage spontaneous and sporting encounters.
What if a game of ping pong could help strangers connect? That is the idea behind a new interactive exhibition at London's Saatchi Gallery, where visitors are being encouraged to put down their phones, pick up a bat and play.
Called Conversations with Strangers, the exhibition is the work of British-French artist and filmmaker Charlotte Colbert. It features specially designed sculptural ping pong tables, transforming the traditional gallery experience into a space for play and face-to-face interaction.
The artist says the exhibition builds on her recent experience creating large-scale public sculptures in unexpected locations, including New York and Egypt.
Bringing that approach into the gallery, Colbert wanted to create an environment where visitors could interact with the artwork rather than simply observe it. "It aims to be part-gallery, part-playground and a connecter," she explains.
For Colbert, the idea carries a wider message about human relationships in the digital age. "In the age of algorithms what information we get and who we speak to is so controlled that chatting to strangers is a form of defiance. A glitch in the matrix that opens up a whole world of possibility," she says.
And she sees table tennis as a particularly effective way of encouraging people to reconnect with the present. "Ping pong is a form of instant mindfulness. It immediately forces you in the here and now. Play is the basis of all reinvention - it frees the mind and body - even for just a second and in that space allows possibility to exist."
Timing is everything in life and the exhibition comes as table tennis is enjoying a renaissance in France due to the Olympic triumphs of the Lebrun brothers, Felix and Alexis. Actor Timothée Chalamet's starring role in Marty Supreme also put the sport in the global spotlight.
"That was a game changer for ping pong!" says Colbert. "I think even Zinedine Zidane came to watch. Epic. And off the back of Marty Supreme it's a real table tennis renaissance.
Colbert argues that play should not be seen as something reserved for children, but as an essential part of creativity and human connection.
Alongside the exhibition, Colbert has created a limited-edition collection with Art of Ping Pong, featuring playable wall-mounted tables, bats and nets decorated with her signature surrealist designs.
Conversations with Strangers runs at Saatchi Gallery until January 2027. Admission is free.
