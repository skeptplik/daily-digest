Two fly-tippers were able to dump huge amounts of household waste over their garden fences without being detected for several months, with the pile eventually reaching 20 tonnes.
It was spotted on land owned by a business in South Wigston, Leicestershire, earlier in the year.
However, due to the location of the fly-tip, it was not immediately visible to employees of the business, which Oadby and Wigston Borough Council said "allowed the offenders to act over several months".
The two individuals responsible were each issued with a Â£600 fixed penalty notice following an interview under caution, the authority added, while a third person was cautioned for their role in the offence.
Before the rubbish could be cleared, the council's environmental health officers worked with the business to gather evidence to prove who had been responsible.
The authority has not specified which business was affected by the fly-tipping, or from where the rubbish was sourced.
The council said it had also recently issued another Â£600 fine after a resident's waste was found in a lay-by off Gartree Road in Oadby.
They had not fly-tipped the rubbish themselves, the authority added, but had paid a rogue operator in good faith to take it away.
According to the council, residents have a duty of care for their waste until it is disposed of correctly and should ensure it is given to a licensed waste operator.
Councillor Samina Haq, leader of the council, said: "There is simply no excuse for fly-tipping. It damages our environment, costs money to clear up and is unfair on the overwhelming majority of residents and businesses who dispose of their waste responsibly.
"These successful cases send a clear message that we will investigate reports, gather evidence and take action against those responsible.
"Please continue to report fly-tipping whenever you see it and, if you pay someone to remove your waste, always make sure they are properly licensed."
