Grand Theft Auto 6 is a game about criminals doing crimes. Car crimes, gun crimes, drug crimes, and first degree decorating crimes. Early in the game, Jason's buddy Cal convinces him that he's got to turn his r/malelivingspace shack into a place he can bring his girl Lucia without feeling like she's still stuck in a jail cell. Cal is right, but his decorating decisions should be illegal.
This is how Jason's living room looks at the beginning of the game while he's still flying solo. Not great, Jason. If I've learned anything from lurking in interior decorating communities online it's that furniture should never be touching your walls, no matter how small the room is, and that nobody's TV is ever at the right height.
Cal hassles Jason into moving some things around and, while I love his motivation, he makes everything worse. What they wind up with is this: couch blocking prime navigation space and the TV sitting in front of the sliding glass door. Look, I know the point is that Cal is an incompetent dork. He's slugging beers and insisting they make the room feng shui. I can't forgive him for this one though.
Cal does get one thing right here: using the couch and arm chair to create a space that feels like a living room within the open area. Once Lucia moves in she and Jason continue making the waterfront shack more homey. They get a rug in the living room, a classic choice to finish tying that space into a room. Lucia brings home a coffee table to assemble (cute domestic conversation ensues). The dining table gets centered properly in its corner.
But that damn TV stays parked in front of the glass door. It's criminal! There would be so much glare on that screen from the east and west facing windows and light all day long drowning it out from behind that north facing glass door. My sad, myopic eyes are tearing up just thinking about squinting at it.
So I took Jason's conundrum to one of the interior decorating communities on Reddit to ask for some group workshopping. They immediately grasped the unforgivable TV placement and the inherent issue with the number of windows in this place. Almost no wall is safe here.
One suggestion was to keep those window blinds in the living room down and place the TV stand in front of it. Makes sense to me—Jason wouldn't be losing much light with all the other windows in here. Another more minor fix was to just put the TV at a 45 degree angle in its corner so it's less directly in front of the glass door, though I don't think that solves the glare problems.

The more popular suggestion (and my favorite) was to swap the dining and living room. The TV can sit on the rear wall, slightly tucked away with the blinds pulled down, while the dining table gets easier access from the kitchen and doesn't suffer for being lit from all sides.
Alas, I have it on good authority that the TV never manages to move from where Cal stuck it. I can forgive drug smuggling, but I draw the line at suboptimal viewing angles. Ah well, perhaps Jason and Lucia have a better floor plan to work with in the next place they move to as their criminal exploits grow.
