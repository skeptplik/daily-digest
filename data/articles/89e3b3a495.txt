In short:
The 1948 Caulfield Cup has been stolen, just days after several Melbourne Cup trophies were stolen.
A man has been arrested in connection with the theft of the Caulfield Cup, which was previously stolen in the early 50s.
Victoria Police said alcohol was also stolen from Caulfield Racecourse as part of a string of break-ins.
Thieves have smashed their way into Caulfield Racecourse and stolen the 1948 Caulfield Cup.
That theft is believed to be one in a string of break-ins at the track during Melbourne's Spring Racing carnival.
Victoria Police confirmed this afternoon that an alleged offender or offenders entered the Caulfield premises on four occasions between November 4 and 11.
"Alcohol and trophies, including the Caulfield Cup, were allegedly stolen during the course of offending," the police spokeswoman said.
"A 39-year-old was arrested at a residence on Orrong Road in Elsternwick on 16 November.
"He was charged with attempted burglary and possess drug of dependence (methylamphetamine)."
The police spokeswoman said the Elsternwick man was charged and bailed to attend Moorabbin Magistrates Court on March 1 next year.
It comes after another brazen heist at Flemington this week, where thieves disguised as construction workers stole three Melbourne Cups won consecutively by legendary mare Makybe Diva in 2003, 2004 and 2005.
"Detectives are investigating if (the Caulfield Cup) incident is linked to the theft of the three Melbourne Cup trophies on 16 November," the police spokeswoman said.
Following the theft earlier this week, a Melbourne Racing Club spokesman said the cup was stolen along with some bottles of wine and some other "low-value" items.
"The club has been requested by Victoria Police not to speak to the media during the investigation," a MRC spokesman said.
"As this is an active police investigation, the Club can make no further comment at this stage."
It is understood the 1948 Caulfield Cup, won by then five-year-old Australian thoroughbred Red Fury, had been kept in a secure area of the racecourse off-limits to the public.
The cup, worth $55,000, was sold in March and was on loan to the MRC during this year's racing festivities.
The 2026 theft is not the first time the very same Caulfield Cup has been stolen.
Three men were committed for trial over the theft of the 1948 cup, less than two years after Red Fury's racetrack success.
In 1950, The Argus newspaper reported that the cup was stolen from the now-defunct Ress-Astoria Hotel in central Melbourne when it had a value of 200 pounds (about $15,500 in 2026, adjusted for inflation).
One of the men accused of the theft was later acquitted.
