The European Commission is inviting public feedback on its proposed Digital Fairness Act (DFA), which would introduce new rules on loot boxes, virtual currencies, and in-game purchases by children.
The European Commission published the proposal on November 11, outlining legislative proposals aimed at addressing practices that exploit or harm consumers in digital markets, including the games industry. These include in-game purchases, with specific rules for virtual currency, loot boxes, pay-to-win mechanics, and subscription-based games and services.
Feedback on the DFA is now open and will run until midnight Brussels time on January 14, 2027, although the deadline continues to be extended day-by-day until the proposal is available in every EU language. Responses will be published online and summarized for the European Parliament and Council, which must negotiate the proposal before the act can be adopted.
UK trade body Ukie said the proposal could undermine parental choice, increase costs for developers, and weaken the UK games industry.
"We are particularly concerned about undermining parental agency by placing blanket restrictions on certain features, rather than giving parents the ability to make decisions based on individual circumstances," Ukie told PocketGamer.biz.
German trade association game has also criticized the proposal. It argued that treating every game rated below 18 as aimed at children would undermine the USK and Pegi age rating systems, and called on the European Parliament and Council to fundamentally revise the draft. The CEOs of Supercell and King have shared similar views.
