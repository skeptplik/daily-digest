Naha – A Japanese government-sponsored ceremony was held Sunday to mark the completion of restoration work on the Seiden main hall of Shuri Castle in Naha after a 2019 fire.
Incorporating findings from research conducted since the 1992 restoration, the hall has been reconstructed to more faithfully reflect its original appearance. Fire safety measures have also been strengthened. It will be open to the public starting Monday.
The Seiden was destroyed by fire in the early hours of Oct. 31, 2019, along with the Hokuden north hall, the Nanden south hall and other structures.
The cause of the fire has not been identified. A third-party committee established by the Okinawa Prefectural Government has pointed to the possibility of a problem with the electrical system.
Work on the restoration of the main hall began in November 2022. While aiming to recreate its 18th-century appearance, as was done during the 1992 restoration, the project has enhanced the accuracy of the reconstruction by incorporating research based on newly discovered historical documents and studies of high-resolution versions of old photographs.
For the exterior walls and other surfaces, a pigment called Kushimagiribengara, which is derived from bacteria found in waterside areas in the northern part of the prefecture, was used. It is characterized by a deeper, more subdued red color than that of the previous building.
The lack of an automatic fire extinguishing system was also cited as a contributing factor to the spread of the fire in 2019.
In addition to the sprinkler system, the latest restoration project included the installation of ground-fault circuit interrupters and seismic-sensing circuit breakers to prevent electrical fires. Low-light surveillance cameras capable of capturing clear images, including at night, and an automatic system for notifying the fire department in the event of a fire were also installed.
According to the Okinawa General Bureau of the Cabinet Office, the restoration of the main hall is being led by the national government, with a total cost of some ¥12 billion. The progress of the work has been shown to the public in select visitor areas.
The prefectural government has been soliciting donations for the restoration, raising about ¥6.2 billion from both domestic and international donors by the end of October this year. Reconstruction of the north and south halls is also due to proceed.
