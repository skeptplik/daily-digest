Almost a million Americans left without power on Thanksgiving as storm sweeps East Coast
Nearly a million people were left without power Thursday after a severe storm lashed the East Coast of the US, tearing up trees and powerlines on Thanksgiving. New York City authorities had been forced to postpone the annual Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade due to the intense winds.
A powerful storm disrupted the Thanksgiving holiday along much of the US East Coast on Thursday, knocking out power to nearly 1 million customers at one point ‌as wind gusts of up to 112kph toppled trees and power lines.
The storm contributed to flight delays, with FlightAware reporting a total of 454 US-related cancellations on Thursday and 2,411 delays. ‌Major airports in the Washington and New York City areas were among the hardest hit.
Heavy rains flooded coastal areas, while utilities warned of an increased risk of falling trees and power outages.
More than 908,000 US utility customers had no power as of 9:56pm ET (0256 GMT), according to PowerOutage.us, down from 961,000 ‌at 7:30pm ET.
The data included more than 207,000 outages in Pennsylvania, over 179,000 in New Jersey, 139,000 in Maryland, 106,000 in Virginia, 100,000 in Missouri and more than 68,000 in New York state.
Wind ‌gusts of 80 to 112kph were reported or forecast across parts of Virginia, Maryland, Delaware, New Jersey, New York, and coastal New England, according to the US National Weather Service.
It said conditions would gradually improve across the Mid-Atlantic overnight as the heaviest rain and strongest winds moved into New England, although blustery conditions with gusts of 40 to 55kph were expected across parts of the East Coast on Friday.
New York City Emergency Management reported winds intensified on Thursday afternoon, reaching 90kph at LaGuardia Airport.
The NWS said strong onshore winds associated with the storm, combined with king tides, were causing coastal flooding in low-lying communities along portions of the East Coast.
Officials had already postponed Thursday's 100th edition of the annual Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York City because of the storm threat, the first time the event has been rescheduled due to severe weather.
People were rescued from their cars in New York City because of flooding, Mayor Zohran Mamdani said, and similar rescues were reported in other coastal communities.
“There is no destination worth your safety,” Mamdani said.
Separately on Thursday, US President Donald Trump said he had approved about $100 million in federal aid to Florida following severe storms and flooding there. He said the total included funding previously approved in connection with Hurricane Isaias.
