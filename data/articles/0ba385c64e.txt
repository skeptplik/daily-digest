Architecture studio Hawkins\Brown has unveiled the Harold Hill Family Welcome Centre in London, the first purpose-built development to provide temporary accommodation for homeless families in the UK.
Situated in the London Borough of Havering, the centre contains 74 flats for families and two floors of community space built around a central courtyard.
Forming part of the wider regeneration of Harold Hill Estate, Harold Hill Family Welcome Centre was designed to help alleviate the growing demand for temporary housing in the local borough.
Intending to set a benchmark for temporary family housing in the UK, Hawkins\Brown aimed to create a welcoming building that eases the stress of families facing homelessness.
“Many of the families who come here are fleeing hugely painful domestic situations,” said Hawkins\Brown partner Nick Gaskell.
“The Harold Hill Family Welcome Centre aims to make a traumatic period a little more bearable by providing a caring, calming and uplifting environment for as long as they need it.”
Designed to be in keeping with the neighbouring buildings, the exterior of the U-shaped building is lined with striped bands of orange-toned bricks and pops of pale green metal sheeting.
A secure, landscaped courtyard with play space at the centre of the building helps bring natural light into the surrounding flats.
Inside, the apartments were designed to offer higher-quality living arrangements than typical temporary housing, with tall windows, underfloor heating and open-plan kitchen, living and dining rooms.
Community and family support spaces on the ground floor of the Harold Hill Family Welcome Centre include a reception, visitor breakout areas, children’s indoor play spaces, communal laundries and staff facilities.
Based on the studio’s research into dementia-friendly design, Hawkins\Brown used colour zoning, visual landmarks, accessible graphics and high-contrast colours in the centre, aiming to make the space easy to navigate for residents and visitors of all ages and abilities.
Elsewhere in London, architecture studio HTA Design recently unveiled what it says is the largest new-build council housing development in the UK.
In Ukraine, architecture studio Office for Future is working on plans to transform a school into temporary housing for families affected by the war in the country.
