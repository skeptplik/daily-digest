Marvel Studios has revealed the release date for its Spider-Man: Brand New Day re-release, promising to bring new footage "and a couple more surprises" to theaters this December.
The official Spider-Man X/Twitter account confirmed plans to bring Peter Parker's (Tom Holland) first run-in with Jean Grey (Sadie Sink) back to moviegoers when the re-release arrives December 4, 2026. That means we're just a few weeks away from its premiere, with the full post teasing that this version of the movie is "an extended cut featuring new scenes and a couple more surprises."
Tickets go on sale this Thursday, November 19. Hope you're not busy!
As reported by Deadline earlier this year, Sony had plans to bring Spider-Man: Brand New Day back to theaters just months after the MCU movie's original premiere shattered records. Officially titled Spider-Man: Brand New Brand New Day, the outlet reported today that the plan is still for at least some of that new footage to include a cut scene featuring Rosario Dawson as her Defenders-era character, Night Nurse.
Even if you've had your fill of Holland's web-swinging antics for the year, Disney, Marvel, and Sony definitely know what they're doing when it comes to convincing MCU fans for a second round of their movies. Avengers: Doomsday will premiere just two weeks after Spidey returns. Although the December re-release may not be quite as likely to include any tie-in teaser footage like September's Avengers: Endgame Encore, Marvel fans will probably show up just to make sure, especially considering that "couple more surprises" tease.
There's also the fact that the wait for Avengers: Doomsday has already been quite long. Marvel has refrained from saying or showing too much about the film's actual plot, making that early December window a nice opportunity to capitalize on the hype and move Spider-Man's box-office needle just a little more.
Spider-Man: Brand New Day premiered July 31, 2026, and was immediately a box office hit. It finally arrived on digital October 6 after grossing more than $2.5 billion worldwide, securing its place as one of the highest-grossing movies of all time.
