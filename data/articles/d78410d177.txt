Unreal Engine 6 will discontinue support for Blueprints and the Actor Gameplay Framework, instead utilising Verse and Epic's new Scene Graph framework.
In a blog post, Marcus Wassmer, EVP of Development, acknowledged this change contradicts Epic Games' June announcement that Blueprints and Actors "would coexist in UE6 Early Access and 6.0, so UE projects could upgrade incrementally while the new Scene Graph framework matures."
"However, with recent advances in AI capability, we expect AI to massively help with converting UE5 projects to UE6, if not make that process fully automatic. That also means we can deliver UE6 on time (or sooner) while meeting its design goals: a new data model, a Verse-first architecture, modularity, and AI operability."
Wassmer stated that Epic expects AI will make converting projects from UE5 to UE6 more "practical and cost efficient."
"The cost of any given task with AI has been going down 13x year over year, and the complexity of converting a game remains more or less constant," said Wassmer. "We expect this trend to continue and to accelerate, which should result in modest conversion costs by the time UE 6.0 is out in the world."
"Fortnite has one of the largest libraries of Blueprints anywhere. Two weeks ago, we experimented with using AI to convert all of the Blueprints for Battle Royale to Verse. This project was expected to take years, but we now have a working prototype of Fortnite that is about 3 million lines of Verse code after porting more than 20,000 Blueprints.
Wassmer concluded: "The team is hard at work iterating to validate the resulting performance, memory usage, and architecture. We’re doing this at scale now, and this conversion will help make the process as easy as possible for everyone else."
The latest build of Unreal Engine is scheduled for 2027 and recently entered preview ahead of the Epic AI MegaJam.
UE 5.9 will introduce several UE6 AI tools, including expanded Python scripting, Model Context Protocol (MCP) improvements, browser deployment capabilities, and the shared workspace Unreal Canvas.
