Kangaroos and Jillaroos reach World Cup finals with wins over Samoa and Fiji
The Kangaroos will face New Zealand in the Rugby League World Cup final after overwhelming Samoa 44-12 at Sydney Football Stadium.
Fullback Reece Walsh crossed for a hat-trick and five-eighth Cameron Munster was influential with three try assists as Australia produced by far its most convincing performance of the tournament.
It sets up a rematch of the tournament opener, when the Kangaroos snuck past the Kiwis 24-22 four weeks ago, but Sunday night's win over a poor Samoa side was a comfortably better performance for Kevin Walters's side.
The fireworks started before the game when the teams came face to face during a dynamic siva tau, with winger Brian To'o touching foreheads with Kangaroos prop Tino Fa'asuamaleaui, who is eligible to play for Toa Samoa.
It was one of the moments of the World Cup and ignited the atmosphere at the Sydney Football Stadium, sold out and dominated by Samoa fans.
Samoa drew first blood with a penalty goal, but the next time the crowd favourites troubled the scorers early in the second half they had conceded five tries and were down to 12 men courtesy of prop Spencer Leniu's dissent to referee Adam Gee.
The Kangaroos, who had looked so clunky for the first three matches of their campaign, clicked almost every time they were given chances by an error-riddled and ill-disciplined Samoa.
Tom Dearden's opener, using Walsh as a distraction to allow the halfback to scythe through off a scrumbase play, set up the onslaught as Samoa seemed powerless to stop the surging Australians.
Munster and Walsh wreaked havoc with their footwork and ball playing to lead Australia to a 26-2 advantage at half-time.
Samoa crossed twice in the second half, through Stephen Crichton and Izack Tago, but the comeback attempt never really got off the launchpad as Walsh completed his hat-trick and Jack Bostock dove in late while the game devolved into messy footy and occasional spot fires.
Earlier, the Jillaroos were caught cold early by a red-hot Fijian outfit and trailed 8-0, but ultimately hold off the Bulikula to win 30-14 and set up a fifth straight World Cup final against New Zealand.
The women's and men's finals will be played at Brisbane's Lang Park next Sunday at 4:10pm and 7:35pm AEDT respectively.
