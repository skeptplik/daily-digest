Hollow Knight: Silksong developer Team Cherry debuted a special 'Cherry Time Special Presentation' today (27th November), with the aim of bringing more information to light about the game's first big DLC offering, Sea of Sorrows, but also provide a nice little springboard for some other indie titles.
But first, the reason we're all here: Sea of Sorrow is "an expansion like we've never done before", says Team Cherry, before showing off a trailer for the content - which will be free for all players, in case you've forgetten. The trailer confirms the release date for the DLC, 11th December, and teases some new environments, enemies, bosses and - right at the end - what looks like a fishing mini-game. But of course, this is a nautical-themed expansion, after all.
Team Cherry has hinted at the future of Silksong before. Recently, and more than a year after its initial release, physical versions of Hollow Knight: Silksong finally began arriving on players' doorsteps. These editions seemed to tease previously unconfirmed "free content packs" (note the plural). "The world of Hollow Knight: Silksong will expand with free content packs," the booklet read in the physical editions. "Prepare for even more adventures filled with new friends, new abilities, and new ferocious foes!" We have no hint on what will happen next for Silksong, though, off the back of this presentation.

At the start of the show, the two founders of Team Cherry (that's William Pellen and Ari Gibson) used their platform to show off three cool little indie games from other developers in the scene:
- The first was a fun little title that began as a graphic novel that turned into a narrative adventure game, called Blokkan: Secret of the Walking City.
- The next was a a 2D pixel art side-scrolling game called Radio Rain, which is "all about exploring the ruins and listening to music", and it looks neat. It's supposed to feel like "listening to a radio station and hearing music you haven't heard for years", and is inspired by 80s music.
- The third game comes from an animator/illustrator/voice actor/game developer called The Botanist and the Translator, in which you travel to a distant planet to solve a plant-based mystery. It's all inspired on Sailor Moon, and aims to be "cute, charming, and appealing."
In September last year, Eurogamer gave five stars to Hollow Knight: Silksong, calling it "pretty and charmingly mean-spirited" whilst praising it as a "game filled with revelations and genuine personality". I'm excited to get my hands on more of this sumptuous Metroidvania.
