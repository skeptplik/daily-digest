Ebola cases reach 12,000 in Congo as second-largest outbreak on record grows
Government data says 5,904 people have died in the outbreak so far
The number of confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo now exceeds 12,000, government data showed on Thursday, as responders continue to struggle to bring the second-largest outbreak on record under control.
Confirmed infections total 12,021, and 5,904 people have died, the data showed.
The outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo species of the Ebola virus, officially began in mid-May in the northeastern province Ituri, and has since spread to eight of the country's 26 provinces.
There are no approved treatments or vaccines for this strain of Ebola, complicating efforts to combat the disease.
Delayed detection, armed conflicts in the region and strained health services have also led to rapid spread, with mistrust and misinformation keeping some residents from seeking medical care.
The current epidemic is the second-largest and deadliest on record behind only the 2014-16 West African outbreak, which infected more than 28,600 people and killed more than 11,000. But this outbreak is spreading much faster, having reached 12,000 cases in a fraction of the time, leaving officials worried that it could surpass the West African outbreak if it’s not brought under control soon.
Currently, only 15 per cent of new cases come from contacts known to health authorities, according to data presented by Africa CDC earlier this month. That means many chains of transmission continue to be missed.
A Reuters analysis of satellite imagery found the official death toll may be largely underestimated in two of the country's hardest-hit areas. Fields of new graves that widely outstrip previous years indicates that many deaths connected to the virus may not be included in the official count, the investigation found.

Ebola cases have been detected in five countries, but the only active cases right now are in the D.R.C. Uganda reported 21 cases and three deaths, but has had no new reported cases since June, while Kenya has had nine confirmed Ebola cases and two deaths. Kenya is two weeks into a 42-day countdown to declare the country Ebola-free after discharging its final patient earlier this month.
The spread includes one imported case each for Nigeria and France, but further cases have not been reported in these countries.
