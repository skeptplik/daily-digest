Law Roach’s latest archival pull for Kim Kardashian was for the fashion (and art) history buffs.
In October of 2007, Marc Jacobs sent models down the Louis Vuitton runway dressed as an army of over-the-top 1950s-inspired nurses. As seen on the likes of Naomi Campbell, Stephanie Seymour, Eva Herzigova, and Nadja Auermann, baggy plastic coats were layered over colorful dresses and cinched with leather belts, and black lace masks were embroidered with the house’s logo. Each uniform was styled with white tights and white heels, and topped with traditional nurse’s caps—stitched with red sequin letters that together spelled out “Louis Vuitton.”
The campy Spring/Summer 2008 collection, presented during Paris Fashion Week under the helm of then-artistic director Jacobs, was inspired by Richard Prince’s 2003 “Nurse Paintings” series. The pop artist, who rose to prominence in the 1980s, shared Jacobs’s love for a reference—and for this particular body of work, he transformed the covers of mid-20th century medical pulp novels into eery, gory paintings, with messy paint runs and blurry red splotches that read as blood.
The result was a vibrant commentary on society’s portrayal of women as both pure caregivers and sexualized fetishes—an idea which further came to life on the LV models.
(One such painting was even featured on the cover of a Sonic Youth album, 2004’s Sonic Nurse.)
Nearly two decades later, the seminal collection returned to the fashion discourse thanks to Roach (and Kardashian). The stylist, a dedicated archivist who has pulled rare vintage gems for everyone from Zendaya to Ariana Grande, dressed the All’s Fair actor as a Louis Vuitton nurse.
The Spring/Summer 2008 ensemble was complete with the season’s Papillon Frame bag, made in collaboration with Prince, and the duo even worked with milliner Stephen Jones on a custom “K” headpiece.
