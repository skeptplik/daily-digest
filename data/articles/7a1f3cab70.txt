“There is and will always be only one Veruschka,” Richard Avedon wrote in the May 1972 issue of Vogue in an impassioned tribute to his muse. More than half a century later, his words still ring true. Since news broke of the German supermodel’s death at the age of 87, generations of fashion’s image-makers, designers, and models have been reflecting on her immeasurable influence.
A protagonist of some of the most fantastical fashion editorials ever committed to the pages of Vogue and a prolific cover star, Veruschka helped expand the possibilities of what a model could be. Her body was her artistic medium, her imagination as instrumental to the making of a photograph as that of the person behind the lens. Her creative curiosity would endure long after her heyday in the 1960s and ’70s, finding expression in her experiments with body painting and camouflage, her performance art, and her occasional, much-feted returns to the runway.
For those who knew and worked with Veruschka, though, there was as much to marvel at away from the camera: her generosity of spirit, quiet eccentricities, intellectual curiosity, and her sense of humor. In the tributes shared with Vogue below, fellow models recall their first encounters with a woman whose photographs once adorned their bedroom walls, while designers and photographers reflect on the creative communion they shared with her—and how rare a model like Veruschka is today.
Twiggy
I am so sad to hear the news of Veruschka’s passing. When I started modeling in 1966, she was one of my idols—she was so beautiful and exotic. I had always wanted to meet her, and then in the 1980s, while I was appearing on Broadway in the musical My One and Only, a knock came on my dressing room door after the performance. I opened it and, to my surprise and huge delight, Veruschka was standing there. It was so lovely to finally meet her, and she was absolutely divine, and we chatted for ages. She certainly is one of the greatest of all models. RIP, beautiful lady.
Steven Meisel
Veruschka was an extraordinary artist that I was blessed to know. Thank you, Vera…
Giles Deacon
I remember sitting in the library at Saint Martins, transfixed by the camouflage photographs in Veruschka: Trans-Figurations, the book she made with Holger Trülzsch. Years later, when I had the chance to work with her, I knew I wanted her to close my spring/summer 2011 show. We flew her in from Berlin and made her a bespoke dress for the finale. What I loved about working with her was how open she was to the ideas behind the collection and how wholeheartedly she embraced them. She was 71 and absolutely majestic on the runway. She had a presence unlike anyone else. A creative powerhouse and a true original.
Jonny Johansson, Acne Studios
Being a model is an art form, and she was one of the people who truly mastered it. She had both creativity and respect for what she was doing. When we worked with her, she was incredibly impressive. There was an energy, a grace, and a lasting presence that you don’t see so often today. The lifespan of a model’s career seems to have become so much shorter. I sometimes wonder if, in the way the industry works today, we’re leaving less room for someone to become truly iconic.
Pat Cleveland
When I was a young girl, my bedroom walls were covered in photographs of Veruschka. To me, she looked like she had stepped out of another world—a dream I looked up to, one of the incredible women who had paved the way before me. I used to lie in bed, staring at those pictures, wondering what it would be like actually to know someone like her. Years later, that dream literally knocked on my door.
We ended up living together at our agent Zoli’s house on East 63rd Street. For months, after a long day’s work, we would settle in for the evening with our sketchbooks. I can still see her drawing her little cats—curled up, stretching out, or peeking from the corners of the pages—while I sketched beside her. We didn’t need to say much; the quiet between us always felt so full.
Veruschka was always an enigma to me, a woman with a poetic soul and a nature I could never quite put a name to. She had a wicked little smile and a twinkle in her eye when she’d look up from her drawing, as if she knew a secret about the world she might someday share. Her voice was low and sensuous, and she was always kinder than anyone needed to be.
Sometimes, while watching her sketch, I would think back to that little girl staring at the pictures on her wall. I wished I could tell her that one day she would be sitting right beside her icon, and that Veruschka would be her friend. I have always loved Veruschka, and I always will. Knowing she taught me that dreams really do come true. She was mine.
