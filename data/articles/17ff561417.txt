The Azzedine Alaïa Foundation has nearly doubled its exhibition space in Paris, having transformed a historic boutique within the same complex of buildings as a museum and cultural space.
Galerie Moussy, named after the boutique address at No. 7, opened Saturday with an inaugural exhibition of sketches by French artist and illustrator Aurore de la Morinerie, displayed next to the dresses that inspired them.
It runs through Jan. 3, 2027, and can be seen concurrently with the “Azzedine Alaïa and Africa” exhibition currently on in the main, glass-roofed hall that sometimes hosted fashion shows by the late founder.
Carla Sozzani, president of the foundation, told WWD the new 3,300-square-foot Moussy space, still illuminated by the futuristic Marc Newson ring lights that Alaïa bought in 2003, “gives us many more possibilities for talks, conferences, student events, or dinners if we want to raise funds.”
Many original features were kept intact, including hulking bronze fixtures by artist Julian Schnabel which once sheltered jewelry and leather goods, and now displays drawings and documentation.
What was once an expansive fitting room now displays furniture designs by Gaetano Pesce and others, plus a massive, broken-plate portrait of Alaïa by Schnabel.
De la Morinerie originally made the sketches, realized via monotype printing, for the 2013 Azzedine Alaïa retrospective at the Palais Galliera, and has donated them to the Azzedine Alaïa Foundation.
Unlike most designers, Alaïa never sketched.
“Azzedine was working on the model, making the patterns, and the drawings were made by the studio after,” Sozzani explained. “It was sculpting…. There was always a model sitting next to him all day long, weekends also.”
A good number of the dresses on display at the Galerie Moussy are black or white, some with metallic embroideries, heightening their sculptural allure.
Among guests trickling through the exhibition preview on Saturday were former Chanel creative director Virginie Viard, photographers Koto Bolofo and Sarah Moon, plus art historian Fabrice Hergott, director of the Museum of Modern Art in Paris.
If only the walls could talk. The founder lived and worked under the same roof from 1987 until his death in 2017 at age 83. The complex of buildings united his ateliers and studios, private apartments and a kitchen, where he loved to entertain, plus a boutique, which operated from 1991 until it went dark over the summer.
Founded in 2007, and initially called the Association Azzedine Alaïa, the Azzedine Alaïa Foundation safeguards and exhibits the work of the late Tunisian couturier and the works of art and fashion from his personal collection.
Located at 18 Rue de la Verrerie in the Marais district, the foundation also comprises a bookshop and a café restaurant.
