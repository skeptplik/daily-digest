LONDON — Menswear is moving upmarket, driven by sales in the Europe, the Middle East and Africa region, higher price points and the growing popularity of outerwear, according to the latest data from Joor, a digital wholesale platform for fashion.
Citing proprietary transaction data over the past three years, Joor said the menswear category, which includes apparel, footwear and accessories, has been on an upward trajectory, with total menswear gross merchandise value rising 11 percent in 2026 compared with 2024.
The New York-based company said that while volume has remained relatively consistent, the composition of orders has shifted. The number of units bought by retailers decreased in 2026 while average price points increased toward the $250 to $1,000 retail price bracket.
Globally, the number of units purchased by retailers with a retail price under $250 decreased 11 percent, while the number of units ordered in the $250 to $1,000 price bracket increased 2 percent.
The under-$250 price bracket has reduced in share from 67 percent to 64 percent of all units sold, whereas the $250 to $1,000 price bracket has increased in share from 30 percent to 33 percent.
Sales of outerwear, which commands the highest average price in menswear, are also on the upswing, with the number of units sold rising 16 percent in 2026 compared with 2024.
Joor said 62 percent of outerwear units sold had a suggested retail price of more than $500, with only 11 percent in the sub-$250 price segment. By contrast, men’s apparel and accessories were mostly in the under-$250 retail price segment, making up 69 percent and 88 percent of their share of units, respectively.
The company said that EMEA is the dominant region in the global menswear market, accounting for 61 percent of total global units purchased. That is significantly more than Asia-Pacific and North America combined, according to Joor.
EMEA-based retailers had the lowest apparel penetration at 82 percent. At the same time, they placed a “significant investment” in footwear, accessories and outerwear.
By contrast, North America was dominated by apparel, at 86 percent, and had the highest footwear penetration at 9 percent of total units. APAC had the highest penetration of accessories at 7 percent of total units.
Joor said the data came from more than 23,000 unique retailers placing orders for menswear on its wholesale platform in 2026. Brands on the platform include Axel Arigato, Berluti, Boggi, Dunhill, Emporio Armani, Golden Goose and Thom Browne.
Amanda McCormick Bacal, global head of marketing and fashion director at Joor, said the data highlights “a consistent global shift toward quality over quantity, with retailers buying fewer units at higher price points — most notably in high-ticket categories like outerwear.”
Joor has also unveiled its first men’s retailer showcase, Just His Style: The World’s Top Menswear Retailers, which it describes as a “valuable resource” for brands looking to do business internationally.
It lists 140 stockists of men’s clothing, footwear and accessories, including Harry Rosen in Toronto; B1ock in Hangzhou; Amicis Men in Vienna; and Essx in New York.
Joor describes itself as the leading wholesale management ecosystem for fashion with nearly $20 billion in transactions processed annually. Its tech provides an end-to-end wholesale solution for brands and retailers.
