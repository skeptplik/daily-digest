The US Defence Secretary met Delcy Rodríguez in Caracas ahead of a planned visit to Colombia, where he is due to discuss bilateral security cooperation.
US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth announced a military cooperation agreement with Venezuela on Wednesday, state TV reported, during an unexpected visit to Caracas.
Hegseth was received by Venezuela's interim leader, Delcy Rodríguez, in the capital ahead of a planned visit to Colombia later Wednesday.
"We see this as a renewal of our defence partnership and an opportunity for our two great armed forces to work together," Hegseth said, according to footage broadcast by VTV, Venezuela's state television channel.
The trip, which had not been publicly announced by the Pentagon, marks another step in the rapprochement between Washington and Caracas following the capture in January of Nicolás Maduro.
What does the agreement include?
The cooperation agreement announced by Hegseth and Rodríguez provides for the resumption of technical and military cooperation between Washington and Caracas, which had been suspended for years amid deteriorating diplomatic relations between the two countries.
According to the Venezuelan leader, one of the main objectives is to strengthen the training of Venezuelan armed forces personnel through joint training and cooperation programmes. Rodríguez said the initiative would help improve the military's preparedness to respond to shared threats.
The agreement also aims to improve the operational equipment available to Venezuelan forces and provide access to new technologies. So far, neither government has provided details on the timeline for implementing the agreement, the resources to be allocated or the specific mechanisms through which cooperation will take place.
Washington and Caracas strengthen ties
Rodríguez took power after Maduro was captured, although her continued leadership of the country remains shrouded in uncertainty. Her interim mandate expired in July and there is still no announced date for the next presidential election.
The rapprochement between Washington and Caracas has also been marked by agreements over Venezuelan oil and by the United States' strategy against drug trafficking in the Caribbean and the Pacific, which has caused the deaths of at least 240 people since September 2025 in attacks on vessels suspected of carrying drugs.
Hegseth's visit is part of efforts by Donald Trump's administration to expand its influence in Venezuela. The defence secretary is the third member of Trump's cabinet to travel to the country this year, following trips by the interior and energy secretaries, focused on Venezuela's oil and mineral resources.
