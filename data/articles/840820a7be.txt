Makeup artist superstar Dame Pat McGrath — who speaks in capital letters and exclamation marks and creates looks that are larger than life — lives, works and breathes in the superlative.
“Major” is one of her favorite words — a fitting vocabulary choice for a woman who has created some of the most memorable looks and products in the beauty industry over the last three decades. Crystal-encrusted lips, glass skin, feather lashes, the list goes on. Whether working with top designers on their fashion shows, leading photographers like Steven Meisel and A-list artists like Taylor Swift (who fittingly tapped McGrath to play the queen in her Cinderella-inspired short film for “Bejeweled”), McGrath is a bottomless well of inspiration, ideas and innovations.
She’s also a virtuoso product developer as well, who has worked with everyone from CoverGirl to Gucci over the course of her career and most recently ideated Louis Vuitton’s entry into makeup. There have been stumbles. In early 2026, Pat McGrath Labs filed for bankruptcy and was acquired by GDA Luma in April. But McGrath isn’t one to look back — instead, she’s advanced with her trademark mix of momentum and creativity, unveiling a collaboration with Pandora, new products for Vuitton and starring in Asos’s fall campaign with stylist Law Roach.
Here, McGrath reflects on her legendary (another favorite word) career and hints at what’s next.
What does impact mean to you?
Impact is about making an indelible mark, about making a difference in a meaningful way. As a creative, it’s not just about making something beautiful or original or trying to be disruptive just to be disruptive. To have an impact, creativity has to be purposeful, pushing boundaries to push things forward, affect change, spark conversation. The work needs to resonate, motivate, inspire, live beyond the moment to be impactful. Or really, it’s just another pretty picture.
I’ve spent my career asking what beauty is capable of, not what it’s supposed to look like, but what it can be. I’ve always looked at what it can be, what it can say, how it can look, how a look can make someone feel. When an idea that began backstage finds its way onto the face of someone experimenting and playing with makeup at home, that’s incredibly meaningful to me.
As you assess your career, how do you think about your impact — on style, on product creation, on the beauty business?
I’ve collaborated with some of the smartest and most creative teams on planet Earth with the best houses, designers, photographers, stylists, editors, brands. I’ve been very spoiled, and I feel very blessed. But over the past three decades, it’s been so much fun and beyond inspiring to create looks, images and products that will be remembered, referenced and revered for years to come. I’m incredibly proud to have that work as a tangible legacy.
What excites me is to bring the color, textures and techniques we create backstage to people everywhere, the extraordinary pigments, multidimensional finishes, the freedom to experiment. What means the most is seeing creativity travel into the hands of new generation, each interpreting it their own way.
How do you nurture your creativity? What’s your creative process like?
I’ve always had an unconventional perspective on beauty, a fearless approach to makeup and I embrace the spirit of experimentation. That’s the core of my creative process. I’m driven by the idea of being able to change how people think about makeup and challenging the status quo. Whenever I’m standing in front of a face, I’m looking for what hasn’t existed before, what hasn’t been seen yet, and that’s what drives me, looking for new.
I’m endlessly curious. For any creative, curiosity is the most important thing. Also, I have a tremendous archive of my ideas and inspiration, a ridiculously large library of photography, fashion, cinema, dance, ancient art, modern art, so much art. All of it feeds my creativity.
And I’ve worked closely with some of the best labs throughout my life, pushing for formulas that don’t exist, but also ones that I needed to elevate my artistry.
Early on, I would say much of my work was about creating an effect with whatever materials we could find, mixing pigments, playing with finishes, changing proportions, taking something beautiful and pushing it until it takes on a life of its own.
The experimentation is my favorite part, and the moments when something completely unexpected happens is where creativity lives. My creative process never stops evolving because it’s constantly fed by new voices, new visions, new partners, culture. What’s happening in music? What’s happening in film, art, the streets? I’m very obsessed with what’s around me.
You’re famous for so many different looks — what are your favorites?
Oh my god! It’s so hard because every look and moment has a special hold on you. It’s impossible to choose, but some that come to mind are the glass skin moment at Margiela, the bold, winged eyes that I’ve done with Steven Meisel on many editorials, and sparkling jewel lips with Galliano and glitter lips with Versace. The jewel-encrusted faces at Givenchy, feathered lashes at Valentino, double-sculpted lashes at Louis Vuitton. I remember we did this kind of manga anime lashes on the top and bottom, and I also loved playing with that bold take on the ’80s with Nicolas Ghesquière. I also remember the golden lips at Prada and the otherworldly skin at Schiaparelli. The extravagance of feathers, sequins, jewels, accouterments and everything that we did with Dior and Galliano and Comme des Garçons and Yohji Yamamoto — every moment means something.
I also really love all of the images that we created with Steven Meisel. And creating Louis Vuitton Beauty, making products that are timeless, that will last forever. Working with the genius team at LV has been such an honor. Being able to create my trunk — it’s a lifelong dream! As someone who’s known for traveling with 85 suitcases, that’s something I’ve always wanted to do.
Earlier this year you decoupled from Pat McGrath Labs. How did that feel?
I’ve spent my life creating products and brands, and it’s what I love to do. I’ve been honored to help create some of the most incredible products in the market today for some of the most iconic brands, from Giorgio Armani to Dolce & Gabbana to Gucci to Labs and now with Louis Vuitton. For me, I’m just excited for what’s next.
This fall you starred in Asos’ fall campaign and launched a jewelry collection with Pandora. What’s next?
Those were a lot of fun. Both collabs felt very natural for me. Makeup is my first language — color, texture, light, transformation — but in my mind I have spent so many years turning lips into jewels and covering faces in crystals, it was great with Pandora to use that inspiration to create objects you can wear.
With Asos it was a different way of bringing my creative world into fashion. It was so different to everything that I’m normally used to. I’m a very curious person, very energized, and there is so much more for the future that I’m excited about.
