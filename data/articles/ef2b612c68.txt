The Scientific Crown
Red-light therapy didn’t start as a treatment for antiaging and recovery. It actually came about as an accidental solution for hair loss in the 1960s, when Hungarian scientist Endre Mester was researching whether exposure to red light could treat cancer in hairless mice. Imagine his surprise when the mice started growing hair.
Red-light therapy has come a long way since then. According to Rahman, red-light wavelengths are thought to activate the mitochondria in skin cells, which may encourage an uptick in the production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) to promote new growth. Red-light therapy may also improve blood flow to hair follicles.
