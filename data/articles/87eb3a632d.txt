52% of Hong Kong’s underprivileged children spend over 2 hours a day on devices
Survey finds low-income children’s screen time far exceeds government guidelines, with many citing devices as cheap entertainment
More than half of Hong Kong’s underprivileged children spend over two hours a day on electronic devices, often turning to screens for cheap entertainment despite problems with their eyesight, attention, sleep and addiction, a survey has found.
The Society for Community Organisation (SoCO), which released the findings on Sunday, urged authorities to keep students on campus for longer, introduce full-day kindergartens and subsidise more extracurricular activities for underprivileged pupils.
Wong Chi-yuen, assistant director of SoCO, said 52 per cent of the children surveyed spent more than two hours a day on devices, well above the government’s screen-time guideline for children aged six to 12.
