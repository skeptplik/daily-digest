A man has been charged with attempted murder after a local councillor was stabbed while working in the community in Bradford, police have said.
Arfan Ali, 32, is accused of attacking Ibrar Ali, 52, at about 11am on Thursday in the West Yorkshire city.
The alleged attacker has also been charged with assault and possession of an offensive weapon in a public place, West Yorkshire police said.
He appeared at Leeds magistrates court on Saturday, the Crown Prosecution Service confirmed. He was reportedly remanded in custody ahead of his next appearance, at Bradford crown court on 21 December.
A statement on behalf of Ibrar Ali and his family, shared on Friday, said he had been supporting council wardens with community clean-up activities.
They said: “During the incident, councillor Ibrar intervened to protect a member of council staff and, in doing so, sustained multiple stab wounds.”
The family said the councillor’s injuries were serious but not life-threatening, and that he was in a stable condition in hospital, adding that he was “grateful for the overwhelming kindness shown and hopes to personally thank everyone once he has recovered”.
Officers were called just after 11am on Thursday to reports the councillor had been found seriously injured on Manse Street.
He is believed to have been injured first in Thornbury Avenue before being driven to Manse Street, according to the force.
Emergency services attended and took him to hospital.
