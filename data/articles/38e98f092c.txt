One person was taken to hospital and more than 50 were assessed by medics after a substance was let off in the crowd at Manchester’s Co-op Live arena, officials have said.
Police declared a major incident after the release of the substance, which was initially thought to be teargas, as they sought to protect the 12,000 people in the crowd at a Don Toliver concert.
Footage shared on social media appeared to show concertgoers coughing and covering their mouths as they left the venue, and medical officials urged anyone who had become unwell after leaving to get in touch.
Some of the dozens assessed by doctors reported eye and respiratory symptoms, the North West ambulance service said. “Decontamination is being carried out as a precaution and one patient has been taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment,” the service said. “We deployed several emergency crews, including our hazardous area response team, to support the safe assessment and treatment of people at the scene.”
Greater Manchester police said the major incident was declared shortly after 10pm. Shortly after 2am on Monday, the force said concertgoers had been safely evacuated, with several people being assessed onsite for side-effects.
The assistant chief constable Steph Parker said: “Our priority was to ensure the safety of every person that was attending the concert. Several people were treated onsite, and one has been taken to hospital at this time. Anyone who has left the event and since become unwell is advised to attend their local hospital.
“We understand the concern that this incident will have raised – particularly those with loved ones at the venue last night – and we’d like to thank everyone for their patience as we dealt with it. There were over 12,000 people in attendance during this concert who were safely escorted from the arena so they could make their way home.”
She added: “Our declaration of a major incident has allowed us to ensure emergency services and partner agencies can work together quickly and effectively to coordinate a safe response. We will now begin to fully understand what this substance was and how this incident came about.”
Co-op Live said: “Following an incident in the crowd at last night’s show, we are working closely with Greater Manchester police to identify the cause and action appropriately.”
Toliver, a US rapper whose real name is Caleb Zackery Toliver, was performing in Manchester as part of his Nitrous tour. He is scheduled to perform at London’s O2 Arena on Monday and OVO Arena Wembley on Tuesday. Co-op Live is scheduled to host the US singer Phoebe Bridgers on Thursday.
