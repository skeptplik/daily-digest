Authorities are investigating why a small plane carrying six members of a Florida family crashed off the Mississippi coast on Tuesday (November 24, 2026). They were returning home from a fishing trip, relatives say.
The plane’s pilot was Brent Stokes, the 50-year-old president of a marine contracting company based in Fort Myers, according to Jackson County Coroner Bruce Lynd Jr. Stokes’ company’s website described him as an “avid fisherman and lifetime boater.”
The other victims were Stokes’ 16-year-old son, Hunter Stokes; Stokes’ sister, Arlyn Knox, 43; her husband, Michael Knox, 43; and their two sons, 12-year-old Charles Holden Knox and 10-year-old Truett Knox.
Arlyn and Michael Knox ran Knox Technologies, a Fort Myers-based IT company they founded in 2012, according to the company's website.
The Knox and Stokes families confirmed their deaths in an emailed statement provided to The Associated Press.
“They died doing what they loved: celebrating the start of a wonderful holiday season with a fishing trip together before heading home to be with the rest of our family,” the statement said. “In the middle of our grief, we are holding on to that and to how much love they gave us and how much love surrounds us now. We will carry them with us always.”
Lynd said that autopsies are being performed on Brent Stokes and Michael Knox, who was also a licensed pilot.
The air traffic control regional tower in Mobile, Alabama, reported at about 3:45 p.m. that it had lost contact with the aircraft, according to the Coast Guard. The closest National Weather Service station, in Pascagoula, shows there were light winds, no precipitation and clouds blanketing the sky in the area at the time of the crash.
The pilot doesn’t appear to have reported any problems when the plane took off from an airport in New Orleans or made a distress call during the flight, according to air traffic recordings publicly available on the website LiveATC.net.
Shortly before losing contact with the plane, an air traffic controller at Gulfport–Biloxi International Airport advised the pilot of precipitation and adverse weather in the area.
Todd Curtis, an aviation expert and former Boeing safety engineer, said that nothing appeared out of the ordinary based on the publicly available audio recordings. But he noted that precipitation below freezing temperatures can cause dangerous icing, potentially leading to inaccurate instrument readings or loss of lift. It is unclear whether the plane had a de-icing system.
The plane had been flying at around 9,000 feet (2,743 metres), above the bottom of the cloud layers, when it began descending rapidly in a right-hand turn. That’s according to FlightAware tracking data analysed by Steven Arroyo, a retired airline captain with 50 years of flying experience.
“It’s highly, highly irregular,” Arroyo said. “I think it’s safe to say this aircraft had some serious problems.”
Responders searched for hours and initially only found three life jackets, a life raft and the plane’s cabin floating on the water, according to Coast Guard spokesperson Juan Ros.
Divers found their bodies in the fuselage after the Cessna 210 went down in the Gulf of Mexico between Pascagoula, Mississippi, and Horn Island, which is part of the Gulf Islands National Seashore.
The Cessna was en route from New Orleans to Fort Myers, Florida, according to FlightAware.
Both Stokes and Knox had received private pilot certificates in 2023, according to the Federal Aviation Administration.
The Page Field airport in Fort Myers said in a statement that its community is “shocked and devastated.”
“We have lost great friends and outstanding aviators,” the airport added.
Cmdr. Trevor Blount, the Coast Guard coordinator for search and rescue in the Mobile area, called the situation heartbreaking.
“While this is not the outcome any of us had hoped for, we are grateful for the strong interagency coordination that enabled us to swiftly locate those aboard the aircraft and provide closure to their families,” he said in a statement.
The FAA and the National Transportation Safety Board will investigate the crash.
Earlier this month, a Learjet 60 business jet nosedived into the ground in South Florida, killing all nine people on board. People inside their cars captured the crash on video.
