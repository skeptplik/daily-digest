The International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) said Wednesday that it will appeal the Court of Arbitration for Sport’s (CAS) ruling to reinstate Russia in next year’s world championship to the Swiss Federal Tribunal.
Russia had been banned from IIHF competitions since the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.
Last month, CAS upheld appeals lodged by the Russian Ice Hockey Federation over its exclusion from IIHF competitions for the 2026-27 season, arguing that the IIHF had not provided sufficient evidence to justify its assessment that security concerns required Russia’s continued exclusion.
The IIHF said it filed a challenge to the CAS ruling due to alleged ties some of its panel members have with Russia.
“The decision is based on concerns around the independence and impartiality of two of the three arbitrators on the CAS Panel,” an IIHF statement read.
“Especially following the information that has recently come to light regarding potential links between the two arbitrators and Russia,” it added.
“Given the significance of the ruling and its implications for international ice hockey, the IIHF considers it definitively essential to pursue the matter before the Swiss Federal Tribunal,” the statement read.
Although CAS rulings are considered final, sporting bodies can appeal to the Swiss Federal Tribunal on valid legal grounds.
CAS has acknowledged the divisive reaction to its ruling, but reiterated that the decision was based solely on the evidence submitted.
Teams from Austria, the Czech Republic, Denmark, Finland, Latvia, Norway and Sweden have said they would refuse to play against Russia. One major hockey sponsor, AJ Products, has withdrawn, while others have said they could do so if Russia takes part.
Russia will not compete in next month’s 2027 World Junior Championship after Canada said it would deny entry to Russian and Belarusian athletes coming to compete, even those with valid visas.
The Russian Ice Hockey Federation called the IIHF’s decision to keep the tournament in Canada "unacceptable and unlawful.”
Russian athletes will also not be allowed into the Czech Republic, which is hosting the women’s Under-18 ice hockey world championship in January.
