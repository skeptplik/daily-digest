India celebrates Constitution Day
India on Thursday marked 77 years since the formal adoption of its Constitution on November 26, 1949.
Prime Minister Narendra Modi, who officially declared November 26 as Constitution Day or Samvidhan Divas in 2015, hailed the Constitution as "the priceless heritage of Indian democracy."
Before 2015, the date was observed as Law Day.
"Greetings on Constitution Day, a day to pay homage to the makers of our Constitution and an occasion to reaffirm our commitment to strengthening the values enshrined in it," he wrote on X.
Modi also used the observance to attack the opposition.
In an article published on his website, the prime minister wrote that the constitutional democracy had a "clearly defined role for the opposition."
He deemed the opposition as becoming "more of an obstructionist."
Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi called India's Constitution "the foundation" of Indian democracy and the "greatest weapon against injustice."
"It is the shield of every citizen's dignity, the strength of every voice - the guardian of justice, liberty, equality and fraternity." Gandhi said — echoing the four core values enshrined in the Preamble to the Constitution of India.
India's Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution two years after the country gained independence from British colonial rule.
It came into force two months later on January 26, 1950, establishing India as a Republic.
The most prominent leader of India's Dalit Community, Dr B R Ambedkar — also referred to as Babasaheb — was the chief architect of the Indian Constitution.
CJP to hold protest on Constitution Day
This year, "Constitution" has been the political buzzword in India. Copies of 'The Constitution of India' were a dominant protest prop in a flurry of demonstrations against the Modi government that gripped the country, first over educational reforms and then over alleged voter roll manipulation.
Gandhi too has been seen holding up India's Constitution book several times in protests and press conferences in recent months.
Meanwhile, India's youth-led "Cockroach" movement which has mounted one of the biggest public challenges to Modi in his three terms in power, is planning to hold a "Save Democracy, Save Constitution" rally in the central state of Maharashtra's Nagpur city later in the evening.
In a press conference in Nagpur on Wednesday, the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP's) founder Abhijeet Dipke accused the Modi government of attempting to "dilute" the Indian Constitution.
Dipke went to say that "even 100 dictators like Modi cannot destroy Babasaheb's Constitution and its values."
India seeks 'early conclusion' of trade deal with US
In its statement on planned new US tariffs, India's commerce ministry urged the US to resolve trade disputes and complete negotiations over a new trade deal.
The ministry said that it was "committed to working closely" with the US towards an "early conclusion" of the trade deal.
The US proposed a tariff rate of 25% on goods from India, reduced to 17% if a trade deal is reached.
The state of India-US trade deal negotiations
US President Donald Trump and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi first announced talks for the US-India Bilateral Trade Agreement (BTA) last year.
But negotiations were delayed over strained ties and global conflict, reestablishing headway only this year.
The two nations released the framework for an interim trade agreement in February. As part of that deal, India would "eliminate or reduce tariffs" on all US industrial products and a range of US food and agricultural goods.
However, the negotiations for a final deal were once again stalled after the US Supreme Court invalidated much of Trump's sweeping global tariffs.
In October, India's Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman described the talks as having reached "a plateau, beyond which giving or taking might be very, very difficult."
But US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said a few days later that New Delhi and Washington were "about asclose as you can be without having an executed agreement."
India rejects US charge of excess capacity
Facing a new United States tariff threat, India has stressed that any trade concerns be addressed within the framework of the ongoing trade deal talks between the two countries.
New Delhi's statement comes after Washington on Wednesday unveiled a new wave of tariffs on 14 of its major trade partners, including India, claiming that the economies were promoting excess industrial capacity.
"India and the United States are engaged in negotiations for a Bilateral Trade Agreement. It has been India's position that any trade-related concerns should be addressed within the framework of these negotiations," a statement from New Delhi's Ministry of Commerce and Industry said late Wednesday.
The Office of the US Trade Representative (USTR) announced new import duties of 20% to 40% to counter the alleged excessive manufacturing abroad as it completed a monthslong probe into alleged unfair trade practices involving 16 trading partners.
The new tariffs will be subject to a period of public comment before taking effect.
"Today, after lengthy investigations, Ambassador Greer has determined that the acts, policies, and practices of various economies designed to artificially support manufacturing exports are unreasonable and burden or restrict US commerce and has proposed responsive action," the USTR said on Wednesday.
The USTR said that it will reduce the tariff rate for Indian imports to 17% if a trade deal is sealed between New Delhi and Washington in the long-drawn-out talks trade negotiations between the two sides.
India said that it had "engaged constructively" throughout USTR's investigation and said that it did not have "structural excess capacity in any sector."
