Multinational force to hold first excersise in Poland
A force of about 2,800 troops from 10 European countries will hold the first exercise of a new multinational force in support of Ukraine, the UK and France said.
The force is only to be deployed in the case of a ceasefire that would end the war between Kyiv and Moscow, with its aim being supporting Ukraine's army in securing the country.
"The exercise will allow the testing of the speed and efficiency at which several nations can deploy forces on land, sea and air to support Ukraine once hostilities have finished," a joint statement by the two countries reads.
With Russia not showing any intention of agreeing peace, the move could further antagonize Moscow.
The exercise will take place between November 9 and 22 in Poland, not far from the country's Ukraine border.
Russian, Ukrainian representatives to meet in Abu Dhabi — Witkoff
A trilateral meeting between officials from the US, Russia and Ukraine will take place in Abu Dhabi next week, US President Donald Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff said.
According to Witkoff, the US will mediate "working-level" negotiations between Russia and Ukraine on November 10 and 11, in a bid of "advancing President Trump's mission to end the war."
"Discussions will focus on post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian efforts, and next steps toward peace," Witkoff posted on his X account, adding that meetings will also take place with representatives from NATO, Germany, the UK and the EU.
The goal, Witkoff said, is a "comprehensive, durable peace agreement that delivers lasting security for generations to come."
Meanwhile, a senior US official told Reuters that Witkoff and Jared Kushner, the lead U.S. negotiators, are expected to represent the US in Abu Dhabi.
Zaporizhzhia: At least 26 killed in Russian strikes on aid center
At least 26 people were killed and five more injured on Monday when the Russian air force launched repeated strikes on a school which was functioning as an aid distribution center in the southern Ukrainian region of Zaporizhzhia.
The multiple attacks, in which Russian planes reportedly launched more than 10 glide bombs, took place in the village of Komyshuvakha, about 35 kilometers (31 miles) east of the city of Zaporizhzhia and a dozen kilometers away from the frontline.
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the attacks as "utterly horrific" and accused Russia of being well aware of the humanitarian purpose of the site.
"After the first aerial bomb strike, there was a double-tap strike with nine more bombs, specifically targeting the shelter where people were taking cover," he wrote on social media.
"The Russians could not have been unaware that every week, local residents who had remained in Komyshuvakha received humanitarian aid at that very location."
Ukrainian emergency services said two members of a rescue crew were among the dead after falling victim to the so-called "double-tap" strike in which a location is targeted again with the aim of hitting first responders.
The attacks came less than 24 hours after a localized six-day humanitarian ceasefire in and around the town of Oleshky in the neighboring region of Kherson had expired.
Zelenskyy said the temporary truce could potentially have led to further steps toward a possible deal to end the ongoing Russian invasion, but said after Monday's strikes:
"This is yet further proof that Russia does not want any kind of de-escalation or any real justice... With these killings, Russia has sent a completely opposite signal; Russia is counting on further killings."
The Zaporizhzhia region has been targeted repeatedly for months by heavy Russian airstrikes.
Ukraine says Russia used ceasefire to rearm, rotate troops, Kremlin says 'it's normal practice'
Ukraine's General Staff on Monday blasted Russia's actions during a six-day humanitarian ceasefire in Oleshky, saying the Kremlin had used the break in fighting to rearm its forces and rotate troops in the front-line area.
In a social media post on X, the General Staff wrote: "Defense Forces of Ukraine ensured that the ceasefire was observed and acted exclusively within the framework of the agreements reached," adding, "The enemy, by contrast, used the humanitarian pause to improve its positions."
Residents in the Kherson region were warned to be vigilant and told to pay attention to air raid sirens, noting that an intensification of attacks was to be expected after Russia used the brief pause to "strengthen its capabilities."
In Moscow, the Kremlin brushed off the critique, saying Ukraine, "could have used the ceasefire to re-group as well," calling such activity, "normal practice."
Kyiv region sees emergency power outages
Ukraine energy provider DTEK on Monday said that emergency power cuts had been initiated in Kyiv and the surrounding region at the request of national grid operator Ukrenergo.
The outages follow Russian attacks in central Ukraine that left over 200,000 Ukrainians in the area without power over the weekend.
DTEK had previously announced that, "equipment in the region has been seriously damaged due to enemy attacks," adding that energy workers were trying to "restore power to critical infrastructure facilities."
The attacks follow the Kremlin's playbook of seeking to destroy Ukraine's energy infrastructure, especially in cold months, in an attempt to break the will of Ukraine's citizens defending themselves against Russia's invasion by "weaponizing winter."
Ukraine says it evacuated more than 150 from Kherson region before ceasefire expired
Ukraine's military has said that about 170 people were evacuated from the Kherson region (news agencies cite numbers ranging from 167 to 180) before a six-day ceasefire in the Russian-occupied Ukrainian city of Oleshky expired at midnight (Moscow time, 2100 GMT) on Sunday.
No resumption of hostilities was immediately reported after what was the longest pause in fighting since Russian forces invaded in 2022.
Kherson's regional governor on Sunday urged residents remaining behind to avoid "red zones" where he said the threat of Russian strikes "remains high, and the threat to life is extremely high."
Kherson has been subjected to drones attacks, shelling, glide bombs and airdropped mines for nearly five years.
In all, nearly 800 people were reportedly evacuated from the region during the temporary ceasefire, with residents shuttled to Russian-annexed Crimea, then to the Russia town of Anapa, before being flown to Belarus and then Ukraine.
Among those evacuated were 22 children and 21 individuals requiring mobility assistance said Ukraine's army.
US First Lady Melania Trump and Pope Leo XIV — both of whom were reportedly involved in securing the pause in fighting — praised cooperation between the sides, voicing hope that such work might lay the groundwork for a broader peace.
Before Russia launched its illegal invasion on February 24, 2022, and took control of 70% of Kherson, the area was home to more than one million people. Now it is estimated that about 140,000 remain.
Russia hits military facility, cargo vessel, in Ukraine's Odesa
Russia's forces struck a military facility in Ukraine's Odesa region, the Russian Defense Ministry said on Telegram on Monday.
The statement added that a cargo vessel, which the ministry claimed was used for military purposes, was also hit 58 kilometers (36  miles) south of the Black Sea port city of Odesa.
Separately, news agency Reuters cited an official in Odesa as saying that four people had been injured in a Russian attack.
US reportedly pushing to restore Russian gas sales to Europe via Ukraine
The United States is backing a quick resumption of Russian natural gas exports to Europe via pipelines crossing Ukraine as part of talks aimed at securing an energy ceasefire before winter, the Wall Street Journal reported on Sunday, citing US, European and Ukrainian officials.
The proposal involves selling Russian gas via a US intermediary to circumvent European sanctions, the report said, adding that it was mooted by US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff during trips to Moscow and Kyiv in September.
DW could not immediately verify the report.
Russia's share in European gas supply has declined to about 11% now from 45% on the eve of Moscow's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.
The European Union currently aims to completely phase out Russian gas imports by the end of 2027. And EU leaders have said they don't want to go back to relying on Russia for energy supplies.
Zelenskyy says Ukraine will stop refinery strikes if US sends Patriot missiles
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said that his country would stop hitting Russian oil refineries if Washington provided Kyiv with powerful Patriot interceptor missiles.
Speaking to US broadcaster Fox News, Zelenskyy said he had had "different and several" conversations with Trump and his team on the issue.
"We asked that we can't not respond after Russia's attack on our energy, after Russia is killing our people, attacks on our electricity, gas, refineries, on everything," he said.
"We said that if you want us not to attack Russia's refineries, they have not to attack our energy system. Another alternative, give us Patriot missiles, we will find money, not just aid," Zelenskyy added.
The Ukrainian leader said if Washington provided the missile systems for one month Kyiv would cease attacks on Russian refineries for the same period.
Asked if he was concerned that this would be seen as trying to "leverage" Trump, Zelenskyy said: "I don't have any concerns when I share the truth between partners."
Ukrainian authorities have been warning for weeks that their supplies of the Patriot interceptor missiles were dwindling.
Ukraine, Russia report separate hacking attacks
Emergency alert apps in both Ukraine and Russia have been hacked, officials said.
Ukraine's air force said an air raid alert app was hacked late on Sunday, with fake alerts being sent to smartphones, warning people of nuclear and chemical threats.
SPRAVDI, Ukraine's Center for Strategic Communications, blamed Russia for the hack in a post on its X account.
Hours later, Moscow said the mobile app of the Russian Emergencies Ministry was hit by "a massive cyberattack."
"Attention! The mobile application of the Ministry of Emergencies of Russia has been subjected to a massive cyberattack, enabling attackers to send false information to the public," the Emergencies Ministry posted on Telegram.
It said authorities were working to fix the issue.
