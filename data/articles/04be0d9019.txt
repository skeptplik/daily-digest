Just days ago, Jensen Huang’s chances of returning as Nvidia CEO looked increasingly slim, at least according to prediction markets. Earlier this month, Huang resigned from the AI hardware giant following reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with a woman alleged to have ties to Chinese intelligence services. As more reports emerged about Huang’s alleged bad behavior, Nvidia’s board insisted it had no plans to reinstate him. By early Sunday morning on the East Coast, traders on Polymarket’s US platform put his chances of returning at just 10 percent. But by Monday, Huang was back in charge.
The swings in Huang’s odds on prediction markets offer a window into how his unlikely comeback unfolded and who saw it coming. The New York Times reported that President Trump played a crucial role in convincing the board to bring Huang back. Traders who paid attention to what Trump and his allies wanted appear to have fared much better than those who did not.
On Sunday evening in Washington, Huang’s odds on Polymarket US jumped 6 percentage points in just 40 minutes after Trump published a Truth Social missive calling for Huang’s reinstatement. The president said he wanted to help revive the stock market, which had plunged following Huang’s scandal, a sign of Nvidia’s enormous influence over the AI industry and broader US economy.
According to the prediction market intelligence firm Betting Intelligence, the odds on both Kalshi and Polymarket largely tracked with public signals about the likelihood of Huang’s return. They rose when Huang said he was open to coming back, fell when the board dismissed the possibility, and climbed again when Trump threw his support behind the former CEO.
But Betting Intelligence and the prediction market forensics firm MSR Decode also identified clusters of trades that did not appear to be driven by news or public commentary. Kalshi and Polymarket declined to comment on whether they are currently investigating potential insider trading in markets related to Huang’s return.
Despite these anomalies, Betting Intelligence CEO Dan Zimmermann says the Huang-related markets showed fewer signs of potentially suspicious activity than usual. “The group that could have known the board’s decision in advance was small. So the market was mostly headline chasers and long-shot buyers,” he says.
For traders who profited from Huang’s comeback, the lesson is clear: Don’t discount Trump.
Isaac Rose-Berman, a research and policy fellow at the American Institute for Boys & Men and a professional sports bettor, says he made approximately $10,000 wagering on Huang’s return on Kalshi. He placed his trade after the Nvidia chief said on X that he was open to reclaiming his old job. Rose-Berman says he figured that people like Trump, who had an interest in boosting Nvidia’s stock price, would successfully pressure the board to reinstate Huang. (Never mind the concerns about national security or Huang allegedly threatening employees that he would “pee in your brain.”)
“It’s 2026 and Trump is president, scandal schmandal,” Rose-Berman says. “Should’ve bet more.”
A pseudonymous prediction market trader known as Domer tells WIRED he cleared approximately $600,000 by placing an early wager last week on Huang’s return. He wasn’t focused explicitly on Trump’s desires, but he says his understanding of power dynamics ultimately guided his approach. “Boards can be very malleable,” he tells WIRED.
Other prediction market “sharps” say that commentary from Trumpworld was the strongest signal available about Huang’s return. As some bettors noted, there was a notable opportunity for profit after Trump suggested to a PBS NewsHour reporter that Huang would be back in power soon, but before Nvidia officially announced his reinstatement. “The key was looking at Trump’s support and the recent performance of the stock market,” says Kalshi trader Caleb Davies.
Aaron Courtney, a data analyst and cofounder of a prediction market analytics firm, says he took a loss on his Huang position. Looking back, he says his mistake was not paying attention to what Trump and other administration officials said over the weekend. “Trump saying he hoped Nvidia would bring Jensen back would be the clearest signal,” he says.
