Christopher Nolan has spent much of his career championing the theatrical experience. From shooting on IMAX film to encouraging audiences to see his movies on the biggest screens possible, the director has made no secret of his belief that movies are meant to be experienced in theaters.
But that doesn't mean Nolan thinks watching a movie at home is a lesser experience. In fact, the filmmaker believes home viewing offers some advantages that even the most impressive theater can't replicate. And with The Odyssey arriving on digital, 4K Ultra HD and Blu-ray on Nov. 17, he's encouraging audiences to take advantage of them.
Speaking with Polygon ahead of the film's home release, Nolan discussed how watching a movie in your living room can change the experience, particularly when it comes to appreciating the work of the actors.
A chance to appreciate the performances
"The fun thing about watching a film in the home is a different kind of concentration," Nolan tells Polygon. "When you go to a movie, you're in the dark, you're really focused for a couple hours. In the home, it's a different type of concentration because you can pause the movie, you can go get a snack, come back."
That freedom means viewers engage with a movie differently than they would during a theatrical screening.
"It does allow you to control the timeline and really look at the detail of things," he said. "And for me, it's really about the performances."
While The Odyssey features enormous ships, mythical creatures, and elaborate action sequences, Nolan believes the film's greatest rewards on repeat viewings may come from paying closer attention to its actors.
"The human element is what connects you with the story," Nolan says. "But I think the work of these actors, this cast, this incredible cast in the film, it rewards multiple viewings."
Nolan knows that better than most, having spent countless hours reviewing footage during the editing process. He pointed to the layers in performances by Matt Damon, Anne Hathaway, Tom Holland, Robert Pattinson, and Samantha Morton as details that audiences might appreciate more fully when watching again.
"There's subtleties and nuances in what they're doing that I, in the edit suite, get to watch again and again and again and sort of pick up on all the different things that Matt or Anne or Tom or Rob, what they're bringing to things, or Samantha Morton," Nolan says. "It's really fun for people to be able to look at that in the home and see all the different layers that these guys have put into the story."
Christopher Nolan has some home theater advice
When I mentioned that my wife and I enjoy discussing movies while watching them at home, something that isn't exactly encouraged in a theater, Nolan acknowledged the experience has its own appeal.
"It's a different form of engaging with the story, and it has strengths and it has weaknesses," he says. "Nice to be able to sit on your own couch and stretch out, you know what I mean? It's been really exciting over the course of my career to see the leaps forward in terms of technology, in terms of what you're able to do in the home."
Nolan also appreciates how much home-viewing technology has improved during his filmmaking career.
"It's been really exciting over the course of my career to see the leaps forward in terms of technology, in terms of what you're able to do in the home," he says.
And for anyone interested in bringing a little more of the theatrical experience into their living room, Nolan has a suggestion.
"I'm a big fan of projectors," he said. "You can get a relatively low-cost, pretty high-quality projector, darken your living room, maybe introduce some popcorn into the equation or whatever. Have a mini movie theater and do it on your own timeline. That's tremendous fun."
But Nolan doesn't believe a movie necessarily loses its theatrical identity once audiences begin watching it at home. In fact, he suggests that the excitement surrounding a major theatrical release can follow a film into people's living rooms.
"When you have a movie that's played, as The Odyssey has, at length in movie theaters, even viewed in the home, there's a kind of excitement that comes with an event like that," Nolan says. "Even if people are seeing it for the first time, I think the film sort of carries that with it."
