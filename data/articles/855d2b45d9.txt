DAKAR – The kamikaze quadcopter banks sharply, locks on to a Russian military vehicle and dives for the kill. Another drone hovers over an airfield. A strike narrowly misses a jet but hits three men identified as Russians.
Posts of first-person-view drones targeting Russians have become commonplace as the war in Ukraine drags on, but these videos aren’t from Ukraine, they’re from Mali, 5,000 kilometers away on the edge of the Sahara Desert.
Filmed by separatists and an al-Qaida affiliate during their simultaneous joint assault on Mali’s army and its Russian paramilitary backers in April, the videos show how battlecraft honed in Ukraine’s kill zone has become a major factor in the resurgence of rebels across the Sahel.
