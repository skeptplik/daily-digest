The first piece of furniture by architecture office Studio Hagen Hall and a pleated cabinet by Didi Ng Wing Yin feature in this roundup of design pieces spotted by Dezeen’s Jane Englefield over the last month.
SHH-001 table by Studio Hagen Hall
Louis Hagen Hall of London-based Studio Hagen Hall has made his first foray into furniture with this sculptural coffee table, delivered as two legs and a weighty glass tabletop that require no mechanical fixings.
Hagen Hall originally designed the asymmetric piece for Pine Heath, a modernist Hampstead renovation with a corner sofa that required a bespoke coffee table to offset the negative space left by the seating.
Cherry veneer legs are paired with recyclable brown smoked glass to create the made-to-order piece, informed by the design philosophy of modernist American architect John Lautner.
Exo 01 vase by Flora Lechner
Austrian, Netherlands-based designer Flora Lechner originally caught the attention of London’s Max Radford Gallery with her alternative aluminium chandeliers that look like ornate robots
Lechner has now translated her distinctive style into an unusual vase, composed of aluminium clips held together by stainless steel bolts. Stems can be slotted into the clips, which are supported by a base that can be filled with water.
“Exo 01 approaches the vase more as a support structure than as a container,” explained Lechner. “Inspired by braces used to hold and align parts of the body, the work applies this principle to flowers.”
Low-relief table by Anne Holtrop
Anne Holtrop is known for his rough-hewn furniture pieces, which conjure images of volcanic craters and mountainous landscapes.
For his inaugrual exhibition with Carpenter’s Workshop Gallery in London, the Dutch architect and designer is showing off his Low-relief collection of tables made from cast glass.
Their unrefined legs were informed by stone reliefs Holtrop visited in Aswan, Egypt.
Pleats Cabinet No 1 by Didi Ng Wing Yin
Helsinki designer Didi Ng Wing Yin is known for his distinctively pleated wooden furniture, which he makes from locally sourced Finnish timber.
Last week, the designer debuted his first cabinet at Paris’s Spazio Nobile Gallery. Made from a mixture of fir and pine, finished with tung oil, the cabinet features a delicately pleated door made using Ng’s combined splitting, brushing and carving technique.
“I wanted to explore the dynamic contrast between form and texture,” Ng told Dezeen.
“The chunky pedestal base is built from straightforward architectural beams that retain their natural knots and raw characteristics, providing a heavy, structural anchor for the light, organic silhouette of the pleated cabinet door.”
OBJ-05 Chair by Manu Bañó
One of many design events in October was PAD London, a collectible fair that takes place annually in Mayfair’s Berkley Square.
Among this year’s exhibitors was Mexico-based Manu Bañó, who showed a duo of striking patinated brass chairs formed from stacked, sculptural slabs of metal.
The chairs are a continuation of Bañó’s existing brass and stainless steel furniture, and were presented as part of the Objects with Narratives exhibition.
Ballon furniture by Seungjin Yang
South Korean designer Seungjin Yang has been making playful furniture shaped like balloons for years, using colourful epoxy resin.
At PAD London, Yang showed his most recent balloon-like creations in the form of a sofa, two armchairs and two stools.
The designer coats real balloons in resin, painstakingly building their strength. Each piece is complete after around two weeks of delicate testing.
Don’t be misled by the intriguingly delicate appearance of Yang’s finished pieces, which are solid enough to be sat on.
