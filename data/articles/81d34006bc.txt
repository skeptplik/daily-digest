The British embassy in Tokyo has launched a social media campaign warning visitors to exercise caution after a rise in cases of drink-spiking and credit card fraud against tourists in the Japanese capital.
In a recent clip made in conjunction with the embassy, the British rapper and manga artist Shao Dow, a frequent visitor to Japan, notes that although the country “is incredibly safe, that doesn’t mean you can let your guard down”.
He warns that young male tourists are particularly susceptible to being lured into bars on the promise of being able to chat to attractive, English-speaking women. They are then served drug-laced drinks and enormous sums are charged to their credit cards.
Drink-spiking cases involving British and other international tourists have been on the rise in the past two years in popular nightlife districts such as Roppongi, Shibuya and Kabukicho.
Some victims have lost up to £30,000 (US$39,000) in a single night, Dow says, and wake up with no memory of what happened. “Look, do me a favour,” he adds, “Stay alert, trust your gut, keep an eye on your stuff and don’t follow strangers into bars.”
Police in Tokyo have come under pressure to tackle the rise in incidents targeting tourists. Earlier this month, 160 riot police conducted predawn raids on two establishments in Kabukicho after reports they had ripped off customers. Five people were arrested.
Between January and October, police recorded 340 cases – up 195 from last year – in the Shinjuku area after customers, some of whom were lured into bars by street touts, were charged extortionate sums. In one case, ¥9.6m ($60,000) was allegedly charged to a customer’s credit card.
According to the newspaper Asahi Shimbun, victims have paid ¥390m in total and foreign nationals had been the targets in 70% of cases. The newspaper quoted a senior investigator as saying the reported crimes were the “tip of the iceberg” since many visitors returned home before they realised they had been swindled, while others had left Japan without reporting the incidents to police.
Reports say the rise in cases was being fuelled by Japan’s tourism boom. A record 42.7 million people visited Japan last year, including 530,000 from the UK.
The Guardian has approached the Japan National Tourism Organisation for comment.
