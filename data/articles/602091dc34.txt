Once you’ve coordinated your wedding guest schedule, the next step is figuring out what to wear to all those nuptials. Between decoding dress codes (just how casual is a “casual” wedding, really?) and navigating different climates, you’ll quickly realize there’s no one-size-fits-all solution to this sort of occasion styling. So if you find yourself in the run-up to a fancy affair with a closet full of not-quite-right frocks and a looming deadline—don’t panic. We pulled together an edit of the best wedding guest dresses to suit every possible style, venue, and budget.
The Best Wedding Guest Dresses, According to Vogue
While some eveningwear requirements are set in stone, many leave room for creativity depending on the wedding theme. For instance: A black-tie ceremony does necessitate floor-length gowns—but that doesn’t mean a fuchsia-hued dress is off the table. A spring or summer wedding might have you gravitating toward sleeveless silhouettes and airier fabrics in the name of practicality, so why not pick a rich color like chartreuse or cerulean for an unexpected twist on the palette of the season? And while a cocktail dress code or daytime fête definitely suggests more laid-back attire, there’s plenty of opportunity to stand out with the details—think, a lace-trimmed midi or a tiger stripe-inspired number.
Whatever’s in the cards (we mean this literally—please read the invite carefully), our picks for the best wedding guest dresses cover a lot of ground.
Winter Wedding Guest Dresses
The coldest days of the year come with the quandary of formal dressing for freezing temps (is there anything worse than having to cover a great dress with a puffer coat?). Stay warm in Staud’s long-sleeve maxi or a velvet option by Veronica Beard. Or, fully embrace winter glam in Simkhai’s halterneck gown, which has a low back for some drama.
Black-Tie Wedding Guest Dresses
Formal weddings are your moment to go all out with dramatic lengths and unexpected draping. Labels like Victoria Beckham, Fforme, and L’Agence show us how it’s done—just don’t forget the right jewels for a finishing touch.
Spring Wedding Guest Dresses
Spring nuptials call for soft hues—though we’re all for trying a zippier take on pastels. H&M’s floral jacquard maxi dress in light turquoise will get you in the mood for a garden affair, while Totême’s soft pink midi pretty much works for any venue, as far as we’re concerned.
Cocktail Wedding Guest Dresses
Toast the new couple in a spirited cocktail dress. COS’s gray midi delivers modern Art Deco glamour, while Ralph Lauren’s mock-neck style is all easy elegance. Then there’s Diotima’s LBD, a halterneck design bound to become a closet staple for future events.
Daytime Wedding Guest Dresses
Attending a courthouse ceremony or a more intimate backyard celebration? Elevated comfort is the name of the game. Slip on something easygoing with a modern finish—like Blaze Milano’s satin dress in a golden hue—or go for a more striking, unexpected piece from Tory Burch for a look that’s both playful and polished.
Black Wedding Guest Dresses
The best way to bring personality to a dress code with a limited palette? Play with silhouette and texture. Aje keeps things party-ready with fringe detailing; Kallmeyer brings evening elegance with a floor-length hem; and Banana Republic’s high-neck, long-sleeve option suits wintry affairs well.
Floral Wedding Guest Dresses
This is one prompt that eliminates the guesswork; plus, you can never go wrong with a romantic bloom, no matter the silhouette. Keep accessories simple (sleek kitten heels and minimalist jewelry are the right choice here) and let the pattern do all the work.
Destination Wedding Guest Dresses
From Italian villas to rolling countrysides, the best way to go about a destination wedding is to take notes from your surroundings. Lean into the romance of a cobblestoned town with a silky, lace-trimmed dress from Simon Miller, or buy into the breezy style of an island venue with Christopher John Rogers’ striped crinkle-chiffon gown.
Summer Wedding Guest Dresses
Bold citrus hues and sultry silks define the mood for summer wedding dressing, we say—and Aflalo and Tove are both on board. For a more formal option, Taller Marmo’s one-shoulder gown with a fringe-trimmed cape will make you the best-dressed guest.
Beach Wedding Guest Dresses
A shoreside ceremony is your cue to relax a little. Lighter-weight fabrics and simple silhouettes are fair game, especially when you play with texture and pattern. Coastal shades and sun-washed hues feel perfectly at home here, too. Style yours with open-toed sandals and nautical-inspired accessories, and you’re good to go.
Fall Wedding Guest Dresses
Autumn wedding season calls for dresses in a deep, rich palette. La Ligne’s long-sleeve design in navy and Anine Bing’s modern slip in burgundy both offer universally flattering colors, while Maria McManus’s lace bodice dress and Dries Van Noten’s floral midi are splashier options for those with an affinity for details. Complete the look with a silk wrap draped around your shoulders once temperatures dip after cocktail hour.
Quick Guidelines for Wedding Guest Dressing
What colors should I wear to a wedding?
When in doubt, let the season lead—jewel tones and deep neutrals for fall and winter, and soft pastels or vibrant hues for spring and summer. Be sure to also check the invitation and wedding website, as some couples may ask guests to dress within a color palette, and it’s important you honor their wishes.
What colors should I avoid as a wedding guest?
If you remember anything, remember this: Steer clear of white, ivory, and anything that could read as bridal, unless the couple says otherwise. Keep cultural customs in mind, too, as red and black are off-limits at some ceremonies. Again, if you’re unsure, check the invitation or wedding website, or ask a member of the bridal party for guidance.
How do I know what style and silhouette to wear?
The dress code, venue, season, and time of day should guide your choices. Here’s a quick rundown: Floor-length for black tie, knee- to midi-length for semi-formal and cocktail, minis for more casual celebrations, airy silhouettes for outdoor, beach, or daytime affairs, and thicker fabrics for the cooler months. And don’t forget about your accessories, whether that’s block heels for a lawn ceremony or a shawl for a more conservative house of worship.
How do I find a dress that works for multiple weddings?
If you have several weddings on the horizon, opt for a look in a solid hue and an easygoing shape, like a sheath style or handkerchief hemline. These pieces are extremely versatile, easier to rewear than trend-driven prints, and simple to restyle with different shoes, jewelry, and outerwear.
