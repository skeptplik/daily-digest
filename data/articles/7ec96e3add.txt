Clinics in England are promoting seasonal deals – including for Black Friday – on liquid Brazilian butt lifts (BBLs), despite concerns about the risks of the procedure.
The government is consulting on limiting liquid BBLs, vampire facials and deep chemical peels in England to qualified healthcare professionals.
The proposals follow the death of Alice Webb, a 33-year-old mother of five, after a botched liquid BBL in a Gloucester salon in 2024. Her family have called for laws against “cosmetic cowboys” to be introduced before more people die.
A liquid BBL involves injecting dermal filler into the buttocks. Plastic surgeons say it can carry significant risks and requires expert skill and training to perform, with large amounts of filler potentially causing serious side effects such as blood clots, fat embolisms and sepsis.
The Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) has already acted against time-limited promotions, concluding that Black Friday offers could rush consumers into deciding on cosmetic surgery without enough time to consider the consequences.
Between April and December 2025, the watchdog found more than 900 ads for liquid BBLs in its advert monitoring system. During that period, only 11.5% of the ads they checked stuck to the ASA’s rules.
It said some ads made claims such as “0% infection rate” or described procedures as “safe”, without properly reflecting the risks involved. Other ads played on body insecurities, promising to “boost your confidence” or “transform your body effortlessly”.
Ashton Collins, the director of Save Face, a government-approved register of accredited non-surgical treatment practitioners, said she was “outraged” by BBLs being advertised as “party-season bargains” with time-limited discounts.
“These are not routine beauty treatments; they are serious medical procedures that can lead to devastating complications,” she said.
“Marketing them this way trivialises the risks, and pressures people to make snap decisions without the time, information or proper consultation they need to make an informed choice.”
She added that Save Face had received 1,004 patient-reported complaints about non-surgical BBLs, hip-dip and breast-augmentation procedures since 2023. More than 50% of the complainants experienced sepsis, 94% required hospital treatment and 43% required surgery.
“The impact on people’s lives can be profound and lasting,” Collins said.
One London provider, Clinic Blvd, recently advertised on social media for a 48-hour deal of 800ml of filler plus 200ml free for £1,300. The post, accompanied by a picture of a woman’s bottom, stated that demand was at “an all time high” and that the deals would not be available to everyone. The clinic also listed breast filler as an option, at £799 for 400ml.
A spokesperson for Clinic Blvd said patient safety and informed consent were its top priorities. “All treatments are subject to individual consultations and clinical suitability assessments,” they said. “We take responsible advertising seriously and remain committed to maintaining appropriate professional standards.”
An Instagram post from De Vere Signature Aesthetics, also based in London, promised 100ml of free filler as a “special limited time offer” added to procedures. Their price for filler falls as the volume rises, from about £5 per ml at the bottom of the scale to about £1.50 at the top, rewarding customers for choosing larger amounts. De Vere Signature Aesthetics was approached for comment.
The Belle Clinic, also in London, advertised a deal online offering 40% off a liquid BBL in autumn to “get ready now for your Christmas party”.
A spokesperson said: “Our current promotion is an autumn and winter seasonal offer … It is not intended to create urgency or pressure anyone into making a decision about an aesthetic procedure.
“We take the ASA’s guidance seriously and are willing to review our promotional wording to ensure it communicates our approach responsibly.”
Nora Nugent, a consultant plastic surgeon and former president of the British Association of Aesthetic Plastic Surgeons (BAAPS), expressed concern over time-limited discounts.
“This is a sales ploy that makes people make quick decisions in case they lose out on the offer,” she said, warning that any special offers, even if not time limited, could encourage people to have treatments they might not otherwise choose.
Gerard Lambe, a consultant plastic and cosmetic surgeon and a spokesperson for BAAPS, said: “People encouraging you to have increased volumes and reduced prices, that’s the worst thing that you could ever do. [You] should never ever do that to people. It increases your risk of complications.”
However, Lambe acknowledged that some private plastic surgeons do offer deals on procedures, saying that the issue of finance is complex, and that lack of affordability could lead people to choose cheaper surgery abroad with a greater risk of complications.
The ASA said its rules made clear that adverts for liquid BBLs must be socially responsible.
“We’ve taken widespread and targeted enforcement action against advertisers breaking the rules, including by trivialising the risks of these procedures, using time-limited promotions to create a sense of urgency and exploiting women’s insecurities,” a spokesperson said.
“We support the introduction of further government measures to regulate the sector.”
