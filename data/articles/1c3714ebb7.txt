Anthropic prices IPO at $83 US per share, valuing AI giant at $1.51 trillion US
What you need to know about the company as it prepares to debut on Nasdaq
Anthropic has set a stock price of $83 US per share for its hotly anticipated initial public offering (IPO), with shares of the U.S. artificial intelligence giant set to debut on the Nasdaq exchange on Thursday.
At $83 US per share, Anthropic, the company behind the popular Claude chatbot, is valued at approximately $1.51 trillion US, a figure that includes unvested employee stock options and other promised shares.
This surpasses the $70 to $78 US range from Anthropic's Nov. 10 regulatory filing. In a news release Wednesday, the company also upsized its offering by 155 million shares.
The debut valuation is massive compared to anything in Canada, representing more than a quarter of the combined market value of every company listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange.
The money raised in the offering alone exceeds every major Canadian IPO combined over the past 30 years.
Ernest Wong, head of research at Toronto-based Baskin Wealth Management, notes that Anthropic dwarfs the most valuable company on the TSX, Royal Bank, which is currently worth $377 billion.
"That really speaks to the global nature of large U.S. tech companies today," he told CBC News on Wednesday. "These are large companies that touch every aspect of our lives, and the revenue and valuation reflect that."

The $83 US per share IPO price announced on Wednesday is the price paid by institutional investors and retail buyers who secured pre-IPO allocations through participating brokers. The actual market price will be determined after trading is underway on Thursday.
The company's ticker symbol is "ANT."
Here's what you should know about the company and its stock market debut.
What is Anthropic?
Anthropic started as a niche AI research lab in 2021. Its founders left rival OpenAI because they felt that company was too focused on profits, ignoring risks and giving too much control to investors.
The company gained momentum in 2024 when it released the Claude 3.5 Sonnet chatbot. This version beat OpenAI's GPT-4o on major performance benchmarks at a fraction of the cost of previous frontier AI models.
Unlike other tech giants like Apple or Microsoft, Anthropic is not a traditional for-profit company. It's organized as a public benefit corporation, meaning its board has a legal mandate to balance public safety against maximizing shareholder returns.
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei is a leading advocate for AI safety, arguing the industry should slow development to let safety measures catch up.
"We will slow down as much as necessary in order to make sure that every successive AI technology that we release is actually safe," he said at a United Nations Security Council meeting on artificial intelligence in September.
When does Anthropic start trading?
Under Nasdaq's listing and trading rules, newly listed shares do not begin trading immediately after the 9:30 a.m. ET opening bell.
Nasdaq temporarily holds the stock in a price-discovery auction, giving the exchange and bankers leading the sale time to match incoming bids and offers into an opening price.
Some Canadian brokerages, like Wealthsimple and major bank platforms, had options for everyday investors to request shares in advance at the fixed offer price, prior to the Nasdaq auction.
However, the supply of shares is often limited, and investors may face holding restrictions or penalties for selling right away.
What do we know about Anthropic's financial performance?
Anthropic lost $42 billion US in 2025, according to regulatory filings with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.
In the first nine months of this year, the company booked an $104 billion US net loss. However, this was largely driven by non-cash accounting charges tied to its soaring pre-IPO valuation.
Meanwhile, sales have been brisk. In the first nine months of 2026, Anthropic generated $33.24 billion US in revenue — a 1,206 per cent increase year-over-year. Its annualized sales pace hit roughly $81 billion US in October.
Anthropic also has big spending plans. Unlike Google or Microsoft, Anthropic has historically rented its computing power rather than owning physical data centres. It's locked into roughly $450 billion US in long-term computing and data-centre commitments through 2030.
How are Anthropic's peers trading?
Since Anthropic is laser-focused on building and selling AI models, its shares are seen as a unique "pure play" on the artificial intelligence boom.
That sets it apart from most stocks associated with AI, which typically have other businesses.
Take Google's parent company Alphabet, for example. It's considered an AI "hyperscaler," but it has a massive suite of non-AI businesses, like YouTube and Android smartphones.
AI-related stocks have been on a roller coaster lately, swinging between panic-driven sell-offs and relief rallies.

Shares of companies like Oracle, Microsoft, and Amazon saw massive gains through the summer and early fall. Since then, a trio of headwinds have emerged: the leadership crisis at Nvidia; U.S. regulators putting tighter controls on China's access to AI chips; and fears of a data centre slowdown in the U.S. driven by the "blue wave" of Democrat victories in the recent midterm elections.
As a result, the tech-heavy Nasdaq index is down nearly seven per cent over the past month.
Wong says investors buying Anthropic stock should brace for big price swings early on. He says his firm does not typically buy IPOs, and will take a wait-and-see approach.
"I think it's [going to be] very volatile," Wong said. "It really takes some time before the trading of the stock kind of settles into where it's supposed to be."
