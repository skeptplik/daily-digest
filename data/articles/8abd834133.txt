PARIS — “Price can hardly be an excuse to throw out things because they’re imperfect,” said Tingit founder Indre Viltrakyte.
It’s on that premise that the Lithuania-based entrepreneur with a background in law and business consulting founded the repair marketplace, which this week got a big boost of visibility in France through a partnership with Vinted Go.
Tingit’s services were touted on millions of labels supplied by the logistics arm of the Vinted sales platform to French consumers.
Arnoldas Kevalaitis, Vinted Go’s senior director of network development, said its network of lockers and partner shops is already part of people’s everyday routines, and “[there is] a lot of potential to make it even more useful.”
“With Tingit, you can drop off a jacket that needs fixing and pick it up once it’s repaired, as easily as sending a parcel,” he continued. “We’re glad to support a start-up that makes repair simpler, and it’s exactly the kind of service we want to bring to the Vinted Go network.”
Since operations began in 2024, Tingit has accrued a network of about 150 craftspeople and handles up to 1,000 repairs a month across France, the biggest market it serves, and Lithuania. Although Viltrakyte declined to say how many active users were on Tingit, she said a large proportion of them used the service multiple times.
Artisans on its roster cover routine jobs such as sneaker cleaning to more specialized work including invisible mending, a technique that reconstructs a weave or knit from matching fibers to make damage almost undetectable. Shoes are its biggest segment while bags are the highest-value one, with knitwear and simple mending growing.
Viktrakyte said the objective was make seamless the common practical conundrum of repairs faced by secondhand shoppers and vintage lovers like herself, who don’t always have the right specialists nearby.
“With this system, somebody in Paris can reach a very good artisan across the country,” she told WWD. “[Tingit] can make repairs accessible and eventually affordable as well, which has not been the case for a very long time.”
In addition to Vinted Go, where Tingit has a 2.40-euro price for two-way shipping, the platform has Chronopost and Mondial Relay as options.
Under the hood, an AI-powered algorithm matches consumers with the craftspeople best suited for the job, who then provide estimates. Once accepting a quote, the customer receives a shipping label to send their item. The repair specialist likewise receives one to ship the item back once the work is complete.
In May, Tingit’s parent UAB Rebel Origin secured 1.5 million euros in funding, for a total of 2 million euros raised since an initial pre-seed round in 2024, which it is using to further develop its technologies. An app is also slated for release in coming months.
For the founder, the “realistic goal” is to have a million users by 2027, and the platform is weighing Germany, the Netherlands and Spain as possible next markets. Last year, Tingit began to consider sourcing specialists for other categories such as electronics and furniture.
But in her opinion, there’s even bigger potential for Tingit: helping brands build repairs into the customer journey.
“When I think about repairs, it’s just like an infrastructural layer on top of commerce, and especially secondhand commerce,” said the founder, who feels brands will “likely be a big part of our path to educating and reaching consumers.”
The Lithuanian platform has developed several pilots with fashion brands to automate assessments and quotes, with ambitions to become a remote repair department for them, although Viltrakyte declined to name any. She said a first partnership would likely go live in the next six months.
The timing is ripe in light of the European Union’s ongoing regulatory framework on the “right to repair.” France was the first country to implement repair bonuses in 2023, which offer an instant discount of up to 25 euros per repair at certified providers. Ireland is looking to introduce a similar system in 2027.
According to a report by media and foresight platform Circular Fashion News, the European clothing and footwear repair market was worth an estimated 2.8 billion euros in 2025 and is projected to grow 5.5 percent a year to 3.7 billion euros by 2030.
In a separate study, KPMG and Fédération de la Mode Circulaire estimated that the European Union’s circular fashion sector could generate more than 104 billion euros in annual revenue and create 88,000-plus jobs by 2030.
