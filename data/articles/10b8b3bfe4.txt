A decade after moving out of Place Vendôme, Pasquale Bruni is back with a new Paris flagship.
Located at 5 Rue du Faubourg Saint Honoré, the plush 1,330-square-foot unit featuring a sculptural chandelier made of Murano glass leaves marks a fresh push into France and Europe for the Italian jeweler, which sees the city as a chance to build its local clientele as well as serve international visitors.
“This is obviously a very, very special moment,” the company’s global commercial director Laurence Brunel told WWD ahead of the official opening on Thursday evening. “[Founder] Pasquale always kept the dream of having one foot in Paris because [it] is, for everybody outside of France, a fascinating city for luxury, for savoir-faire – for everything, from education to business.”
An achievement on the one hand, this is also “the first step of a longer journey” in the French and European markets, she added.
The Paris opening comes on the heels of the brand’s first North American flagship, a five-floor 6,800-square-foot address on Madison Avenue in New York that bowed in March.
Paris, where the jeweler is also present through high-end jewelry stores and La Samaritaine, represents “a fantastic opportunity in terms of a clientele of local residents but it’s also important for all the tourism [as] we have all the world and nationalities coming to Paris,” for the jewelry executive.
These prospects remain undimmed despite the recent spate of protest-driven unrest in France. Noting the “very direct and concrete” impact, Brunel said the jeweler had taken measures to reinforce security.
She described the French shopper as a sophisticated customer looking for jewelry with a strong identity and classic, lasting pieces that can be passed down to the next generation. The average basket in Paris is “roughly the same” as in the U.S., Brunel added, while there’s a balance between demand for diamond- and colored-stone collections in Western Europe.
The Paris opening brings Pasquale Bruni up to 18 monobrand boutiques, including the existing directly operated stores in Milan and Rome, and the house’s franchised network is expected to comprise 14 boutiques by the end of 2026.
Outside Europe, the company opened earlier in November a shop-in-shop at Palacio de Hierro in Mexico City’s Polanco neighborhood, a move Brunel said would help the company develop its presence across the South American continent.
Its next major European step is London, with the city’s Casa Pasquale Bruni, initially planned for the final quarter of this year, now expected to open in March at 1 Old Bond Street.
In the meantime, the jeweler is opening next week a temporary address at 18 Albemarle Street, just off Old Bond Street, to introduce the brand to British clients and residents.
