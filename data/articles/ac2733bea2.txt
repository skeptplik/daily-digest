Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Thursday (November 12, 2026) that two tankers carrying over four million barrels of oil had caught fire after an "accident" on an unauthorised route in the Strait of Hormuz.
Since the outbreak of the war in West Asia in late February, Tehran has heavily restricted traffic through the strategic shipping channel, insisting vessels use routes it has approved.
The United States has in response imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports and helped tankers travel the strait, which normally carries about one-fifth of global oil.
"Last night, two tankers - acting in violation of regulations and despite repeated warnings from the Revolutionary Guards Navy regarding the unauthorised and dangerous nature of the southern maritime route - were involved in an accident while traversing this prohibited path in the strait," the Guards said in a statement.
They did not specify the nature of the "accident" but said it left the two tankers immobilised after they were hit with a "violent explosion".
"These two supertankers were carrying a combined total of over four million barrels of oil, which are now ablaze - a consequence of the blind trust placed in the aggressive American military," the statement said.
Iranian forces have made multiple announcements in recent weeks of ships catching fire attempting to use routes that Tehran considers off-limits.
Tehran has often said they struck mines, but the U.S. military recently claimed all mines had been cleared from the strait.
