Malaysia is seeking to resettle 10,000 Rohingya migrants in Turkiye as part of efforts to resolve a long-running refugee challenge involving foreign asylum seekers, the majority from Myanmar, a senior official said Friday (November 20, 2026).
The announcement by Deputy Prime Minister Ahmad Zahid Hamidi comes a day after the UN's top court, in a landmark ruling, said that Myanmar committed genocide against the ethnic minority.
"Following my recent official working visit to the Republic of Turkiye, the Cabinet has agreed to a proposal to resettle 10,000 Rohingya refugees from Malaysia to Turkiye through a structured skills training programme," Mr. Hamidi said.
"I put forward this proposal as part of the government's efforts to address the situation of Rohingya refugees in Malaysia in a more coordinated, effective and humane manner," he said. Rohingya refugees will undergo a Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) programme in Turkiye which would last three to six months.
The training will focus on practical skills that are in demand among employers across various industries in Turkiye, Mr. Hamidi said.
"With these skills, participants will be better equipped to secure employment and contribute to Turkiye's economy, subject to the country's rules and regulations," Mr. Hamidi said in a statement.
On Thursday (November 19, 2026), the International Court of Justice (ICJ) in The Hague, in its ruling, also ordered that refugees be given a safe passage home and awarded compensation.
Malaysia in late September repatriated about 1,500 Myanmar nationals under what authorities described as a voluntary programme, part of a scheme that rights groups warn could put migrants at risk by returning them to conflict-ridden Myanmar.
It was not immediately clear whether Rohingya refugees who would be selected for resettlement in Turkiye would also participate on a voluntary basis.
Mr. Ahmad Zahid however, said the initiative "is in line with the principles that guide the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) in seeking lasting solutions for refugees".
This included resettlement in a third country, "with due regard for their safety, protection and dignity".
The Malaysian Government said in October it would seek clarification from authorities in Myanmar over reports that hundreds of migrants recently returned to the country were being held in detention.
Rohingya, a persecuted mostly Muslim minority, make up two-thirds of the 190,000 Myanmar refugees and asylum seekers in Malaysia, according to the United Nations.
Malaysia, which shelters one of Southeast Asia's biggest refugee populations, is not a signatory to the 1951 Refugee Convention and does not formally recognise their status.
AFP fact-checkers have found that Rohingya refugees in Malaysia have been targeted by online disinformation campaigns that rights advocates say are fuelling xenophobia and intimidation.
