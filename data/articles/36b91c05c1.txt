An anti-vaccine group led for years by Robert F Kennedy Jr has been allowed back on Facebook and Instagram, four years after Meta removed the group for repeatedly violating its misinformation policies.
A review by the Guardian found that Children’s Health Defense (CHD) has reopened or opened six accounts on Facebook and Instagram from May through September this year. The group is using its accounts to share false and misleading information about topics including vaccines and deadly measles outbreaks at a time when measles has come roaring back. Health officials have reported 10 measles-associated deaths in the United States this year, and the country this week lost its measles elimination status in an official recognition that it is again endemic in the US after being eliminated for 26 years.
The CHD news comes after a Guardian investigation this month found health and vaccine misinformation was rampant on Facebook in a massive public group, Crunchy Moms, and that Facebook was recommending it to users despite apparent violations of its policies. Crunchy Moms was started a few months after Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg last year announced a rollback of moderation and factchecking. Zuckerberg defended the move at the time by saying he wanted to restore “free expression on our platforms”.
Kennedy now serves as the US health secretary, and CHD continues to have close ties to him. He headlined their annual conference in September.
The group was removed from Facebook and Instagram by Meta in 2022, after years of criticism that Kennedy and his group were top spreaders of false information about vaccines, Covid-19 and other topics on the platforms. Meta said at the time it “removed these accounts for repeatedly violating our policies”, and told CHD it was because its accounts went against community standards on “misinformation that could cause physical harm”.
In 2023, Meta restored Kennedy’s Instagram account, which he had lost in 2021 for sharing false information about Covid and vaccines. The company said it made the decision because Kennedy was an active candidate for US president. But it also said it would not restore CHD’s accounts.
Meta declined to comment on why it allowed CHD back on its platforms and why it was allowing apparent violations of its misinformation policy by CHD.
CHD did not respond to requests for comment.
Kennedy frequently tussled with Facebook before he entered the government. CHD sued Facebook in 2020, when Kennedy was leading the group, claiming it was being censored. The case was dismissed the following year, with the judge finding that Facebook, as a private entity, could not be held liable for violating the group’s constitutional rights. Earlier this year, the Washington Sun reported that Meta’s lawyer argued in a lawsuit in New Mexico that the judge should consider Kennedy’s trustworthiness and “unusual” views on vaccines when urging it not to enter a government-produced report into evidence in a lawsuit over the company’s child safety practices.
Dorit Reiss, a professor at UC Law San Francisco who has closely monitored CHD’s legal cases, said she did not believe there was a legal reason that Meta would be compelled to put CHD back on its platforms, and that it was possible Meta had simply made a political calculation and ceased to enforce its own anti-misinformation policies.
“Facebook has the right to decide what to do here,” Reiss said. “Meta decided it’s more profitable … that it’s more in their interest to let the misinformers back up.”
The Guardian found several posts on the reinstated and new CHD accounts that appear to violate Meta’s harmful health misinformation policy.
A video posted recently by one of its Facebook accounts is titled: “Vaccines: Your child could die,” and makes the misleading claim that vaccines are deadly. Another one earlier this month said: “Somehow there’s this narrative, vaccines are safe and effective, and we know that’s just a lie.”
A video posted last month to at least two of its accounts says of the six measles-associated deaths reported in Pennsylvania at that time: “None of them died from the measles.” Another said the MMR vaccine is “not so safe and effective”. Another said none of the vaccines ever eliminated any epidemic in history.
Another post this month suggested it was unsafe to follow doctors’ recommendations to get multiple vaccinations at once. A June 2026 video posted by the account says the HPV vaccines are “very dangerous”, have “caused hundreds, thousands of deaths” and do not prevent cervical cancer.
