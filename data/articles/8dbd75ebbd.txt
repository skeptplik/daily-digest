NEW YORK – Puig said Friday that it has agreed to acquire 75 percent of Artessence, the parent company of Parfums de Marly and Initio Parfums Privés, from Advent International for 1.2 billion euros, or $1.31 billion, in cash.
News of the deal that will strengthen Puig in the percolating niche fragrance category confirms speculation that the group was in final stages of talks with Advent, appearing in WWD on Nov. 17.
The transaction, which should be finalized in the second quarter of 2027, values Artessence at about $1.9 billion. Once the deal is closed, the remaining 25 percent of the group will be held by Julien Sprecher Holding.
In Artessence’s current fiscal year, the company is expected to generate about $420 million to $440 million in net sales and approximately $180 million to $190 million in EBITDA, equating to a less than 10-times multiple valuation.
Puig and the Julien Sprecher Holding also have agreed to an earn-out based on Artessence’s future performance, plus on put and call options over the holding’s remaining 25 percent stake, allowing Puig to own the company fully over time. Future payments will be subject to Artessence delivering on its business plan growth and profitability targets.
Media reports had been swirling for months that Parfums de Marly and Initio Parfums Privés are for sale.
“Parfums de Marly and Initio are exceptional brands that combine desirability with an impressive track record of profitable growth,” said Marc Puig, executive chairman of Puig, in the statement. “They are highly complementary to our portfolio and reflect the kind of distinctive love brands we aim to nurture at Puig. This transaction reflects our selective approach to M&A: We invest in attractive brands with a strong strategic fit that complement our organic growth, while preserving our financial flexibility. Even after completion of this acquisition and the previously announced Isdin transaction, we expect to remain within our 2.0x leverage threshold at FY2027 yearend.”
“Niche fragrances offer one of the most attractive opportunities within our core fragrance business,” said José Manuel Albesa, chief executive officer of Puig. “Parfums de Marly brings meaningful growth and an established position in North America, while Initio is a distinctive brand with significant potential ahead. We look forward to welcoming Julien and the Artessence team to Puig and building alongside them.
“Together with our other niche houses, they give us a stronger platform from which to accelerate our ambition in the category, and we look forward to supporting the continued growth of each fragrance house, while preserving the creativity and uniqueness of each of our brands,” Albesa continued. “This transaction will reinforce our core business and is fully aligned with our Vision 2032 ambition to scale what works profitably.”
Puig said that the transaction will be highly accretive and accelerate its delivery of its Vision 2032, which involves surpassing the 8-billion-euro mark by that year. (In 2025, company sales hit 5.04 billion euros.) The plan also entails Puig’s niche fragrance business breaking through the 1 billion-euro-threshold, allowing it to rank second in the segment worldwide.
Fragrance is Puig’s largest business segment today, with the company owning Byredo, Penhaligon’s, L’Artisan Parfumeur and Dries Van Noten in the niche perfume category. Each of those increased their sales by high-double-digits and outperformed the market in the third quarter of this year.
Niche has been the fastest-growing segment in the perfume category since 2022 and is expected to represent close to 20 percent of the global selective fragrance market by 2032, according to Puig.
Worldwide, the niche fragrance market is valued at about $35 billion this year and is projected to reach $68.2 billion by 2035, which represents a compound annual growth rate of approximately 7.9 percent, Business Research Insights data shows.
Puig further highlighted the importance of niche fragrances in late October, during its first Capital Markets Day, where it presented the new Vision 2032 strategy and the 8-billion-euro goal was first shared. One of the four main drivers to reach that are niche fragrances, which are the fastest-growing segment of the buoyant perfume category.
Puig is homing in on scaling niche fragrances, especially in the Asia-Pacific region and focusing on U.S. niche fragrances, by expanding Byredo and Penhaligon’s selectively to lasso the opportunity in that country, which represents 25 percent of the niche fragrance market worldwide.
Parfums de Marly and Initio have been on a strong growth trajectory. In Artessence’s most recent fiscal year, ended March 31, retail sales for the two niche fragrance brands combined increased 39 percent against the same prior 12-month period, breaking through the $1 billion mark for the first time.
Retail sales for Marly, which surpassed $780 million, gained 42 percent. Those for Initio at slightly above $220 million were up 32 percent.
Julien Sprecher founded Artessence, currently the largest independent niche fragrance platform, in 2009. Today, he serves as its executive chairman and as creative director of Marly.
“I created Parfums de Marly with a very personal vision of perfumery, one that brings together French heritage, creative freedom and a contemporary spirit,” Sprecher said. “Our partnership with Advent has allowed us to accelerate that vision and build a truly international business. Puig shares our belief in the power of distinctive brands and longterm brand building. I am delighted to have the opportunity to continue this journey with a family company that understands fragrance deeply and respects the entrepreneurial and creative culture that has brought us this far.”
Marly was launched in 2009 and Initio, in 2015.
Artessence was formerly called Sprecher Berrier Group of Companies, or SBGC.
Private equity group Advent International took a majority stake in the brands in June 2023 in a deal industry sources valued at more than $700 million.
Under Advent’s ownership, the business grew around threefold, and a number of senior hires were made, including of Patrice Béliard as company CEO.
The Artessence business, present in about 70 countries, is particularly strong in the U.S., Europe and Middle East. North America is its largest market, a spread Puig said is complemantory to its priority markets.
“The acquisition is consistent with Puig’s approach to M&A, complementing organic growth through selective investments characterized by presence in attractive categories and brands with aligned strategic fit, while preserving financial flexibility,” Puig said in the statement.
The transaction will be financed using new debt and existing resources. Puig said it expects to remain within its 2.0x leverage threshold by the end of the current fiscal year, assuming the close of the deal with Artessence and for the remaining 50 percent of Isdin it does not already own, as previously reported.
Puig said once the deal is closed, Artessence will continue operating with a high level of independence in the medium term.
“Over time, business will increasingly benefit from Puig’s global scale, fragrance expertise and capabilities,” the company said.
