Japan’s economy stalled in the third quarter as Iran war-fuelled inflation, bad weather and an earthquake weighed on growth. Analysts still expect the Bank of Japan to raise interest rates again in December.
Japan’s real gross domestic product was virtually flat from July to September compared with the previous quarter, according to preliminary data released on Monday by the Cabinet Office.
This came in below forecasts for a 0.1% expansion and was well down from the 0.4% recorded in the previous three months.
On an annualised basis, the economy shrank by 0.1%, against expectations of 0.2% growth.
Higher oil prices pushed up costs for businesses and households. Japan, which imports virtually all its oil, has been hit hard by the surge in prices caused by the Iran war. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a major route for oil shipments, has been severely disrupted since the outbreak of the war.
Meanwhile, the weak yen is adding to the problem of rising energy prices, making imports more expensive. The US dollar has recently traded at about 160 yen, up from below 115 yen five years ago.
Inflation, excluding fresh food, rose to 2.1% in September, up from 1.7% in August and above the Bank of Japan’s 2% target.
Real private consumption fell by 0.3% quarter on quarter, matching the decline in the previous three months. Bad weather likely also played a part, “as typhoons and heavy rain kept consumers away from shops”, according to Taro Kimura of Bloomberg Economics.
Net exports knocked 0.6 percentage points off growth as imports rose 2.5% and exports fell 0.2%. A July earthquake on the southwestern island of Kyushu disrupted factory operations. Business investment rebounded by 1.6%, or 6.8% on an annualised basis, helped by strong demand for digital technology. A build-up in public inventories also supported growth.
Marcel Thieliant of Capital Economics said the GDP reading was slightly weaker than economists had expected and described the details as “rather discouraging”.
Before the data release, Shumpei Fujita, chief researcher at Mitsubishi UFJ Research and Consulting, said that “considering the headwinds from the worsening situation in Iran, the domestic economy is maintaining its resilience”.
The government of Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi is trying to pass legislation to cut the tax on food. The plan faces uncertainty over rising public debt and how the cut would be funded.
Another rate hike in December?
The latest growth figure is unlikely to dissuade the Bank of Japan from raising interest rates again in December as inflationary pressure builds, analysts say.
“In fact, it looks increasingly likely that the Bank will have to tighten policy more aggressively than we’ve been anticipating as inflation is about to surge,” Thieliant said.
Last month, the central bank said underlying inflation was broadly in line with its 2% target, which it adopted in 2013 to pull the economy out of deflation.
The International Monetary Fund projects that Japan will fall from fourth place in 2026 to sixth in 2027 among the world’s largest economies by nominal GDP in US dollars, with India and the UK overtaking it.
