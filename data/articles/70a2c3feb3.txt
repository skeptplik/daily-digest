U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called on Nvidia on Sunday (November 15, 2026) to explain whether it knowingly allowed its most advanced AI chips to reach China, saying this was more important than ex-CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged spy for Beijing.
Speaking on “Fox News Sunday Morning Futures” three days after Mr. Huang resigned, Mr. Bessent said he wants to know if Nvidia used shell companies to dodge U.S. restrictions and sell its most powerful chips to China.
But late on Sunday (November 15, 2026), tech tycoon Elon Musk and several other Silicon Valley leaders added their signatures to more than 165,000 others on an open letter demanding that the company’s board members “bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts.”
The United States forbids sales to China of the most cutting-edge AI chips, hoping to stay ahead of the Chinese in the race to dominate artificial intelligence.
In late 2025, President Donald Trump did authorize sales of Nvidia chips called H200s to the Chinese — but not the most advanced chips used to run AI models reshaping modern society.
“Nothing above the H200s can be sold to the Chinese,” Mr. Bessent said.
“What’s more important here,” he said, “is to examine whether Nvidia had a culture of compliance around the U.S. export restrictions.”
Mr. Bessent alluded to companies linked to Megaspeed, the Singapore-based data center company that Huang’s alleged lover Alice Huang once worked for.
“And who knew? Because I mean, these shell companies were obvious. They were being investigated. And did Nvidia use them as a workaround to sell chips to the Chinese?”
Of Alice Huang he said: “It doesn’t matter to me whether she’s a spy or not. You know, Jensen’s foibles are his foibles.”
Investigation deadline
Mr. Bessent said he wanted answers from Nvidia and its internal investigation of Jensen Huang’s behaviour within the next 10 days, and said the company needed “a dramatic shake-up,” which should include looking “outside” for its next chief.
The United States suspects Megaspeed helped Chinese firms get around US export controls by buying Nvidia chips for its data centers in Southeast Asia, then routing them to China.
The Justice Department is carrying out a criminal probe.
Nvidia late last month said it had investigated these allegations last year and refuted them.
The Financial Times has reported that four federal agencies pushed to have Megaspeed blacklisted by the Commerce Department run by Howard Lutnick, but the department did not do so.
“Many other departments were pushing for this, and Commerce had the pen,” Mr. Bessent said, repeating remarks he made Friday that suggest fissures in the Trump administration on this issue.
Jensen Huang and other Nvidia officials have agreed to testify in one session before representatives of three House of Representatives committees — Foreign Affairs, Intelligence, and a special committee focusing on China — Republican Congressman John Moolenaar, the chairman of that select committee, said Sunday on CBS. He said no date has been set yet.
“And we think that’s going to be a very important meeting to understand to what degree privileged information was accessed, also to what degree this potential alleged Chinese spy influenced Nvidia’s philosophy,” Mr. Moolenaar said.
On Friday (November 13, 2026), two major shareholders, investors Brad Gerstner and Gavin Baker, urged the board to keep “all options” open.
Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor, the company said earlier this week.
The end of Mr. Huang’s 33-year-long run at the helm of Nvidia sent its stock spiraling.
When trading closed on Friday (November 13, 2026), the stock was down more than 12% over the week, wiping approximately $573 billion off its market value.
Nvidia is due to publish its quarterly report on Tuesday (November 17, 2026).
