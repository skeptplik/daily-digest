At least 15,000 people have registered interest in a law firm’s bid to pursue a class-action lawsuit against Rakuten Securities following a massive data breach earlier this month, a lawyer overseeing claimants said Friday.
Rakuten Securities announced on Tuesday that login credentials and branch codes for around 11.25 million accounts, as well as personal information such as addresses, names, phone numbers, income and withdrawal bank details for 4.47 million accounts, were exposed in a security breach that took place on Nov. 11.
Makino Law Office in Tokyo, which is organizing the potential legal action, said that the breach was more serious than other incidents, as it could provide bad actors with a list of people along with their assets and addresses, and could also be used to make scams seem legitimate.
Even if customers do not experience any losses related to the potential leak, they may still be entitled to damages due to emotional distress, details on the law firm’s website soliciting claimants said.
Rakuten Securities told customers to consider locking their accounts to temporarily suspend transactions and services, and to remain vigilant against impersonators, but said they had not identified any cases of wrongdoing so far.
The securities company has not announced if they will be compensating any customers for the leak.
Makino Law Office, which is also representing plaintiffs in a similar class-action suit announced last month against Times Car services, created its site related to the Rakuten Securities incident earlier this week, urging those affected to sign up.
It will consider whether to go ahead with filing the lawsuit depending on how many people register their interest and what measures Rakuten Securities take following the security incident.
Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said that the Financial Services Agency was monitoring the “regrettable” situation and had instructed Rakuten Securities to report back to the agency on how it happened.
“It should respond earnestly and do everything possible to alleviate concerns,” Katayama said, adding that the company should take action toward protecting its customers.
In the Times Car case, Park24, the parent company of vehicle-sharing and rental service operator Times Mobility, offered ¥3,000 to each affected user, including those who had images of personally identifying documents such as a driver’s license accessed in the breach.
The lawsuit led by Makino Law Office seeks ¥50,000 for each customer who had images of personal identification documents leaked, and ¥30,000 for every other affected customer.
