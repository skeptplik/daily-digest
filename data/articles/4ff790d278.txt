With Thanksgiving just ahead of us and December around the corner, winter coat season is getting underway. The usual puffers and wool overcoats aren’t going anywhere, but Jennifer Lawrence has been making room for something a little more extravagant but still cozy: the duster coat.
Last week in New York, the actress wore two Dior versions in less than 24 hours, taking one to a “Hunger Games” premiere and the other to WWD’s Beauty Inc The Catalysts conference with distressed vintage Levi’s.
The first came on Nov. 16, when Lawrence reunited with her former costars for the premiere of “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping.” She wore a black silk robe coat from Jonathan Anderson’s Dior pre-fall 2026 collection, complete with an oversize white faux-fur collar, floral embroidery in pink and green, and a sweeping train. A tie belt gathered the fabric at her waist, leaving a generous opening in front that revealed black satin Gianvito Rossi mules.
Lawrence took a more casual approach the following day at Beauty Inc The Catalysts, where she joined Jasmina Aganovic to discuss their hair care brand, Mes. Her white Dior duster featured long lace lapels, with feathers trimming the sleeves and hem.
She paired it with a ribbed All Three Studio tank, distressed vintage Levi’s, a black Khaite Lucca belt and Aquazzura’s Alex Sandal 85 in black satin. Dior’s swan-shaped bag from the resort 2027 collection and Completedworks’ pearl-and-black-onyx earrings added to the outfit.
The Oscar-winning actress has plenty of company heading into winter. Dakota Johnson, Alexa Chung, Angelina Jolie and Inde Navarrette have also been testing the loose, elongated silhouette, bringing silk, velvet and fur-trimmed variations into their late-fall/early-winter wardrobes.
The duster itself is hardly new. In the 19th century, travelers wore loose-fitting coats to protect their clothes from the soot and dirt associated with railroad journeys. An unlined linen example dating from 1877 to 1880 in the ASU FIDM Museum’s collection features a high buttoned neck and a cape-like panel that could be pulled over the sleeves. Early motorists later adopted similar designs to contend with open automobiles and dusty roads. Keeping clothes clean was the priority, rather than showing off the outer layer.
The fall 2026 runways had considerably more decorative ideas. Conner Ives opened his London Fashion Week show with Tish Weinstock in a yellow silk robe coat featuring embroidered vintage textiles and reworked fur trim. Rather than an evening dress underneath, Ives paired the elaborate design with gold-trimmed jeans and a black “I Work Nights” T-shirt. At Calvin Klein Collection, Veronica Leoni took a quieter approach with a long ivory wrap coat, loosely belted at the waist. The robe-like shape remained, minus the elaborate embroidery and plush trim.
And that influence from the fall 2026 runways is becoming more and more apparent. Chung wore a mustard-and-cream velvet wrap dress from Tory Burch’s fall 2026 collection to the CFDA Fashion Awards on Nov. 2.
The long-sleeved design gathered at the waist with a tie, with Chung adding pear-green Tory Burch pumps, a long tassel necklace and a black clutch. Technically a dress, it had more in common with a luxurious housecoat or duster coat than a conventional evening gown.
Johnson went further into more sultry aspect of the duster trend at a Nov. 12 screening of “Flesh Impact” in Los Angeles. She wore Loretta Caponi’s Sinalunga embroidered satin midi dress with its front buttons undone, turning the piece into a robe over a sheer black lace bodysuit and matching satin trousers.
Gold floral embroidery covered the black fabric, with scalloped lace along the edges. Johnson added a small leopard-print Valentino bag, bringing another pattern into the mostly black ensemble.
Jolie’s Nov. 2 appearance at a Los Angeles screening of “Wild Horse Nine” was a more literal take on the trend. The actress wore Maria McManus’ Reversible Duster Coat, an oversize, midi-length design made from black-and-champagne Japanese Naia Renew sateen.
Jolie pushed up the sleeves and paired the coat with a billowy white top, floor-length black skirt and pointed heels.
Navarrette has gotten in on the fall outerwear trend, too. At an Oct. 16 dinner hosted by W Magazine and Colman Domingo in partnership with Boucheron in Los Angeles, the “Obsession” actress wore a long black coat with substantial dark plush trim running down its open front. Broad shoulders and relaxed sleeves added to the dressing-gown proportions, while a black V-neck minidress and narrow black-framed glasses kept the rest of the outfit relatively simple.
On Nov. 19, Navarrette returned to the retro-inspired outerwear at BBC Radio Studios in London, this time with leopard print. Her coat appeared to be Rixo’s Milly design in the brand’s Bohemia Leopard print, a midlength style inspired by 1970s outerwear. While less robe-like than her October look, the relaxed cut and vintage print fit comfortably into the wider duster conversation.
The idea has already extended into spring 2027. At Ralph Lauren’s September runway show in New York, an embroidered velvet robe was paired with a ruffled white blouse and floral-print jeans, bringing the same extravagant outerwear approach to another denim look. As the days get shorter (and chillier), perhaps we will see even more stars counting a duster coat as one of their cold-season layers.
