Six European game workers' unions have published a manifesto examining the use of generative AI in the games industry and its implications.
The manifesto, titled 'On Generative AI Usage in the Games Industry', is led by Great Britain's IWGB Game Workers Union in collaboration with members of the Republic of Ireland's GWUI, Spain's CSVI, France's STJV, Germany's GDRT, and Italy's FIOM-CGIL Milan Work Council.
"Generative AI is currently harming the games industry, its workers and the games they produce," the manifesto read. "Left unchecked, this harm will continue to grow and impact more workers, more games, and more players, even more acutely."
The manifesto also highlighted generative AI's environmental impact, "destruction of communities and the exploitation of the Global South."
"Our goal is to bring to light the risks and dangers generative AI poses to the workforce, as well as to set guidelines and standards for games industry workers, stakeholders, and regulators, suggesting actions which can be taken to reduce – and ideally eliminate – these dangers."
The manifesto outlines five main concerns: generative AI worsening working conditions, damaging worker-employer relations, appropriating workers' output, perpetuating discrimination, and introducing hidden costs.
"We do not have to accept the use of generative AI to make video games; it is not inevitable," the group said. "If generative AI was just a useful tool, as advertised, it would make our lives easier and we would have more free time, but the opposite is happening."
"The owners of these tools are aware of this and that is why they push it: to normalise it, to spread its use, and to ensure it becomes indispensable before raising prices. They are the ones in power, not workers, not even CEOs in video game studios."
The manifesto also provides recommendations for workers, employers, regulators, and players. These include avoiding generative AI, training staff to identify its use, involving employees in AI policy decisions, and encouraging players to share their views.
It also referenced the UTAW Tech Workers' Inquiry 2026, which includes policy proposals for regulators concerning generative AI use.
Laws, regulations, and guidelines on generative AI use in the industry continue to grow, with organisations such as SAG-AFTRA placing restrictions on the use of AI with a performer's voice or likeness.
UKIE has also partnered with law firm Sheridans to offer an AI Compliance and Accreditation Service, supporting UK studios in using AI lawfully and responsibly.
