Mystery Science Theater 3000 fans have always had a special relationship with American Thanksgiving. The show originally premiered on Thanksgiving Day in 1988, back in its public access era on KTMA TV in Minneapolis. Three years later, Comedy Central ran the first "Turkey Day" MST3K marathon, as both an anniversary celebration for the show and a 30-hour Thanksgiving special.
The Turkey Day tradition got dropped when MST3K moved from Comedy Central to the Sci-Fi Channel in 1997. (While Sci-Fi did run a MST3K marathon on Thanksgiving 1997, it wasn't labeled "Turkey Day.") That was the end of the tradition until 2013, when MST3K’s DVD distributor Shout! Factory revived Turkey Day via livestream.
2026's Turkey Day celebration, Back in Orbit, features six episodes from the Sci-Fi Channel run, as introduced by Mike Nelson, Kevin Murphy, and Bill Corbett. This is both a nod to their time as the hosts of the show during its Sci-Fi Channel run and a tie-in to the recently concluded MST3K/Rifftrax crossover spinoff, The Rifftrax Experiments.
This year's Turkey Day starts on Nov. 26 at noon EST on the official MST3K YouTube and Twitch channels, and is currently scheduled to feature:
- 1006: Boggy Creek II
- 907: Hobgoblins
- 811: Parts: The Clonus Horror
- 808: The She-Creature
- 910: The Final Sacrifice
- 821: Time Chasers
That's a solid lineup of late-season MST3K. However, if you're interested in alternative programming (or you simply want a marathon on a day other than American Thanksgiving this year), just about all of the first 10 seasons of MST3K can be found for free on YouTube, either from old fan uploads or via the official MST3K channel. Due to MST3K's famous "keep circulating the tapes" mantra, the old uploads are the next best thing to officially endorsed, so you can binge guilt-free. Here are some more MST3K episodes to consider for your own Thanksgiving entertainment, sorted by their original broadcast order.
7 301: Cave Dwellers
“I’m the luckiest boy in the world! I have slipped the surly bonds of Earth and touched the hand of God!”
MST3K really hit its stride in season 3. There are a few good episodes in the first two seasons, such as Catalina Caper and Robot Monster, but season 3 features a stacked roster of series-defining classics, and it starts with this hastily-made Italian fantasy film.
Cave Dwellers is a rebrand of the second movie in a four-part franchise starring the fantasy hero Ator (Miles O'Keefe). There's this whole rich vein of cheap B-movie sword-and-sorcery films from the mid-to-late 1980s that were all trying to cash in on the success of Arnold Schwarzenegger's Conan the Barbarian, and most of them are Italian knock-offs that were made in a week. The best of them are dumb fun; the worst are cocaine-infused unwatchable dreck.
What sets Cave Dwellers apart from the pack is that it takes some really big swings. It feels like one big continuity error even before the climactic moment where Ator instantly and inexplicably invents both hand grenades and the hang glider. It's a nearly perfect bad movie, both in its own right and as fodder for an MST3K episode.
6 303: Pod People
“Hey, what gives? I’m on the milk carton.”
This was the episode that made MST3K click for me. Any B-movie fan knows there's a world of difference between "fun bad" and simply bad, and Pod People is about as fun-bad as it gets.
The backstory is reportedly that Spanish director Juan Piquer Simón (Pieces) set out to make a straightforward horror movie about a vacationing pop band that stumbles across a murderous alien. Then E.T. got popular, so Simón's producers demanded a reshoot to include an additional subplot about a boy who befriends an alien child.
The result was released in France and Spain as 1983's Extra Terrestrial Visitors, before being retitled to The Unearthling and Pod People for overseas release. It's a baffling mixture of at least three major plotlines, stitched together with little care for the final product. One moment is a heartwarming story of a child and his plush marketable alien pal; the next is some dude in a fursuit killing his way through a set of backup singers. That tonal mismatch makes Pod People a nearly perfect target for MST3K, which led to several of the show's most famous jokes.
5 424: Manos: The Hands of Fate
“Every frame of this movie looks like someone’s last known photograph.”
To no one's real surprise, Manos is still generally considered the best episode of MST3K's run on Comedy Central, if not the single best episode. There are other good episodes in the fourth season, like City Limits and Hercules Unchained, but Manos overshadows them all. Like Pod People, this episode dug a notorious dud out of the back alleys of cinematic history and turned it most of the way into comedy gold.
I only say "most of the way" because Manos is a rough watch, even with Joel and the bots running interference. It's a truly baffling independent horror film from 1966, made with faulty equipment on a shoestring budget by a fertilizer salesman who allegedly did it all to win a bet. Manos is just so damn weird that it’s difficult to sit through. It was only ever screened at a handful of theaters in Texas before it appeared on MST3K, at which point it instantly became a legend.
Every frame of Manos is simply uncomfortable. The cast and crew of MST3K were always quite clear in behind-the-scenes featurettes that by the time they sat down to film an episode of the show, they'd watched the entirety of that episode’s featured movie at least five times. Despite that, Joel and the bots still manage to come off as realistically flabbergasted by each bizarre twist in Manos. There's a distinct point at which it stops being a classic MST3K episode and becomes pure trainwreck syndrome, whether you’ve seen the movie before or not.
4 705: Escape 2000
“Hi! Kill us! Thank you!”
Much like how Cave Dwellers is a reaction to the success of Conan the Barbarian, Escape 2000 (originally released as Escape From the Bronx, and not to be confused with the 1982 Australian humans-hunting-humans film) is an Italian clone of the first two Mad Max movies. It's set in a dystopian near-future New York City, as the gangs of the Bronx team up to defend their borough from a mega-corporation that wants to destroy and rebuild it. Come to think of it, Escape 2000 predicted gentrification.
Escape 2000 isn't actually that bad of a film. It's just cheaply made, overwrought, and has the same low-quality English dub as a lot of other '80s Italian B-movies, so if you’ve got a taste for that sort of thing you can have a good time with it. That makes it a solid target for the MST3K crew, which instantly lock in on a half-dozen running jokes in the first 10 minutes. It's hard to hear the word "Bronx" after this episode and not immediately think, "None of this would have happened if they'd just left the Bronx."
3 910: The Final Sacrifice
“I wonder if there’s beer on the sun.”
The first 10 seasons of MST3K were filmed in Minnesota, which neatly explains why the show was always so comfortable poking fun at Canadians. With that in mind, The Final Sacrifice is one long joke at Canada's expense, even before Mike and the bots sit down.
In another example of MST3K pulling a movie out of obscurity, 1990's The Final Sacrifice was made by a film student in Alberta on a budget of CA$1,500. Apparently it aired a few times on Canadian television in the early '90s, but was virtually unknown outside the country until its MST3K appearance.
The saving grace of The Final Sacrifice, both as a film and as an MST3K episode, is Zap Rowsdower (Bruce J. Mitchell). He might have the dumbest name of any character in film history, he's the sidekick until he's suddenly the hero in the last act, and he's a big buttery beer-fed ball of Canadian stereotypes. In other words, he's perfect. No notes. In a more perfect universe than this, Rowsdower would be on his tenth major motion picture, as he fights and drinks his way through unexplored Canadian action history. It's easily the highlight of this year's Turkey Day celebration, as it was hard to find online until recently, and it's worth incorporating into your own custom marathon.
2 820: Space Mutiny
”Why is he so impossible to hit? How can they keep missing this slow, giant white thing?”
Space Mutiny is still perhaps the most quotable episode of MST3K, even 29 years after it aired. It's given the internet decades of running jokes and forum handles, all at the expense of famous chunkhead Blast Hardcheese Punch Rockgroin Dave Ryder (Reb Brown, Yor, the Hunter From the Future) and his battle to save a colony ship from mutineers.
The co-stars of Space Mutiny have insisted since its release that it was meant to be a campy spoof of science fiction, but that's obviously damage control. Instead, it's obviously a million-dollar script paired with a buck-fifty budget. At its best, Space Mutiny is funny for all the wrong reasons, from Reb Brown’s random screaming to every woman on the colony ship wearing exercise gear as a “uniform” to the space battles that consist of visibly recycled footage from the original Battlestar Galactica. Its MST3K episode is one of the best in the series' run, often held up as the best in the Sci-Fi Channel seasons, and a genuine classic.
1 1104: Avalanche
“You asked for an avalanche of flavor, and now you got it, huh? Not enjoying it, are you?”
While MST3K has always been known for its high volume of jokes, the Netflix seasons have a particular rushed delivery that can work against them. There are still a few great episodes in the two-season run, however, and Avalanche is a dark horse contender for the best of the lot.
Avalanche is a 1978 disaster movie produced by schlock auteur Roger Corman, starring Rock Hudson and Mia Farrow, who have less than zero chemistry with one another. They play a divorced couple, and it's obvious within seconds of seeing them together that there were multiple good reasons for that divorce.
That isn't Avalanche's biggest problem, however. In the best low-budget tradition, it saves all its special effects for the finale, which makes it a disaster movie where the titular disaster doesn't show up until the last 20 minutes. That leaves the viewer to sit through an hour of awkward foreshadowing, personal dramas, and horrible '70s dinner parties (there's a baked Alaska in Avalanche that's so unappetizing that it deserves its own unique content warning) until half the cast gets murdered by snow. The MST3K crew makes the best of it with some of the best jokes in season 11. It's an unsung hero of Jonah Ray's time as MST3K's host.
