The boss of the British investment group M&G has called on the government to “take a stand” against the long line of companies leaving the London Stock Exchange, after the FTSE 100 caterer Compass announced plans to switch its primary listing to New York.
Andrea Rossi, chief executive of M&G, said his fund managers would vote against the Compass’s proposed move.
In a letter to the Financial Times, Rossi warned that its decision to switch away from London was a “warning about the health of the UK stock market”.
Rossi wrote that “when one of the largest members of the FTSE 100 concludes that its interests are better served elsewhere, policymakers cannot regard it as an isolated corporate decision.”
“The continued departure of major companies from London’s stock market should concern anyone who cares about Britain’s economic future,” he said.
Compass, which is the 18th biggest company on the FTSE 100 by market capitalisation at £45.9bn, said it had become a “predominantly North American business” and that a move would give it better access to “the world’s largest and deepest pool of capital”.
The group, which is the largest caterer in the world, makes about two-thirds of its profit in North America, while about two-fifths of its shareholders are based in the US.
There are concerns that other big firms could make the same argument, given that across the FTSE 100 only about a third of reported revenue is derived from the UK.
The M&G boss added that the government should introduce “targeted” interventions to help reduce the number of companies leaving the UK stock market, such as reducing or removing stamp duty on UK shares.
The government would lose about £40m in stamp duty revenue when Compass moves its primary listing, according to analysis by the Financial Times.
Rossi also argued that pension providers could allocate more of their default funds to British stocks.
He wrote: “Where taxpayer support is provided to private companies, a future UK listing should form part of the bargain.”
M&G Investment Management owns 0.75% of Compass’s shares, according to LSEG data, making it the 18th largest shareholder
Compass’s departure would mark the latest in a string of exits by companies seeking deeper pools of capital and higher valuations in the US.
In recent years, the equipment rental company Ashtead, the building material supplier CRH, and the owner of Paddy Power, Flutter Entertainment, have all moved their primary listings to the US.
The UK’s biggest drugmaker, AstraZeneca, listed its shares directly on the New York Stock Exchange this year, putting its US shares on an even footing with its London-listed stock.
Meanwhile, three FTSE 100 companies – the fund manager Schroders, the insurer Beazley, and the product testing group Intertek – have left the stock market after being acquired by overseas groups this year.
The UK government was approached for comment.
