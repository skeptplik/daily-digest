Back to Ukraine, the country’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy has warned that “the situation with power supply is difficult” amid continuing Russian strikes against Ukraine’s energy infrastructure.

Sweden remains stuck in government talks as 'no conditions' to from centre-left administration
Over in Sweden, election winner Magdalena Andersson has repeated that there are still “no conditions” for her to form a new centre-left government, SVT reported.
72 days on from the election, Sweden is still unable to confirm a new government, with the speaker, Andreas Norlén, increasingly running out of patience.
The four centre-left parties remain deadlocked as the Left party and the Centre party keep making conflicting demands on the government’s composition.
The Centre party, which previously favoured discussions with Andersson, has now resumed talks with the centre-right bloc, led by the incumbent prime minister, Ulf Kristersson, about teaming up to form the next government.
The task of exploring potential majorities now rests with Kristersson, after two failed attempts by Andersson (Europe Live, 15 October) and the Centre party’s leader, Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist (3 November) with a third vote – on Kristersson - expected in the coming days.
Norlén said last week that he wanted to exhaust the process – with four votes – before Christmas, either getting a new government in place or calling a snap election to break the standoff (Europe Live, last week).
After the 2018 election, it took 134 days to form a government, so the current process is still some way off that record.
Hungary's Magyar to meet Zelenskyy, visit Ukraine border region inhabited by ethnic Hungarians
Meanwhile, Hungary’s prime minister Péter Magyar announced a visit to the border region of Transcarpathia for talks with Ukraine’s Volodymyr Zelenskyy.
Announcing the visit on Facebook, Magyar said:
“Together with [foreign] minister Anita Orbán, we will visit several Hungarian institutions. This is the first visit by a Hungarian prime minister to Ukraine since 2015.”
The region, inhabited by some 60,000 ethnic Hungarians, has been one of the flashpoints in relations between Hungary and Ukraine under the previous government of Viktor Orbán.
But the visit comes just days after Hungary dropped its opposition to opening of the remaining “clusters” in Ukraine’s talks on accession to the EU, as reported by Euronews, with Magyar seeking a more constructive relationship with Ukraine.
