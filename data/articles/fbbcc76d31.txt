Gamers across Australia are preparing for the once-in-a-generation release of Grand Theft Auto VI, a day acolytes of the franchise say will be bigger than the biggest sporting events.
Details surrounding GTA VI, set in the fictional world of Leonida and its metropolis, the Miami-esque Vice City, have been scarce. But Rockstar Games has been building hype for months, driving supercars along Florida streets, planning a music festival and drip-feeding clips to a ravenous public.
With a global release time of the stroke of midnight on Thursday 19 November, Australians will be able to access the game second only to New Zealanders.
Joe Randle, an online creator known as ToastedShoes, said the game’s release was an unprecedented moment for the industry.
“It is by far the biggest gaming event that’s ever been,” he said. “It’s not like the Super Bowl, that happens every year. I would probably call it the moon landing of gaming.”
Like other streamers, Randle will go live on Twitch just before the game drops and stream for hours into the night, with videos set to drop on YouTube shortly afterwards. Business aside, the release is an “incredibly exciting” time to be a fan.
“It’s a little bit surreal almost, like it’s too good to be true” he said. “I’ve waited years for this.”
The scale of the release is staggering. Rockstar hasn’t released any public figures, but it’s thought the game cost upwards of A$1.5bn to develop. Estimates for preorders are approaching 16m copies, meaning the game has already brought in more than A$2.2bn in revenue before its launch.
Analysts say that number could jump to A$4.5bn to A$7.5bn in its first week and keep climbing, figures that are even more stunning considering the game’s age restrictions: It’s rated 17+ in the US and R18+ in Australia, meaning it’s legally restricted to adults aged 18 and over.
NBN Co, the federal enterprise that operates the country’s broadband system, said data suggested Thursday will be a massive moment for Australian gamers.
Last Thursday, the first day people could pre-download the GTA VI, saw a new record set for the level of traffic on the NBN network. Every second, the equivalent of 57 days of full HD streaming video flowed through the NBN, about 10% higher than average.
In total, NBN said the excess data amounted to about 330,000 downloads of GTA VI since the preload period began.
Matt Judge, an Australian streamer with nearly a million Twitch followers under the handle DarkViperAU, has built a career within the world of Grand Theft Auto 5. The game came out in 2013, and in the intervening years Judge has spent more than 13,000 hours in the fictional world of Los Santos, based on Los Angeles.
GTA VI has promised to build on the good will Rockstar has cultivated with the franchise and beloved games such as the Red Dead Redemption series, he said.
“Waiting that long for anything obviously is going to build a lot of anticipation and hype,” Judge said, saying he has been adjusting his sleep schedule to get ready for a playing marathon, shifting his wake-up time from around 8.30am to 3.30pm. “There was some expectation that the game would be released in 2018, it’s not only that we’ve waited 13 years but we’ve waited eight years longer than we expected.”
As a fan, Judge can’t wait to immerse himself in the world of GTA VI. But as a creator, he’s a little daunted.
“A lot of what made me be successful online was my immense knowledge of the single player version of GTA V,” he said. “But now I’m starting on the ground floor with everyone else in a new world that I don’t know as well … that makes me a bit nervous.
“Everyone recognises that a new hit game could be your meal ticket.”
Maggie Brennan, a tattoo artist from Victoria who streams under the handle retromaggie, said the impending release feels like the drop of the year’s biggest action movie, paired with a major musical release. A 34-song soundtrack comes out on Friday.
“As a big fan of the GTA series, tomorrow’s game release is really exciting for myself and other gamers who have loved the worlds that Rockstar games have created,” she wrote in an email, adding the community surrounding the game had helped her deal with her own personal health struggles.
“I have made a lot of friends along the way.”
