Trappes racist attack suspect says he acted 'on behalf of France'
In police custody, Kévin Tessier, the main suspect of the October 23 racist terror attack in Trappes, said he was acting "on behalf of France" when he went on a deadly rampage that killed one and left five injured. Investigators also found a sticky note at his home where he had listed names and phone numbers of left-wing figures.
Investigators found a sticky note with the names and mobile phone numbers of four public figures linked to the political left wing during a search of the home of the suspect in an October shooting in a Paris suburb, France’s anti-terrorism prosecution office said on Wednesday.
The suspect, Kévin Tessier, 39, was formally placed under investigation on charges including murder and attempted murder in connection with a terrorist enterprise.
Tessier told investigators that he had taken the phone numbers from a website listing leaked data and described the four as "leftists", the prosecution office said.
The sticky note included the names and numbers of two Green Party MPs Aymeric Caron and Sandrine Rousseau, France Unbowed MP David Guiraud and Assa Traoré, a prominent French activist who leads campaigns against racism and police violence.
France Unbowed leader and presidential candidate Jean-Luc Mélenchon expressed alarm about the existence of the list on X, as did Green Party leader Marine Tondelier.

Asked whether he intended to target them, Tessier said no: “No, no, no, I had just taken the numbers but I had no project under way.”
Prosecutors said nothing else in the case file supports the existence of a considered plan of violent action against the four.
Prosecutors said at the time of the shooting that the crime was committed because of the victims’ actual or supposed membership of a particular race, ethnic group, nation or religion, an aggravating circumstance under French law.
Tessier admitted having shot "a black man who was walking alone in the street", then two men, "possibly of North African origin", he said while in police custody.
During his bloody rampage, he also tried to "shoot a woman [...] wearing a veil, but the gun jammed."
Investigators recovered 128 bullet casings from various locations.
“A young girl was walking past on her own, a black girl wearing a veil” Tessier described. “I stopped in the middle of the road, ran towards her, and said to her, ‘Go on, pray, my dear’”, he recounted. “I repeated that twice and then shot her”.
When police explained to him that she was an 11-year-old girl, now seriously injured, he replied that he would “never have done that to a child”, but expressed no regret for his actions. “It’s too late anyway. Why regret it?”
Tessier refuses to be labelled a "terrorist". "For me, it's about liberating my country. It has nothing to do with that. I'm acting for France," he said. “Perhaps people need to wake up, open their eyes instead of burying their heads in the sand.”
One person was killed, five were injured in the shooting.
