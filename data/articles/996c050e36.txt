Microsoft-owned developer platform Github stepped in it earlier today when it posted a message on X celebrating the success of AnyPS5, an open source project that aims to let users port PS5 executables to PC, and then linked that success directly to efforts to port Grand Theft Auto 6 to PC. The message was deleted shortly after it was posted, but not before it was widely noticed and called out for being a very bad idea.
"GTA 6 is finally here, sparking one of the biggest weeks in gaming and open source on Github," the message stated (via Kotaku).
"The repo AnyPS5 has passed 58K stars since its August launch, rewriting PS5 executables to run on Windows and Linux. Does changing where games run cross a legal line? Share your take. Happy GTA VImas!"
Lest there be any misunderstanding, a video that accompanied the post made the GTA 6 connection explicit: "The project rewrites a game's executable so Windows and Linux can run it directly. Performance seems to be the main issue for users wanting to run GTA 6, with one contributor telling the project's community to 'lock in and get your agents to help' if they need to."
The question of whether or not this sort of thing crosses a legal line has, predictably, sparked debate among those who think they have a clear understanding of the answer. What caught most by surprise, though, is that Github—which is owned by Microsoft—would put forth the question in the first place, implicitly suggesting that it might not. GTA 6 publisher Take-Two Interactive has been pretty aggressive with its efforts to keep recompiled versions of GTA 6 from landing on PC, and I can't imagine it would too thrilled with Github—which, I must emphasize, is owned by Microsoft—telling the world that it might actually be okay to go ahead and vibe-code it up.
That point wasn't missed by redditors, one of whom described it as Github "openly taunting Sony and Rockstar." Even the AnyPS5 subreddit seemed baffled: Redditor superkickstart asked, "Do they have AI running their socials? Lol"
There's also a little bit of irony in the fact that, until earlier this week, the leading effort to get GTA 6 running on PC was based on an Xbox build of the game posted by GTA 6 leaker Cyberleeks. The whole thing is weird, really, because it seems like such an obviously bad idea. The only explanation I can come up with is that a social media manager either didn't think about it, didn't understand it (which would be weird in itself for someone doing social media posts for Github), or just had a big ol' brain fart. Whatever the reason, I imagine someone got a good old-fashioned talking-to about it.
