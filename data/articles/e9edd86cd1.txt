England has roared to a record victory over New Zealand at Twickenham in the Nations Championship, after emotional scenes before the game involving former skipper Lewis Moody, who has Motor Neurone Disease (MND).
The English beat the All Blacks 37-12 to the delight of home fans, inflicting New Zealand's second-straight defeat after last week's loss to Wales.
Moody, who played 71 Tests for England between 2001 and 2011 and was part of the team's 2003 Rugby World Cup win, came onto the ground to a huge ovation and took his place with the team to face the NZ pre-match haka.
After the haka, various England players came to embrace Moody, who announced his MND diagnosis last year.
"Lewis is such an inspirational man, he was an inspirational player and, given his diagnosis, the players jumped at the opportunity to support him," current England captain Maro Itoje said.
"I think he and his family gave us that extra lift today. He came to spend some time with us yesterday. He presented us our shirts for today's game.
"He addressed us and there's no doubt that his presence, him joining us for the haka, him spending time with us, gave us an extra lift."
Itoje, though, played down the impact of how England responded to the haka.
"The most important thing is how you start the game against New Zealand," he said.
"You can do whatever you want. There are teams historically who've done different things, different formations.
"It's important, but the most important thing is how you start the game. I wouldn't pay too much attention to whatever anyone does to respond to the haka."
The English ran in five tries against NZ, but Itoje was determined that his side must keep improving.
After four victories in a row over Fiji, Argentina, Australia and Japan, England were braced for a much sterner test against the All Blacks, who were hurting from a shock loss to Wales.
But England demolished NZ, racing into an early lead and ultimately winning at a canter to record back-to-back victories over the All Blacks for the first time since 2003.
"I thought we took a step forward today," Itoje told reporters.
"I thought we took advantage of the opportunity we had, but … we don't want this to be our ceiling, we don't want this to be as good as it gets for England.
"We want to push, we want to get better. We have an amazing opportunity next week, however the chips fall to do that."
In other matches in the Nations Championship, Ireland beat world champions South Africa 20-17 in Dublin, Scotland overwhelmed Japan 67-5, and Fiji defeated Italy 21-16 in Udine.
South Africa's losing bonus point meant they topped the Southern Hemisphere standings, and will play in next week's final.
