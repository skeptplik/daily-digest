Avendus Capital, majority-owned by Mizuho Financial Group, plans to double its wealth managers to 150 over the next four years as it seeks a bigger share of India’s fast-growing but increasingly competitive market for rich clients.
“That would mean 15 to 20 relationship managers every year for the next four years,” Umang Papneja, chief executive officer of Mumbai-based Avendus Wealth Management, said in an interview. The firm will mainly recruit from campuses and tap experienced hires from rivals, he said.
The wealth unit, which manages around $9.4 billion of assets, aims to grow them by 20% every year, said Papneja, who joined the firm in September after heading the Indian unit of Swiss private bank Julius Baer for four years.
India’s wealth management industry is expanding as private equity exits, stock market listings and a new generation of entrepreneurs swell the ranks of the rich. That opportunity has drawn firms ranging from homegrown players such as Avendus and 360 One WAM to Axis Bank and HSBC Holdings, which are expanding their teams and offerings to capture more client assets.
Mizuho agreed last year to buy KKR’s 61.6% stake in Avendus and sought approval from other holders to increase that to 78.3%, boosting the Japanese financial group’s foothold in India.
“Almost 90% of new client money is coming from entrepreneurs and business owners versus traditional wealthy families,” Papneja said. Avendus will focus on two segments: wealthy Indians with over $20 million in investable assets and families with more than $100 million in assets, he said.
The firm is betting on its mix of institutional broking, investment banking, lending and family office services to grow its share of the wealth market.
India is among the fastest growing wealth markets in the world with about 944,000 millionaires and 211 billionaires as of 2025, a report by UBS Group shows. The country is also poised for one of the largest intergenerational wealth transfer, estimated at as much as $1.5 trillion over the next decade, according to Julius Baer and EY India.
Competition is spreading beyond the big centers. Investment firms backed by venture capital and private equity are gaining market share, while established money managers and private banks are expanding into smaller cities to reach newly wealthy clients.
At the same time, margins are coming under pressure as the industry gradually shifts away from the product-distribution model that has dominated in India for the past decade. More clients are increasingly opting to pay advisory fees rather than commissions embedded in investment products, forcing wealth managers to compete more aggressively on pricing and service.
“Going forward, advisory fees and client retention will define the wealth management industry and not products,” Papneja said.
