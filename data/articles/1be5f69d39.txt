Macy’s Herald Square put a celebrity spin on the unveiling of its iconic holiday windows Thursday evening with performances by Grammy-, Golden Globe- and Academy Award-winning recording artist and songwriter Ejae, and R&B and soul singer and actress Darlene Love.
Love sang her holiday classic “Christmas Baby Please Come Home” while Ejae performed “Christmas in New York.”
This year’s Macy’s windows celebrate the return of the “Blue Hippopotamus,” which mysteriously disappeared during Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade in 1931, but will be returning to next week’s parade for a return flight. This year’s Macy’s Thanksgiving Parade is its 100th.
Decades ago, Macy’s would release its giant helium balloons into the air and extend $25 rewards for any that were returned. That practice was eventually ended due to safety concerns. After the Blue Hippopotamus was released, there were reports of it flying close to the Empire State Building and heading toward the ocean.
Macy’s windows depict a young boy following the Blue Hippo and its adventures through the decades, discovering where the character animal has been and the magic he encounters along the way. “The windows bring the story to life through immersive scenes, interactive elements and opportunities for visitors to become a part of the experience themselves,” Macy’s indicated in a statement on its holiday windows.
For the windows unveiling, Macy’s situated a Blue Hippopotamus balloon outside, giving the crowd a small taste of what lies ahead with the parade. Considering it’s the 100th edition of the parade, the retailer will be going all out with several new floats and giant balloons.
Inside Macy’s Herald Square this year, there’s an expanded Holiday Square marketplace as well as new shops, restaurants, gifts and other items for sale throughout the flagship. Spanning more than 50,000 square feet, Holiday Square features more than 35 vendors, operated by local artisans and makers alongside specialty foods and festive experiences. There’s also a new Santaland experience.
