The fight over defense spending in Japan is heading toward a final showdown as a year-end deadline looms for a five-year plan, with proponents of fiscal sustainability pushing for a ¥50 trillion to ¥60 trillion budget and defense hawks arguing for at least ¥70 trillion.
Depending on the ultimate amount, progress in Japan’s defense buildup, as well as its relationship with the U.S., could hang in the balance.
As part of revisions to the country’s three key security documents due by the end of the year, the Defense Ministry had lobbied for an increase to as much as ¥80 trillion over the next five years — nearly doubling the ¥43 trillion plan announced in 2022.
