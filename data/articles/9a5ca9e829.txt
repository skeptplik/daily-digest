It is that time of the year again where hope springs eternal for all AFL clubs.
It is draft day and all 18 clubs are dreaming of selecting players who might play in their next premiership.
The draft can be a complicated beast these days, with academies and father-son selections muddying the waters for clubs looking to secure the best talent.
Here is everything you need to know about the 2026 AFL draft.
When is the AFL draft?
This year's draft will be held across two nights on November 19 and November 20.
November 19 will see the first round of prospects having their names called before the second round and beyond takes place on the following night.
How can I follow the AFL draft?
ABC Sport will have live blogs running on both nights of the draft, bringing you news and analysis of every pick.
The draft will also be broadcast live on Fox Footy and Kayo Sports.
What time is the AFL draft?
Both nights of the draft will start at 7pm AEDT.
Here are the corresponding times for other regions of Australia.
- 6:30pm ACDT: South Australia
- 6pm AEST: Queensland
- 5:30pm ACST: Northern Territory
- 4pm AWST: Western Australia
Who are the players expected to be selected early?
The top few picks of this year's draft appear to be set in stone as night one approaches.
Two prospects — Dougie Cochrane and Cody Walker — are already tied to clubs and are expected to go within the first five picks.
Cochrane is eligible to join Port Adelaide as a part of its Next Generation Academy (NGA), while Walker is tied to Carlton under the father-son rule after his father, Andrew, played 202 games for the club.
In the open pool, Ethan Drever is expected to have his name called by Essendon after its bid on Cochrane is matched by Port at the top of the draft.
The draft will get interesting when Richmond selects after Essendon, with names such as Arki Butler, Harry Van Hattum and Heath Mellody all in consideration at that spot.

How does the bidding system work in the AFL draft?
The AFL assigns each draft pick with a points value. The first pick in the draft has the most value, with 3,000 points, and the points value of each pick decreases all the way down until pick 54, the final pick of the third round, which is worth 14 points. Any picks from then onwards have no points value.
There have been several changes to the bidding system this year to make it tougher for clubs that finish higher up the ladder to match a bid on one of their academy prospects:
- Clubs can now only use two picks to match a bid, up to and including pick 36
- A discount or loading will be applied when clubs match a bid, depending on where that club finishes on the ladder
- The previous year's grand finalists have to pay 20 per cent extra to match any bid, while teams that made the preliminary final have to pay 10 per cent extra
- Teams that finished between 5th and 10th have no discount or loading, while teams that finished outside the top 10 receive a 10 per cent discount on bids
- Clubs that finish in the bottom five on the ladder and have their top pick pushed back due to a matched bid will be compensated with an extra pick in the second round
Can teams do trades during the draft?
They sure can!
Since being introduced in 2018, the ability for teams to trade during the draft has added an extra wrinkle to the whole process.
Teams can trade with each other using their own picks in the current draft as well as using picks in the 2027 or 2028 drafts.
