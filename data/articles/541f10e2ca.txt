Another day, another Rihanna sighting at Giorgio Baldi. While most stars reserve their grandest looks for red carpets, galas, or film festivals, Rihanna’s preferred runway is the entrance to her favorite upscale Italian restaurant in Santa Monica, California. Over the years, the Fenty Beauty founder has used the venue to unveil some of her most-memorable looks—the sculptural white Alaïa set, the patchwork denim-and-leopard-print trench, the satin oyster-hued car coat with stacks on stacks of pearls. And she has yet to run out of ideas.
On Tuesday, Rihanna attempted a more under-the-radar visit, entering the restaurant through a tucked-away back entrance. And while her arrival may have been quiet, her outfit certainly wasn’t.
As one of the masters of high-low dressing, the “Umbrella” singer flexed her talents yet again, pairing an ornate, baroque duffle coat with a casual pair of baggy blue jeans.
Her oversize outerwear, courtesy of the Dries Van Noten Fall/Winter 2026 collection by Julian Klausner, was made with a golden floral jacquard that looked like a hanging tapestry transformed into wearable art. The piece featured a wide hood and toggle buttons in front that added a nautical spin, while around back, it featured a single vent and a martingale belt.
As a steadfast champion of a statement piece, RiRi chose a garment that was bold and fabulous, but could also help her attempt to remain incognito (a reality that’s nearly impossible when, well, you’re Rihanna).
The piece’s large hood and enveloping silhouette concealed the superstar, while in contrast, its gilded hue made her hard to miss. The coat made such a statement, in fact, that the mother of three knew she didn’t need to overwhelm it, which is why she paired it with a simple pair of barrel jeans and a gold collar necklace that complemented its metallic color. She finished things off with a pair of thin, rectangular sunglasses for a last low-key touch.
With this endorsement from Rihanna, and recent stamps of approval from other stars like Jennifer Lawrence, it appears Dries Van Noten coats are going to rule the outerwear game this season.
