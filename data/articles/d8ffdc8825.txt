Final Avengers: Doomsday trailer racks up over 500 million views in 1st day
While many fans are hyped, some have called out some of the trailer's visuals
It's safe to say there's a fair amount of hype around the upcoming release of Avengers: Doomsday.
Marvel released the final trailer for the upcoming superhero movie on Tuesday, and after just 24 hours it had already generated 563 million views.
That makes it the most viewed Disney trailer of all time, beating out the previous Avengers: Doomsday teaser, which hit 503 million views in the first 24 hours. It's also already the second most viewed movie trailer of all time, just below Spider-Man: Brand New Day, which has over 718 million views.
The film, directed by Anthony Russo and Joe Russo — who also directed Avengers: Infinity War and Avengers: Endgame — is set to hit theatres Dec. 18.
The trailer starts by showing Steve Rogers, played by Chris Evans, as he talks about fatherhood, and if he should tell his son about his past life as Captain America and an Avenger. He's then approached by Reed Richards, played by Pedro Pascal, and the rest of the Fantastic Four.
That's followed by a shot of Doctor Doom, played by Robert Downey Jr., accompanied by three mysterious masked women.
More heroes meet each other (and in some cases fight) including Canadian Simu Liu as Shang-Chi, Ian McKellen as Magneto and Chris Hemsworth as Thor. Though he doesn't appear in this trailer, Doomsday will be the final big-screen role for Patrick Stewart, who plays Charles Xavier, the leader of the X-Men.
Mixed reviews
"My family and I have booked the entire movie theatre for ourselves for the premiere of Avengers: Doomsday," posted X user @JesabelRaay. "They all couldn't stop talking about the new trailer."
But not all the hype is positive.
In the final scene, many of the heroes come together on a sort of battleground, and viewers weren't impressed.
"Avengers: Doomsday looks hideous … it visually looks awful," said @constantine_james7 in a video on Instagram.
Another Instagramer, @screened_scored, said it looked like "they took a bunch of individual characters, cut them out, and placed them against a digital background."
What is hype worth?
The last Avengers film, 2019's Avengers: Endgame, made $2.9 billion US at the box office (With a bump from the recently released slightly updated version) making it the highest-grossing movie of all time, not accounting for inflation.
But other recent films with high trailer views have done well.
While many fans are excited for the upcoming Avengers: Doomsday movie, some took to social media to complain about some of the visuals in the latest trailer. (Marvel Studios)
The hype around Spider-Man: Brand New Day translated into box office success, with the movie pulling in $2.52 billion US worldwide
Deadpool & Wolverine's 2024 Super Bowl teaser scored 365 million views if you include those who watched the trailer during the Super Bowl broadcast, and that movie took in $1.3 billion US globally.
Possible hidden secrets
Keen-eyed viewers believe they aren't exactly seeing everything in these trailers.
At the end of the recent release, many of the heroes team up for a classic "Avengers, assemble" shot that often accompanies these big team-up movies when all the heroes unite to fight against common enemy.
But some viewers have pointed out that there are some gaps where they believe characters have been digitally edited out.
While much of the cast has been announced, there are still some unknowns around who will appear in the movie, such as three characters pictured here. (Marvel Studios)
It wouldn't be the first time.
In a trailer for Spider-Man: No Way Home, Tobey Maguire and Andrew Garfield playing their versions of Spider-Man were edited out of the trailer that otherwise would've shown them swinging into action with Tom Holland's Spider-Man.
In trailers for Endgame, Thor and Bruce Banner's Smart Hulk were edited out of scenes, so it wouldn't give away their appearance.
Tickets for the Dec. 18 release of Avengers: Doomsday have been on sale since July. It will premier the same day as Dune: Part Three directed by Canadian Denis Villeneuve.
