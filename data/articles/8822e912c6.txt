Did you see the Facebook post about Japan making an “urgent appeal” to its citizens to stock up on three days’ worth of food and supplies, implying that there was some kind of secret disaster in the offing? Or the one screaming “BREAKING NEWS” about a magnitude 4.6 earthquake that is about as routine as it gets? Or the one about the 95% chance of a major earthquake striking the Tokyo area on one of two days in early November on a page claiming to “forecast” temblors?
Welcome to social media in the era of mass-produced slop.
Online spaces becoming overwhelmed with sensational posts designed to drive engagement is not a Japan-only problem. But when it comes to English-language information about Japan, especially information about life and death situations, it is alarming to see so much in my feeds that is either overhyped, misleading or outright false. This is especially key for foreign residents of Japan, who naturally have varying levels of Japanese language skills and may not know where to go for reliable information.
