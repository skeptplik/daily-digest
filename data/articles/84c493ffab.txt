Despite the dramatic timing — before Grand Theft Auto 6's release, even before reviews for Rockstar's game have been published — nominations for The Game Awards 2026 weren't very surprising. This was always going to be GTA 6's year.
If there was one surprising thing, it's that, after what seemed to be an unpredictable run-up, the Game of the Year lineup was resolved in the most predictable way imaginable. We could have had Pokémon Pokopia, Big Walk, and Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave. Instead, we got yet more games made in GTA's image.
Here are our five big takeaways from The Game Awards 2026.
1 GTA 6 is a strong as we thought
Ahead of reviews going live, The Game Awards' international jury of games media gave Grand Theft Auto 6 a resounding vote of confidence with eight nominations, including in Game of the Year. It's a preview of the wave of critical acclaim that's always been expected from a studio, Rockstar Games, whose last two games both achieved ratings of 97 on Metacritic.
Eight nominations might not sound as overwhelming as the 13 secured by Clair Obscur: Expedition 3 last year. (Once the Players' Voice nominees are revealed, that number will surely go up to nine.) But the important thing is that GTA 6 secured a nomination in every single category it was reasonably eligible for, sweeping all the craft awards as well as getting into its genre category (Action/Adventure Game) and Game of the Year. The nods in the Best Narrative and Best Performance categories are particularly notable, indicating that GTA 6 has dramatic substance as well as technical virtuosity.
Whether it will win every one of those categories is another matter, but it remains the overwhelming favorite for Game of the Year.
2 The AAA action-adventure reigns supreme once more
The Game Awards has always been associated with its penchant for cinematic action-adventure games with slick production values. But recent years have seen much more diverse line-ups in Game of the Year. Since 2021, GOTY has been won by a family co-op game, a platformer, and three fairly hardcore RPGs. In 2024, three RPGs, one platformer, one action-adventure, and one indie card game were nominated. In 2025, it was two RPGs, two indie action games, one platformer and one action-adventure.
In 2026, the field is five action-adventures and one action game that looks an awful lot like an action-adventure. They're not all from big publishers — three are self-published by the developers, an interesting trend — but they can all be reasonably described as AAA productions. This is a massive backward step for representation of the full breadth of what gaming can offer.
This can't be laid entirely at the jury's door. It was a weak year for RPGs, and while there were excellent indie games, there were no breakout indie hits that grabbed widespread attention. But that just makes the lineup a demonstration of the flattened taste and lack of adventurousness of The Game Awards' large international jury.
3 The Game Awards sure loves Remedy Entertainment
With six nominations, including Game Direction, Narrative, and Game of the Year, you would be forgiven for thinking that Control Resonant is a critical smash with a 90-plus Metacritic rating. In fact, it's quite divisive, and its Metacritic rating of 83 is at the lower end for a GOTY nominee.
But The Game Awards jury just loves Remedy games. The original Control was nominated despite a Metascore of 82. The admittedly great Alan Wake 2 had eight nominations and won Best Game Direction and Best Narrative from under the noses of The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom and Baldur's Gate 3.
With the perfect combination of commercial production values, aspirational storytelling, and outsider cred, Remedy remains catnip for the jury.
4 In the end, Crimson Desert was absolutely nowhere
After an initial wave of hype, Crimson Desert's GOTY hopes were dashed against the rocky shore of a distinctly mediocre critical reception. But the narrative that it remained a contender proved hard to shake, powered by a vocal fanbase and developer Pearl Abyss' relentless schedule of updates and improvements.
Its one nomination for Best Community Support is fitting and well deserved. Crimson Desert might have been elbowed out of the running in other categories by Phantom Blade Zero, another ambitious game from an insurgent Asian developer, this one with better review scores. But striking out in all the craft categories and even Best Role-Playing Game — in a quiet year for RPGs — just shows how weak Crimson Desert really was in the eyes of The Game Awards jury.
It's a vital reminder that The Game Awards are largely voted on by game reviewers, and if game reviewers don't like your game, you're toast.
5 Inconsistent category rules undermine the results
At The Game Awards, there are six nominees for Game of the Year and five for most other categories. Which ones? Well, that varies.
This year, there were six nominees for Game for Impact, Best Action/Adventure Game, and Best Multiplayer Game. Last year, there were six nominees for Best Performance, Best Independent Game, Best Family Game, and Best Sim/Strategy Game. In 2023, it was Best Game Direction and Games for Impact.
The reasoning is that this happens when there's a tie in the voting tabulation. That sounds fair enough, but really, The Game Awards needs to find a way to resolve such ties by changing its voting system (preferential ballots would be one option). Somehow, ties that might expand the fixed GOTY category are avoided.
The loosey-goosey category limits undermine all aspects of the results, especially the total tallies of nominations attached to particular games or publishers. One reason Clair Obscur's nominations record may never be beaten is that it had three Performance nominees in a field of six. That's not fair. The Game Awards needs to fix this.
