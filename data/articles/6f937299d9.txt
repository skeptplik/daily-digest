Some customers have raised concerns about Backblaze’s privacy policy taking effect next month, which says the backup and cloud storage company may sell certain personal information to advertisers.
Backblaze’s current privacy policy says that in addition to the personal information that users may provide, such as their email address when creating an account, it may also “automatically” collect information, such as “your software, the operating system you use when accessing our service, your Internet Protocol address, and the date and time of each request you make to Backblaze.”
The current policy adds: “Under no circumstances do we rent, trade, or share your address or e-mail address with any other company for their marketing purposes without your consent.” It also makes no mention of advertisers.
On December 1, a new privacy policy takes effect. It includes a table listing the types of “general personal information” that Backblaze says it has been collecting and sharing with third parties, including “advertising and marketing partners” since December 1, 2025, This information includes identifiers (such as “name, address, Internet Protocol address, [and] email address”); commercial information (such as “records of personal property, products or services purchased, obtained, or considered, or other purchasing or consuming histories or tendencies”; geolocation data that “can be used to determine a device’s physical location”; and “sensory or surveillance data” (which includes “call recordings or other audio, electronic, or visual monitoring or surveillance”).
