JD Vance blames Iran war for midterm thumping. Is he really blaming Trump?
VP's public disavowal of the war is clear sign he's positioning himself for 2028 presidential run
U.S. Vice-President JD Vance is blaming the Republican Party's decisive defeat in the midterm elections on the war that President Donald Trump launched against Iran.
Vance's comments, on the morning after the Republicans lost control of both the House and the Senate, are a sharp rebuke of one of Trump's most consequential actions.
"The real reason we lost is obvious to almost everyone," Vance wrote in a lengthy post Wednesday on X. "The reason we lost is the war. The president himself said so last night."
It's far from clear that Trump is admitting the war cost his party the midterms. In his comments on election night, the president blamed the results on the fact his name wasn't on the ballot and maintained launching the war was the right call.
Asked by reporters to respond to Vance's post on Wednesday, Trump pulled his punches somewhat.
"I think it's largely true, because [the Iran war] drove the gasoline up a little bit short-term," he said at an event to show off construction progress of the new White House ballroom.
"But at the same time, if I had it to do again, I'd have to do it again, because long term, it's the only answer. You can't let Iran have a nuclear weapon."
Vance questions political price of war
The full scale of the Republican defeat will only become clear when all House races are called, but it's already being described in historic terms. This is only the third time in the past 80 years that a sitting U.S. president has seen his party lose control of both chambers in a midterm election.
While Trump is insisting the war was worth it, Vance, who is considered a likely contender for president in 2028, is questioning its political price.
"If you'd like to argue it was worth it, that losing the Senate for the next 6 years was worth it, then do so," Vance said in the post.
A former marine who served in the Iraq war, Vance had been critical of that conflict and said after becoming vice-president that a Trump administration would not send troops into the "open-ended conflicts" of the past.
After Trump launched the war with Iran this past spring, Vance was noticeably quiet in speaking about it, a conspicuous change for a politician known for being a pugnacious defender of the president.
This summer, Vance tried to put forward a rationale for the conflict, telling conservative podcast host Megyn Kelly in an interview: "If we transformed the Middle East, this is fundamentally worth it."
In his Wednesday post, Vance laid out the top four things Republicans have to do to win back disaffected voters, based on focus group research by a pollster he did not name:
- End the war.
- Focus on cost of living/inflation.
- Put America first.
- Change the country's relationship with Israel.
Unlike Trump, Vance declined to endorse Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for re-election in that country's recent vote and this summer publicly chastised members of Israel's government for opposing Trump's efforts to resolve the war with Iran.
Republicans are now conducting a full-scale autopsy of the midterm thumping. Vance was not the only prominent member of the party pointing fingers over the loss.
Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis described the results in epic terms.
"This is as close to an extinction level event for Republicans in many parts of the country as we've seen probably in the last 100 years," DeSantis told a news conference Wednesday. "This is probably the political worst-case scenario for a midterm election."
DeSantis said some Republican messaging didn't address the real concerns of voters, specificially questioning why hundreds of millions of dollars were spent in campaign ads on transgender issues.

Republican Rep. Don Bacon of Nebraska, a frequent critic of the president, blamed the loss squarely on Trump and his policies.
"The President had a 35% favorable rating and voters disliked his gas prices, chaos, tariffs, abusing allies and hugging Putin," Bacon said in one of a series of critical posts on X.
"The President’s global tariffs were brutal to the GOP in the Midwest," Bacon wrote. "We should have never defended them."
