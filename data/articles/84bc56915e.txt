Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi met with her Chilean and Peruvian counterparts in Tokyo Friday to boost ties with the two countries.
The Latin American countries are home to critical mineral resources that would be a major asset for Japan.
“We confirmed that Chile, as a major supplier of copper and lithium to Japan, will deepen its cooperation and coordination with Japan to strengthen the resilience of supply chains for critical minerals,” Takaichi said in a joint news conference after meeting with Chilean President Jose Antonio Kast, adding that they had also agreed to strengthen defense cooperation.
Japanese Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi also met with his Chilean counterpart Fernando Barros on the sidelines of the leadership summit to further discuss the strengthening of security cooperation.
A similar pledge to cooperate on securing critical mineral supply chains was made at a meeting earlier on Friday with Peruvian President Keiko Fujimori. Peru is a major supplier of copper to Japan.
Tokyo has sought alternative sources of rare earth minerals since China choked off exports to Japan in January this year in response to Takaichi’s comments in support of Taiwan.
In addition to agreeing to collaborate with both Chile and Peru on agriculture, education, information and communications technology, and economic investment, Japan pledged disaster response support — particularly through the transfer of disaster response equipment to Peru — as part of its El Nino assistance.
Friday’s meetings marked the first bilateral summits between Takaichi and her Chilean and Peruvian counterparts since Kast and Fujimori took office earlier this year.
Japan and Chile are preparing to mark the 130th anniversary of diplomatic relations in 2027, and a logo commemorating the anniversary was publicly unveiled at Friday’s post-summit news conference.
Citing Prime Minister Shinzo Abe’s 2014 visit, Kast extended an invitation to Takaichi to meet again in Chile and encouraged Japanese companies to participate in the Choose Chile Summit planned for March 2027.
The talks also come fresh on the heels of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, hosted by Chinese President Xi Jinping. In contrast to Takaichi, who was given the cold shoulder by Xi during her visit, Peru and Chile received a warm welcome by China — both countries’ largest trading partner — with Peru receiving a pledge of $22.5 million in assistance for El Nino.
