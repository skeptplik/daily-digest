Do you know absolutely nothing about cricket, but want to get your head around the ongoing Sandpapergate scandal?
Here's a quick guide to the drama — absolutely zero knowledge of cricket rules needed.
What is Sandpapergate?
During a Test match against South Africa in March 2018, Australia's Cameron Bancroft was caught using an object, which was later revealed to be sandpaper, to rough up one side of a ball.

Uneven wear on the ball can cause it to move through the air in unpredictable ways.
That would have made it harder for the South African batters to see and hit it and therefore, given Australia an unfair advantage.
A whole lot happened in the fallout, but the dust mostly settled after an investigation by Cricket Australia (CA), completed just four days after the match, found only three players were involved in the plan to use sandpaper.
The Cricket Australia investigation found the rest of the squad was not involved in the plan to tamper with the ball.

Why are we talking about it now?
That's thanks to Usman Khawaja.
In an autobiography published this month, he said the entire team had known about the plot to tamper with the ball.
"It was all of us. Not the batters, not the bowlers, not the staff. All of us," he told the Sydney Morning Herald.
He also said sandpaper had been used to rough up the ball in the Test prior to the one in which Bancroft was caught.
While Khawaja declined to name names, his account implicated several men who still play for the national team, including current captain Pat Cummins.
Warner added his weight to Khawaja's account, saying it was "100 per cent accurate", adding that the CA investigation at the time was "rushed and botched".
On November 13, Cummins categorically denied Khawaja's comments.
"Usman's a friend, but friends aren't casual with each other's lives and reputations," he said at one stage in a 68-minute press conference."In his book, Uzzy's been casual with my life and the lives of many others, and I think Australian cricket."
But a 4,000-word article written by Cameron Bancroft and published in the West Australian on Wednesday cast significant doubt on denials by Cummins and others.
Bancroft said Cummins's press conference was "the final straw" and had left him feeling "angry, betrayed and alone".
Where Khawaja had dodged questions about precisely who knew what, Bancroft's article offered unprecedented detail about what he said happened.
He said he had discussed the condition of the ball with Cummins and that Lehmann, now an ABC commentator, knew about the use of sandpaper.
Lehmann declined to offer a detailed response on Wednesday, saying he needed time to read Bancroft's account.
CA announced on Wednesday it would launch a voluntary forum for players and staff involved in the infamous Test match to discuss their recollections of the incident.
When asked if a future investigation was on the table, CA chief Todd Greenberg stressed that "the investigation has been completed".
"The forum is designed [for] people who weren't interviewed or weren't offered the opportunity or didn't feel like they had the opportunity to contribute," he said.
